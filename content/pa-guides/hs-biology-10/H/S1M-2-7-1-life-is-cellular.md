Cell: The common structure that makes up living things.

Resolution: the shortest distance between two points that can be distinguished

Calculation of Magnification:

- Method 1: eyepiece lens x objective lens
- Method 2: size of image / actual size of specimen
- Method 3: measured length of scale bar (image) / actual value scale bar represents (real size units)

Microscopes: Produce magnified images of structures that are too small to see with the unaided eye.

**Light Microscope:**

- Use glass lenses to focus light and magnify an object
- Magnification < 1000x
- Compound light microscope has two lenses
- Scientists can use fluorescent dyes to show structures inside the cells

**Electron Microscopes:**

- Focus beams of electrons with magnetic fields instead of light
- Higher magnification (1 billionth of a meter in size)
- Samples are dried out (vacuumed)
- Therefore electron microscopes cannot view living cells; only dead, prepared specimens
- **Types of Electron Microscopes:**
- **Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM):**
- **Shine beams of electrons through thin slices of cells**
- **2D image**
- **Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM):**
- **Electrons scan back and forth through surface**
- **3D image**

**The Cell Theory Timeline:**

- Robert Hooke
- First to observe dead cells from a thin slice of cork (1665)

Defined “cell”

- Auton Van Leeuwenhoek
- First to observe living cells in a drop of water (1674)
- Matthias Schleiden
- Proposed that all plants are made of cells (1838)
- Theodor Schwann
- Proposed that all animals are made from cells (1839)
- Rudoff Virchow
- Proposed that all cells come from existing cells (1855)

**The Cell Theory**

- All living things are composed of cells
- Cells are the basic units of structure and function in living things
- New cells are produced from existing cells

**Prokaryotes vs. Eukaryotes**

Eukaryote: an organism whose cells contain a nucleus

- Contain nuclei, cell membrane & cytoplasm
- Contain membrane bound organelles
- Carry out all life activities inside specialized organelles
- e.g. plants, animals, protists, fungi…
- DNA associated with protein (chromosomes)

Prokaryote: a unicellular organism that lacks a nucleus

- Smaller & simpler than Eukaryotes
- DNA is circular, naked
- Have cell membrane & cytoplasm
- no internal membrane (membrane-bound organelles)
- carry out all life processes in one cell
- e.g. bacteria

## 7.2 Cell Structures

**Cell Organization:**

- Eukaryotic cell:
- Nucleus
- Cytoplasm
- Cell membrane
- …
- Prokaryotic cells:
- Cell wall
- Plasma membrane
- Cytoplasm
- Ribosomes
- Nucleoid (Region containing naked DNA)
- Some have flagella or pili (hair-like protection for attachment and conjugation)

**Nucleus:**

- Control cell processes and contain heredity information of DNA
- Coded instruction to make protein and other molecules
- Chromatin:
- Material of eukaryotic chromosomes (coiled condensed to form chromosomes)
- DNA bound to histone proteins
- Chromosomes:
- Condense chromatin during cell division
- Distinct threadlike structures visible under a microscope
- Carry genetic information
- Nucleolus – small dense region in nucleus where the assembly of ribosomes begins
- Nuclear Envelope – double membrane surrounding the nucleus; nuclear pores allow materials to move through, such as RNA and other molecules

**Vacuoles:**

- Saclike, membrane enclosed structure to store water, salts, proteins, carbohydrates, etc.
- Plants:
- Large central vacuole
- Can support heavy structures such as leaves & flowers
- Can be found in some unicellular organisms
- e.g. contractile vacuole that pumps excess water out of cell

**Vesicles:**

- Smaller vacuoles involve in storing and transporting substances between organelles/ to and from the outside of the cell
- Nealy all eukaryotic cells have vesicles

**Lysosomes:**

- Small organelles filled with enzymes
- Break down food materials
- Break down outlived organelles
- Removing “junk” that might otherwise pile up in the cell

**Cytoskeleton:**

- Prefix “cyto-” means cell
- Network of protein filaments
- Help cell maintain shape
- Facilitate cell movement

e.g.:

Microfilament

- Long fibers made of actin
- Movement & support

Microtubules

- Hollow tubes made of tubulin
- Maintain cell shape
- Tracks that organelles are moved along
- Help separate chromosomes during cell division

Centrioles

  - Found in pairs
  - Organize microtubules
  - Play a key role in cell division
  - Only in animal cell

Cilia & Flagella

  - Hair-like projections formed from cell surface
  - Produce force to help move
  - “9+2” microtubules structure
- Functions: structure & support/ intracellular transport/ contractility & motility/ spatial organization

**Ribosomes:**

- Small particles made of RNA & proteins
- Make protein according to instructions from nucleus

**Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER):**

- Rough ER
- Has bound ribosomes on the surface
- Modify proteins made by ribosomes (membrane proteins/ secretory proteins)
- Receive proteins from ribosomes
- Some proteins made on the rough ER will leave the cell; others are membrane proteins and proteins that go to lysosomes and other specialized locations inside the cell
- Smooth ER
- No ribosomes on surface
- Synthesis of lipids & detoxification

**Golgi Apparatus/ Golgi Body:**

- Stacks of membrane to modify, sort, package protein & other materials
- Pathway of proteins: Ribosomes -> rough ER -> vesicle -> Golgi Body -> vesicle -> destination

**Chloroplasts:**

- Exist in plants
- Use solar energy to make energy-rich food molecules by photosynthesis
- 2 envelope membranes
- Large stacks of photosynthetic membranes containing chlorophyll

**Mitochondria:**

- Use chemical energy from food to make high-energy compounds (ATP)
- 2 envelope membranes
- Inner membrane is much larger than the outer one
- Inner membrane is highly folded to increase surface area (more enzymes can react)
- In nearly all eukaryotic cells

**Organelle DNA:**

- Chloroplast & mitochondria contain DNA
- Mitochondria DNA inherited from cytoplasm of ovum (egg)
- Mitochondria come from mother
- Circular strand DNA
- In mitochondria
- Similar in those in prokaryotes
- Evidence for endosymbiotic theory

**Cell Wall:**

- Plants, algae, fungi, most prokaryotes
- Not in animal cells
- Lie outside the cell membrane
- Porous: allow substances pass through
- Provide support & protection
- Made from fibers of carbohydrates & protein
- Plant cell wall (made of mainly cellulose)
- Fungus cell wall (made of mainly chitin)
- Bacteria cell wall (made of peptidoglycan)

**Cell Membrane:**

- Boundary of cell
- Regulate what enters and leaves the cell
- Provide protection & support
- Structure: fluid mosaic model
- Selectively permeable
- Only some substances can pass through
- Structure: Phospholipid bilayer
- Form barrier between cell and surroundings
- Lipids are large molecules made mostly from carbon and hydrogen atoms
- Composed of a hydrophilic head and a hydrophobic tail
- Compose of 1 glycerol + 2 fatty acid + 1 phosphate group
- Tail (Fatty acid portion): hydrophobic
- Head (glycerol with phosphate): hydrophilic
- In water, fatty acid tails cluster together to form bilayers
- Membrane proteins:
- Provide many functions
- Integral proteins embedded in membrane:
- Cytoplasmic/extracellular side: hydrophilic
- Cone: hydrophobic
- Typically, transmembrane
- Peripheral protein
- Most attached to surface of integral proteins
- Carbohydrates
- Attached to some membrane proteins
- Cell-to-cell identification
- Cholesterol (only in animal cells)
- Located in the hydrophobic region (fatty acid tails)
- Regulate membrane fluidity

## 7.3 Cell Transport

- Solution (e.g. cytoplasm)
- Liquid mixture of at least 2 substances
- Solute dissolve in solvent
- Concentration
- Mass/volume
- Mole/liter

**Types of Cell Transport:**

**Passive Transport:** Does not require energy (ATP)

- **Diffusion**
- Molecules tend to move from high concentration to lower concentration (reach equilibrium)
- Equilibrium
- Concentration on both side is the same
- No further change in concentration
- Solutes continue to move in both directions in equal numbers (no net movement)
- Substances move across membrane
- e.g. carbon dioxide, oxygen can be carried by diffusion
- properties of these substances include:
- non-polar
- small (so they can directly cross the membrane)
- Factors that influence the rate of reaction:
- Temperature (higher temperature means faster rate)
- Concentration Difference (greater concentration difference, faster rate)
- **Facilitated Diffusion**
- Movement of specific molecules across cell membrane through transport proteins
- From high to low concentration
- e.g. water, glucose
- property: polar, large
- Channel protein
- shape will not be changed
- Carrier protein
- Shape will change
- Factors that influence the rate of reaction:
- Temperature (if temperature is too high, the proteins will be distorted and stop working)
- Concentration difference
- The number of specific carrier or channel proteins in membrane
- **Osmosis**
- Involve only the diffusion of H2O access a selectively permeable membrane
- Selectively permeable membrane
- Only some substances can pass through
- Biological membranes
- Aquaporins
- Many cells contain water channel proteins named aquaporins
- allow water to pass faster through cell membrane
- water moves from high water concentration to low water concentration
- water moves from low solute concentration to high solute concentration to dilute the concentrated solution
- water moves from more free water to less free water
- Isotonic solution:
- Concentration of water & solute are the same both sides
- Hypertonic solution:
- High concentration of solute
- Cell shrinks
- Hypotonic solution:
- Lower concentration of solute
- Cell swells
- Water moves from hypotonic solution to hypertonic solution
- Osmotic Pressure
- It is the pressure exerted by osmosis on the hypertonic side of selectively permeable membrane (force produced by the difference in concentration)
- The pressure can cause the movement of water out of or into a cell through osmosis
- Osmotic pressure can cause cell in hypotonic solution to swell and eventually burst
- Plants & Bacteria have cell wall to prevent cells from expanding
- Contractile vacuole: will excrete excess water to prevent bursting

**Active Transport:** Requires energy (ATP)

Two forms:

- Protein pump:
- Moves materials against a concentration difference (from low to high concentration)
- e.g. Sodium ion and potassium ion pump
- use to maintain the resting potential
- 3 Na+ moving out of the cell and 2 K+ in
- Cause electrical concentration difference
- Bulk Transport:
- Transport larger molecules or a large number of molecules/ions by movements of cell membrane
- Include exocytosis & endocytosis
- Endocytosis
- Process of taking material into the cell by infoldings, or pockets of the cell membrane
- Form a vesicle entering the cytoplasm
- e.g.
- Amoebas use this method to take in food
- White blood cells use endocytosis to engulf damaged cells
- Phagocytosis:
- The process of large particles (solid) is taken into the cell by endocytosis
- Pinocytosis
- The process of solutions (liquid) is taken into the cell by endocytosis
- Exocytosis
- Removal of large amounts of materials from cell
- Membrane of vesicles fuses with the cell membrane
- Steps (reverse of endocytosis):
- Proteins (made by ribosomes) enters the ER
- Protein exists ER, entering Golgi apparatus by a vesicle
- Protein is modified and exists on the trans face of Golgi apparatus inside a vesicle
- Vesicle with the modified protein moves to and fuses with the plasma membrane
- Cells use exocytosis to get rid of wastes and give off chemical signals
