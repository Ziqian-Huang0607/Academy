Forms of Energy

- Include light, heat, electricity and chemical energy
- Living things use chemical energy as fuels

ATP and ADP

- Energy can be stored in chemical compounds such as ATP
- Storing energy:
  - Cells can store small amounts of energy by adding a phosphate group to ADP
    - ADP + Pi → ATP
  - ATP is used by all types of cells as basic energy source
- Released energy:
  - ATP → ADP + Pi
  - Subtract 3rd phosphate group
    - Energy released
- Adenosine triphosphate (ATP)
  - Adenine (containing-nitrogen)
  - Ribose (5-carbon sugar)
  - Three phosphate groups
- Adenosine diphosphate (ADP)
  - Adenine (containing-nitrogen)
  - Ribose (5-carbon sugar)
  - Two phosphate groups

Using Biochemical Energy

- Active transport
  - Sodium-potassium pump
    - Move Na+ out and K+ in
    - Energy provided by one ATP molecule
      - 3Na+ out, 2K+ in
- Movement within the cell
  - Motor protein moves organelles along microtubules
  - Energy source: ATP
- ATP also powers other events
  - Protein synthesis
  - Responses to chemical signals at cell surface
  - Produces light

ATP and Glucose

- ATP can only store small amount of energy
- Glucose can store 90 times more energy than ATP
- Cell regenerates ATP from ADP by using energy in food (e.g. glucose, protein, lipid)

Autotrophs and Heterotrophs

- Autotrophs
  - Make own food
  - Plants and some other organisms use light energy to produce food by photosynthesis
  - Some organisms use chemical energy to produce food
- Heterotrophs
  - Obtain energy from foods they consume

8.2 & 8.3 Photosynthesis

Discovery of Photosynthesis

- Jan Van Helmont
  - Watered the plant for 5 years and measured the mass of soil
  - Since only water was added, he concluded the mass came from water
  - Soil was not the main source for plant's growth
- Joseph Priestley
  - Used a candle, a jar and mint plant
  - Mint released oxygen gas
- Jan Ingenhousz
  - Light is necessary for plants to produce oxygen gas

The Photosynthesis Equation

- In the presence of light, plants transform carbon dioxide and water into carbohydrates and release oxygen
  - 6CO2 + 6H2O --light--> C6H12O6 + 6O2
  - Remember: light is not a reactant

Location

- Chloroplasts
  - Outer and inner membranes
  - Thylakoids
    - Saclike photosynthetic membrane containing chlorophyll
  - Stroma
    - Fluid portion, outside of thylakoids
  - Grana (sig. granum)
    - Stacks of thylakoid membrane

Light and Pigments

- The "white" sunlight is a mixture of different colors
  - The visible sunlight makes up the visible spectrum
- Photosynthesis requires light and pigments, molecules in chloroplasts
- Pigment
  - Light-absorbing molecules of plants
  - Chlorophyll (principal pigment)
    - Chlorophyll a
    - Chlorophyll b
- Chlorophyll absorbs well in violet-blue & red regions, but not well in green region
- Carotenoids
  - Red, orange and yellow pigment
  - Absorb light in violet and blue-green region
- As temperature drops, chlorophyll breaks down first, leaving carotenoid pigments to see in the autumn

Energy Collection

- Chlorophyll absorbs the light
  - Energy level of electrons in chlorophyll raised
- High-energy electrons make photosynthesis work

Electron Carriers

- A compound that can accept a pair of high energy electrons and transfer them to another molecule
  - E.g. NADP+
  - NADP+ + 2 high energy e- + H+ → NADPH
- A substance loses electrons, or is oxidized
- A substance gains electrons, or is reduced

Two Sets of Reactions

- Light-dependent reactions
  - In thylakoid membrane
  - Need light and water
  - Photosystems (PSII and PSI)
    - Clusters of chlorophyll and proteins on thylakoid membrane
    - Contain light-harvesting complex
      - Accessory pigment
      - A matrix of protein
    - Contain reaction-center complex
      - A pair of chlorophyll α
      - A matrix of protein
      - A primary electron acceptor
  - Use solar energy to produce O2 and convert ADP & NADP+ into energy carriers ATP & NADPH
    - 2H2O + ADP + Pi + NADP+ + 2e- + H+ --light--> O2 + ATP + NADPH
  - 1. PS II
    - Electrons (P680) are excited after absorbing energy from light
    - Electrons in chlorophyll are resupplied via photolysis (splits water into protons, electrons, and oxygen)
  - 2. Electron transport chain
    - Carrier proteins move high energy electrons from PS II to PS I
    - Use energy from electrons to pump protons from stroma into thylakoid lumen
    - PQ, Cytochrome complex (pumps protons), PC
  - 3. PS I
    - Electrons (P700) are reenergized
    - Electrons pass down another electron transport chain to protein Fd
    - NADP+ picks up electrons and becomes NADPH (reaction catalyzed by NADP+ reductase
  - ATP formation
    - Because of the concentration difference of protons between the two sides of the thylakoid membrane, protons diffuse from lumen into stroma
    - As protons pass through ATP synthase, potential energy causes ATP synthase to rotate and synthesize ATP from ADP and Pi
      - Chemiosmosis
- Light Independent (Calvin Cycle)
  - Creates high energy carbohydrates that can be stored for a long time
  - 1. Carbon dioxide enters cycle
    - Enzyme in stroma combines carbon dioxide with 5-carbon compounds already present, producing 3-carbon compounds that continue in cycle
    - Other enzymes convert 3-carbon compounds into high energy compounds (energy comes from ATP and NADPH)
    - At midcycle, two of twelve 3-carbon compounds are removed from the cycle
      - They become the building blocks for sugars, lipids, and amino acids
    - Remaining ten molecules converted back into 5 carbon compounds that cycle back to begin next cycle

Factors affecting photosynthesis

- Temperature
  - Enzymes involved work best between 0 to 35 degrees Celsius
- Light
  - High light intensity increases rate of photosynthesis up to a certain point
- Water
  - Plants living in dry conditions have adaptations to minimize water loss

Photosynthesis under extreme conditions

- Plants living in bright, hot conditions have special forms of photosynthesis:
- C4 photosynthesis
  - Can capture extremely low levels of CO2
  - C4: first compound formed in the pathway has four carbon atoms (most plants are C3)
  - Requires extra ATP to function
  - Ex: corn, sugar cane, etc.
- CAM plants (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism)
  - Members of the family Crassulacae
  - Minimizes water loss by opening stomata only at night
  - Carbon dioxide is absorbed at night and stored for photosynthesis next day
  - Ex: pineapple trees, many desert cacti, etc.
