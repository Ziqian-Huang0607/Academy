Science as a way of knowing

Characteristics of living things

- Made up of units called cells
  - Cells
    - Collection of living matter enclosed by a barrier
    - Smallest unit considered fully alive
    - Grow, respond, and reproduce
    - Complex, highly organized
  - Unicellular organisms
    - Only one cell
    - Ex: Bacteria, yeast
  - Multicellular organisms
    - Contain many cells
    - Ex: Plants, animals
- Reproduce
  - Sexual reproduction
    - Two parents
    - Unique offspring
  - Asexual reproduction
    - Single parent
    - Identical offspring
    - e.g. Binary fission
- Based on a universal genetic code
  - DNA
    - Carry directions of inheritance
    - Determine the inherited traits
- Grow & develop
  - Life cycle: Particular pattern of growth and change
  - Growth: Increase in size
  - Development: Cells increase in number and become different (Differentiate)
- Obtain and use materials & energy
  - Metabolism
    - Combination of chemical reactions
    - An organism builds up or breaks down materials
  - Photosynthesis
    - Autotrophs convert light energy into chemical energy
    - Chemical energy is stored in sugar
  - Heterotrophs
    - Eat autotrophs or other heterotrophs to obtain energy
  - Decomposers
    - Obtain energy from remains
- Respond to environment
  - Organisms respond to changes in their own ways
  - Stimulus
    - Signal to which an organism responds
- Maintain a stable internal environment
  - Homeostasis
    - The process by which organisms keep their internal conditions relatively stable
- Change over time (taken as a group)
  - Evolution
    - Populations change over time

The chemistry of Carbon

- Carbon Atom
  - Has 4 valence electrons → forms at most four covalent bonds
  - Bonds with other elements (HOPSN: H, O, P, S, N)
  - Bonds with other carbon atoms
    - Can form chains, rings, or complex structures
    - Can form single, double, or triple bonds
- Macromolecules
  - Giant molecules
  - Polymer = macromolecule made of repeating similar building blocks (monomers)
    - Formed by polymerization
  - Polymers in living things:
    - Synthesis → dehydration → releases H2O
    - Breakdown → hydrolysis → requires H2O
- Macromolecules in Living Things
  - Carbohydrates
    - Made of CHO in a ratio of 1:2:1
    - Monosaccharides = single sugar molecules (sugar monomers)
      - Glucose: main source of energy
      - Fructose: in fruit
      - Galactose: in milk and blood
    - Disaccharides = formed by 2 monosaccharides
      - Sucrose = glucose + fructose → common table sugar
      - Lactose = glucose + galactose → major sugar in milk
      - Maltose = glucose + glucose → product of starch digestion
    - Polysaccharides = many monosaccharides joined together
      - Function: store excess sugar
      - Glycogen (animals) → many glucose connected together
      - Starch (plants)
      - Function: structural support
      - Cellulose → plant cell wall
      - Chitin → fungal cell wall, arthropod exoskeleton
  - Lipids
    - Not soluble in water
    - Made mostly from C and H
    - Include fats, oils, waxes, steroids
    - Not polymers
    - Fats = glycerol molecule + fatty acids
      - Saturated fats
      - Only single bonds between carbons in fatty acids
      - Linear shape → easier to stack
      - Contain max possible number of H atoms (saturated)
      - Solid at room temperature (e.g., butter)
      - Unsaturated fats
      - At least one C=C double bond in fatty acid
      - Causes a bending shape
      - Liquid at room temperature (e.g., olive oil)
    - Functions:
      - Store energy
      - Parts of biological membranes and waterproof coverings
      - Chemical messengers
  - Nucleic Acids
    - Contain NCHOP
    - Monomer = nucleotide
    - Structure
      - 5-carbon sugar
      - Phosphate group
      - Nitrogen base
    - Nucleotides joined by covalent bonds → polynucleotides (nucleic acid)
    - Function: store and transmit genetic information
      - DNA code → transcription → RNA code → read by ribosome → translation → protein
    - DNA
      - Deoxyribonucleic acid
      - Sugar: deoxyribose (one fewer OH)
      - Bases: A, T, C, G
      - Double-stranded
    - RNA
      - Ribonucleic acid
      - Sugar: ribose (has an OH)
      - Bases: A, U, C, G
      - Single-stranded
  - Proteins
    - Contain NCHO, sometimes S
    - Monomer = amino acid
    - Structure
      - Central carbon
      - Hydrogen
      - Amino group (NH₂)
      - Carboxyl group (COOH)
      - R group → different for different amino acids
      - Some Functional groups:
      - methyl (CH₃)
      - hydroxyl (OH)
      - amino (NH₂/NH₃⁺)
      - carboxyl (COOH/COO⁻)
      - phosphate (PO₃⁻)
      - sulfhydryl (SH)
      - Chemical property of R group determines chemical property of amino acid
    - Formed by bonding amino group to carboxyl group
      - Bonded by covalent bond (peptide bond)
      - Dehydration occurs between carboxyl and amino groups
    - Functions:
      - Some control rate of reactions and regulate cell processes
      - Some form bones and muscles
      - Others transport substances or help fight disease
    - Four levels of organization
      - Primary structure: sequence of amino acids (linear chain)
      - Secondary structure: folding or coiling of polypeptide chain
      - Formed by hydrogen bonds between backbone atoms (C, H, NH₂, COOH)
      - Easily broken by high temperature/pH change
      - Apha helix or Beta sheet
      - Tertiary structure: secondary structure itself coiled or folded
      - Precise 3D shape
      - Formed by all kinds of bonds between R groups
      - Quaternary structure: association of different polypeptide chains
      - Does not occur in all proteins

## 2.4 Chemical Reactions and Enzymes

Chemical Reactions

- A process that changes one set of chemicals into another set
- Reactants: elements or compounds that enter into a reaction
- Products: elements or compounds produced by a reaction
- Involve
  - Breaking of bonds in reactants
  - Formation of new bonds in products
- Energy in Reactions
  - Energy is released or absorbed whenever chemical bonds are formed or broken
  - Energy-releasing reactions
    - Occur spontaneously
  - Energy-absorbing reactions
    - Will not occur without an energy source
    - Energy source:
      - Autotroph: sunlight
      - Heterotroph: food
  - Use energy:
    - All life processes
    - Metabolism
  - Activation energy: energy needed to get a reaction started

Enzymes

- Act as biological catalysts
  - Speed up the chemical reaction
  - Reduce the activation energy
- Naming: part of name derived from catalyzed reaction, many end in -ase
- Most enzymes are proteins
- Process
  - Enzymes provide an active site for substrates to bond together and react
    - Substrates: reactants of enzyme-catalyzed reactions
  - Induced fit: shape of active site is changed slightly to accommodate the shape of substrates
  - Enzyme-substrate complex forms
    - Enzymes are specific → catalyze only one chemical reaction
    - Shape and charge of substrate must be compatible with the active site of the enzyme for catalyzing to occur
  - Products released
  - Enzyme is free again
- Regulation of Enzyme Activity
  - Temperature, pH, and regulator molecules can affect enzyme activity
  - Enzymes work better under optimal conditions
    - Different enzymes have different optimal conditions
  - Inhibitors can bind to enzymes and reduce enzyme activity
    - Competitive inhibition
      - Blocks the active site
      - Substrate cannot bind
    - Non-competitive inhibition
      - Binds to a location other than the active site → allosteric site
      - Enzyme shape changed → inactivated
