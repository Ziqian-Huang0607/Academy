**Chemical Energy and ATP**

-Energy is the ability to do work

-Organisms need energy to stay alive.

**Adenosine triphosphate (ATP)**

-a chemical compound cells use to store and release energy

- An ATP molecule consists of adenine, the sugar ribose, and three phosphate groups.

- Cells store energy by adding a phosphate group to adenosine diphosphate (ADP) molecules. (energy is stored in its high energy phosphate bonds)

- Cells release energy from ATP molecules by subtracting a phosphate group.

- Energy provided by ATP is used in active transport, to contract muscles, to make proteins, and in many other ways.

- Cells contain only a small amount of ATP at any one time. They regenerate it from ADP as they need it, using energy stored in food.

**Heterotrophs and Autotrophs**

- The energy to make ATP from ADP comes from food.

- Organisms get food in one of two ways.

**-Heterotrophs**

- Cannot make their own food.

- Obtains food by feeding on others.

- Animals, fungi, etc.

**-Autotrophs**

- Make their own food

- Photosynthesis comes from the Greek words, photo means "light", and synthesis means "putting together".

- Plants, algae, some bacteria

**-Photosynthesis**

- the process that uses light energy to produce food molecules.

### Chlorophyll and Chloroplasts

-In eukaryotes, photosynthesis occurs in organelles called chloroplasts. Chloroplasts house light-absorbing chemicals.

-light

- Light is a form of energy.

- Sunlight is a mixture of all the different colors of visible light.

-Light-absorbing molecules called pigments capture the sun’s energy.

-Chlorophyll

- the principal pigment in photosynthetic organisms

- absorbs blue-violet and red light but reflects green light.

-Chloroplasts have a complex internal structure that includes:

- thylakoids:

- saclike photosynthetic membranes that contain chlorophyll and other pigments and are arranged in stacks called grana.

- stroma:

- the fluid portion outside of the thylakoids.

### High-Energy Electrons

The energy in light raises some of the electrons in chlorophyll to higher energy levels. These high-energy electrons are used in photosynthesis.

-Electron carriers are used to transport the electrons from chlorophyll to other molecules during photosynthesis.

-NADP⁺

- a compound that can accept and hold 2 high-energy electrons and 1 hydrogen ion

- This process converts NADP⁺ into NADPH.

### Overview of Photosynthesis

-The light-dependent reactions

- require light, light-absorbing pigments, and water

- form NADPH, ATP, and oxygen.

-The light-independent reactions

- do not use light energy

- use carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, NADPH, and ATP

- make energy-rich carbon compounds

## 8.3 The Process of Photosynthesis

**Light-dependent reaction**

- **On thylakoid membrane there are:**

- Photosystems: clusters of chlorophyll and proteins

- Electron transport chain: series of electron carrier proteins that shuttle highenergy electrons

- ATP synthase: enzyme that synthesizes ATP->H⁺ ions flow down their concentration gradient through ATP synthase, providing energy to produce ATP (chemiosmosis)

- **Photosystems**

- clusters of chlorophyll and proteins contained by thylakoids

- Photosystems absorb sunlight and generate highenergy electrons

- Highenergy electrons are then passed to a series of electron carriers embedded in the thylakoid membrane

Water is split by sunlight to form oxygen, protons while the electrons are grabbed by chlorophyll

**A. Photosystem II**

- Light energy absorbed by PS II produces highenergy e⁻

- highenergy e⁻ pass to the electron transport chain

- water is split to replace those electrons, releasing H⁺ and oxygen

- PSII happens before PSI

**B. Electron transport chain (ETC)**

- highenergy e⁻ move from PSII to PSI through the ETC

- e⁻ causes pumping of H⁺ from stroma to the thylakoid space (active transport)

**C. Photosystem I**

- e⁻ passed to PSI are reenergized

- Highenergy e⁻ then pass to the secondary electron transport chain

- NADP⁺ picks up the H⁺ and e⁻ at the end of the ETC

- NADP⁺ + 2e⁻ + H⁺ → NADPH
