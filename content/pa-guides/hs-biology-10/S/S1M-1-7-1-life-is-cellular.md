**The Discovery of the Cell**

The invention of the microscope(1600s)enabled researchers to see cells for the first time.

-Robert Hook

- English

- used an early microscope to look at thin slices of cork(1665)

- first to observe dead cells

- defined ‘cell’

-Anton Van Leeuwenhoek

- Dutch lens maker

- first to observe living cells in a drop of water(1674)

-Matthias Schleiden

- all plants are made of cells(1838)

-Theodor Schwann

- all animals are made of cells(1839)

-Rudolf Virchow

- all cells come from existing cells(1855)

**CELL THEORY**

by Schleiden, Schwann, and Virchow(German)

-All living things are made of cells.

-Cells are the basic units of structure and function in living things.

-New cells are produced from existing cells.

**Exploring the Cell**

Scientists use **light microscopes** and **electron microscopes** to explore the structure of cells.

**Compound light microscopes**

-have lenses that focus light

-magnify objects by up to 1000 times

-chemical stains and fluorescent dyes make cell structures easier to see

**Electron microscopes**

-use beams of electrons focused by magnetic fields

-offer much higher resolution than light microscopes

-two main types of electron microscopes：transmission and scanning

-transmission electron microscope

- Use beams of electrons to examine thin slices of materials

- 2D range(inner structure

-scanning electron microscope

- Use beams of electrons to view the surface of a specimen

- 3D range(cell surface

Scientists use computers to add color to electron micrographs, which are photos of objects seen through a microscope.

-basic cell parts

## 7.2 Cell Structure

**-Major parts of eukaryotic cells**

- nucleus

- cytoplasm

- between the nucleus and the cell membrane

- contains organelles (specialized structures to perform cellular functions)

- cell membrane

**Nucleus**

-Structure

- Double nuclear envelope

- Inside: Chromatin / chromosome

- Inside: nucleolus

-Function

- control most cell processes

- contain DNA

**Chromatin and condensed chromosome structure**

-Chromosomes

- Condensed chromatin during cell division

- Distinct threadlike structures visible under a microscope

-Chromatin

- DNA packed by protein

**Nucleolus**

-Structure: small dense region in nucleus

-Composition: rich in DNA

-Function: make ribosomes

**Nuclear envelope**

-Structure: double membrane with nuclear pores

-Function: allow material to move in or out of the nucleus

-Additional: RNA and other informative molecules are sent out

**Vacuoles and vesicles**

- Vacuoles

-Structure: saclike membrane-enclosed

-Function: store water, salts, proteins and carbohydrates

-Examples:

-Central vacuole in plant cell

- A single large one filled with liquid

- Increase rigidity, support heavy structures

-Contractile vacuoles in some unicellular organisms

- Pump excess water out of the cell

- Vesicles

-Structure: smaller membrane-enclosed sac

-Function: store and move materials between organelles and to and from the cell surface

**Lysosomes**

-Structure: small organelles filled with enzymes

-Function: break down lipids, carbohydrates, and proteins / wornout organelles

**Cytoskeleton**

-Structure: network of thin protein fibers

- Two types: Microtubules and microfilaments

-Function:

- maintain cell shape

- transport materials in cell

- help many forms of cell movement

-Microfilaments

- Structure: threadlike, made up of protein actin

- Function: movement and support of the cell

-Microtubules

- Structure:

- hollow tubes

- made up of protein tubulin

- Function:

- maintain cell shape

- serve as "tracks" to move vesicles (with materials)

- Examples:

- spindles

- centrioles

- Flagellum and cilia

**Cytoplasm**

**Ribosomes**

-Structure: small particles made of RNA and proteins

-Function: make proteins

-Attached ribosomes:

- proteins that are to be released or secreted out of the cell

- membrane proteins

- proteins destined for lysosomes and other specialized locations within the cell

-Free ribosomes:

- make other proteins to be used within the cell

**Endoplasmic reticulum**

-Structure：internal membrane system

-Function：

- Rough ER：

- with ribosomes attached

- protein synthesis

- Smooth ER

- no ribosomes attached

- synthesis of membrane lipids

- detoxification of drugs

- Liver cells have lots of smooth ER

**Golgi apparatus**

-Structure: stack of flattened membranes

-Function: modify, sort and package proteins

**Chloroplast**

-Structure: double membrane

-inside:

- large stacks of photosynthetic membranes containing chlorophyll

- found in plants and some other organisms like algae

-Function:

- use solar energy to make energyrich food molecules by photosynthesis

**Mitochondria**

-Structure: double membrane

- smooth outer membrane

- folded inner membrane

-Function:

- use energy from food to make highenergy compounds (ATP)

- site of cellular respiration

**Organelle DNA**

-chloroplasts and mitochondria contain their own DNA

-supposed to be descended from independent prokaryotic microorganisms

-supposed to be descended from independent microorganisms (endosymbiotic theory)

-Mitochondrial DNA inherited only from the mother

**Cell wall**

-plant, algae, fungi, most prokaryotes all have cell wall

-not in animal cells

-lie outside the cell membrane

-Structure: porous wall, allows many substances to pass through easily

- Plant cell wall: Cellulose

- Fungi cell wall: Chitin

- Bacterial cell wall: Peptidoglycan

-Function: support, shape and protect the cell

**Cell membrane**

-Function

- selectively permeable, regulate what enters and leaves the cell

- protects and supports the cell

-Fluid mosaic model of cell membrane

- The proteins embedded in the lipid bilayer can move around and float among the lipids.

- Many kinds of molecules make up the cell membrane.

## 7.3 Cell Transport

**Passive Transport**

-the movement of materials across the cell membrane without using cellular energy

**Diffusion**

-the process by which particles move from an area of high concentration to an area of lower concentration.

**Facilitated diffusion**

-the process by which molecules that cannot directly diffuse across the membrane pass through special protein channels.

**-Osmosis**

- the net facilitated diffusion of water through a selectively permeable membrane

- **Aquaporins**

- water channel proteins that allow water to pass through cell membranes

- **isotonic** solutions

- two adjacent solutions have the same concentrations of solute

- water molecule still move across the membrane in both direction, no net movement of water

- have a higher concentration of solute compared to another solution

- **Hypotonic** solutions

- have a lower concentration of solute compared to another solution

**-Osmotic pressure**

- the force caused by the net movement of water by osmosis.

**Active Transport**

-The movement of materials against a concentration difference

-requires energy.

- Transport proteins that act like pumps use energy to move small molecules and ions across cell membranes.

- The bulk transport of large molecules and clumps of materials into and out of cells occurs by movements of the cell membrane, which require energy.
