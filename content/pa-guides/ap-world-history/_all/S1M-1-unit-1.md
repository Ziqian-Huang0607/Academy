## Topic 1 EAST ASIA

**Tang Dynasty 618AD-907AD** **(not required by AP but good to know)**

- Political
- Expanded into Central Asia and exchanged commodities and culture with other civilizations through Silk Road
- **Taizong:** excellent scholar and calligrapher, emphasized **Confucian** ideas but also supported Daoism and Buddhism when practical, expanded power and trade through military force and diplomacy, reduced forced labor through limiting public works projects, lowered taxes, amazing conqueror (honored with title of 天可汗 by nomads from the northeast, which means supreme ruler), got to power by killing his elder brother Li Jiancheng and younger brother Li Yuanji and forcing his father 唐高祖李渊 (started the bad trend of heirs fighting politically instead of having eldest son from official wife inherit throne)
- **Wuzetian**-first and only female emperor in Chinese History, established the temporary Wu Zhou 武周 administration or Zhou dynasty (not to be confused with the prior Zhou dynasty before this), eliminated many members of extended imperial family to come to power (was once a minor concubine of Taizong but when Taizong died avoided being buried live for the sake of Taizong’s funeral along with other fruitless concubines through becoming a concubine and eventually official queen/wife of Gaozong, Taizong’s son and the next emperor) very Buddhist, generous to lower class, raised social status of women through giving women positions in religious procedures and in the court (e.g. Shangguan Wan'er, famed poet and female prime minister)
- **Xuanzong**: poet and musician, extended cse to commoners, established standard equal measures for sivler, grain, and silk to help merchants, got obsessed with concubine Yang Guifei and fell power in rebellion led by An Lushan (military general of Iranian and Turkish descent who amused Xuanzong through his dances and thus weakened Xuanzong’s alertness of his presence) and later on, Shi Siming (rebelled under Suzong)
- Fiscal power got regionalized as military governors like An Lushan gained power
- Side Note for Clarifications: Yang Guifei was entirely the excuse for people to blame the decline of the Tang Dynasty on and the representation of the overly extravagant lifestyles of Xuanzong
- Innovations
- Astronomy
  - Sphere for mapping the heavens by Li Chunfeng
  - a planetary model driven by water by Yi Xing
  - National project to observe, identify, and locate stars, by Yi Xing, measured leangth of a degree of the meridian
  - Yi Xing published the Da Yan calendar based on 24 solar terms, sun’s orbit, new moon full moon, and more
- Medicine
  - Revised Materia Medica (first recorded phramacopeia in the world)
  - Supplements of the Materia Medica by Chen Zangqi
  - The Thousand Golden Formulae (book of acupuncture by Sun Simiao)
- Black powder (in written record by Sun Simiao)
- Saltpeter--most important ingredient for producing gunpowder--introduced to India and Iran
- Woodblock printing, reprinted ppapers like Tripitaka (Buddhist scripture collection)
- Environment
- Inventions of East River plough, human operated water wheel
- Numerous irrigation projects, flood-control projects, waterway transportation projects throughout country with a special bureau established
- Terraces in Guangdong, Fujian, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Sichuan
- Culture
- Buddhism
  - Came from India
  - Caused compilation of encyclopedias of knowledge, helping preserve Chinese cultural material
  - Inspired advancements in math, engineering, medicine, etc
- Poetry
  - Captures the fleeting and the profound
  - Sense of national confidence and celebrated spontaneous feelings due to power of the Tang
- Civil Service Exam (tested poetry composition thus encouraging chinese literature)
  - Religious frescoes in caves along Silk Road and in royal tombs’ walls
  - Ceramics embraced Indian, Persian, and Greek forms
- Economics
- Two-Tax-System: taxed landholding and wealth twice a year, recognizing private landownership and is a shift to money economy
- Grand Canal: connects fertile south to politically powerful north
- Commercial cities of Hangzhou, Luoyang, Chang’an (Silk Road hub), Chengdu, Guangzhou (maritime trading)
- Social
- Embraced Taoism, Buddhism, Christianity, Islam
- Peaceful rivalries and coexistence between different religions, religions learned from each other; Boundaries of religion existed only for priests; individuals can worship multiple
- **Song Dynasty 960AD-1279AD** **(focus of AP 1.1)**
- Political
- Government expanded funding for prefectural schools and had stricter standards for Civil Service Exams => stronger meritocracy
- **Taizu**: first emperor of the Song, replaced military governors with civilian governors (cuz he was a military governor who seized power from his boss and thus he was alert on internal military rebellions), created Council of State枢密院 to freely debate policy, Board of Censors御史台 controlled abuses and centralized power
- **Huizong**: painter and poet, developed 瘦金体 (a famous style of calligraphy), established first Academy of Painting, devout Daoist, got captured by Jurchen (former alliues) and imprisoned along several thousand imperial relatives, died a captive
- **Xiaozong**: Southern Song emperor, extended sea trade to India and Persian Gulf, oversaw growth of urban merchant class and increased tax revenue from trade, expanded paper money system
- General Outline of Song Dynasty History (capital: Kaifeng->Lin’an)
  - Northern (960 - 1127) and Southern (1127- 1279) Song
  - **Taizu** started off as a military governor who took power from his boss->very wary of power of military governor may cause internal trouble->Song emphasized civil service ovre military power in terms of governors to centralize power
  - **Liao Dynasty** of the **Khitans** and **Western Xia** (**Tangut**) Dynasty threatened Song, and Song paid annual silver and silk to Liao to avoid war as stated in **Covenant of Chanyuan**
  - **Wang Anshi** attempted to make reforms on strengthening state finances and creating militias and making CSE more practical, but faced opposition from people like **Sima Guang**->internal disputes weakened court’s ability to respond to external threats
  - **The** **Alliance Conducted at Sea** established between Song and **Jurchen/Jin Dynasty** to destroy Liao, but the Jin soon turned on the Song, sacking capital **Kaifeng** and capturing much of royal family and numerous emperors in JingKang Incident->**North Song** collapsed at 1127AD
  - Gaozong and part of nobility fled to **Lin’an** (modern Hangzhou) and established **Southern Song Dynasty**, chose peace faction over war faction, became a vassal of the Jin
  - Southern Song allied with Mongols against Jin but again Mongols turned on Song afterwards, held on for decades, got conquered in the end.
- Innovations
- Astronomy
  - Extensive star maps and an array of complex and beautiful astronomical instruments for measuring heavenly bodies’ movement and locations
  - Su Song, prime minister, made a star map
  - Astronomical water clock tower, announced time of day
  - New calendar based on 24 solar terms though not adopted during Song
  - improvements on magnetic compass
- Medicine
  - Wang Weiyi compiled Illustrated Manual of Acupuncture Points on a Copper Puppet
  - Acupuncture introduced to even England and France
  - Medicine factory, medicine surveys by an official bureau of medicine
- Moveable type printing -> more books -> increased literacy -> more civil servants through CSE
- Weaponry
  - Gunpowder arrow, gunpowder ball, barbed gunpowder packages, fire lance, fire-spitting lance, primitive rocket weapon, bombs, grenades
    - 950 CE: first recorded use of guns and grenades
    - “crouching tiger” catapults
  - Firearms introduced to Mongols and thus to Central Asia and Middle East and Europe
- Canals, bridges, enormous seafaring vessels
- Improved irrigation systems (ex. “dragon backbone pump”) -> support agricultural development
- Environment
- Terraces in Guangdong, Fujian, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Sichuan
- Dams and floating farmland (natural or manual soil deposition on masses of floating Feng grass) to turn wetlands into farming land
- Culture
- **Neo-Confucianism** created by people (e.g. Zhu Xi); **absorbed numerous aspects of Taoism and Buddhism**
- Fluidity between religions, commoners chose religion entirely freely (despite calling them religions, Chinese culture had the unique feature of turning these into daily philosophies that directed the course of everyday action instead of focal points of spiritual comfort)
- Neo-Confucianism searched for the pattern of all things—— “Li”
  - **emphasis of filial piety, respect for and expected deference from women, etc.**
- Weak power of Song caused people of the Song to observe the world closely with stronger appreciation for daily life and more political involvement
- Economics
- **Champa rice** (Early ripening, drought resistance, harvested twice a year etc) from Vietnam supported a much larger population and established Jiangnan region (South of Yangtze) as a major economic hub -> food surplus -> population increase
- **Exported silk, porcelain, cash crops (ex. tea)**
  - silk monopoly
- Guilds and brokers organized trade
- Shift of economic focus to Jiangnan region
- Wealthy urban middle class; High urbanization, capitals had populations over a million
- **monopoly over iron and steel industry**
- **Paper money along with copper coins**
- Maritime trade ports in Ningbo, Guangzhou, etc
- Social
- Vastly expanded **CSE** to accept more government officials; CSE shifted focus from poetry to classics
  - CSE allowed some degree of social mobility
- Women
  - foot binding practices, mostly among upper class
  - some ability to inherit property and control family finances

**Korea** **(influence of China on East Asia)**

- Tributary relationship with China
- Similar CSE to staff their bureaucracy
- **Confucian principles** for family structures
- Marginalized women--to a greater extent too
- However Chinese influence mainly on elite members (e.g. CSE was nobility only)

**Heian Japan** **794 - 1185** **(influence of China on East Asia)**

- 7th-9th century CE: learned imperial bureaucracy, Buddhism, and writing system from Chinese -> Chinese influence

**Vietnam** **(influence of China on East Asia)**

- Tributary relationship
- Confucianism, Buddhism, Chinese literary techniques, CSE adopted by elite Vietnamese
- Women not as marginalized as in China (e.g. Female nature deities, Female Buddha)

**Buddhism**

- Introduction
- **Originated in India**
- **Spread to China during Han dynasty**
- Life is suffering -> We crave so we suffer -> we cease suffering when we cease craving ->eightfold path leads to cessation of suffering and craving
- Eightfold path: outlines principles and practices (moral lifestyle+meditation) that a Buddhist must follow
- **Branches of Buddhism: Theravada, Mahayana, Tibetan**
- Theravada Buddhism
- Sri Lanka
- Mainly restricted to monks
- Emphasis on escapiong cycle of birth and death and rebirth, similar to original buddhism
- Mahayana Buddhism
- East Asia
- Teachings available to all
- Emphasized compassion
- Buddha was an object of devotion
- Tibetan Buddhism
- Same basic doctrines with a few additions
- More mystical practices like lying prostrate or elaborate imaginings of deities

## Topic 2 DAR-AL-ISLAM

Cultural

**Judaism**

- Ethnic religion of Jews
- Monotheistic
- Originated in Middle East

**Christianity**

- Established by Jewish prophet Jesus Christ--claimed to be Messiah-->got crucified by Roman authority-->followers spread his salvation by grace message
- Persecuted at first but then adopted by Roman Empire->significantly influential
- Doctrine focused on not accumulating wealth due to Jesus’s teachings unlike Islam

**Islam**

- Founded by prophet Muhammad in 7th century CE who claimed to be final prophet in line of God’s Messengers (which stretched back and through Jewish and Christian scripture)
- Salvation would be found in righteous actions like almsgiving, prayer, and fasting
- Muhammad died in 632 CE and followers spread Islam to North Africa, Sub-saharan Africa, South Asia, Europe, Middle East, etc
- **Spread of Islam**
  - **Military** expansion (e.g. dehli sultanate)
  - **Trade** (e.g. empire of Mali converted to Islam chiefly due to increased access to trade among Dar-al-Islam)
- Muslim Missionaries: **Sufism and Sufis** (Emphasize mystical experiences that were accessible to all; Disliked by Islamic scholar class but quite significant)
- Political

**Dar-al-Islam: The House of Islam**

- Created trading connections within Dar-al-Islam, as Muhammad was originally a trader/merchant
- Abbasid Caliphate
  - 8th-13th century, rose in power replacing the Umayyad Caliphate with battles with Umayyads
  - Ethnically Arab
  - In power during golden age of Islam->several new islamic empires largely made up of Turkic people and not Arabic people
- **Seljuk Empire**
  - 11th-14th century in Central Asia
  - Seljuks were a pastoral people from Central Asia， brought in by Abbasids as a professional military force
  - Gained more political power as Abbasids were in power only by name
- **Mamluk Sultanate 1250 - 1517**
  - located in Egypt
  - Egypt used to be ruled by Ayyubid dynasty under Saladin
  - Enslaved fierce turkic warriors named Mamluks (means enslaved people) to gain more labor force
  - After Saladin died, Mamluks seized power from Saladin’s incompetent successors thus creating the Mamluk Sultanate
- **Delhi Sultanate**
  - South Asia
  - 13th century to 16th century
  - Invading Turks created Delhi Sultanate
- **Politics** for the three sultanates overall--how they resembled previous Arab states (continuation)
  - **Military** in Charge of Administration
  - **Sharia Law**: code of laws derived from Islamic principles, esp. from Quran
- Innovations
  - **Math** (e.g. Nasir al-Din al-Tusi: invented trigonometry to better understand movement of planets and stars in sky, his stuff inspired Copernicus)
  - advances in literature
- House of Wisdom in Baghdad (e.g. Preserved Greek moral and natural philosophy works->basis for renaissance and scholars came to study)
- Scholarly and cultural transfers in Muslim and Christian Spain

## Topic 3 SOUTH AND SOUTHEAST ASIA

- Cultural
- **Hinduism**
- Polytheistic
- Reunite individual souls to all pervasive world soul known as Brahman
  - Cycle through death and rebirth to achieve it
- Unified culture for india: Caste system: Dalit Shudra Vaishya Kshatriya Brahmin (low to high) -> also social hierarchy
  - Caste based on how virtuous you are
- Ethnic religion
- Change: **Bhakti** Movement:
- encourage believers to worship one particular god within entire pantheon of gods
- Rejected hierarchy of Hinduism
- Reject elaborate doctrines and religious requirements of the elite
- Encouraged spiritual experiences to all
- Focused on mystical experience

**Buddhism**

- Founded in India
- Cycle of birth and death and reincarnation; to dissolve into oneness of universe
- Reject caste system, emphasized equality for all
- Universalizing religion
- Access to enlightenment of all
- Change
- Enlightenment got more exclusive by this time in south asia
- Only monks in monasteries were very religious

**Islam**

- 1206: Turkic invaders set up Delhi Sultanate
- Religion of elite as Muslims were in charge of large parts of India
- Went throughout SE Asia too
- Change: **Sufism**--mystical spiritual version of Islam
- Mystical experience
- Rejected elaborate doctrines and religious requirements of the elite
- Access to spiritual experience of all people
- Political

**Delhi Sultanate**

- North India
- Had trouble spreading Islam over India as Hinduism was really integrated into life and culture
- Competition from:
  - **Rajput Kingdoms**--warring Hindu kingdoms started earlier than Muslims, surrounded by the Delhi Sultanate
  - **Vijayanagara Empire**--powerful southern India Hindu Kingdom started up by emissaries sent by Muslims (they had converted to Islam but converted back to Hindu once realizing they were free in the south)

**Sea-based states in SE Asia**

**Srivijaya**

- 7th-11th century
- Indonesia
- Mainly Buddhist but also influenced by Hindu and Indian culture
- Source of power was control on strait of Malacca (strait between Indonesia and Singapore-Malaysia)--important strait to Indian Ocean->taxes

**Majapahit Empire**

- Established in 13th century on Java
- Hindu but also strong Buddhist influence on it
- Tributary relationship with surrounding regions

**Land-based states in SE Asia**

- **Sinhala Dynasties**
- 2k year old Buddhist state
- Sri Lanka
- **Khmer Empire**
- Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam, Laos
- Founded as a Hindu Empire
- Angkor Wat: most magnificent Hindu Temple designed to represent entire Hindu Universe
- Converted to Buddhism later so added Buddha statues to Angkor Wat--a blending of religions, also called syncretism
- **Sukhothai kingdom** was founded in the mid-13th century when a local Tai ruler led a revolt against Khmer rule

## Topic 4 AMERICAS

**Maya**

- Mesoamerica
- Math: created the zero
- Huge urban center
- Sophisticated writing system
- Decentralized collection of warring city-states
- Fought to create a vast network of tributary states with neighboring regions
- Human sacrifice: sun deity was constantly losing energy so needed neergy of human blood

**Aztec and Mexica**

- 1345-1521
- Semi-nomadic that migrated south and started building military and strategically married with more powerful families
- 1428: alliance with two other states->aztec empire
- **Political**
  - Decentralized power: tributary relationship
  - Religious motivations for expansions: sun god need blood
  - Claimed heritage from older more renowned mesoamerican people
- **Architecture and city**
  - Tenochititlan: 150-200 thousand people
  - Markets->commercialized economy
  - Pyramids and palace

**Wari**

- Andean Society in modern day Peru
- Collapsed at around 1000CE

**Inca**

- Borrowed much from older civilizatrions
- Outsiders who rose to power via military prowess and expanded their empire rapidly
- **Political**
  - Centralized power
  - Massive bureaucracy
  - Mit’a system: required labor of all people for a period of time each year to work on state projects like mining or military service
  - Roads and bridges

**Mississipian culture**

- Emerged at around 8th or 9th century CE
- Fertile soil->agriculture
- **Political**:
- Powerful chiefs known as the Great Sun which ruled each town and extended political power over smaller satellite settlements
- Hierarchical society
- **Architecture**
  - Mound building projects
  - Memorial and burial sites
  - Religious ceremony sites
  - Could get enough people to build the mounds->social and political hints
  - Many built in Cahokia, largest urban center of Mississipian culture

**Ancestral Puebloans**

- **Interaction with Environment**
  - Chaco canyon and Mesa Verde
  - Really dry, not many trees for timber too
  - adapted to farming in arid environments
- **Innovations**
- Sandstone cliff dwellings out of massive quarries
- Import timber from distant locations
- Massive structures
- Irrigation systems

## Topic 5 AFRICA

**Swahili**

- East coast of sub-saharan africa
- Collection of independent city-states which rose to prominence because of strategic location that allowed access to Indian Ocean trade
- Merchants came from Arabia, Persian, India interested in gold, ivory, timber and enslaved laborers
- Imported goods from interior Africa’s farmers and pastorialists

Culture

- Islam became dominant religion
- Conversion was voluntary->connected them Dar-al-Islam
- Islam influenced Swahili language: hybrid of indigenous Bantu family of languages and Arabic
- Political
- Each city-state ruled by own king
- Fierce competition for trade
- No larger unified political structure
- Economic
- Expanded wealth by participating in trade beyond boundaries
- Merchant elite>commoners

**Great Zimbabwe**

- Inland, but got rich from Indian Ocean trade through controlling ports on coast
- Exported gold though local stuff worked around cattle and farming
- Capital city was massive, second largest structure in all of Africa after Egyptian pyramids
- **Hausa Kingdoms**
- Collection of city-states that were politically independent and gained power and wealth through trade across trans-saharan trade network
- West Africa north of Sahara
- Urbanized and commercialized and were trade middlemen to more inner people
- Ruled by a king with social hierarchies
- Rulers converted to Islam later on

**Kingdom of Ethiopia**

- Christian
- Massive stone churches
- Grew wealthy through trade in Mediterranean and larger Indian ocean network
- Traded salt
- King at top+stratified hierarchy below

## Topic 7 COMPARISON IN THE PERIOD

- **State-building and New Empires**
- Song Dynasty: long period of technological and cultural progress
- Abbasid Caliphate: fragmented by invaders and declined->new Muslim states arose in Africa, Middle East and Spain
- S and SE Asia: Chola Kingdom and Vijayanagar Empire used trade to build strong states, Delhi Sultanate in N India was more land-based
- Africa: Mali Empire was even greater than preceding Ghana Empire
- Americas: Aztecs founded tributary empire in Mesoamerica, Inca empire in Andea region used mit’a system to support state-building (though most of Americas lacked centralized states)
- Europe: feudal ties declined in significance, centralized states developed (development more clear in W European kingdoms of England and France)
- Japan: more decentralized and feudal

| Four-types of State-building |  |  |
|---|---|---|
| Emergence of New States | States arise in lands once controlled by another empire | Mamluk Empire (formerly Abbasid territory); Seljuk Empire (formerly Abbasid territory); Delhi Sultanate (former Gupta territory) |
| Revival of Former Empires | New leadership continued or rebuilt a previous empire with some innovations | Song Dynasty (based on numerous previous Chinese dynasties); Mali Empire (based on Ghana Kingdom); Holy Roman Empire (claimed to be revival of Roman Empire) |
| Synthesis of Different Traditions | A state adapts foreign ideas to local conditions | Japan (Chinese and Japanese); Delhi Sultanate (Islamic and Hindu); Neo-Confucianism |
| Expansions in Scope | Existing state expands influence through trade, conquest, etc | Aztecs in Mesoamerica; Inca in S America; City-states in E Africa; City-states in SE Asia |

- **Role of Religion in State-Building**
- China and East Asia
- Confucian belief system tied to CSE
- relied on Confucian scholars to run a powerful enduring bureaucracy->well-established and extensive system for conducting government affairs across large territory
- Neo-Confucianism spread to Korea and Japan->East Asian regions’ rulers consolidated and justified political power as well
- SIMILARITY: SE and S Asia relied on Hinduism and Buddhism to aid them in strengthening states
- Europe
- Church provided alternative system for organizing society in Middle Ages due to decline of European states
- As more powerful states (e.g. France, HRE) rose, Church sometimes rivaled with them
- Diffusion of Religion
- Missionary activity often caused decline in practice of local religions in Sub-Saharan Africa, SE Asia, and E Asia
- Invasions of Islamic armies from Central Asia converted many S Asians to Islam, though Hinduism still predominant->periods of conflict and tolerance between Islam and Hinduism
- Trade networks spread religion
- **State-Building Through Trade**
- Champa Rice-> food surplus-> population growth and job specialization->urban citizenry + more manufacturing
- Paper manufacturing reached Europe->increased literacy rates
- House of Wisdom in Baghdad and more learning centers->advances in math and medicine
- Some exchanges were violent
  - Muslims conquered Spain by force in 8th century
  - Crusades between 1095 to 1291
  - Mongols fostered knowledge transfer after brutal conquest
- 1200-1450: Process held back by manorialism and serfdom
- **The Impact of Nomadic People**
- Mongols: conquest and Pax Mongolica **(see 2.2 for more)**
- Turkish People: increased their dominance over large land-based empires in eastern Mediterranean, Persia and S Asia, but were separate empires (e.g. Seljuks, Ottomans)
- Played role in commerce and cross-cultural exchange, but will later be replaced in this role by merchants and trading companies
- **Patriarchy and Religion**
- Religion often reinforced power of men and most social organization remained patriarchal
- Convent lifes for Christians in Europe, Jainist and Buddhist religious communities in S Asia gave women opportunity for learning and leadership
- Women lost independence in China (e.g. footbinding)
