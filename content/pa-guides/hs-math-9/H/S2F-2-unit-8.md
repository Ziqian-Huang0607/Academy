**Geometric Models and Nets**

Isometric Drawing: A 3D depiction of a cube model, drawn on dotted paper(indicating that its perspectives are often fixed).

Orthographic Drawing: A 2D depiction of a cube model, containing front, right, and top views(where directions are often defined by the teacher).

Foundation Drawing: shows the number of accumulated cubes from a top perspective.

Net: The unfolded 2D figure originating from a 3D model of the cubes.

**Polyhedrons and Solids**

Polyhedron: A 3D depiction of a cube model.

Edges: sides of the polyhedron.

Faces: 2D surfaces of the polyhedron.

Vertex: Point on polyhedrons where 3 or more than 3 edges meet.

Lateral face: Side faces of a polyhedron.

Base: Top and bottom(opposite) faces of a polyhedron.

Altitude/Height: Perpendicular segment from top to base of a polyhedron/Length of this perpendicular segment.

-Formula for 3D models: F+V=E+2.

-Formula for nets: F+V=E+1.

-Types of polyhedrons: Prisms-with lateral faces as rectangles(or parallelograms if oblique), Pyramids-with triangles as lateral face and contains only one base, Cylinder-with bases as circles, Cone-with only one base and the base must be circle, Sphere-perfectly Ball-shaped polyhedron.

-Oblique prisms/pyramids/cones/cylinders: Slanted, as its distance from top to bottom’s base’s center is not perpendicular to the bottom base.

Similar polyhedrons: polyhedrons with base’s side lengths(or radius): height equivalent.

**Cross Section**

Cross section: Intersection of a plane and a solid. It is a 2D shape and has no thickness.

Giant circle: Cross section of a sphere that essentially passes through its center.

**Lines, Planes, and Points**

Plane: a 2D figure that extends its size infinitely.

Line: a figure with only the dimension of length, extends infinitely to 2 opposites.

Point: no height, length, or width. Infinitely small.

-Postulate 1: 3 points non-colinear determines a plane.

-Postulate 2: if a line has 2 points on a plane, the line is on the plane.

-Postulate 3: If 2 planes cut on each other, their intersection is a straight line.

-Corollary 1: a line and a point not on the line determines a plane.

-Corollary 2: one and only one plane passes through 2 intersecting lines.

-Corollary 3: 2 parallel lines determine a plane.

-Theorem 1: If a straight line is perpendicular to any line on a plane, that line is perpendicular to the plane.

-Theorem 2: if a straight line is perpendicular to 2 lines, it is perpendicular to the plane formed by that 2 lines.

**3 Relationships of 2 lines in space:**

-coplanar: parallel or intersecting

-skew: do not exist on one plane.

**2 Relationships of 2 planes in space:**

-Intersecting: one line of intersection. (Dihedral angle: angle of intersection of 2 planes)

-Parallel: no intersection.

**3 Relationships of a plane and a line in space:**

-Intersecting: Either oblique line or perpendicular.

-Parallel: no intersection.

**Stats**

**Box and Whiskers:**

Visibly displays Q1/2/3, max and min values.

-Q1 means quartile 1, lower quartile. It is the median of the lower half sorted data( all values below median). It marks the 25th percentile, as 25% data lies below it.

-Q2 means median of the whole data. 50% data lies below/above it.

-Q3 means quartile 3, upper quartile. It is the median of the upper half sorted data( all values above median). It marks the 75th percentile, as 75% data lies below it.

-IQR, or the interquartile range: Q3-Q1.

Determining whether a number is an outlier:

-if less than Q1-1.5*IQR, the number is outlier

-if larger than Q3+1.5*IQR, the number is outlier

Right-skewed: the right whisker is longer than the left whisker

Left-skewed: the left whisker is longer than the right whisker
