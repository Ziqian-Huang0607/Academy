## Section 2.2 Congruent Triangles

**Congruent Figures**

**-**Congruent figures have congruent corresponding parts

– matching sides and angles.

-Always list corresponding vertices IN THE SAME ORDER when naming.

-Congruence between two triangles is expressed as: ∆ABC ≅ ∆A′B′C′

Corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent (CPCTC)

-Important Note: Sliding, Flipping, and Turning DOES NOT Impact overall congruence, so feel

free to take advantage of those transformations

**Determination of Congruent Triangles**

**SSS,SAS,AAS,ASA,HL(Right Triangle).** **SSA DOES NOT WORK**

-However, some SSA could prove to work: Use Auxiliary line such as the altitude to construct

Congruent components e.g.: Construct DF and EG perpendicular to the sides AB and AC,

and utilizing AAS and HL congruence to prove SSA valid

## Section 2.3 Special Triangles

**Isosceles Triangles**

-The congruent sides of an isosceles triangle are its legs, and the third side is the base.

-The two congruent sides form the vertex angles, and the other two angles are the base

Angles

Isosceles Triangle Theorem: In ∆ABC, if AB = AC, then ∠B = ∠C.

Converse of Isosceles Triangle Theorem: In ∆ABC, if ∠B = ∠C, then AB = AC.

-Therefore, in ∆ABC, AB = AC if and only if ∠B = ∠C.

Property of Isosceles Triangles: In an isosceles triangle, the angle bisector of its vertex angle

and the perpendicular bisector of its base are the same line. (In other words, the ALTITUDE,

MEDIAN, and ANGLE BISECTOR coincides)

Perpendicular Bisector Theorem: The point on the perpendicular bisector of a segment is

equidistant from the endpoints of the segment.

Converse of Perpendicular Bisector Theorem: If a point is equidistant from the endpoints of

a segment, then it is on the perpendicular bisector of the segment.

Angle bisector theorem: The point on the angle bisector is equidistant from both sides of the

angle.

Converse of angle bisector theorem: If a point is equidistant from both sides of the angle,

then the point is on its angle bisector.

**Equilateral Triangles**

-A triangle with three congruent interior angles is an equilateral triangle.(Definition)

-An isosceles triangle with an interior angle of 60 is an equilateral triangle.(Definition)

**Right Triangles**

Pythagorean theorem: In Rt∆ABC, m∠C = 90, AC2 +BC2 =AB2

Converse of Pythagorean Theorem: In Rt∆ABC, if AC2 +BC2 = AB2 , then m∠C = 90.

Property of Right Triangles: In Rt ∆ABC, m∠C = 90. If D is the midpoint of AB, then CD =1/2

AB.

- In other words, the median of the hypotenuse of a right triangle is half the measure of its

Hypotenuse

30-60-90 right triangles: In Rt∆ABC, m∠C = 90, if m∠A =30, then BC =1/2AB

NOTE: The Converse is also TRUE

## Section 2.4 Inequalities in Triangles

Segment Inequality: Given three distinct points A, B, C -> AB ≤ AC + BC

Triangle Inequality: In ∆ABC with sides a,b,c, a + b > c, a + c >b, b + c > a.

Similarly: a > |b − c|, b > |a − c|, c > |a − b|.

Determination of the shape of a triangle:

In a triangle, when the square of the longest side is greater than the sum of the squares

of the other two sides, the triangle is obtuse

Similarly, if it is equal, the triangle is right, and if less, acute

Also, in ∆ABC, ∠A > ∠B IF AND ONLY IF side BC > AC

You could remember this by “little angle corresponds to little side”
