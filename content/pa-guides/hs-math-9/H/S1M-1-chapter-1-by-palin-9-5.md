## Section 1.1 Sets and Venn Diagrams

**Key concepts and notations:**

**Set:** a collection of distinct numbers or objects. Notation: capital letters

- n(A)or |A|: the number of elements in set A

**Elements/members of the set:** the objects in the set. Notation: lowercase letters

- An element “is an element of” or “is in” a set: ∈
- An element “is not an element of” or “is not in” a set: ∉

**Natural numbers** **(Contains zero):** ℕ

**Integers:** **Positive** **integers:** ℤ+ or ℕ*

**Rational numbers:** ℚ

**Real numbers:** ℝ

**Finite set:** set with a particular defined value of elements

**Infinite set:** set with endless number of elements.

**Empty set:** a set which contains no elements Notation: $\emptyset$

**Closed interval:** {x | a ≤ x ≤ b} notation: [a, b]

**Open interval:** {x | a < x < b} notation: (a, b) or ]a, b[ to avoid misunderstanding

**Universal set:** the set of all elements we are considering. Notation: U

**Venn diagram:** a diagram representing sets pictorially as circles within an enclosing rectangle (the universal set), which common elements of the sets being represented by intersection of the circles.

**Representations:**

**Representing a set:**

**Listing method:** use a bracket { } to list all the elements in the set (Only for finite set!)

**Descriptive method:** Use specific conditions or rules to express the elements. i.e. {x ∈ A|P(x)} (This method can represent both finite and infinite set)

**Relationships between sets:**

**A is the Subset of B:** For any x ∈ A implies x ∈ B. Notation: A ⊆ B

- **A=B:** A ⊆ B and B ⊆ A
- **A is the proper subset of B:** If A⊂B and there exist x ∈ B such that x ∉ A

**Number of subsets:** If |A|=n, then A has ${2}^{n}$ subsets

**Operations in sets:**

**1. Intersection of A and B:** The set elements that belong to both A and B. i.e. {x| x ∈ A and x ∈ B} Notation: A ∩ B

- **mutually exclusive:** A ∩ B= ∅

**2. Union of A and B:** the set of elements that are in either set A or set B. i.e. {x| x ∈ A or x ∈ B}

Notation: A U B

**3. Compliment of A:** The elements of U that not elements of A.

i.e. = {x | x ∈ U and x ∉ A}. Notation: A′ or$\overline{A}$

**Extended properties:**

- De Morgan’s Law: $\overline{A\cup B}$ = A̅∩ $\overline{B}$ and $\overline{A\cap B}$ = A̅∪ $\overline{B}$
- A ∩ (B ∪ C) = (A ∩ B) ∪ (A ∩ C);
- A ∪ (B ∩ C) = (A ∪ B) ∩ (A ∪ C)

## Section 1.2 Inductive Reasoning

**Definitions:**

**Inductive reasoning:** The process of making a general conclusion from specific observations or patterns (ex. Write the rule for the terms in a sequence)

**Conjecture:** The conclusion reached by inductive reasoning

**Counterexample:** an example for which the conjecture is incorrect (You can use one counter example to prove the conjecture wrong)

**Representing a sequence:**

**Recursive formula:** defining ${a}_{n}$ using its preceding terms.

i.e. ${a}_{n}$ = f(${a}_{1}$, ${a}_{2}$, … , ${a}_{n-1}$)

**Explicit formula:** defining ${a}_{n}$ using n.

i.e. ${a}_{n}$= $f(n)$

## Section 1.3 Logic Statements

**Definition:**

**Statement:** a declarative sentence that is either true or false

- True or false id the **truth value** of a statement
- **Conditional statement:** if p, then q (p is the **hypothesis** and q is the **conclusion**)

**p implies q:** if q then q

- p is the **sufficient condition** of q
- q is the **necessary condition** of p

**p is** **equivalent (both sufficient and necessary) to q:** p implies q and q implies p

**Negation of “if p, then q”:** “if p, then not q”

- negations always have opposite truth value

**Law of detachment:** If p implies q, and p is true, then q is true

**Law of syllogism:** If p implies q and q implies r are both true, then p implies r

For “if p, then q”:

- **Conditional:** “if p, then q”
- **Converse:** “If q, then p”
- **Inverse:** “If not p, then not q”
- **Contrapositive:** “If not q, then not p”

(Conditional statement and its converse are equivalent statements)

## 1.5 Basic Geometric Elements

**Key Concepts:**

**Points:** a location; no size. Notation: capital letters: A,B,C

**Lines:** a set of points that extends in two different directions without end. Notation: lowercase letter: a,b,c

**Segments:** part of a line consisting of two endpoints and all the points between

them. Notation: a bar on two capital letters representing each endpoint of the

segment: $\overline{AB}$

**Ray:** The part of a line consisting of one endpoint and all the points of the line on one side of the endpoint. Notation: an arrow on letter A representing the one endpoint and B representing the other point on the segment

**Opposite rays:** two collinear rays with the same end point with opposite direction.

- opposite rays form a line

**Collinear points:** points that are on the same line.

**Planes:** a flat surface that has no thickness and extends without end in the

directions of all the lines on it. It contains infinitely many lines. (A plane is a set and the line on it is its elements) Notation: lowercase Greek letter (We usually draw parallelogram to represent a plane: When a plane is horizontal placed, we make the acute angle be 45° and also make the consecutive sides be 2: 1.)

Classification of Angles:

**acute angles** — angles with degree between 0 and 90**right angles** — angles with degree of 90

**obtuse angles** — angles with degree between 90 and 180

**straight angles** — angles with degree of 180

Special angle pairs you have to know:

**vertical angles** — two angles with opposite rays as sides

**adjacent angles** — coplanar angles with a common side, a common vertex with no shared interior points

**complementary angles** — two angles whose measures have the sum 90

**supplementary angle** — two angles whose measures have the sum 180

**Postulates:** accepted statement of fact (you don’t need to prove them)

**Theorem:** statement that needs proof

**Compilation of Properties:**

**Postulates:**

1. Only one straight line can be drawn through two given points.

2. Three non collinear points can determine a plane.

3. If two lines intersect, they interact in exactly one point.

4. If two distinct points on a line is on a plane, then the line is on the plane.

5. If two planes interact, they intersect in exactly one line.

6. If two distinct planes have one point in common, they have only one line in

common which contains this common point.

7. If A,B,C are collinear, and B is between A and C, then AB+BC=AC

8. If B is a interior point of AOC, then m$\angle$ AOC = m$\angle$ AOB+m$\angle$ BOC

**Theorems:**

- Vertical angles theorem: vertical angles are congruent.
- Congruent complement/supplement theorem: if two angles are the complement/supplement of the same angle, they are congruent.

**Reasonings for proving**

- Addition P.O.E: If a=b, then a + c = b + c.
- Subtraction P.O.E: If a=b, then a - c = b-c.
- Multiplication P.O.E: If a=b, then ac = bc.
- Division P.O.E: If a=b and c$\ne$0, then a/c=b/c
- Reflexive P.O.E: a=а
- Symmetric P.O.E: If a=b, then b=a.
- Transitive P.O.E: If a=b and b=c, then a=c.
- Substitution P.O.E: If a = b, then b can replace a in any expression.
- Distributive P.O.E: a(b + c) = ab + ac

**Properties of Congruence(They are different from P.O.E!!!)**

1. Reflexive P.O.C: AB = AB; $\angle$A = $\angle$A

2. Symmetric P.O.C: If AB = CD, then CD = AB; If $\angle$A = $\angle$B, then $\angle$B =$\angle$ A.

3. Transitive P.O.C: If AB = CD and CD = EF, then AB = EF; If $\angle$A = $\angle$B and $\angle$B =$\angle$C, then $\angle$A = $\angle$C.

## 1.6 Intersecting and Parallel Lines

**Key concepts:**

**Transversal:** a line that intersect two different lines at two different points (line t)

**Corresponding angles:** the pair of angles on the same side of the transversal, and on the same side of the two lines (ex. $\angle$5 and $\angle$4)

**Alternate interior angle:** the pair of angles on the opposite sides of the transversal and are outside of the two lines (ex. $\angle$1 and $\angle$2)

**Alternate exterior angle:** the pair of angles on the opposite sides of the transversal and between the two lines (ex. $\angle$5 and $\angle$8)

**Same-side interior angle:** the pair of angles on the same side of the transversal and between the two lines (ex. $\angle$1 and $\angle$4)

**Same-side exterior angle:** the pair of angles on the same sides of the transversal and are outside of the two lines (ex. $\angle$5 and $\angle$7)

**Compilation of properties:**

**Postulate:**

**Corresponding angle postulate:** If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, the

corresponding angles formed are congruent (Its converse is also correct)

**Theorems** (assume a transversal intersects two parallel lines for the theorems below):

1. Alternate Interior Angles Theorem: alternate interior angles are congruent

2. Same-Side Interior Angles Theorem: same-side interior angles are supplementary

3. Alternate Exterior Angles Theorem: alternate exterior angles are congruent

4. Same-Side Exterior Angles Theorem: same-side exterior angles are supplementary

(Converses for the above four theorems are correct)

**Properties of parallel lines:**

1. If two lines are parallel to the same line, then they are parallel

2. For two parallel lines, the distance from any point on one line to the other is a constant, and it’s defined as the distance between the two parallel lines.
