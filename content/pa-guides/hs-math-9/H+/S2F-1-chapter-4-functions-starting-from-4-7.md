**4.7+1** **Radical Functions**

When manipulating equations involving algebraic fractions or expressions inside radicals:

- Isolate the radical expression on one side of the equation.
- Raise both sides of the equation to the power of the radical’s index (e.g. square both sides for square roots).
- Substitute solutions back into the original equation since there may be extraneous solutions that are algebraically valid in the modified equation but invalid in the original root function.

Continuous at a point – A function f(x) is continuous at point (a, f(a)) if:

- it is defined at the point (f(a) exists)
- passes through it without a break (limxaf(x) = f(a)).

Continuous on an interval (a, b) – A function is continuous on an interval (a, b) if it is continuous for every point in the interval.
