## Unit 2, 3, 4

### 1. TRIANGLE CONGRUENCE – Specific Conditions

#### A. SSS Postulate (Side-Side-Side)

- Requires: Three pairs of corresponding sides congruent
- No angle information needed
- Most fundamental congruence criterion

#### B. SAS Postulate (Side-Angle-Side)

- Requires: Two sides and INCLUDED angle congruent
- Critical: Angle MUST be between the two sides
- Non-included angle (SSA) is NOT valid (except HL)

#### C. ASA Postulate (Angle-Side-Angle)

- Requires: Two angles and INCLUDED side congruent
- Side MUST be between the two angles
- Often used with parallel lines → alternate interior angles

#### D. AAS Theorem (Angle-Angle-Side)

- Requires: Two angles and NON-INCLUDED side
- Works because third angle automatically congruent (Triangle Sum Theorem)
- More flexible than ASA in some proofs

#### E. HL Theorem (Hypotenuse-Leg)

- Applies ONLY to right triangles
- Requires: Hypotenuse and one leg congruent
- Special case of SSA that works due to right angle

#### F. CPCTC Application

- Use ONLY after proving triangles congruent
- Proves: Corresponding parts (sides/angles) are congruent
- Common in overlapping triangle proofs

### 2. SPECIAL TRIANGLE THEOREMS – Specific Statements

#### A. Isosceles Triangle Theorems

- **Theorem 4-3**: If two sides of triangle are congruent → angles opposite those sides are congruent
- **Converse (Theorem 4-4)**: If two angles of triangle are congruent → sides opposite those angles are congruent
- **Theorem 4-5**: Bisector of vertex angle of isosceles triangle is perpendicular bisector of base
  - Is both altitude and median to base

#### B. Equilateral Triangle Corollaries

- Corollary 1: Equilateral triangle → equiangular triangle (all angles = 60°)
- Corollary 2: Equiangular triangle → equilateral triangle
- All triangle centers coincide (circumcenter, incenter, centroid, orthocenter)

#### C. Right Triangle Properties

- Hypotenuse = side opposite right angle (longest side)
- Pythagorean Theorem: a² + b² = c²
- Special Right Triangles:
  - 45-45-90: legs equal, hypotenuse = leg × √2
  - 30-60-90: sides ratio = 1 : √3 : 2

### 3. TRIANGLE CENTERS – Specific Definitions

#### A. Circumcenter

- Formed by: Intersection of perpendicular bisectors of sides
- Property: Equidistant from all three vertices
- Circle: Circumscribed circle passes through vertices
- Location: Inside (acute), on hypotenuse (right), outside (obtuse)

#### B. Incenter

- Formed by: Intersection of angle bisectors
- Property: Equidistant from all three sides
- Circle: Inscribed circle tangent to all sides
- Location: Always inside triangle

#### C. Centroid

- Formed by: Intersection of medians
- Property: Divides each median 2:1 ratio (vertex:centroid = 2:1)
- Coordinates: Average of vertex coordinates ((x₁+x₂+x₃)/3, (y₁+y₂+y₃)/3)
- Physical: Center of mass/gravity
- Location: Always inside triangle

#### D. Orthocenter

- Formed by: Intersection of altitudes
- Location: Inside (acute), at right angle vertex (right), outside (obtuse)
- No fixed distance properties

### 4. QUADRILATERAL PROOFS – Specific Conditions

#### A. Proving Parallelogram (4 Methods)

- Both pairs of opposite sides congruent (Theorem 6-5)
- Both pairs of opposite angles congruent (Theorem 6-6)
- Diagonals bisect each other (Theorem 6-7)
- One pair of opposite sides both congruent AND parallel (Theorem 6-8)

#### B. Proving Rhombus

- Parallelogram with perpendicular diagonals (Theorem 6-13)
- Parallelogram with one diagonal bisecting two angles (Theorem 6-12)
- All four sides congruent (definition)

#### C. Proving Rectangle

- Parallelogram with one right angle → all right angles
- Parallelogram with congruent diagonals (Theorem 6-14)

#### D. Proving Square

- Must satisfy BOTH rhombus and rectangle conditions
- OR: All sides equal AND all angles 90°

#### E. Isosceles Trapezoid Properties

- Legs congruent
- Base angles congruent (each pair)
- Diagonals congruent (Theorem 6-16)
- Lower base angles supplementary to upper base angles

#### F. Kite Properties

- Two pairs of adjacent sides congruent
- Diagonals perpendicular (Theorem 6-17)
- One diagonal bisected
- One pair of opposite angles congruent (between unequal sides)

### 5. POLYGON ANGLE FORMULAS – Specific Calculations

#### A. Interior Angles

- **Sum for n-gon**: S = (n-2) × 180°
  - Triangle (3): 180°
  - Quadrilateral (4): 360°
  - Pentagon (5): 540°
  - Hexagon (6): 720°
  - Heptagon (7): 900°
  - Octagon (8): 1080°
- **Each interior angle (regular n-gon)**: [(n-2) × 180°] / n

#### B. Exterior Angles

- **Sum for any polygon**: Always 360° (one per vertex)
- **Each exterior angle (regular n-gon)**: 360° / n
- **Relationship**: Interior angle + Exterior angle = 180° at each vertex

#### C. Diagonals

- Number from one vertex: (n-3)
- Total diagonals: n(n-3)/2

### 6. SIMILARITY CRITERIA – Specific Conditions

#### A. AA Similarity Postulate

- Requires: Two pairs of corresponding angles congruent
- Most commonly used similarity proof
- Automatically gives third angle congruence

#### B. SAS Similarity Theorem

- Requires: Proportional sides with INCLUDED angle congruent
- Ratio must be constant for both sides
- Angle MUST be between the proportional sides

#### C. SSS Similarity Theorem

- Requires: All three pairs of corresponding sides proportional
- Check all three ratios equal: AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF

#### D. Similarity Ratio Effects

- Linear dimensions: Scale factor = k
- Areas: Scale factor = k²
- Volumes (3D): Scale factor = k³

### 7. CONSTRUCTION STEPS – Specific Procedures

#### A. Perpendicular Bisector

- Center compass at each endpoint
- Draw arcs with radius > ½ segment (same setting both ends)
- Connect arc intersection points

#### B. Angle Bisector

- Center compass at vertex, draw arc intersecting both sides
- Center compass at each intersection, draw intersecting arcs (same radius)
- Connect vertex to arc intersection

#### C. Congruent Segment

- Measure given segment with compass
- Draw ray for new segment
- Transfer compass measurement to ray

#### D. Congruent Angle

- Draw arc from vertex of given angle
- Draw same arc from new vertex
- Measure chord distance between arc intersections on given angle
- Transfer chord distance to new arc

### 8. PROOF STRUCTURES – Specific Formats

#### A. Two-Column Proof

- Left column: Statements (numbered)
- Right column: Reasons (theorems, postulates, definitions, given)
- Must flow logically step-by-step

#### B. Flow Proof

- Boxes with statements, connected by arrows
- Shows logical dependencies
- Multiple paths can converge

#### C. Paragraph Proof

- Complete sentences
- Must explain logical connections clearly
- Requires strongest writing skills

#### D. Coordinate Proof

- Assign coordinates to vertices
- Use formulas (distance, slope, midpoint)
- Prove properties algebraically

### 9. INEQUALITY THEOREMS – Specific Statements

#### A. Triangle Inequality Theorem

- For any triangle: a + b > c, a + c > b, b + c > a
- Also: |a - b| < c < a + b
- Determines if three segments can form triangle

#### B. Angle-Side Relationships

- **Theorem 5-10**: Larger angle opposite longer side
- **Theorem 5-11 (Converse)**: Longer side opposite larger angle

#### C. Comparison Property

- If a = b + c and c > 0, then a > b
- Used in exterior angle inequality proofs

### 10. COORDINATE FORMULAS – Specific Equations

#### A. Distance Formula

- d = √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²]
- Derives from Pythagorean Theorem

#### B. Midpoint Formula

- M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)
- Average of coordinates

#### C. Slope Formula

- m = (y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁)
- Parallel lines: m₁ = m₂
- Perpendicular lines: m₁ × m₂ = -1

### 11. SPECIFIC VOCABULARY

#### A. Triangle Terms

- Legs, base, vertex angle, base angles (isosceles)
- Hypotenuse, legs (right triangle)
- Median, altitude, perpendicular bisector, angle bisector

#### B. Quadrilateral Terms

- Bases, legs, base angles (trapezoid)
- Adjacent sides, opposite sides/angles
- Diagonals

#### C. Polygon Terms

- Convex vs. concave
- Regular vs. irregular
- Interior vs. exterior angles
- Diagonal

#### D. Proof Terms

- Given, prove, statements, reasons
- CPCTC, reflexive property, transitive property
- Postulate, theorem, corollary

### 12. COMMON PROOF PATTERNS

#### A. Overlapping Triangles

- Identify shared side/angle (reflexive property)
- Redraw triangles separately if needed
- Prove congruence with SSS/SAS/ASA/AAS
- Apply CPCTC for desired conclusion

#### B. Using Parallel Lines

- Identify parallel lines (given or previously proved)
- Use: corresponding angles congruent
- Use: alternate interior angles congruent
- Use: same-side interior angles supplementary

#### C. Indirect Proof

- Assume opposite of what you want to prove
- Show this leads to contradiction
- Conclude original statement must be true

### 13. SPECIFIC APPLICATIONS

#### A. Finding Values Algebraically

- Set up equations using:
- Triangle Sum Theorem (sum = 180°)
- Supplementary angles (sum = 180°)
- Congruent parts (equal measures)
- Polygon angle sums
- Solve for variables

#### B. Determining Possible Side Lengths

- Use Triangle Inequality Theorem
- Check all three conditions: a + b > c, etc.
- Often yields range of possible values

#### C. Classifying Quadrilaterals

- Step 1: Check if parallelogram (4 methods)
- Step 2: If parallelogram, check for:
- Rhombus (perpendicular diagonals or angle bisectors)
- Rectangle (congruent diagonals or right angle)
- Square (both)
- Step 3: If not parallelogram, check:
- Trapezoid (exactly one pair parallel)
- Kite (two pairs adjacent sides equal)
