**9-1 Translations**

**Terms:**

- Transformation: A change of a geometric figure in the position, shape, or size
- Preimage: The original figure
- Image: The resulting figure
- Isometry: A transformation in which the preimage and image are congruent (Each transformation is an isometry)
- Translation: An isometry that maps all points of a figure the same distance in the same direction (no rotation, no reflection, no Dilations)
- In translations, preimage ≅ image
- In a translation, all corresponding sides, angles, and vertices are congruent, and must be listed in matching order

**9-2** **Reflections**

A reflection is an isometry where a figure is mapped to its mirror image across a line called the line of reflection

**9-3** **Rotation**

A rotation is an isometry that maps every point of a figure to a new position by rotating it around a fixed point (center of rotation) by a specific angle (angle of rotation)

**Direction**:

- **Counterclockwise**: Positive angle (Assume as default direction)
- **Clockwise** (CW): Negative angle
- Rotations are always isometries, preimage ≅ image
- Points on the center of rotation map to **themselves**
- The angle between any two corresponding segments equals the angle of rotation
- A regular n-gon maps to itself after a rotation of 360°/n

**9-4** **Symmetry**

- A figure has symmetry if there exists an isometry (translation, reflection, rotation) that maps the figure onto itself.

**Reflectional (Line) Symmetry** - A figure has reflectional symmetry (line symmetry) if a reflection across a line maps the figure to itself

**Rotational Symmetry** - A figure has rotational symmetry if a rotation of 180° or less about a center maps the figure to itself

**9-5** **Dilations**

A dilation is a non-rigid transformation (not an isometry) that changes the size of a figure but not its shape. It maps every point on a figure to a corresponding image point via a center of dilation and scale factor (n)

- Enlargement: Scale factor n>1 (image is larger than preimage)
- Reduction: Scale factor 0<n<1 (image is smaller than preimage)
- Dilations do not preserve distance (so they are not isometries), but they do preserve similarity
