- Terms: Congruent polygons, corresponding (matching) sides/angles/vertices
- If 2 angles of one triangle are congruent to 2 angles of another triangle, then the third angles are congruent, two triangles are similar.

## 4.2 Triangle Congruence by SSS and SAS

- Side-Side-Side Congruence (SSS): If 3 sides of a triangle is congruent to 3 sides of another triangle, then two triangles are congruent.
- Side-Angle-Side Congruence (SAS): If two sides and the included angle of a triangle are congruent to two sides and its included angles of another.

## 4.3 Triangle Congruence by ASA and AAS

- Angle-Side-Angle Congruence (ASA): If 2 angles and its included side of two triangles are congruent, then the two triangles are congruent.
- Angle-Angle-Side Congruence (AAS): If two angles and its non-included side of a triangle are congruent to those in another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent.

CPCTC: Corresponding parts of congruent triangles congruent.

## 4.5 Isosceles and Equilateral Triangles

- Terms: legs / base / vertex angle / base angles of an isosceles triangle
- Isosceles Triangle Theorem: Given AC ≅ BC, then ∠A ≅ ∠B.
- The bisector of the vertex angle of an isosceles triangle is the perpendicular bisector of the base.

- If a triangle is equilateral, then the triangle is equi-angular.

- If a triangle is equi-angular, then it is equilateral.

## 4.6 Congruence in Right Triangles

- Terms: hypotenuse / legs

- HL congruence: If the hypotenuse and a leg of one right triangle are congruent to the hypotenuse and a leg of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

- Note: HL is SSA in right triangles. Usually SSA can't be used to prove congruence. But in right triangles, it works.

What are data?

- Structured Data and Unstructured Data
- Unstructured Data: reviews are typically text-based and do not follow a fixed format. You can easily get detailed information from the customers with their feedback.
- Unstructured Data is very common in our daily life, the email you send, the news you read, the moment you share, even the picture you take, are all unstructured data. These data are easy to read and contain great information, but they are usually given in natural language and can vary greatly in length, style, and content, making them difficult to analyze.
- Therefore, to make the data be easier to be analyzed, it is necessary to convert unstructured data into Structured Data.
- Structured Data refers to any data that is organized in a predefined manner, typically in a table format with rows and columns. Each column has a specific data type, and each row represents a single record or entry. The table is called Data Table.

Variable

- The rows of a data table correspond to individual cases about the recorded characteristics.
- The characteristics recorded about each individual are called Variables.
- Generally, we can divide the variables in this table into two groups: Categorical Data and Quantitative Data (qualitative).
- When a variable names categories and answers questions about how cases fall into those categories, we call it a Categorical Variable.
- When a measured variable answers questions about the quantity of what is measured, we call it a Quantitative Variable.

Categorical Data Analysis (One variable)

- Tabular Display:
- frequency table: count the number of individuals in each category.
- Counts are useful, but sometimes we want to know the fraction or proportion of the data in each category, so we divide the counts by the total number of cases. We multiply by 100 to express these proportions as percentages.

- relative frequency table displays the percentages, rather than the counts, of the values in each category. Both types of tables show how the cases are distributed across the categories.

Graphical Display

- Bar Chart: A bar chart displays the distribution of a categorical variable, showing the counts for each category next to each other for easy comparison.
- Pie Chart: Pie charts show the whole group of cases as a circle. They slice the circle into pieces whose sizes are proportional to the fraction of the whole in each category.
- The Area Principle: The best data displays observe a fundamental principle of graphing data called the area principle. The area principle says that the area occupied by a part of the graph should correspond to the magnitude of the value it represents.

Categorical Data Analysis (Two variables)

- Contingency Table / Crosstabulation: a table that shows how the individuals are distributed along each variable, contingent on the value of the other variable.
- Marginal Distribution: in the margins of a contingency table, the frequency distribution of one of the variables.
- Each Cell of the table gives the count for a combination of values of the two variables.
- Conditional Distributions: distributions that show the distribution of one variable for just those cases that satisfy a condition on another variable.
- Segmented bar chart: treats each bar as the "whole" and divides it proportionally into segments corresponding to the percentage in each group.
- In a contingency table, when the distribution of one variable is the same for all categories of another, we say that the variables are Independent.
- Side-by-side bar chart: showing percentages of one variable broken down by categories of the other variables

Sets and Venn Diagrams

- A set is a collection of distinct numbers or objects. Each object is called an element or member of the set.
- ∈ means "is in"; ∉ means "is not an element of"
- n(A):|A| means "the number of elements in set A"
- Property of sets:
- An element is either in the set or not in the set.
- The elements in a set are distinct. Same element can appear only once.
- There is no fixed order when we describe the elements in the set.
- Two sets are equal if they contain exactly the same elements.
- Important number sets symbols: (in order of narrow to wide)
- Natural numbers N
- Integers Z (positive integers Z+ or N*
- Rational numbers Q
- Real numbers R
- Classification:

- Finite set: |A| has a particular defined value.
- Infinite set: endless number of elements.
- Empty set (∅ or {}): a set which contains no elements.
- Representation:

- Listing: Use { } to list all the elements in the set.
- Descriptive method: {x∈A∣P(x)}
- Subsets: given sets A and B, all elements of set A belongs to set B

- #of subsets= #of elements2
- Note 1: Any set is the subset of itself.
- Note 2: A = B if and only if A⊆B and B⊆A.
- Note 3: ∅ is the subset of any set.

- Proper subsets: subsets, but excluding the set itself

- #of proper subsets= #of elements2 -1

- Intersection: The intersection of two sets A and B is the set of elements that are in both set A and set B: {x∣x∈A and x∈B}, written (A∩B). We call A and B disjoint (mutually exclusive) if A∩B = ∅.

- Properties:

1)   A∪B = B∪A

2)   A∩A = A

3)   A∩∅ = ∅

4)   A∩B ⊆ A, A∩B ⊆ B

5)   A∩B = A iff A ⊆ B

6)   (A∩B)∩C = A∩(B∩C)

- Union: The union of two sets A and B is the set of elements that are in either set A or set B: {x∣x∈A or x∈B}, written A∪B.

- Properties:

1)   A∪B=B∪A

2)   A∪A=A

3)   A∪∅=A

4)   A⊆A∪B,B⊆A∪B

5)   A∪B=B iff A⊆B

6)   (A∪B)∪C=A∪(B∪C)

- Complement: The complement of A is the set of all elements of U (universal) that are NOT elements of A, written as A' or A = {x∣x∈U and x∉A}.

- Properties:

1)   (A5 )=A

2)   ∅=U

3)   A∩A=∅, A∪A=U

4)   De Morgan's Law: A∪B = A∩B, A∩B=A∪B

- Venn diagram: consists of a universal set U represented by a rectangle, and subsets within it that are represented by circles.
- Inclusion principle: n(A∪B) = n(A)+n(B)-n(A∩B)
- Interval notation: e.g. {x∈R∣-2≤x<4}, x is a real number, AND is larger or equal than 2 and smaller than 4
- Bracket notation:

- [a,b] = {x∣a ≤ x ≤ b}
- [a,b) = {x∣a ≤ x < b}
- (a,b] = {x∣a < x ≤ b}

Problem. Blue eyes island problem

1000 residents are living in a tribe on an island. There is a taboo saying that “They should not know the color of their eyes and they are prohibited from talking about it. If someone knows the color of his/her eyes, then he/she will commit suicide on the most recent noon in public.” All of the residents in this tribe will absolutely obey this rule and all of them have good eye sight which means they can see the color of others’ eyes. In other words, everyone knows the color of others’ eyes. They don’t use mirror and they never see their eyes in the water. To sum up, they are unable to know the color of their eyes from outside. All of them are rational and smart enough.

Given that there are two different colors: 900 are red, 100 are blue. All residents don’t know this fact.

One day, a tourist arrived. He has blue eyes and he made a speech facing all residents in this tribe. He doesn’t know the taboo and he mentioned: “I’m so glad to meet people like me who has blue eyes!”

What will happen on this island?

Solution. Because all of them are rational and smart enough, anyone who sees 999 red eyes will commit suicide because they know that they have blue eyes. Since no one commits suicide on the first noon, anyone who sees 998 red eyes will commit suicide because they know that if the person with blue eyes sees 999 red eyes, they will commit suicide on the first noon, so the person who sees 998 red eyes must know that they have blue eyes, so they commit suicide on the second noon. Similarly, on the 100th noon, nobody commits suicide so none of them sees 901 red eyes. So the people who sees 900 red eyes commit suicide. On the 101st noon, everyone who has red eyes commit suicide because they know that the people who commited suicide the last noon sees 900 red eyes.
