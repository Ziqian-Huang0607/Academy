**11-1 Space Figures and Cross Sections**

**Terms:**

- Polyhedron: A three-dimensional figure whose surfaces are polygons
- Face: Each polygon of the polyhedron
- Edge: A segment that is formed by the intersection of two faces
- Vertex: A point where three or more edges intersect
- Cross Section: The intersection of a solid and a plane

**Formula:**

**Euler’s Formula for Prism**

Number of Face + Number of Vertices - Number of Edges = 2

$$F+V=E+2$$

**Euler’s Formula for two-dimensional net**

Number of Region + Number of Vertices - Number of Edges = 1

$$F+V=E+1$$

**11-2** **& 11-4** **Surface Areas of Prisms and Cylinders**

**Terms:**

- Prism: A Polyhedron with exactly two congruent parallel faces
- Bases: Parallel faces in prism
- Lateral faces: Other faces in the prism except the bases
- Altitude of a prism: A perpendicular segment that joins the planes of the bases
- The height h of the prism is the length of an altitude

**Right and Oblique Prism:**

**Lateral and Surface Areas of a Prism:**

- Lateral area of a prism: the sum of the areas of the lateral faces

$$L.A. =p\times h$$

**-** Surface area of a right prism: the sum of the lateral area and the areas of the two bases

$$S.A. =L.A. +2 \text{ Base }$$

**Cylinder:** A cylinder has two congruent parallel bases. However, the bases of a cylinder are circles. An altitude of a cylinder is a perpendicular segment that joins the planes of the bases. The height h of a cylinder is the length of an altitude.

**Formula:**

$$L.A. =2\pi r\times h$$

$$S.A. =L.A. +2\pi {r}^{2}$$

$$V= \pi {r}^{2}\times h$$

**11-3** **& 11-5** **Surface Area** **and volume** **of Pyramids and Cones**

**Terms:**

- Pyramid: a polyhedron in which the base can be any polygon and the other lateral faces are triangles that meet at a common vertex (called the vertex of the pyramid). We name a pyramid by the shape of its base.
- Altitude: the perpendicular segment from the vertex to the plane of the base
- Slant Height: the length of the altitude of a lateral face of a pyramid
- Cone: A cone is “pointed” like a pyramid, but its base is a circle. In a right cone, the altitude is a perpendicular segment from the vertex to the center of the base. The height h is the length of the altitude. The slant height is the distance from the vertex to a point on the edge of the base

**Formula:**

Pyramid:

$$L.A. =\frac{1}{2}\times p\times \text{ Slant } \text{ Height }$$

$$S.A. =L.A. + \text{ Base }$$

$$V= \frac{1}{3}\times b\times h$$

Cone:

$$L.A. =\pi \times r\times \text{ Slant } \text{ Height }$$

$$S.A. =L.A. + \text{ Base }$$

$$V= \frac{1}{3}\times \pi {r}^{2}\times h$$

**11-6** **Sphere**

**Terms:**

- Sphere: a set of all points in space equidistant from a given point called the center. A radius is a segment that has one endpoint on the center and the other endpoint on the sphere. A diameter is a segment passing through the center with endpoints on the sphere.
- When a plane and a sphere intersect in more than one point, the intersection is a circle. If the center of the circle is also the center of the sphere, the circle is called a great circle of the sphere. The circumference of a great circle is the circumference of the sphere. A great circle divides a sphere into two hemispheres.

**Formula:**

Sphere:

$$S.A. =4\pi {r}^{2}$$

$$V= \frac{4}{3}\pi {r}^{3}$$

**10-6 Circles and Arcs**

**Terms:**

- Circle: Set of all points in a plane equidistant from a fixed point (the center). Named by its center
- Radius: Segment from center to any point on the circle
- Diameter: Chord passing through the center (twice the radius: d=2r)
- Chord: Segment with both endpoints on the circle
- Central Angle: Angle with vertex at the circle’s center and sides as radii

- Minor Arc: Arc measuring less than $180^\circ$
- Major Arc: Arc measuring more than $180^\circ$ (named with 3 points)
- Adjacent Arcs: Arcs of the same circle with exactly one point in common

**12-1 Tangent Lines**

**12-1 Theorem**:

- If a line is tangent to the circle, then the line is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency.

**12-2 Theorem** **(Converse of 12-1 Theorem):**

- If a line in the plane of a circle is perpendicular to a radius, then the line is tangent to the circle.

**12-3 Theorem:**

- The two segments tangent to a circle from a point outside the circle are congruent.
- A circle is inscribed in the polygon = the sides of the polygon are tangent to the circle
- The same with a polygon that is circumscribed about the circle

**12-2 Chords and Arcs**

**12-4 Theorem:**

- Within a circle or in congruent circles
- Congruent central angles have congruent chords
- Congruent chords have congruent arcs
- Congruent arcs have congruent central angles.
  - **Theorem** **(Equidistant Chords Theorem):**
- Within a circle or in congruent circles
- Chords equidistant from the center are congruent
- Congruent chords are equidistant from the center
  - **Theorem:**
- In a circle, a diameter that is perpendicular to a chord bisects the chord and its arcs.
  - **Theorem:**
- In a circle, a diameter that bisects a chord (that is not a diameter) is perpendicular to the chord.
  - **Theorem:**
- In a circle, the perpendicular bisector of a chord contains the center of the circle.
