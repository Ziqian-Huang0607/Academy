**1-3:**

-Point: A location; no size; represented by a small dot and is named by a capital letter.

-Line: A series of points that extends in two opposite directions **without end**; named as any two points that lie on the same line.

-Collinear points: Points that lie on the same line.

-Plane: a flat surface that has no thickness; contains many lines; extends without end in the directions of all its lines; named as a single capital letter or at least 3 of its noncollinear points.

-Coplanar: Points and lines in the same plane are coplanar.

-Postulate 1-1 Through any two points there is exactly one line.

-Postulate 1-2 If two lines intersect, then they intersect in exactly one point.

-Postulate 1-3 If two planes intersect, then they intersect in exactly one line.

-Postulate 1-4 Through any three noncolinear points there is exactly one plane.

**3-1:**

-Transversal: A line that crosses two or more other lines.

-Alternate Interior Angles
Angles that are inside the two lines and on opposite sides of the transversal.

-Alternate Exterior Angles
Angles that are outside the two lines and on opposite sides of the transversal.

-Same-Side Interior Angles
Angles that are inside the two lines and on the same side of the transversal.

-Corresponding Angles
Angles that are in the same position on each intersection where the transversal crosses.

-Postulate 3-1 Corresponding angles postulate

If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then corresponding angles are congruent.

-Theorem 3-1 Alternate interior angles postulate

If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then alternate interior angles are congruent.

-Theorem 3-2 same-side interior angles postulate

If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then same-side interior angles are supplementary (add up to 180 degrees).

-Theorem 3-3 Alternate exterior angles postulate

If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then alternate exterior angles are congruent.

-Theorem 3-4 Same-side exterior angles postulate

If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then same-side exterior angles are supplementary.

**3-2:**

-Postulate 3-2 Converse of the corresponding angles postulate

If two lines and a transversal form corresponding angles that are congruent, then the two lines are parallel.

-Theorem 3-5 Converse of the alternate interior angles theorem

If two lines and a transversal form alternate interior angle that are congruent, then the two lines are parallel.

-Theorem 3-6 Converse of the same-side interior angle theorem

If two lines and a transversal form same-side interior angle that are supplementary, then the two lines are parallel.

-Theorem 3-7 Converse of the alternate exterior angle theorem

If two lines and a transversal form congruent alternate exterior angle, then the two lines are parallel.

-Theorem 3-8 Converse of the same-side exterior angle theorem

If two lines and a transversal form same-side exterior angle that are supplementary, then the two lines are parallel.

The sum of the measures of the angles of a triangle is 180 degrees.

-Theorem 3-13 Triangle Exterior Angle Theorem

The measure of each exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the measures of its two remote interior angles.

**4-2:**

-Postulate 4-1 SSS (Side-Side-Side)

If all three sides of one triangle are congruent to all three sides of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

-Postulate 4-2 SAS (Side-Angle-Side)

If two sides and the included angle of one triangle are congruent to two sides and the included angle of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

Practice Question:

Answer: SSS Postulate.

**4-3:**

-Postulate 4-3 ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)
If two angles and the included side of one triangle are congruent to two angles and the included side of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

-Postulate 4-4 AAS (Angle-Angle-Side)

If two angles and a non-included side of one triangle are congruent to two angles and a corresponding non-included side of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

Practice Question:

Answer: ASA Postulate.

**4-4:**

-CPCTC stands for “Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent.”

It means that if two triangles are proven congruent, then all their matching sides and angles are also congruent.
