**12-1**

**Tangent Lines**

A tangent to a circle: A line(tangent line/ray/segment) in the plane of the circle that intersects the circle in exactly one point, which is called the point of tangency.

12-1 Theorem: If a line is tangent to the circle, then the line is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency

12-2 Theorem(Converse of 12-1 Theorem): If a line in the plane of a circle is perpendicular to a radius, then the line is tangent to the circle.

12-3 Theorem: The two segments tangent to a circle from a point outside the circle are congruent.

A circle is inscribed in the polygon = the sides of the polygon are tangent to the circle

The same with a polygon that is circumscribed about the circle

**12-2**

**Chords and Arcs**

12-4 Theorem: Within a circle or in congruent circles

1: Congruent central angles have congruent chords

2: Congruent chords have congruent arcs

3: Congruent arcs have congruent central angles.

12-5 Theorem(Equidistant Chords Theorem): Within a circle or in congruent circles

1:Chords equidistant from the center are congruent

2: Congruent chords are equidistant from the center

**12-3**

**Inscribed Angles**

Inscribed Angle Theorem: The measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc.

Corollaries to the Inscribed Angle

1: Two inscribed angles that intercept the same arc are congruent

2: An angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle

3: The opposite angles of a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle are supplementary

12-10 Theorem: The measure of an angle formed by a tangent and a chord is half the measure of the intercepted arc.

**12-4**

**Angle Measures and Segment Lengths**

A secant is a line that intersects a circle at two points.

Angles formed by secants, tangents, and chords intercept arcs on circles. The measure of the intercepted arcs can help you find the measures of the angles.
