point: a location; no size; represented by a dot and is named by a capital letter

line: a series of points that extends in two directions without ending points; named by any two points on the line or by a lowercase letter

collinear points: points that lie on the same line

plane: a flat surface that has no thickness; contains many lines; extends without end in the directions of all its lines; named by a letter or by at least three non-collinear points

coplanar: points and lines in the same plane

postulate or axiom: an accepted statement of fact

Postulates:

1.Through any two points there is exactly one line.

2.If two lines intersect, then they intersect in exactly one point.

3.If two planes intersect, then they intersect in exactly one line.

4.Through any three non-colinear points there is exactly one plane.

## 1.4 Segments, Rays, Parallel Lines, and Planes

segment: the part of a line consisting of 2 endpoints and all points between them.

ray: the part of a line consisting of 1 endpoint and all the points of the line on one side of the endpoint.

opposite rays: two collinear rays with same endpoint

- always form a line

- lines that do not intersect are either parallel or skew

- parallel lines are coplanar lines that do not intersect

- skew lines are not intersecting nor coplanar

## 1.5 Measuring Segments

congruent segments: multiple segments that are equal in length

midpoint: a point that divides a segment into two equal segments

Postulates:

1.Ruler Postulate

The points of a line can be put into one-to-one correspondence with the real numbers so that the distance between any two points is the absolute value of the difference of the corresponding numbers.

2.Segment Addition Postulate

If three points A, B, and C are collinear and B is between A and C, then AB+BC=AC

## 2.5 Proving Angles Congruent

1.Vertical Angles Theorem

Vertical angles are congruent.

2.Congruent Supplements Theorem

If two angles are supplements of the same angle (or of congruent angles), then the two angles are congruent.

3.Congruent Complements Theorem

If two angles are complements of the same angle (or of congruent angles), then the two angles are congruent.

4.All right angles are congruent.

5.If two angles are congruent and supplementary, then each is a right angle.
