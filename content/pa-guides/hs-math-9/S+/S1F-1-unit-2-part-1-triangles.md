Brief Review for contents Before MIDTERM:

**POE(Properties of Equality)**

- Addition POE: a=b, a+c=b+c
- Subtraction POE: a=b, a-c=b-c
- Multiplication POE: a=b, ac=bc
- Division POE: a=b, a/c=b/c
- Reflexive POE: a=a
- Symmetric POE: a=b, b=a
- Transitive POE: a=b&b=c, then a=c
- Substitution POE: a=b, b can replace a in any expression

**POC(Properties of Congruence)**

- Reflexive POC: segment AB≌segment AB
- Symmetric POC: segment AB≌segment BA
- Transitive POC: segment AB≌segment CD&segment CD≌segment EF, then segment AB≌segment EF

**Other theorems and postulates**

- Distributive property: a(b+c)= ab+ac
- Vertical angles theorem: Vertical angles are congruent
- ≌ supp thm: if two angles are supplements of the same angle, they are ≌
- ≌compl thm: if two angles are compliments of the same angle they are ≌
- All right angles are congruent
- If two angles are congruent and supplements, they are both 90°
- Corresponding angles post: if transversal intersects two parallel lines, then corresponding angles are ≌
- Alternate interior angles thm: alternate interior angles are ≌ (has converse)
- Same side interior angles thm: same side interior angles are supplementary(has converse)
- Alternate exterior angles thm: alternate exterior angles are congruent
- Same side exterior angles thm: same side exterior angles are supplementary

If two lines are parallel to the same line, then they are parallel to each other

In a plane if two lines are perpendicular to the same line then they are parallel to each other

In a plane, if a line is perpendicular to one line of a pair of parallel lines, then it is perpendicular to both lines

A polygon’s interior angles sum is equal to 180*(n-2)° n being the number of sides of the polygon

Triangle exterior angle thm: measure of an exterior angle is the sum of the other two interior angles

Triangle Congruence

SSS: If all three sides of a triangle are congruent to another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent

SAS: if two sides and their included angle of one triangle are congruent to two sides and their included angle of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent

ASA: if two pairs of angles and their included side of two triangles are congruent, then the two triangles are congruent

AAS: if two angles and either of the two non-included sides of two triangles are congruent then the two triangles are congruent

**CPCTC&HL**

CPCTC: If two triangles are proven congruent, all their corresponding parts (including angles and sides) are congruent

HL: if the hypotenuse and a leg of a right triangle are congruent to the hypotenuse and a leg of another triangle then the triangles are congruent

**Isosceles and Equilateral triangles**

Isosceles triangle thm: If two sides of a triangle are congruent, then the two angles that oppose the two sides are congruent

Converse of isosceles triangle thm: if two angles of a triangle are congruent, then the two sides of the triangle that oppose the two angles are congruent

The bisector of the vertex angle of an isosceles triangle passes through the midpoint of the base

## Unit 2 Part 2 TRIANGLES:

**POI(Properties of** **inequality)(Properties present in the parenthesis are the corresponding POEs)**

Addition POI: If a>b, c≥d, then a+c≥b+d(if a=b, then a+c=b+c)

Multiplication POI: If a>b, c>0, then ac>bc(if a=b, then ab=ac)

Transitive POI: If a>b, b>c, then a>c(if a=b, b=c, then a=c)

Comparison POI: If a>b and c≥d, then a+c>b+d(none)

**Other** **theorems and postulates**

Corollary to the triangle exterior angle thm: The measure of an exterior angle of a triangle is larger than the measure of each of its remote interior angles

Converse of Corollary to the triangle exterior angle thm: If two angles of a triangle are not congruent then the larger side lies opposite to the larger angle

Triangle inequality thm: the sum of the length of any two sides of a triangle is greater than the length of the third side

Perpendicular bisector thm: if a point is on the perpendicular bisector of a segment, then it is equidistant from the end points of the triangle

Converse of perpendicular bisector thm: if a point is equidistant from the end point of a segment, then it is on the perpendicular bisector of the segment

Angle bisector thm: if a point is on the angle bisector of the angle, then it is equidistant from the sides of the angle

Converse of angle bisector thm: if a point is equidistant from the sides of the angle, then it is on the angle bisector

**Concurrent lines, Medians, and Altitudes**

Perpendicular bisectors of the sides of a triangle are concurrent at the Circumcenter of the triangle, which is equidistant from the vertices

The bisectors of the angles of a triangle are concurrent at the Incenter, which is equidistant from the sides

The medians of a triangle are concurrent at the centroid. The distance from the midpoint to the centroid is 1/2 of the distance from the vertices to the centroid

The lines that contain the altitude of a triangle are concurrent at the orthocenter of the triangle

**How to describe?**

Concurrent: point of concurrency

Circumcenter: circumscribed about

Incenter: Inscribed in
