**9-1**

**Translations**

Key Definitions:

**Transformation:** A change in the position, shape, or size of a geometric figure.

**Preimage:** The original figure before the transformation.

**Image:** The resulting figure after the transformation.

**Isometry:** A transformation where the preimage and image are congruent (same size and shape; preserves distances and angles).

**Translation (Slide):** An isometry that maps all points of a figure the same distance in the same direction (no rotation, no reflection, no resizing).

Translations are always isometries (preimage ≅ image).

Every point (x,y) in the preimage is shifted by the same horizontal and vertical change.

A translation is written as: (x,y)→(x+a, y+b)

- a = horizontal shift (positive = right, negative = left)
- b = vertical shift (positive = up, negative = down)

In a translation, all corresponding sides, angles, and vertices are congruent, and must be listed in matching order.

**9-2**

**Reflections**

A reflection is an isometry where a figure is mapped to its mirror image across a line called the line of reflection.

For any point P and line of reflection r:

- If A is on r: The image of P is P itself (A′=A).
- If B is not on r: The line r is the perpendicular bisector of the segment BB′. (This means the distance from B to r equals the distance from B′ to r.)

| Line of Reflection | Coordinate Transformation Rule |
|---|---|
| x-axis | (x,y)→(x,−y) |
| y-axis | (x,y)→(−x,y) |
| Line y=x | (x,y)→(y,x) |
| Line y=−x | (x,y)→(−y,−x) |
| Vertical Line x=a | (x,y)→(2a−x,y) |
| Horizontal Line y=b | (x,y)→(x,2b−y) |

**9-3**

**Rotation**

A rotation is an isometry that maps every point of a figure to a new position by rotating it around a fixed point (center of rotation) by a specific angle (angle of rotation).

**Direction**:

- **Counterclockwise**: Positive angle (default unless stated otherwise).
- **Clockwise** (CW): Negative angle (e.g., −90∘).

**Some Rules to Remember:**

- Rotations are always isometries (preimage ≅ image; preserves distances and angles).
- Points on the center of rotation map to **themselves** (O′=O).
- The angle between any two corresponding segments (e.g., CO and C′O) equals the angle of rotation.
- A regular n-gon maps to itself after a rotation of 360∘​/n (central angle).

| **Angle of Rotation** | **Transformation Rule** |
|---|---|
| 90∘ Counterclockwise | (x,y)→(−y,x) |
| 180∘ (CW or CCW) | (x,y)→(−x,−y) |
| 270∘ Counterclockwise | (x,y)→(y,−x) |
| 90∘ Clockwise | (x,y)→(y,−x) |

**9-4**

**Symmetry**

A figure has symmetry if there exists an isometry (translation, reflection, rotation) that maps the figure onto itself.

**Reflectional (Line) Symmetry** - A figure has reflectional symmetry (line symmetry) if a reflection across a line maps the figure to itself.

e.g. the lines of symmetry for a regular hexagon

**Rotational Symmetry** - A figure has rotational symmetry if a rotation of 180° or less about a center maps the figure to itself.

e.g. The equilateral triangle has rotational symmetry. The angle of rotation (the smallest positive angle needed to rotate the figure onto itself) is 120.

**9-5**

**Dilations**

A dilation is a non-rigid transformation (not an isometry) that changes the size of a figure but not its shape. It maps every point on a figure to a corresponding image point via a center of dilation and scale factor (n).

- Enlargement: Scale factor n>1 (image is larger than preimage).
- Reduction: Scale factor 0<n<1 (image is smaller than preimage).

If the center of dilation is the origin (0,0) and the scale factor is n:

(x,y)→(nx,ny)

The center of dilation C maps to itself (C′=C).

**To dilate about a point (h,k) not the origin:**

- Translate the figure by (−h,−k) to move the center to the origin.
- Dilate by scale factor n.
- Translate back by (h,k).

Dilations do not preserve distance (so they are not isometries), but they do preserve similarity.
