**10-1**

**Areas of Parallelograms and Triangles**

**Triangles:**

**When two sides and the included angle of a triangle are known, the triangle is uniquely determined, and we can calculate its area using trigonometry.**

Starting with the standard area formula: A=1/2​bh, where b = base, h = height:

For △ABC with sides a,b and included angle ∠C:

The height h=asinC (from right-triangle trigonometry: sinC=h/a​).

Substitute into the area formula:

**A=1/2absinC**

**For any triangle, the area can be written as half of the product of two sides and the sine of the included angle.**

Example:

Two sides of a triangle are 7 cm and 4 cm, with an included angle of 73∘. Find the area.

Solution:

A=1/2 x 7 x 4 x sin73 = 13.4 cm^2

**Parallelogram**

The area of a parallelogram equals the product of a base and its corresponding height (altitude, perpendicular to the base).

**A = bh**

A triangle can be rotated about a side midpoint to form a congruent parallelogram, so its area is half the parallelogram’s area.

So A = 1/2 bh, which is also discussed in chapter 9.

**Areas of Trapezoids, Rhombuses, and Kites**

The area of a trapezoid is half the product of the sum of the two bases and the height (perpendicular distance between the bases).

**A=1/2 ​(b1​+b2​) h**

The area of a rhombus or a kite is half the product of the lengths of its diagonals.

**A=1/2​x d1x ​d2​**

**10-3**

Core Definitions:

**Regular polygon**: A polygon with all sides and angles congruent, which can be circumscribed by a circle.

**Center**: The center of the circumscribed circle around the polygon.

**Radius**: The distance from the center to any vertex (equal for all vertices).

**Apothem**: The perpendicular distance from the center to any side (height of the isosceles triangle formed by two radii and a side).

**Perimeter (**p**)**: Total length of all sides (p=n⋅s, where n = number of sides, s = side length).

Area of a Regular Polygon:

The area of a regular polygon is half the product of its apothem and perimeter:

**A=1/2 x a x p**

Derivation: A regular n-gon can be divided into n congruent isosceles triangles, each with area 1/2​sa. Summing these gives 1/2​(ns)a=21​ap. (s=side, a=apothem, n=number of sides an n-gon have)

**Trigonometry and Area**

From 10-3, you learned to find the area of a regular polygon by using the formula

A=1/2 x a x p, where is the apothem and p is the perimeter.

By using the following formula you can solve other types of problems:

To find the area of a regular polygon when only side length is known, combine the regular polygon area formula with right-triangle trigonometry. Use the central angle to form a right triangle, then solve for the apothem with the tangent ratio.

- **Find the perimeter length**: p=sn
- **Calculate the central angle**: 360∘​/n
- **Find the half-angle for the right triangle**: θ=180∘/n​
- **Half the side length**: s/2​ (opposite side in the right triangle)
- **Solve for apothem**: a= (s/2​)/tanθ
- **Calculate area**: A=1/2ap

**10-6**

**Circles and Arcs**

Core Definitions:

Circle: Set of all points in a plane equidistant from a fixed point (the center). Named by its center (e.g., ⊙P).

Radius: Segment from center to any point on the circle (e.g., PC).

Diameter: Chord passing through the center (twice the radius: d=2r).

Chord: Segment with both endpoints on the circle (e.g., AB).

Central Angle: Angle with vertex at the circle’s center and sides as radii (e.g., ∠CPA).

Arc Types

Semicircle: Arc measuring 180∘ (half the circle, formed by a diameter).

Minor Arc: Arc measuring less than 180∘ (named with 2 points).

Major Arc: Arc measuring more than 180∘ (named with 3 points to avoid ambiguity).

Adjacent Arcs: Arcs of the same circle with exactly one point in common.

**Key Theorems:**

Arc Addition Postulate: The measure of an arc formed by two adjacent arcs is the sum of the measures of the two arcs:

Arc Length Theorem: The length of an arc is a fraction of the circle’s circumference, proportional to the arc’s degree measure:

Circumference of a Circle: The circumference C of a circle is equal to the product of π (pi) and the diameter d, or 2π times the radius r:

**C= π d or C=2πr**

**Areas of Circles and Sectors**

Area of a Circle:

The area of a circle is the product of π and the square of the radius r.

**A=πr^2**

Sector of a Circle:

A sector is a region bounded by two radii and an arc of the circle.

The area of a sector is the product of the ratio 360m​ (arc measure over 360) and the area of the circle:

Segment of a Circle:

A segment is a region bounded by an arc and a chord.

**Area of a Segment** = **Area of the Sector** − **Area of the Triangle** (formed by the two radii and the chord)
