- **Congruent Figures**
- Congruent polygons are polygons that are identical in both size and shape.
- Corresponding parts refer to the matching sides and angles between congruent polygons, and these parts are equal to each other.
- Notation: When we write ΔABC ≅ ΔQTJ, we're saying that:
- The sides match up: AB ≅ QT, BC ≅ TJ, and AC ≅ QJ
- The angles match up: ∠A ≅ ∠Q, ∠B ≅ ∠T, and ∠C ≅ ∠J
  - **Triangle Congruence by SSS, SAS, AAS, ASA**

**1. Congruent Figures**

- **Definition**: Two figures are congruent if they have exactly the same size and shape.
- **Corresponding Parts**: When figures are congruent, all matching sides and angles are equal.
- **Notation**: ΔABC ≅ ΔDEF means:
  - AB ≅ DE, BC ≅ EF, AC ≅ DF
  - ∠A ≅ ∠D, ∠B ≅ ∠E, ∠C ≅ ∠F

**2. Triangle Congruence Postulates & Theorems**

- **SSS (Side-Side-Side)**: If three sides of one triangle are congruent to three sides of another triangle.
- **SAS (Side-Angle-Side)**: If two sides and the included angle are congruent to two sides and the included angle of another triangle.
- **ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)**: If two angles and the included side are congruent to two angles and the included side of another triangle.
- **AAS (Angle-Angle-Side)**: If two angles and a non-included side are congruent to two angles and the corresponding non-included side of another triangle.

**3. Key Proof Strategies**

- **Identify Given Information**: Mark known congruent parts on the diagram.
- **Look for Common Elements**: Shared sides or angles (reflexive property).
- **Use Parallel Lines**: Alternate interior angles, corresponding angles.
- **Use Definitions**: Midpoints, bisectors, perpendicular lines.
- **Chain Reasoning**: Combine multiple steps to establish congruence.

**4. Special Cases & Tips**

- **SSA Doesn’t** **Work**: Side-Side-Angle is not a valid congruence condition.
- **Order Matters**: List corresponding vertices in the same order in congruence statements.
- **Flow Proofs**: Show logical progression using arrows and boxes.
- **Two-Column Proofs**: Statements with reasons in adjacent columns.

**5. Common Proof Patterns**

- **Using Midpoints**: If a point is a midpoint, it creates two congruent segments.
- **Using Bisectors**: Angle bisectors create two congruent angles.
- **Using Parallel Lines**: Look for alternate interior angles and corresponding angles.
- **Overlapping Triangles**: Identify shared sides/angles using reflexive property.

**6. Problem-Solving Approach**

- Identify what you need to prove.
- List all given information.
- Mark the diagram with known congruent parts.
- Determine which congruence postulate/theorem applies.
- Write the proof in logical steps with clear reasons.
  - **Using Congruent Triangles: CPCTC**

**1. CPCTC Principle**

- **CPCTC** = "Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent"
- Once you prove two triangles are congruent (using SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS), then ALL their corresponding parts become congruent
- This allows you to prove:
  - Corresponding sides are equal
  - Corresponding angles are equal

**2. Proof Strategy with CPCTC**

- **First**: Prove triangles are congruent using a congruence theorem/postulate
- **Then**: Use CPCTC to conclude that specific parts match
- **Finally**: Apply these congruent parts to solve problems

**3. Common Applications**

- Proving sides equal in parallelograms
- Proving segments equal in complex figures
- Finding unknown lengths and angles
- Error analysis in geometric proofs

**4. Important Notes**

- CPCTC can ONLY be used AFTER proving triangle congruence
- Be careful to identify corresponding parts correctly
- Common errors include:
  - Using wrong corresponding angles
  - Assuming congruence before proving it
  - Misapplying angle bisector definitions
  - **Congruence in Right Triangles**

**1. Hypotenuse-Leg (HL) Theorem**

- **Theorem**: If the hypotenuse and one leg of a right triangle are congruent to the hypotenuse and one leg of another right triangle, then the triangles are congruent.
- **Requirements**:
  - Both triangles must be right triangles (have 90° angles)
  - Hypotenuses are congruent
  - One pair of corresponding legs are congruent

**2. Important Notes**

- HL is essentially the SSA (Side-Side-Angle) case that works ONLY for right triangles
- Regular SSA does not guarantee triangle congruence in non-right triangles
- The right angle is the crucial element that makes HL valid

**3. Proof Strategy with HL**

- Identify both triangles are right triangles
- Identify congruent hypotenuses
- Identify one pair of congruent legs
- Apply HL theorem to prove triangle congruence
- Use CPCTC if needed to prove other parts congruent

**4. Common Applications**

- Proving congruence in isosceles right triangles
- Problems involving perpendicular lines and equal distances
- Geometric constructions with right angles
- Finding perimeters and lengths in complex right triangle arrangements

**5. Key Pattern**

- Given: Right angles at ∠P and ∠Q
- Given: SP ≅ QR (hypotenuses)
- Given: PR ≅ PR (shared leg)
- Conclusion: ΔPRS ≅ ΔRPQ (by HL)

**Extra** **-** **Statistic**

- **Statistics**

**1. Types of Data**

- **Structured Data**: Information arranged neatly in tables where each row represents a case and each column represents a different variable
- **Unstructured Data**: Information that comes in text form, like customer reviews, emails, or social media posts
- **Variables**:
  - **Categorical**: Labels that group things into categories (like gender, sentiment, or age ranges)
  - **Quantitative**: Actual numbers you can measure (like someone's age, a product rating, or price)

**2. Looking at One Categorical Variable**

- **Frequency Table**: A simple count of how many items fall into each category
- **Relative Frequency Table**: Shows what percentage or proportion each category represents
- **Visual Representations**:
  - **Bar Chart**: Lets you compare how many items are in each category
  - **Pie Chart**: Shows how the whole is divided into different parts
- **Area Principle**: The size of elements in your graph should accurately reflect the data they represent

**3. Examining Two Categorical Variables Together**

- **Contingency Table (Cross-tabulation)**: A table that helps you see how two different variables relate to each other
- **Marginal Distribution**: The totals you see at the edges of the table (showing just one variable at a time)
- **Conditional Distribution**: How one variable is distributed when you already know something about the other variable
- **Ways to Calculate Percentages**:
  - **Table %**: Divide a cell's count by the total number of cases
  - **Row %**: Divide a cell's count by that row's total
  - **Column %**: Divide a cell's count by that column's total

**4. Important Ideas to Remember**

- **Independence**: When one variable's distribution stays consistent regardless of the other variable's categories
- **Association**: When the distribution changes depending on the other variable (meaning the variables are connected)
- **Charts for Two Variables**:
  - **Segmented Bar Chart**: Bars that always add up to 100%, split into different category sections
  - **Side-by-Side Bar Chart**: Places bars next to each other so you can easily compare percentages between groups

**5. Steps for Analyzing Data**

- Turn unstructured data into structured format
- Figure out what type each variable is (categorical or quantitative)
- Build frequency distributions
- Create visuals to show individual variable patterns
- Use contingency tables to explore how variables interact
- Search for trends and connections
- Make informed conclusions about the relationships you discover
