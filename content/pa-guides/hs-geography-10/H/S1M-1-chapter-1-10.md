3 types of density:

Arithmetic (crude) = total number of ppl/ total land area

Physiological (nutritional) = total number of ppl/ arable land A high physiological density suggests that a region may struggle feeding its population. For example: Egypt’s physiological density is extremely high because nearly all its people depend on the narrow strip of arable land along the Nile.

Agricultural= number of farmers/ arable lands

Developed countries have low agricultural density as only a few farmers using machines work large areas while developing countries often have high agricultural density (many farmers working small plots by hand).

Diffusion processes:

Relocation: physically moving, carrying ideas

Slow, small-scale, and tied to migration patterns.

Contagious Diffusion: rapid, widespread diffusion throughout the population, analogous to the spread of a disease.

Example: A viral internet meme spreading person-to-person.

Stimulus Di usion: The spread of an underlying principle of an idea or innovation, even if the speci c characteristic itself does not spread successfully. The idea is adopted but modi ed to t local conditions. Example: McDonald's adapting its menu to local tastes in di erent countries (e.g., vegetarian options in India)

Hierarchical Di usion: spread of an idea or innovation from persons or nodes of authority or power to other persons or places.

Two Common Patterns: 1. From larger to smaller places: An innovation appears in a major city rst, then spreads to secondary cities, then to towns, and nally to rural areas. 2. From in uential people to the general population.
