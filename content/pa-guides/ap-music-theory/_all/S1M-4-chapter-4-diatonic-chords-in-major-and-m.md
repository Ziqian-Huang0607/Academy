# S1M-4 Chapter 4 Diatonic Chords in Major and Minor Keys

> From the Grade 10 Resource Department's Semester 1 Midterm Music AP review guide (PA). Writers: Sophia Lu 10-7. Range: Chapter 1-6. Student-made reference material: where it differs from class, follow class.

Roman numerals and diatonic construction

**Diatonic chords** use notes belonging to the scale or mode under discussion. A Roman numeral identifies the scale degree of the root and the chord quality. Arabic figures attached to it identify bass position. These are separate pieces of information.

Uppercase numerals represent major triads; lowercase represent minor triads. A small ° identifies a diminished triad; + identifies an augmented triad. Scale degree 5 is a root location; V means the major chord built on that location. A chord with degree 3 in the bass need not be iii: it could be I6.

Triads in major

Sequence: I ii iii IV V vi vii°. Major triads occur on 1, 4 and 5; minor triads on 2, 3 and 6; the diminished triad occurs on 7. There is no diatonic augmented triad in a major scale.

Seventh chords in major

Adding one more alternate scale note yields: IM7, ii7, iii7, IVM7, V7, vi7, viiø7. The M distinguishes a major seventh above a major triad. Lowercase ii7, iii7 and vi7 already identify minor seventh chords in this analysis system; an extra lowercase m is unnecessary.

Common triads in minor

In A minor: i = A C E; ii° = B D F; III = C E G; iv = D F A; V = E G# B; VI = F A C; vii° = G# B D. Here V and vii° contain G#, while III contains G natural.

If every triad is built strictly from A harmonic minor, III becomes C E G#, an augmented triad written III+. This explains why a table of harmonic-minor triads can differ from a table of commonly used minor-key chords.

Natural minor also provides v = E G B and VII = G B D in A minor. Raised degree 6 can produce additional forms, such as IV = D F# A or ii = B D F#. Identify actual notes before deciding which version is present.

Harmonic analysis method

1. Establish the key from the signature and musical context. A signature alone gives a relative major/minor pair, not a final answer.

2. List the actual chord pitches with every accidental and remove octave doublings when identifying chord type.

3. Rearrange the notes into thirds to find the root. Decide whether the chord is a triad or seventh chord.

4. Find the root’s scale degree in the key. Select uppercase, lowercase, °, +, ø7 or M7 as needed from the actual quality.

5. Identify the actual bass and attach the correct inversion figure. Recheck that all chord members agree with the label.
