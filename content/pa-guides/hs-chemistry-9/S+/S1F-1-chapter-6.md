## 6.2 Quantized energy and photons

- The Nature of Energy
- Discovered by Max Planck, energy comes in packets called quanta (singular: quantum)
- E = h
- E=the energy of a single quantum(J)
- h=6.626 10^–34 J·s

(2) The Photoelectric Effect

- Electrons are ejected when light with enough frequency shines on a metal, Albert Einstein used Planck’s quantum theory to explain this phenomenon
- Light consists of quanta of energy called photons
- When a photon strikes the surface of metal, it transfers its energy to an electron of an atom. If the energy reach Ethreshold, the electron would be excited enough to eject from the atom.
- An electron can only receive one photon’s energy, so only frequency matters if the electron would be emitted or not. The brightness (quantity of photons) doesn’t matter.
- With frequency that reach Ethreshold, a greater number of photons means more electrons are ejected
- Ethreshold: The minimum energy required to eject electron
- KE = Ephoton - Ethreshold

## 6.3 Line spectra and the Bohr Model

- Atomic emission spectrum

Visible spectrum-> continuous spectrum (include all frequency)

Atomic emission spectrum->line spectrum (don’t include all frequency), identify elements

-It was formed by the set of frequencies (lights) of the electromagnetic waves emitted by atoms of the element

- Bohr Model of the Atom

Bohr assumed that electrons move in certain circular orbits (2D) around the nucleus

-The level of electron is designated by a quantum number n (n = 1,2,3, …)

- The lowest allowable energy state ( n = 1) of an atom is called its ground state.

Electron on the other allowable energy levels are called excited states.

-Electron can be excited to higher energy level when it absorbs energy. When the atom is in an excited state, the electron can drop from the higher energy level to a lower energy level.

- As a result, the atom emits a photon corresponding to the difference between the energy levels associated with two orbits. Only certain frequencies of the radiation can be emitted.

-ΔE=E higher-energy orbit - E lower-energy orbit=h

-for the gap between upper levels are really small, the kind of electromagnetic radiation was determined by the destination

## 6.4 The wave behavior of matter

(1) The Wave Nature of Matter

-Louis de Broglie theorized that if light can have material properties, matter should exhibit wave properties.

- For the matters which are larger than the atoms, their wavelike character can be neglected, because their wavelength are too short to be measured

(2) The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

- The position and the velocity/momentum of a moving electron cannot simultaneously be measured and known exactly (only in atoms’ level)
- To locate an electron, a photon must strike it and bounce back to detection device. The collision between the photon and the electron changes the motion or location of the electron in unpredictable way!
  - **Quantum mechanics and atomic orbitals**
- Quantum Mechanics
- Schrödinger (German, 1887-1961) discovered Quantum Mechanical Model, combining the ideas of Bohr’s Model, Matter waves, and Heisenberg’s Uncertainty Principle
- Atomic orbitals
- atomic orbital is the mathematical expression as solutions to the Schrödinger equation
- atomic orbital is represented pictorially as a region of space (3D) in which there is a high probability of finding an electron
