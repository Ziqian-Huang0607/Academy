1. Describing Matter

Property: characteristics of a substance for recognition

Physical Property: a quality or condition of a substance that can be observed or measured without changing the composition

e.g. state, color, texture, smell, MP and BP, etc.

Melting Point (MP): the temperature at which a solid changes into a liquid

Boiling Point (BP): the temperature at which a liquid changes into a gas

Chemical Property: the ability of a substance to undergo a specific chemical change

can ONLY be observed when the substance undergoes a change

e.g. flammability, stability, toxicity, etc.

State: a physical property refers to the distinct forms of matter Solid

i. incompressible; definite shape & volume; high density

ii. strong Attraction Force between particles

iii. the motion of particles is in a vibrating form at fixed position

Liquid

i. slightly compressible; no definite shape; definite volume; high density

ii. slightly weaker attraction force between particles, compare to solid state

iii. move freely but can't move far away due to interaction force

Gas:

i. highly compressible; no definite shape/volume; low density

ii. very weak attraction force between particles

iii. move freely, as far away as it can.

Vapor (another form of a substance, NOT a state of matter)

i. the gaseous state of a substance

ii. the substance is generally liquid or solid at room temp.

iii. Vapor itself is not visible to the human eyes. However, the tiny particles formed from vapor are visible, e.g. the tiny liquid water droplets created by condensation.

Matter can neither be created nor destroyed in a given chemical reaction

The total mass of reactant must be equivalent to the total mass of products

5. Separating Mixtures

Differences In the physical properties may help to separate mixtures, e.g. the difference in density between phases.

1. Filtration: a process that separates a solid from a liquid in a heterogeneous mixture (solid + liquid)

the liquid will removed (goes out) by the use of a filter medium that permits the fluid to pass but retains the solid particles.

Key Terms:

Filter paper - a medium that permits the fluid to pass but retains solid particles.

Funnel - responsible for guiding the flow of the liquid Glass rod - for drainage and transfer

Filtrate - clear liquid after filtration

Residue - particles retained by filter paper

6. Symbols and Formulas

Chemists use chemical symbols to represent elements, and chemical formulas to represent compounds

Chemical Symbol: a notation of one or two letters that represents a chemical element

The first letter of the chemical symbol is capitalized, while the second letter is lowercased.

e.g. Au is the symbol for Gold, with uppercase A and lowercase u.

Chemical Formula: tells which elements make up a compound, as well as how many atoms of each element are present

e.g.     = 2 hydrogens + 1 oxygen

Subscript Numbers: tell how many atoms of that element exist in one unit (no subscript means there's only one atom present)

7. Periodic Table

Periodic Table: Arrangement of elements in which the elements are separated into groups based on a set of repeating properties The elements are listed by their atomic number (increasing order) from left to right, and then from top to bottom in the periodic table.

Period: a row of elements in the periodic table whose properties change gradually and predictably

7 periods (rows)

Group: the vertical column of the periodic table

18 groups (columns)

Each group contains the elements that have similar chemical properties

Types:
