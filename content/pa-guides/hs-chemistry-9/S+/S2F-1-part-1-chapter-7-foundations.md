## 1.1 Effective Nuclear Charge (Zₑff)

In a many-electron atom, each electron is simultaneously attracted to the nucleus (positive) and repelled by other electrons (negative). The net attractive force acting on an electron is defined as effective nuclear charge (Zₑff).

> **Key Definition: Effective Nuclear Charge**
> **Zₑff= Z − S**
> Where Z = actual nuclear charge (atomic number), S = shielding constant (total number of core electrons that shield valence electrons)

The shielding effect is the repelling effect that inner (core) electrons have on outer (valence) electrons, reducing the pull of the nucleus on valence electrons.

**Key rules for** **Zₑff** **trends:**

- Across a period (left → right): INCREASES(more protons, electrons fill the same shell; shielding stays nearly unchanged)
- Down a group (top → bottom): SLIGHTLY INCREASES (diffuse core electron clouds provide weak shielding)
- Same energy level: Zₑff order: s > p > d > f (s electrons lie closest to the nucleus)

### 1.2 Atomic and Ionic Radius

The bonding atomic radius is defined as one-half of the distance between two covalently bonded nuclei of the same element. It is a measure of how large an atom is.

**Atomic radius trends:**

- Across a period (left to right): radius DECREASES — more protons increase Zₑff, pulling electrons in closer despite being in the same shell.
- Down a group: radius INCREASES — each new period adds a new electron shell, greatly increasing distance from nucleus, and inter-electron repulsion grows.

Exception: Atomic radii of transition metals decrease much more slowly, as d electrons provide weak shielding.

**Ionic radius rules:**

- Cations < parent atoms: Losing electrons REDUCES repulsion, <mark>so</mark> electrons are pulled tighter by higher Zₑff.
- Anions > parent atoms: Gaining electrons INCREASES repulsion and expands the electron cloud.
- Down a group: Ionic radius INCREASES larger principal quantum number (n).
- Isoelectronic ions (same total electrons): radius DECREASES as nuclear charge rises.Example: O2−>F−>Na+>Mg2+ (all have 10 electrons)

### 1.3 Ionization Energy (IE)

Ionization energy (IE) is the minimum energy needed to remove an electron from a gaseous atom or ion in its ground state. This process is always endothermic (positive energy value)

because you must overcome the attraction between the electron and the nucleus.

**Trends in 1st Ionization Energy:**

- Across a period (left to right): IE generally INCREASES (greater Zₑff means electrons are held more tightly.)
- Down a group: IE DECREASES (valence electrons are further from nucleus and more shielded, so easier to remove.)

**Two key exceptions to the left-to-right increase:**

- Group IIA → IIIA (e.g., Mg → Al): Slight DROP because Al loses an electron from a 3p orbital (higher energy, easier to remove) while Mg loses from 3s.
- Group VA → VIA (e.g., N → O): Slight DROP because O must remove an electron from a doubly-occupied orbital. Paired electrons repel each other, making one easier to remove.

### 1.4 Electronegativity (EN)

### Electronegativity refers to an atom’s ability to attract bonding electrons within a compound. It DIFFERS from ionization energy, which describes electron removal from isolated atoms.

Linus Pauling developed the most widely used electronegativity scale (dimensionless, ~0.7 to 4.0).

- **Most electronegative element: Fluorine (F) = 3.98**
- **Least electronegative element: Francium (Fr) ≈ 0.7**

**EN Trends**

Across a period (left → right): INCREASES (more protons pull bonding electrons more strongly)

Down a group (top → bottom): DECREASES (valence electrons further from nucleus, less attraction for bonding electrons).
