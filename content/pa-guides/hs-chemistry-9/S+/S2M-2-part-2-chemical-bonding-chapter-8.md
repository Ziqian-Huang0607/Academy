## 8.1 Lewis Symbols and the Octet Rule

- A **chemical bond** is the electrostatic attraction that holds atoms together
  - **Ionic bond**: attraction between cations and anions
  - **Covalent bonds**: attraction between nucleus and shared electron pairs
  - **Metallic bond**: attraction between metal cations and delocalized electrons

**Octet Rule**

- All elements tend to reach **8 valence electrons** (stable noble gas configuration).

**Lewis Symbols**

- Consist of an element’s symbol + one dot per valence electron.

## 8.2 Ionic Bonding

**Definition**

- Ionic bond = electrostatic attraction between **cations (+)** and **anions (–)**.
- Formed by **complete electron transfer** from metal to nonmetal.

Cation Formation

- Metals (low electronegativity) lose valence electrons.
- Charge = Group 1A: +1; Group 2A: +2; Group 3A: +3.

Anion Formation (Negative Ions)

- Nonmetals (high electronegativity) gain electrons.
- Charge = group number – 8.

**Naming Ions** **(Monatomic)**

- **Cations**: same as element name (Na⁺ = sodium ion).
- **Anions**: element stem + -ide (Cl⁻ = chloride, O²⁻ = oxide).
- **Transition metals** **with multiple ions**: include Roman numeral for charge (Cu²⁺ = copper(II) Cu1+ = copper(II)).

**Writing Ionic Formulas (Crisscross Method)**

- Write cation symbol first, then anion symbol.
- Swap charge values to become subscripts.
- Reduce to the **lowest wholenumber ratio**.
  - **Ionic compounds don’t have discrete units**

**Properties of Ionic Compounds**

- High melting/boiling points (due to strong electrostatic attraction)
- Hard and brittle
- Conduct electricity **only when molten or dissolved in water**
- Exist as crystal lattices; basic unit = **formula unit**

**Lattice Energy**

- Definition: energy required to separate 1 mole of ionic solid into gaseous ions.
  - Higher ion charge or smaller ionic radius→ higher lattice energy
  - Smaller ion size or larger ionic radius→ higher lattice energy
  - **Covalent Bonding**

**Definition**: electrostatic attraction between **shared electrons** and atomic nuclei.

- Forms between nonmetal atoms

**Single, Double, Triple Covalent Bonds**

- **Single bond**: 1 shared pair (1 σ bond)
- **Double bond**: 2 shared pairs (1 σ + 1 π bond)
- **Triple bond**: 3 shared pairs (1 σ + 2 π bonds)

**Bond Length and Bond Energy**

- Shorter bond = stronger bond
- Bond length: **triple < double < single**
- Bond energy: **triple > double > single**

**Bond Dissociation Energy**

- Definition: energy needed to break 1 mole of a covalent bond.

**Molecular vs. Ionic Compounds**

| **Molecular Compounds** | **Ionic Compounds** |
|---|---|
| Nonmetal+Nonmetal | Metal+Metal |
| Molecule (discrete units) | Formula Unit (no discrete units) |
| Lower Melting Point | Higher Melting Point |
| Formula cannot be written in the simplest whole number ratio (HO and H2O2 are different) | Formula can only be written in the simplest whole number ratio |
| Weaker bonds | Stronger bonds |

## 8.4 Bond Polarity and Electronegativity

**Bond Polarity**

**Definition**: a measure of how unequally two atoms share the electrons in a covalent bond

- comes from a difference in electronegativity between the two bonded atoms.
- Nonpolar covalent: electrons shared equally (ΔEN ≤ 0.4)
- Polar covalent: electrons shared unequally (0.4 < ΔEN < 2.0)
- Ionic: electron transfer (ΔEN ≥ 2.0)

## 8.5 Drawing Lewis Structures

**StepbyStep Method**

- Calculate **total valence electrons** of all atoms.
- Put the central atom in the middle (usually the least electronegative atom except for hydrogen) and terminal atoms around it
- Connect atoms with single bonds.
- Complete octets for all terminal atoms.
- Assign remaining electrons to the central atom.
- Add double/triple bonds if the central atom does not have an octet.

**when more than 1 structure is valid, consider the structure with minimum formal charge**

**when more than 1 structure is valid AND those structures have the same formal charge, consider the structure that puts the negative formal charge on the most electronegative atom OR the structure that is symmetrical (more stable)**

  - **Resonance Structures**

**Definition**: Two or more valid Lewis structures for the same molecule/ion.

- When there are multiple Lewis structures possible, draw all of them and put an arrow between to indicate the resonance strcture
- HOWEVER, in reality, molecules with resonance structure do not match the extreme case shown above
- instead, the electron pair is delocalized and is in a freely moving state
- so, all bonds in the structure are equal (no clear single/double bonds) and have bond lengths that is short than single bonds but longer than double bonds

## 8.7 Exceptions to the Octet Rule

Three common exceptions:

- **Incomplete octets**
  - Fewer than 8 electrons (ex: Be, B).
- **Expanded octets**
  - More than 8 electrons (elements in period 3 and below has access to dorbitals).
- **Oddelectron molecules (free radicals)**
  - Unpaired electron; cannot satisfy octet (ex: NO, NO₂).
