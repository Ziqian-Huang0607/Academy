## 7.4 Ionization Energy

**Definition**: Energy required to remove one electron from an atom in a gaseous state

Successive ionization energy increases because as more electrons are removed, electron-pair repulsions are reduced, so the attraction force between the electrons and the nucleus is stronger. **Once an atom loses all its valence electrons, there is a big jump to the next IE**

**Periodic trends:**

- Down a group (Top Bottom): Decreases
  - Atomic size increasesvalence electrons are farther from the nucleusshielding effect from inner electrons makes the attraction force weaker easier to remove an electron
- Across a period (Left Right): Increases
  - Nuclear charge (number of protons) increase but electrons are added to the same valence shellnucleus pulls electrons more stronglyharder to remove an electron

Exceptions: Groups 2A ↔ 3A: The electron being removed is from a higher-energy orbital.

Groups 5A ↔ 6A: Electron-electron repulsion in a paired orbital.

## 7.5 Electron Affinity & Electronegativity

**Electron affinity**

**Definition**: energy change that occurs when it gains an electron

- Negative electron affinity: the process is exothermic
- Positive electron affinity: the process is endothermic

**Periodic trends**

- Down a group (Top Bottom): Becomes more positive
  - Larger atomic size weaker attraction for the incoming electronendothermic
- Across a period (Left Right): Becomes more negative
  - Increasing nuclear charge pulls the added electron more stronglyexothermic

Exceptions: Group 2A: the extra electron must go in the p orbital, more energy required

Group 5A: adding an electron to a half-filled p orbital would cause repulsion, more energy required

**Electronegativity**

**Definition**: the ability of an atom to attract electrons when the atom is in a compound

- Measured through the Pauling Scale, running from around 0.7(Fr) to 3.98(F)

**Periodic trends**

- Down a group (Top Bottom): Decrease
  - Number of electron shells increasethe valence shell are getting less strongly held by the nucleusharder for the atom to attract electrons
- Across a period (Left Right): Increase
  - Proton number increasesthe attraction from the nucleus is getting greatereasier for the atom to attract electrons

**Noble gases do not have electronegativity values because they do not form bonds**

## 7.6 Metals, Nonmetals, and Metalloids

**Metals**: tend to form cations, luster, malleable, conductive (left side of the periodic table)

**Nonmetals**: tend to form anions, dull, brittle, nonconductive (right side of the periodic table)

**Metalloids**: intermediate properties (between metals and nonmetals)
