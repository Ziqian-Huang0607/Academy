4. The Uncertainty Principle

The position and the velocity of a moving object cannot simultaneously be measured and known exactly.

The principle is inconsequential for macroscopic object

To locate an electron, a photon must strike it and bounce back to detection device. But the collision’s changed the motion or location of electron in unpredictable way

5. The Bohr Model

electrons only move in certain circular orbits with allowed energy states around the nucleus designated by a quantum number n(1,2,3...). *Energy is emitted to move an electron* from 1 allowed energy state to another

Evaluations:

a. Truth:

i. Adequate for explaining single-electron atom and ions

ii. The introduction of quantized energy levels lead to the current model of electronic structure.

iii. Electrons exist only in certain discrete energy levels.

iv. Energy is involved in the transition of an electron from one level to another.

b. Failed:

i. Failed to explain the spectrum of any other element

ii. Can’t explain the different chemical behavior of various elements

iii. Electrons don’t move around nucleus in circular orbits.

7. The Quantum Mechanical Model

The Quantum Mechanical Model is consist of the aforementioned three concept:

a. Bohr's Model - The energy is quantized

b. Matter waves - Electrons have wavelike properties c. Heisenberg's uncertainty principle - It’s impossible to determine the location of the electron in the atom It determines the allowed energies an electron can have and the probability to find the electron in various locations around the nucleus.

The probability can be represented as electron cloud,it’s denser where the probability of finding electrons is higher.

This led to a mathematical equation-atomic orbital (a region of space where the probability of finding electrons is high)

different atomic orbitals are denoted by letters: s, p, d, and f

s orbitals - spherical shape (1 kind)

p orbitals - dumbbell shape (3 kinds)

d orbitals - "four-leaf clover" shape (5 kinds) f orbitals (7 kinds)
