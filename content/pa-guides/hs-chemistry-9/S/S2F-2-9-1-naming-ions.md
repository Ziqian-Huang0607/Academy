# S2F-2 9.1 Naming Ions

> From the Grade 9 Resource Department's Semester 2 Final Chemistry S review guide (PA). Writers: James 9(6). Editor: James 9(6). Range: 8.1 – 8.4, 9.1 – 9.4. Student-made reference material: where it differs from class, follow class.

**Monatomic ions**

- Formed by a single atom gaining or losing valence electrons, carrying positive or negative charges.
- **Cations (positive ions)**: Main group metals (1A, 2A, 3A) lose electrons to form cations. Names are the element name + ion/cation.
- **Anions (negative ions)**: For Group A nonmetals, charge = group number − 8. Names use the element stem + suffix **-ide**.
- **Transition metal cations**: Many have multiple possible charges. Two naming methods:
    - Stock system: Add a Roman numeral in parentheses to show charge.
    - Traditional root names (e.g., ferrous/ferric for iron ions).

**Polyatomic ions**

- Groups of bonded atoms acting as a single charged unit.
- Suffix rules for oxygen-containing ions: **-ate** (more oxygen), **-ite** (less oxygen).
- Prefix rules: per- + -ate (most oxygen); hypo- + -ite (fewest oxygen).

## 9.2 Naming and Writing Formulas for Ionic Compounds

**Binary ionic compounds (two elements: metal + nonmetal)**

- Formula writing: Write cation first, then anion; balance charges (crisscross method).
- Naming: Cation name first, then anion name. Use Roman numerals for metals with variable charges.

**Ionic compounds with polyatomic ions**

- Use **parentheses** around polyatomic ions when more than one is needed.
- Naming rule: Combine the cation name and polyatomic ion name directly.

## 9.4 Naming and Writing Formulas for Acids and Bases

**Bases**

- Definition: Compounds that produces hydroxide ions when dissolved in water.
- Name of cation followed by name of anion
- NaOH = Sodium Hydroxide

**Acids**

- Both monatomic and polyatomic
- When the molecule name ends with -ide or -ate, the acid will end in -ic acid.
- When it ends in -ite, the acid will end with -ous
- Cl- is chloride, and the acid that forms with this is HCl (Hydrochloric acid).
- ClO^2- is Chlorite, and this forms Chlorous acid
- Generally, acids must have hydrogen in them
