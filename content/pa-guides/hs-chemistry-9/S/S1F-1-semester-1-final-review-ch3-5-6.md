**3-1 Using and Expressing** **Measurements**

-Measurements: a quantity that has both a number and a unit

-Scientific notation: m × 10n
-·m: coefficient (a number between 1 and 10)
- n: exponent (n is the number of places decimal point is moved to place the coefficient between 1 and 10 )

**Adding and Subtracting with Scientific Notation**

-Make sure the exponents are the same, add/subtract the coefficients

**-EX: (5.4×103)+(8.0×102)**
=(5.4×103)+(0.80×103)
=(5.4+0.80)×103
=6.2×103

**Multiplying with Scientific Notations**

-Multiply the coefficients, add the exponents

**-EX: (2.1×103)×(4.0×10-7)**
=(2.1×4.0)×103+(-7)
=8.4×10-4

**Dividing with Scientific Notation**

-Divide the coefficients, subtract the exponent in the denominator from the exponent in the numerator

- **EG: (3.0×105)/(6.0×102)**
=(3.0/6.0)×105-2
=0.50×103
=5.0×102

**Accuracy and Precision**

-Accuracy: measure of how close a measurement comes to the actual or true value of whatever is measured (EG the closeness of a dart to the bull’s-eye)

-Precision: Measure of how close a series of measurements are to one another, irrespective of the actual value (EG the closeness of several darts to one another)

-Reliable results: Both high in precision and accuracy

**Error**

-Error: difference between the experimental value and the accepted value

**Error=experimental value - accepted value**

-Percent error: measurement of the absolute value of the error divided by thae accepted value, multiplied by 100%

**Percent error =** $\frac{|\text{ error }|}{\text{ acceptedvalue }}$ **×** **100%**

**Significant Figures**

-Significant figures: include all of the digits that are known, plus a last digit that is estimated

*Measurements must always be reported to the correct number of significant figures because calculated answers often depend on the number of significant figures in the values used in the calculation

-Increment: represents the unit of change on a scale, determines the precision of the measurement instrument

**Counting significant figures**

-There is a decimal point: count from left to right, starting from the first non-zero digit

-There is no decimal point: count from right to left, starting from the first non-zero digit

-Exact defined values (e.g 1km=1000m, 23 people): unlimited number of significant figures

**Significant Figures in Calculation**

-Rounding off: The digit immediately to the right of the last significant digit <5, it is dropped and the value of the last significant digit remains the same. If it ≥5, then the value of the last significant place is increased by 1.

-Adding and subtracting: result has the same number of DECIMAL PLACES as the measurement with the fewest decimal place after the decimal point

-Multiplying and Dividing: Result contains the number of significant digits as the measurements with the fewest significant digits.

-If there are multiple steps, ONLY ROUND THE FINAL ANSWER, don’t round anything in the steps

**6-1 Organizing the elements**

**History**

-only 13 elements identified in the year 1700, so chemists began to use scientific methods to search for elements, the rate of discovery increased. In one decade, chemists discovered five new elements

-1790s, Lavoisier made a list of 23 elements

-Law of Triads( Dobereiner, 1800s): groups of three, elements in a triad had similar chem properties

-Law of Octaves( Newland, 1864): groups of eight, elements were arranged in order of increasing atomic mass, the properties repeat every eight elements

-Mendeleev(1869): arranged the elements in increasing atomic mass, predicted that elements would be discovered to fill spaces which he left blank

*found the right pattern, arranged them according to the wrong property

-H.G.J Moseley (1914): determined an atomic number for each known element, established **periodic law** (when elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number, there is a periodic repetition of their physical and chemical properties)

-modern periodic table: elements arranged in order of increasing atomic number *7 periods (row): properties of the elements change gradually in each
*18 groups/families (columns): elements in a group have similar properties

**Metals, Nonmetals, and Metalloids**

-Across the period the elements become less metallic and more nonmetallic, down a group, the properties of elements become less nonmetallic and more metallic

- **metals**: luster or shine, reflects light, good conductor of heat and electricity, solid at room temp (except for Hg), malleable

*copper is second only to silver as a conductor of electric current

- **nonmetals**: usually gases/brittle solids at room temperature, solids are dull in appearance, poor conductors of heat and electricity (except graphite)

*mostly gases at room temperature, including the main components of air (nitrogen and oxygen), a few are solids (sulfur & phosphorus), one nonmetal is liquid (bromine)

*greater variation in physical properties among nonmetals than metals

- **semimetals/metalloids**: share some properties with metals and some with nonmetals, slight luster, conduct electricity at high temperature, brittle

**6-3 Periodic Trends**

-periodic trends: properties changing in a predictable way

**Trends in Atomic Size**

- Atoms get larger going down a group
  - more energy levels -> attraction decrease -> outmost electrons go further -> size increase
- Atoms get smaller moving from left to right across each period
  - same energy level, more proton -> attraction increase -> outmost electrons are closer -> atom size decrease

- **atomic radius**: one half of the distance between the nuclei of two atoms and the same element when the atoms are joined (measured in picometer - pm)

-attraction force between electrons and nucleus will affect many atom’s properties

**Trends in Ionic Size**

- Ions get larger going down a group
  - loss of electrons -> reduce repulsion between electrons
- Ions get smaller from left to right (negative ions and positive ions of next period)
  - gain electrons -> increase repulsion between electrons
- For the same element, the one with less electrons is smaller

- **ion**: atom that has a positive (cation) or negative charge (anion)

- **octet rule**: atoms tend to gain, lose or share electrons in order to acquire a full set of valence electrons

*most energy levels have 8 valence electrons in a full set (2 in s and 6 in p)

*elements on the right side of the periodic table tend to gain electrons (anions)

*elements on the left side of the periodic table tend to lose electrons (cations)

*noble gases neither lose nor gain electrons

**Trends for Ionization Energy**

- Ionization energies decreases as you move down a group
- Ionization energies increases as you move from left to right across a period
- Ionization energies increase for every electron removed

- **Ionization energy**: energy needed to remove one of its electrons

-Ionization energy trends are exactly opposite of the trends for atomic radius (both depend on attraction between nucleus and outmost electrons)

*radius is **inversely proportional** to nuclear attraction while ionization energy is directly proportional

- **successive ionization energies**: energies required to remove electrons beyond the first electron

*An atom holds the electrons in its noble gas inner core **much more strongly** than it holds its valence electrons -> large increase when the atom begins to lose the inner electron.

**Trends for Electron Affinity**

- The nonmetals have more negative electron affinities than the metals (except for the noble gases)
  - elements that have negative electron affinity do not require energy to gain an electron, instead they release energy

- **Electron affinity**: energy change that occurs when it gains an extra electron

-Affinity is a measure of an atom’s attraction for an extra electron

-atoms that have greater attraction for an added electron have a more negative electron affinity

**Trends for Electronegativity (EN)**

- Electronegativity increases from left to right on the table, and increases as it gets higher on the periodic table

- **EN**: ability of an atom of an element to attract electrons when the atom is in a compound*EN values have no unit, is neither energy or a property that can be directly measured
