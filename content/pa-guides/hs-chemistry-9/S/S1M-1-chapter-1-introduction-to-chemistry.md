1. What is Chemistry?

Chemistry's predecessor protoscience (pre-science): Alchemy

The shift from alchemy to chemistry is through an observation by scientist Antonine-Laurent Lavoisier, where he showed that when things go through the chemical change of burning, they may gain or lose weight because some of the matter is transferred into gas.

Chemistry: the study of the composition of matter and its undergoes changes.

Chemistry affects all aspects of life and most natural events because all living and nonliving things are made of matter also known as the central science as it lays foundation for many other science fields

Matter: anything that has mass and occupies space

Mass: the physical quantity that measures the amount of contained substance in an object.

Composition: Chemical identity of the substance

2. Five Traditional Areas of Chemistry

*There is not a Clear Boundary Between the five fields

1. Organic Chemistry: the study of all chemicals containing carbon

2. Inorganic Chemistry: the study of chemicals that generally do not contain carbon

3. Biochemistry: interdisciplinary field that studies the chemical Processes of living organisms

4. Analytical Chemistry: the study that focuses on analytical methods and theories of chemical information

5. Physical Chemistry: interdisciplinary field that dealing with mechanism, rate, and energy transfer that occurs when matter undergoes a change

3. Scientific Method

Scientific Method

A logical, systematic approach to the solution of a scientific problem

Scientific Method Steps

1. Making Observations: to use one’s senses to obtain information, then ask a question about the observation

2. Testing Hypothesis: a proposed explanation for an observation

3. Do an Experiment: a procedure that is used to test a hypothesis

4. Conclusion: a judgment based on the information obtained. If a large number of experiments after support a hypothesis, the conclusion may be raised to a higher level of ideas —

Theory: a well-tested explanation for a broad set of observations

an explanation that has been supported by many experiments. (not a description) can predict the results of further experiments

explains the cause of a phenemenon Scientific Law: a concise statement that

summarizes the results of many observations and experiments.

describes a relationship in nature that is supported by many experiments (not an explanation)

a generalization that describes what happens when certain conditions are met

If a hypothesis cannot be proven / contradict to the experiment:

then the hypothesis must be discarded or modified (In an experiment, people apply inductive reasoning instead of deductive reasoning, which means that it is mainly based on observations instead of actual proofs.)

4. Things to consider in an Experiment

Variable: A quantity which is not fixed and the factor being tested

Independent Variable: the variable that one's plan to change

Dependent Variable: changes in response to a change in the independent variable

Controlled Variable: the other variables which should be controlled as constants

Quantitative data: measurements consisting both numerical value and corresponding unit

Qualitative data: data that records the properties or occurrences WITHOUT numerical values.

In an experiment, it is crucial for a control group that would be used as a standard for comparison

used to prove that the experimental procedure or regent may work
