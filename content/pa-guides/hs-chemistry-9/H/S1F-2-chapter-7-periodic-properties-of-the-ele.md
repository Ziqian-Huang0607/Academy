## 7.1 Development of the Periodic Table

**Time Table of Events**

- 1790: Antoine Lavoisier made a list of 23 elements
- 1860: A method for determining atomic mass was being agreed upon
- 1964: John Newland proposed to arrange the elements with increasing atomic mass
  - Concluded that their properties repeated every 8th element
  - This type of pattern is named periodic, because it repeats in a specific manner
  - Also (octave)
- 1869: Dmitri Mendeleev and Lothar Meyer demonstrated the connection between atomic mass and elemental properties
  - Mendeleev -> published earlier + more specific -> more famous
- 1870s: 70 known elements
- 1913: Henry Moseley arranged with atomic number
  - This is the creation of the modern Periodic Table
  - A clear periodic pattern of properties arranged

**Mendeleev’s achievements:**

- Noticed that when elements are organized with increasing atomic mass -> repetition / periodic pattern in chemical and physical properties
  - Showed that they repeated from row to row of the table
  - In the same vertical columns -> similar properties
- He correctly predicted the existence and properties of undiscovered elements
- But since he didn’t find all the elements
  - Left some blank spaces in the periodic table
  - With this, he can predict the properties of Scandium, Gallium, and Germanium that were previously undiscovered
    - He named them eka-silicon as they are being categorized in the same group with silicon (with the same properties)
    - Anything with the prefix eka- indicates that it shares similar properties and belongs to the group whose element serves as the root.
- He corrected the atomic mass of elements
  - E.g., Be, In, U, Os, Ir, Pt, Y, and Ti
- To fit in with the pattern of chemical properties
  - He sometimes broke the pattern of the increasing atomic mass when arranging
  - E.g., No.52 (127.6 mass) arranged before No.53 (126.9045)

**Verification of his Prediction: Gallium**

- Mendeleev predicted an aluminum-like element
  - Atomic weight = 68, density=5.9
- In 1875, A French chemist discovered a new element
  - Atomic weight = 69.9, density of 4.7
  - Everything is consistent with what Mendeleev predicted, except the density
- Mendeleev wrote a letter stating that the density is being mismeasured
  - And after a second experiment, it is confirmed that the density is 5.94

**7.2 Effective Nuclear Charge:**

**In a many-electron atom:**

- Electron [1] attracted to nucleus [2] repelled by other electrons
- Both factors affect the nuclear charge an electron receives
- Valence electrons experience less attraction to the nucleus

Because they receive a repulsion of core electrons

- Shielding effect: Repelling effect inner electrons have on valence electrons -> decrease in the amount of attraction felt by the valence electrons from the nuclear charge
- Effective Nuclear charge:

Charged felt by the valence electrons of an atom after the number of shielding electrons that surround the nucleus is taken into account

In Other words:

$${Z}_{\text{ eff }}=Z-S$$

Where:

${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$ Is the effective nuclear Charge

Z is the nuclear charge (number of protons).

S is the shielding constant (near to the number of core electrons).

Z is decided by the number of protons only

To predict the Shielding Constant: *Not tested*

$$S\approx (\# \text{ inner } \text{ electrons })\times 0.85+(\#\text{ same }-\text{ shell } \text{ electrons })\times 0.35$$

${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$in the outermost electrons is smaller than inner electrons due to the screening of the inner electrons

Inner electrons have a greater impact on screening than outer electrons.

**Patterns of** ${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$

- Across a period, S stays the same as they have the same core electrons, but Z will gradually increase with increasing proton.
- increasing ${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$ (significantly)
- Down a group, the Z increases significantly as the number of protons increases, but S increases almost as much as there will be one more full inner shell of electrons being added
- The more diffuse core electron is less able to screen the valence electrons from the nuclear charge
- The inner electron shells become larger with increasing principal quantum number n -> more spread out from the nucleus -> less effective at shielding
- ${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$ increases slightly down a group

This pattern is an exception for d and f-block elements

D block ${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$ increases more notably

F block ${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$ increases even more

For the same principal energy level:

${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$ : S > P > D > F

because s-orbital’s greater electron density near the nucleus = less shielding from other inner electrons.

**7.3 Sizes of Atoms and Ions:**

**Sizes** **of Atoms:**

Bonding Atomic radius: one-half of the distance between covalently bonded nuclei

Atomic Radius (measured in pm)

**Atomic Radius Patterns:**

Move down a group: more principal energy levels -> greater repulsion force between electrons -> atomic radius larger

Larger orbitals -> valence electrons are further away from the nucleus

Although ${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$ decreases slightly within a group from left to right, the fact that the electrons are in a higher principal quantum shell outweighs the slight pull of ${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$

Move left to right in a period -> gain more protons -> ${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$increase -> stronger pull on electrons with the same principal energy level -> atomic radius decreases.

**Ionic Radius Patterns:**

- Cations (positively charged ions) are smaller than parent atoms
- Loss of outer electron
- Increased effective nuclear charge
- Same nuclear charge (# of protons) but fewer electrons
- Reduced electron-electron repulsion
- Anions (negatively charged ions) are larger than parent ions
- Added electrons (but no change in principal quantum number)
- Increased electron-electron repulsion
- More electrons in the same outer shell
- Decreased effective nuclear charge per electron
- Same nuclear charge (#of protons) but more electrons

**Isoelectronic Series:**

Atoms with the same number of electrons but different nuclear charges

Those with the greatest nuclear charge (# of protons) have the smallest atomic radius. This changes dramatically

E.g. ${O}^{2-}$ and ${F}^{-}$ both have the same number of electrons, but since O has 8 protons and F has 9 protons, O is a larger atom compared to F

**Exceptions:**

Transition metals change irregularly:

They have variable Oxidation states (they may have many changes instead of only having one or two charges in the main group elements

Many are catalytic, readily accepting or donating electrons to provide a reactive surface for reactions.

For multiple charges in the Same Element:

Higher charge = smaller ion

E.g. ${Fe}^{2+}\left( 78pm \right)>{Fe}^{3+}\left( 65pm \right)$

Because higher ${Z}_{\text{ eff }}$pulls electrons closer
