**1.2 Slopes of Lines & 1.3 Finding Equations of Line & 1.4 Linear Functions and Models**

Slope: $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=\frac{y2-y1}{x2-x1}$ (Linear functions have constant rate of change)

Parallel Lines: the slopes of two lines are equal

Perpendicular Lines: the slopes of two lines are negative reciprocals

Different forms of Lines

- Slope-intercept form: $y=mx+k$
- General form: $Ax+By=C$
- Point-slope form: $y-y1=m(x-x1)$
- Intercept form: $\frac{x}{a}+\frac{y}{b}=1$

## 1.5 The Complex Numbers

The imaginary unit: $i=\surd -1$

Complex number: $a+bi$ (a: real part; b: imaginary part)

Complex conjugates: $z=a+bi$ and $z̄=a-bi$

$${i}^{1}=i, {i}^{2}=-1, {i}^{3}=-i, {i}^{4}=1$$

$${i}^{4n+1}=i, {i}^{4n+2}=-1, {i}^{4n+3}=-i, {i}^{4n+4}=1$$

## 1.6 Solving Quadratic Equations & 1.7 Quadratic Functions and Their Graphs

Quadratic Equation

- Standard form: $a{x}^{2}+bx+c=0, a\ne 0 \text{ and } a,b,c\in \mathbb{R}$

Method for solving quadratic equations:

- Factoring
  - Only works when the equation has rational roots
- Completing the square
  - Use when the equation has the form ${x}^{2}+(\text{ even } \text{ number })x+\text{ constant }=0$
- Quadratic formula
  - $x=\frac{-b\pm \sqrt{{b}^{2}-4ac}}{2a}$

Discriminant: $D={b}^{2}-4ac$

- If D>0, the equation has 2 real solutions
- If D=0, the equation has one real solution
- If D<0, the equation has no real solutions

**ALWAYS CHECK for losing/gaining a root**

- Losing a root; eg: solve (2x-5)(x+6) = 7(x+6)
- Gaining a root; eg: solve √(3x+6)=2x+1

Quadratic Function ($f(x)=a{x}^{2}+bx+c$ or $f(x)=a(x-ℎ{)}^{2}+k$)

- Axis of symmetry: $-\frac{b}{2a}$
- Vertex: $(-\frac{b}{2a},c-\frac{{b}^{2}}{4a})$
- Opening: if a>0, the vertex is the function’s minimum; if a<0, the vertex is the function’s maximum

Average rate of change between two points of a quadratic function is equivalent to finding the slope of the secant between those points

- The instantaneous rate of change at a point can be approximated by the average rates of change of the function over small intervals containing that point

Slope and concavity:

- Slope sign → tells you if the function is going up or down
- Concavity → tells you which way the curve bends (up = ∪, down = ∩)
- Positive rate of change→graph is increasing
- Increasing rate of change→concave up
- Decreasing rate of change→concave down

**given a data chart, it is quadratic if and only if the second differences (the differences of consecutive average rates of change) are constant**
