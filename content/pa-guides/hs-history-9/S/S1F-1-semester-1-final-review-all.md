**III-The Tang Dynasty Reunifies China**

After the collapse of the Sui dynasty, China became politically divided and unstable. There was a strong need for a powerful central government to restore order, protect borders, and manage the economy.

In 618 CE, the Tang dynasty reunified China under Emperor Gaozu and later Tang Taizong. Tang Taizong expanded the empire, strengthened the bureaucracy, and carried out land reforms, giving land to peasants.

As a result, China achieved political stability and a strong central government.
Agriculture and trade expanded, boosting the economy.The Tang dynasty laid the foundation for a golden age of culture, art, and learning.

- **Causes**
- Collapse of the Sui dynasty
- Political division and instability
- Need for strong central authority
- Desire to: Increase agricultural productivity；Reduce aristocratic power
- **Events**
- Tang dynasty founded in 618 CE
- Rulers: Emperor Gaozu；Tang Taizong
- Key actions: Expanded empire；Strengthened bureaucracy；Implemented land reforms

Built roads and canals

- **Effects**
- Political stability restored
- Strong central government
- Growth in agriculture and trade
- Expansion of tributary system
- Foundation for a cultural golden age

**IV-Checkpoint**

Tang Taizong was considered a successful ruler because he reunified China, expanded its borders, strengthened government, implemented land reforms, and promoted economic and cultural growth.

**Tang Taizong:**

- Reunified China
- Expanded borders
- Strengthened government
- Implemented land reforms
- Promoted economic and cultural growth

**V-Song Dynasty**

After the Tang dynasty collapsed, China once again became divided. A stable government was needed to support a growing population.

In 960 CE, the Song dynasty reunified much of China. They expanded the civil service examination system, allowing talented members of the gentry to serve in government.

China experienced political stability under an efficient bureaucracy. Agricultural surplus supported population growth and urbanization.

- **Causes**
- Collapse of the Tang dynasty
- Political division
- Growing population
- Goals: Improve agriculture and trade; Strengthen civil service; Limit military aristocrats
- **Events**
- Song dynasty founded in 960 CE
- Expanded civil service exams
- Gentry gained government positions
- Agricultural innovations (new rice)
- Growth of cities, trade, and culture
- **Effects**
- Stable bureaucratic government
- Population growth and urbanization
- Expansion of trade and industry
- Use of paper money
- Cultural and technological golden age

**VI-Checkpoint**

- **Song Success:**
- Strong bureaucracy
- Improved agriculture
- Thriving trade
- Cultural and technological development

The Song dynasty was successful because it strengthened government, improved agriculture, promoted trade, and supported cultural and technological development.

- **Song Prosperity Despite Northern Threats**
- Lost northern territory
- Focused on internal development
- Paid tribute and negotiated peace
- Strengthened economy and cities
- Maintained prosperity despite weak military

Although the Song lost territory to northern nomadic groups, they focused on internal development. A strong bureaucracy allowed the Song dynasty to remain prosperous despite military weakness.

**VII-China’s Ordered Society**

Confucian values emphasized hierarchy, duty, and respect for family. The civil service system created an educated governing class.

Chinese society was divided into clear social classes: the emperor, gentry, peasants, artisans, and merchants.

A stable social structure supported political stability and economic growth. Confucian values shaped law, education, and family life.

- **Causes**
- Confucian values: Hierarchy; Duty; Family respect
- Civil service examination system
- Economic stability
- **Events**
- Social hierarchy: Emperor; Gentry; Peasants; Artisans; Merchants
- Gentry gained power through education
- Family traditions: Dowries; Filial piety; Ancestor worship
- **Effects**
- Social stability
- Political and economic growth
- Confucian values shaped: Law; Education; Family life
- Limited social mobility, but education allowed advancement

**VIII-Tang and Song Cultural Achievements**

Political stability and economic prosperity allowed culture to flourish. Poetry, painting, ceramics, and calligraphy reached very high levels. Art, literature, and philosophy shaped Chinese identity for centuries.

- **Causes**
- Political stability
- Economic prosperity
- Influence of: Confucianism; Buddhism;Daoism;
- **Events**
- Poetry and literature
- Painting, ceramics, calligraphy
- Movable-type printing
- Rise of Neo-Confucianism
- **Effects**
- Culture symbolized prestige and legitimacy
- Long-lasting influence on Chinese identity
- Arts and philosophy shaped later generations

**VIIII-Themes in Tang and Song Arts**

- Nature and harmony
- Spiritual and moral values

- Political and social reflection

Art emphasized harmony between humans and nature and reflected Confucian moral ideals.

**III. Historical Background**

By the 1300s, the Yuan dynasty was in serious trouble. Corruption and high taxes caused suffering, while floods and famine increased unrest. Many Chinese people wanted Mongol rule to end.

**Fall of the Yuan Dynasty:**

- Yuan dynasty ruled by Mongols
- Corruption weakened the government
- Heavy taxes burdened peasants
- Natural disasters caused famine
- Widespread rebellions across China

**IV. The Ming Restore Chinese Rule**

The Ming rose because people wanted stability, fairness, and Chinese leadership. Zhu Yuanzhang successfully defeated the Mongols and founded the Ming dynasty in 1368.The Ming restored stability through strong leadership and Confucian bureaucracy.

- **Causes**
- Weak Yuan leadership
- Economic hardship and inequality
- Anger toward foreign (Mongol) rulers
- Desire to restore Confucian traditions
- Need for strong central authority
- **Events**
- Zhu Yuanzhang leads peasant rebellions
- Defeats Mongol forces
- 1368: Yuan dynasty overthrown
- Establishment of the Ming dynasty
- Capital established in Beijing
- **Effects**
- Return of native Chinese rule
- Strong centralized imperial authority
- Revival of Confucian government
- Restoration of civil service exams
- Agricultural and population recovery

**V. How Did the Ming Restore Government?**

The Ming ruled through education and bureaucracy rather than force alone. This helped maintain long-term stability.

- Confucianism emphasized
- Scholar-officials regain power
- Mongol influence removed
- Civil service exams strengthened

**VI. Chinese Fleets Sail the Seas**

The Ming did not explore to colonize, but to demonstrate prestige and build diplomacy.Zheng He commanded some of the largest ships the world had ever seen.These voyages made China known throughout the Indian Ocean world.

- **Causes**
- Display China’s power and wealth
- Strengthen tribute system
- Expand trade networks
- Advanced shipbuilding technology
- Support from Yongle Emperor
- **Events**
- Voyages: 1405–1433
- Fleet of hundreds of ships
- Traveled to: Southeast Asia; India; Persian Gulf; East Africa
- Carried diplomats, soldiers, gifts
- **Effects**
- Expansion of tribute system
- Increased Chinese prestige
- Growth in trade and exchange
- Exotic goods brought to China
- Cultural and diplomatic influence

**VII. Why Did Ming Exploration End?**

Later Ming rulers believed exploration brought little benefit. They chose internal stability over global expansion.

- Voyages were expensive
- Confucian officials opposed exploration
- Focus shifted to agriculture
- Threats from northern borders
- Government cut naval funding

**VIII. Restoring Chinese Culture**

Ming rulers believed cultural unity was essential for stability. They invested heavily in education and tradition.

- Confucian education promoted
- Civil service exams emphasized
- Traditional art and literature supported
- Foreign influence limited
- Chinese customs reinforced

**Qin Dynasty (221-207BC)**

- **Territory:**

Capital Xian’ Yang.

- **Founder:**

Shi Huangdi (the first emperor), original name Ying Zheng, also called Qin ShiHuang, which represented his supreme authority. By establishing Qin dynasty, he ended the Warring States period (475-221BC) and united the other six separated states in China.

- **Political System:**

Emperor ruled as autocrat and a hereditary rule (similar to monarchy in the west), centralization of authority (reduce power of regional ministers and enhance power in the central government）

“Three Councillors and Nine Ministers” (三公九卿制): The central government officials. Direct helped the emperor on different aspects of a country. (For example, in the figure below, “治粟内史” managed finance and economy in the country.)

Commandery-County System: Regional ministers were directly appointed by the emperor, and this system replaced the feudal system and laid the foundation for over two thousand years of centralized administration in China.

- **Ideology:**

Legalism, believed that humans were essentially bad and selfish, so a ruler has to enforce strict laws with harsh punishments to create order.

- **Event of** **“Burning of Books and Burying of Scholars”** **(焚书坑儒):**

Suppressing Confucianism and enhancing ideological control.

- **Unification in China:**

Under Shi Huangdi’s reign, there occurred standardized:

- Currency: Banliang coins (round coins with square holes), which replaced Daobi in the Spring and Autumn Period and the Warring States Period.
- Weights and measures
- Axle widths: The width of road. Not only promoted economy by connecting central and remote areas, but also easier for the emperor to suppress rebellions and receive the messages.
- Script: XiaoZhuan (a type of word)
- **Legal system:**

Qin Code, based on Legalism philosophy, with severe punishments for criminals.

- **Tyranny:**

Heavy taxes + Forced labor to construct national facilities

- **Artifact:**
- The Great Wall: The government called together and forced people to build the wall around the country in order to prevent from Huns, and set boarder for Qin empire.
- Terracotta Army: A large army that acted as burial objects with Qin Shihuang in his tomb.
- **Fall of Qin:**

207BC, Xiang Yu captured the capital Xian’Yang. Qin dynasty fell. Liu Bang established Han dynasty.

**Han Dynasty (West Han 202BC-8AD, East Han 25-220AD)**

Capital Chang’An.

- **Founder:**

Liu Bang (Han GaoZu)

- **Political System:**

Adapted from Qin

- **Ideology:**

Confucianism

- **Society development:**

Recovery policy (recover the stopping economy from national chaos caused by war)-encourage farming

- **Foreign Policy:**

Land Silk Road started by Zhang Qian, 138BC, connected not only the trading route between China, Middle East, and Europe, but also acted as a bridge of cultural, technology, and religion communication.

Impact of Silk Road on Rome (27BC-14AD, Augustus’ rule): Roman politicians tried to ban silk selling because the popularity of it ruined local economy (due to the significant selling reduction of local goods and products).

- **Achievements and Innovations:**

**Prosperity and Stability:**

System: Civil service (by recommendation system through regional ministers), creation of the position civil servant

Governance Strategy: Confucianism, policy of expansionism (later the “Rule of Wen and Jing”)

Economy: Monopoly on iron and salt (people can only by iron and salt from government, in order to increase tax revenue), establishment of both land and sea Silk Road, Wuzhu coin

**Visual Arts and Architecture:**

Horizontal landscape art

**Literature+ Music+ Philosophy:**

“Shi Ji”, “Han Shu”, “Yue Fu” poems

**Science + Mathematics + Technology:**

Paper making technique developed by Cai Lun, traditional Chinese medicine, Gunpowder

**Mongol Empire (Yuan Dynasty in China, 1271-1368AD)**

Capital Da Du (now Bei Jing)

- **Founder:**

First emperor Genghis Khan, set capital in Da Du (now Bei Jing).

- **Military:**
- Strong Military Force + Unique Strategy:

Mongol soldiers were nomadic army on horses, which provided them more flexible mobility + ability to perform frequent small-scale attacks.

- Tolerance + Identity Preservation Policy:

For conquered area, people only had to show their loyalty to Mongolian Empire and regularly pay taxes, so that their original culture and local laws would be preserved.

These two basic conditions laid foundation for the maintenance of the power in such a huge empire.

- **Territory:**

Huge land territory from East Asia to West Europe, controlled the whole Silk Road, lack of sea territory due to only land war ability.

- **Political:**
- No oppressive ruler, only emperor’s family members as regional ministers
- Only Mongols served in military. Only Mongols and non-chinese in government.
- Pax Mongolica- Mongol Peace
- **Society** **Hierarchy (from high to low):**

Mongols- Central Asian Nomadic + Muslim Allies + North Chinese + Han Chinese + Minority in South China

- **Famous Travelers through Silk Road:**

(1): Marco Polo- Italian Merchant

  - Left for China in 1271
  - Spent 17 years in Kublai’s service (live in the palace and serve for Kublai)

(2): Ibn Battuta- African Muslim, world traveler to China.
