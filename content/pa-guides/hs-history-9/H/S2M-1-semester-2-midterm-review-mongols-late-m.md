**Mongols**

**Who were they?**

- Group of nomadic people that came from the steppes
- Steppes: vast, dry treeless grasslands stretching across Eurasia; semi-arid and ideal for nomadic herding and horseback warfare
- Steppes are modern day Mongolia, Southern Russia including Kazakhstan, and around
- Tribes in the steppe combined and grouped together by the Genghis Khan in 1206
- Genghis Khan’s tribe: Khamag mongols –confederation of 38 clans
- Genghis Khan: Originial name is Temujin
- Genghis Khan translated to English is “Universal Ruler”

Accomplishments

- Built the largest land empire in history, stretching from East Asia to Eastern Europe (larger compared to Alexander the Great or Napoleon)
- Developed an organized decimal military system (groups of 10, 100, 1000…)
- Brought political stability to much of Eurasia, known as the Pax Mongolica: peace in Mongolia
- Advanced warfare with short bows, cavalry tactics, and siege engineering
- Laid the foundation for Mongol-ruled states like China’s **Yuan Dynasty** and Golden Horde.

**Characteristics of Mongolian society**

- Strong focus on family, loyalty, and hierarchy
- Men skilled in horse riding, archery, and warfare
- Women managed herds, homes, and often trade, money and economics
- Highly mobile, adaptable to harsh steppe climate
- Little fixed agriculture; relied on animals for food
- Strong oral tradition of stories and poetry
- Meritocratic: Positions based on strength and ability, not birth
- Not oppressive rule
- Respected scholars, artists, and artisans

**Mongolian Religion**

- Tengrism: centered on Tengri (the Eternal Blue Sky). It was worshipped with animism, shamanism, ancestor reverence, and nature harmony
- Later developed religious tolerance/interfaith (unlike European societies: Jewish people hated for not believing in Jesus)

**Military Campaigns**

- Mongol invasion of Eastern Europe: 1237–1242
- Europe at the time: Middle Ages
  - Feudal kingdoms (France, England, Holy Roman Empire)
  - Crusades still ongoing
  - Dominated by knights, castles, and the Catholic Church
  - Mongols unknowingly brought black death to Europe
- They stopped the invasion of Europe because of the death of Ögedei Khan (son of Genghis Khan)
- Genghis Khan never reached Europe — his conquests were all in Asia
- Customs forced them to go back to their capital after Khan died to reelect: Karakorum ceremonial purposes and respect for religion and Khan
- Mongols were organized and mobilized for war at all times

**Economic characteristics**

- Increased trade
- Unified the entire Silk Road under one empire
- Established the **yam (post station)** system for travel and communication.
- Issued **passports (paiza)** for merchants, which guaranteed protection across territories. Demanded goods from conquered populations and land
- Protected merchant caravans with military patrols.
- **Ortogh System**: Mongolian government funded association to support and promote caravans in trade, merchants; ensured safety and prevented banditrobbing
- Welcomed traders of all religions and ethnicities with tolerance
- Built and maintained roads, bridges, and supply depots
- Connected Europe, the Middle East, and Asia in a single safe trading zone
- Diplomatic immunity: Mongols protected and respected messengers because of the ability to build relations and connect countries
- Killing a messenger was threatening war
- A Russian prince did this and was crushed by Mongols
- Religious tolerance – For purposes of strengthening country
- SAQs – Some lands payed taxes to Mongols, but not under Mongol rule
- An example is a part of Russia. Moscow payed taxes for weaker states and combined them, eventually defeating the Golden Horde.

**Accomplishments (Politically/socially)**

- **Khubi System**: gifted lands, shares, and other spoils of war to family and loyal companions largely within a common origin.
- **Yasa code**: forbids lies, thefts, adultery…
- Ata – Malik Juvayni: every crime had specific penalty and consequences
- Reforms of Mongke Khan
- Agricultural workers had safety from taxes and excessive obligations to landowners
- Rich payed more while poor payed less
- Government welfare helped poor

**Fall of the Mongol empire**

- **Genghis Khan** (1206–1227)
- **Ögedei Khan** (1229–1241)
- **Güyük Khan** (1246–1248)
- **Möngke Khan** (1251–1259)
- After the death of Genghis Khan, his sons fought over territory
- After the death of Mongke Khan, the empire broke down in to 4 khanates
- Golden Horde (Russia and eastern Europe)
- Yuan dynasty (China and Mongolia)
- Chagatai Khanate (Central Asia)
- Ilkhanate (Persia and the Middle east)

**Chagatai Khanate**

- Lead by Chagatai Khan (son of Chinggis Khan)
- Became increasing decentralized into sub-Khanates

**Golden Horde**

- 1237-1238 CE: Mongol forces invaded Russia (which was unable to defend due to its division into many small kingdoms)
- Religious tolerance of Orthodox church
- Increased Eurasian trade
- High taxes on peasants
- Russia isolated from Western European commercial and cultural influence
- Russians defeated the Golden Horde in 1380 CE

**Ilkhanate**

- 1258 captured Baghdad, killed Abbasid caliph
- Used local lords to administer taxes, tribute, and census taking
- Tolerant of scholars and artisans
- Assimilated Middle-Eastern values
- Reinvigorated Eurasian trade routes

**Yuan Dynasty**

- Kublai Khan toppled Song in 1279
- Tried to prevent Mongols from being absorbed into Chinese civilization
- Reserved highest government jobs for Mongols and non-Chinese officials
- Religiously tolerant

**Black Death**

**Causes**

- widely believed to be a result of the plague caused by infection with the bacterium Yersinia pestis
- spread by fleas on rats, then human to human
- originated in China and Central Asia in the mid-1300s. It was transmitted to Crimea in 1347 by Mongol armies
- from Crimea, it spread to other ports in Europe through **trade ships**, and was also spread by merchants on trade routes
- germ theory and microbes did not exist yet

**Immediate Reactions**

- devout, pre-scientific people struggled to understand what was happening
- some saw it as **divine punishment**
  - flagellants would whip themselves to seek forgiveness from God
  - others prayed to God for an end to the plague, seeking divine mercy
- people were quarantined for 40 days in attempts to prevent the spread of the plague
- trade and wars were disrupted
- rise in anti-semitism as Jews were blamed; pogroms and violence against Jewish communities increased
- there was also persecution of other groups such as foreigners, pilgrims, and the poor

**Long Term Consequences**

- roughly one third of Europe's population died
- due to high mortality, there was a shortage of workers, so workers demanded better pay and better conditions
- fewer workers meant less agricultural land was cultivated
- there was a loss of faith as people blamed God for the plague
- generally weakened Feudalism and "brought a new fluidity to the thirteenth century"
- there was a breakdown of social structure, and lord/vassal relationships ended
- art focused on mortality and afterlife

**Renaissance**

**Some Earlier Renaissances**

- **Carolingian Renaissance**: 8th to 9th centuries
  - monastic learning, much circulation of Latin literature (both old and new)
- **Macedonian Renaissance**:
  - 10th century, mostly artistic developments
  - more "realistic" painting, early use of simple perspective
- **12th century Renaissance**
  - Thomas Aquinas, Hildegard von Bingen, numerous other scholastic philosophers and theologians

**Contributing Factors**

- Italian city-states increasing trade
- decline of Catholic church political power, rise of city-states and national monarchies
- increase in trade, economic growth, knowledge of world, banking
- **cultural diffusion** (across Europe and the world)
- more social mobility and economic opportunities

**Economic causes**

- wealth from crusades and Mediterranean trade routes
- Hanseatic League (mostly northern Europe)
- economic opportunities after Black Death
- growth of banking

**Italian City-States Trade Dominance**

- breakup of Mongol Empire(s) led to decline of Silk Roads
- rise of Ottoman empire led to change in trade patterns (possibly the decline in some routes)

**Why** **the** **Renaissance began in Italy**

- banking and international trade led to great wealth
- higher levels of urbanization and wealth
- it was the previous center of classical civilization (Roman empire)
- incredibly wealthy benefactors (Medici family) were able to fund artists to justify their wealth and for their family's prestige
- early example of **philanthropy** (ultra rich using their wealth to create art, resources, and fairs for the general public)

**Renaissance Culture**

- deep roots in medieval traditions and cultural development
- not a rejection of religion, not secularism
  - religion deeply present in much Renaissance art and culture in general
- not modern or contemporary individualism
  - community life still critically important

**Humanism**

- in Antiquity, art focused on mythical heroes and kings (Hercules, Achilles, Alexander the Great, etc.)
- in the Middle Ages, art tended to focus on the explicitly religious: God, Jesus, angels, demons, saints, etc.
- **Humanism** is the belief in the value of human life, humanistic values, and experiences, and that it evolved so that even a regular human life is worthy of art and reflection
- **Petrarch**-Florentine scholar
- Wrote of “Three Ages”: Classical, Dark, Golden
- Encouraged Europeans to read classical literature
- in the Middle Ages, the body was seen as a sinful vessel; the body was worthy of analysis in Humanism
- the study of the humanities was influenced by ancient Greece and Rome, which encouraged reading the "classics"
- emphasized human nature
- the founders of Humanism were Christian; the movement was not anti-Christian

**Ideas in Humanism**

- The study of humanities and classical culture
- Individual improvement
- Action rather than passivity
- The use of reason to improve worldly conditions
- Observe lessons of nature
- Fascination with physical form

**Political Aspect**

- Debated characteristics of ideal rulers and ideal forms of government\

**Artistic Aspects**

- Religious symbolism
- Realism and human emphasis in religion
- Linear perspective
- Heavy classical influence

**Spread of the Renaissance**

- Spread by merchants, scholars, bakers, and the elite
- Gutenberg’s printing press contributed
- Growing trade due to wealthy patrons
