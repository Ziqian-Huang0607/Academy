**Islam**

**Doctrine**

- **Monotheistic**; only worships one God, **Allah**
- **Muhammad** is his **Prophet**
- **Major Texts**
  - **The Quran**:
    - the holy Scriptures of Islam
    - “recitation”, also spelled “Koran”
    - believed to originate from Allah’s revelations to Muhammad, later dictated to scribes
  - **The Tafsir**: Exegesis (explanation/commentary) of the Quran
  - **The Hadith**: a collection of sayings and practices of Muhammad, the Prophet
  - **The Sira**: a biography of Muhammad
- **Five Pillars of Islam**
  - **Confession of Faith**: uncompromised monotheistic belief in Allah as the only god and Muhammad as his prophet
  - **Pray**: standard prayers facing Mecca five times a day and public prayers on Fridays at midday
  - **Fast**: to fast from dawn till sunset during the holy month of **Ramadan**, which commemorates Muhammad’s first revelation
  - **Charity**: to give alms, or zakat, to the poor and unfortunate, to demonstrate social responsibility
  - **Pilgrimage (hajj)**: if possible, to make a pilgrimage to Mecca to worship at the Kaaba at least once in a lifetime
- **Jihad**: “striving in the way of the Lord”
  - Jihad of the **tongue/pen**, **heart**, and **sword**

**The** **Founding of Islam**

**Pre-Islamic Arabia**

- Converted into a **Roman** Province (106 B.C.E.)
- The **Bedouin Arabs**
  - A **Semitic**-speaking people of Southwestern Asia
  - Tribal society
    - Each tribe was ruled by the **sheikh**, selected by a council of elders, the **majlis**
  - participated in the caravan trade and actively initiated trade between the **Persian Gulf** and the **Mediterranean Sea**
- **Pre-Islamic Religion**
  - **Polytheistic**, with a supreme deity, **Allah**
  - A communal faith
  - **Animistic** beliefs: spirits embodied natural entities
  - Allah was symbolized by a sacred stone
  - By the time of **Muhammad**, the **Kaaba**, located in **Mecca**, was held in special religious reverence
- The **economic significance** of Arabia increases (5th & 6th century C.E.), benefiting from the instability in Mesopotamia, etc.
- Consequence of the intensified trade, relations between the desert **Bedouins** and the wealthy **merchants** in town become **strained**

**Muhammad (570-632 C.E.)**

- **Early Life & Revelations** **(570-610 C.E.)**
  - Born into the **Quraysh** tribe; orphaned at an early age
  - Became a **merchant** in **Mecca**; married a wealthy widow, Khadijah
  - Was troubled by the widening gap between Bedouin values and the corruption of commercial elites
  - Allegedly received his **first revelation** from God (**610 C.E.**)
- **Founder of Islam** **(610-622 C.E.)**
  - Began to **preach Islam**; gained followers in Mecca
  - Kicked out by the leaders of Mecca **(622 C.E.)**
- **The Hijra/Hegira (622-632** **C.E.)**
  - Fled with family and followers to **Medina**
  - Formed the first Muslim community—the **umma**
  - Gathering an army, he returned to and **conquered** Mecca
  - Destroyed the **idols** of the traditional faith
  - Led prayer at the **Kaaba**, converting the city to Islam.

**The Sunni-Shi’a Split (632-680 C.E.)**

- Muhammad dies without naming a successor (632 C.E.)
- The **Caliph**-**successor**; **political** leader of the umma; an **imam**, or **religious** leader
  - **Ali**:
    - Muhammad’s cousin & son-in-law
    - Deemed too young
  - **Abu Bakr**
    - Muhammad’s father-in-law & close friend
    - Understood the politics of the region & the tribes
- **Abu Bakr** named Caliph (632-634 C.E.)
  - Loosely controlled warriors
  - **United** all Bedouin **Arab tribes**
  - Commenced **expansion** of Islam
- The **Rashidan** (or Rightly Guided Caliphs) (632-661 C.E.)
  - **Abu Bakr**, **Umar**, **Uthman**, **Ali**
- 3rd Caliph Uthman murdered**;** Ali’s supporters named him Caliph (656 C.E.)
- War erupts between Uthman’s & Ali’s supporters (657 C.E.)
- The **Umayyads** (an opposing family to Ali) declare their own Caliph in Jerusalem (660 C.E.)
- **Ali** is **assassinated** (661 C.E.); his sons are later killed in battle (680 C.E.)
- **The Sunni faith**
  - Muhammad cannot be **succeeded as prophet**
  - Abu Bakr was the best choice to be Caliph
  - Caliphs are to be chosen from the **umma**
  - **85%** of contemporary Muslims belong to the Sunni branch
- **The Shi’a faith**
  - **Ali** was the legitimate **Caliph**
  - He was also selected as Muhammad’s **spiritual** successor
  - Caliphs should be kept in Muhammad’s **family**
- Differences in their beliefs and laws developed, leading to major disputes

**The Expansion of Islam**

**Early Campaigns**

- **Conquest of the Sasanian Empire**
  - The last pre-Islamic **heir** to the **Persian Empire**
  - The campaigns saw rapid Muslim victories
  - The capital of the Sasanian Empire was taken
  - The last ruler was assassinated (**651 C.E.**)
- **Conflicts with the Byzantine Empire**
  - The Byzantine Empire was weakened by **overexpansion**
  - The Muslims were **supreme** in military power, especially in the **Mediterranean**
  - Some Byzantine **Christians** also supported Muslim advances, due to their **lower taxes**
  - Arab armies forced Byzantine authority to withdraw from **Egypt** and **North Africa (642 C.E.)**

**The Umayyad Caliphate (661-750 C.E.)**

- Moved the capital from Medina to **Damascus**
- The first Umayyad Caliph was **Muawiya**
- Conquered areas through militaristic **expansion**
- **Administration**
  - Huge **bureaucracy**
  - Treatment of **Dhimmi**, or non-Muslims
    - required the payment of **jizya**, a tax
    - tolerance
    - **“People of the Book”** (Jews & Christians) were treated better
  - Treatment of Muslim Arabs
    - **first-class citizens**
    - paid lower taxes
    - could join the imperial administration & army
    - had a share of riches from conquest
  - Converts to Islam (sing. **Mawal**) were seen as **inferior** to born Muslims
- **Decline**
  - **Decentralized** bureaucracy; the structure became almost **tribal**
  - **Religious tension** between the Sunni & Shi’a
  - Non-Arab **converts** to Islam sought more **rights**

**The Golden Age of Islam**

**The Abbasid Caliphate** **(750-1258 C.E.)**

- **The Abbasid Revolution**
  - The luxurious lifestyles of the Umayyad Caliphs caused discontentment and unrest.
  - Frontier warriors of the **Abbasid** party allied with the **Shi’a** and **non-Arab** Muslims to revolt (747 C.E.)
  - The Abbasids won the **Battle of the Zab**; they quickly conquered the rest of the Empire (750 C.E.)
  - **Abu al Abbas** became Caliph.
  - The **Umayyads** were **massacred** at a “reconciliation dinner”; a survivor, **Abu al-Rahman I**, fled to the Iberian Peninsula to found the **Cordoba Caliphate.**
- **The Golden Age of Islam**
  - **Baghdad** became the center of world **trade** and **education.**
  - The Caliphate faced **political decline** and **social turmoil.**
  - **Cultural vibrance** persisted, and by the 12th century, schools, libraries, and institutes flourished in **Cairo** and **Baghdad.**
- **International Trade**
  - Built a vast **trading network**, covering products from the **Silk Roads.**
  - **Technology**, **knowledge**, and **culture** **exchanges** occurred (e.g. the Indo-Arabic numerals were a result of this).
  - Bank & **economic structures** were **complexified**: the concepts of partnership and credit were introduced.
- **Islamic Literature & Art**
  - **Persian**: considered the language of “high culture”.
    - **Rubaiyat** by Omar Khayyam: a collection of poems.
    - **Shahnameh** (Book of Kings) by Ferdowsi: an epic poem depicting myths of the Persian Empire.
  - **Arabic**: the language of **religion**, **law**, and **natural sciences.**
  - **Calligraphy** & **illustrated poetry** featured themes such as desert journeys, chivalry, romance, etc.
  - **Architecture** advancements manifested in the construction of minarets and mosques.
- **Agriculture**
  - The caliphate covered a wide **variety** of land, and, hence, climates.
  - Massive and advanced **irrigation projects** were carried out to accommodate the needs of the new variety of crops.
  - Hydropower and windmills eased work and increased production.
- **Learning**
  - Scholars **translated** **Greco-Roman** and **Indian** books into Arabic, preserving and building upon knowledge in a variety of fields.
  - Advanced in algebra, geometry, medicine, anatomy, and ethics.
  - **Medicine**
    - Had hospitals and the best healthcare of the time.
    - Passing a test was a required prerequisite to becoming a physician/pharmacist.
    - The eye surgery that removes **cataracts** was developed.
- **Roles of Women**
  - Became **increasingly subjugated** to men.
  - Eventually required to wear veils and cover their bodies fully.
  - Lived increasingly **secluded lives.**
  - The mothers of Caliphs were the most prominent.
  - Wealthy women were restricted to the home & were often married off early.
  - Lower-class women worked.
  - The number of slaves & **concubines** increased with wealth.
    - Concubines were often better off than wives, being usually educated and able to participate in public life.

**The Cordoba Caliphate (756-1031 C.E.)**

- Founded by **Abu al-Rahman**, a **Umayyad** survivor who fled to the **Iberian Peninsula.**
- Located in **Al-Andalus.**
- The Muslim **Moors**, comprising Berbers and North African Muslims, expanded into the Iberian Peninsula and Europe (711 C.E.)
  - Their conquest was halted by Christian kings at the **Battle of Tours** in France (732 C.E.)
- Scholars in the Cordoba Caliphate preserved **Greco-Roman** knowledge.
- Fostered by policies of **tolerance**, a unique **Spanish-Arabic** style emerged in art, vocabulary, and architecture.
- **Cordoba** became another major ancient center of **learning.**

**African Civilizations**

**Axum and Kush**

**Kush**

- Emerged in South of Egypt in **Nubia** by the end of the second millennium B.C.E.
- Established a new capital at **Meroe.**
- Meroe became a trading hub for **iron** goods.

**Axum**

- Located in **Ethiopia.**
- Came to power in the **third century C.E.**
- **Trade**
  - Located in 2 main cities: **Adulis** & **Axum.**
  - Goods & slaves were traded through **ports** on the Red Sea.
  - Exported ivory, hides, and gold.
  - Competed for control of the **ivory trade** with Meroe.
- **Religion in Early Axum**
  - **Monotheistic** with Animist beliefs.
  - Believed that kings were descended from God.
  - Honored dead ancestors.
- **Conversion to Christianity**
  - Christianity was first introduced through trade in the 300s C.E.
  - **King Ezana** made Christianity the **official religion.**
  - Conversion to Christianity initially **strengthened ties** in **trade.**
  - Axum remained Christian **after Islam** was introduced to Africa in the 600s C.E.
  - This eventually led to conflict in the twelfth century C.E.
- **King Ezana** **(325-360 C.E.)**
  - Conquered Meroe, creating an **Axum empire.**
  - Converted Axum to **Christianity.**
- **Axum culture**
  - Blended the traditions of **Arabs** and **Kushites.**
  - The Adulis population comprised Egyptians, Arabs, Greeks, Romans, Persians, and Indians.
  - **Greek** was the international language.
- **Axumite innovations**
  - Developed a **writing system**: “Ge’ez”.
  - Became the only ancient African kingdom to have a written language besides Egypt and Meroe.
  - **Minted coins** in bronze, silver, and gold; became the first sub-Saharan state to do so.
  - Developed terrace farming.
  - Employed canals and dams for irrigation.
- **Pillars of Axum**
  - Constructed **tall pillars** made from solid granite as monuments/tomb markers.
  - Recorded achievements on them.
  - **Stelae**: **exploits** recorded on stone pillars.

**East Africa**

**Coastal trade centers**

- **Indian Ocean Trade**
  - Preferred due to **lower transportation costs.**
  - Carried more **bulk** & **staple** goods, compared to the Silk Road, which carried more luxury items.
  - African exports: **raw materials**, such as furs, ivory, gold, salt, and timber.
  - African imports: Indian, Islamic, and Chinese **luxuries.**
  - **Monsoons**, or alternating wind currents, aided transportation.
  - Trade occurred between **decentralized**, individual merchant towns.
- **Coastal trade centers develop**
  - City-states formed by gatherings initiated by trade.
  - Kilwa, Sofala, and Mogadishu were major centers.
- **Swahili culture** **(12th** **& 13th** **centuries C.E.)**
  - International trade led to cultural diffusion and **integration.**
  - **Swahili** (Arabic for “of the coast”) culture: a **cosmopolitan** culture that involves a mixture of **African** and **Arabic** cultures.
  - Swahili language: a blend of **Bantu** and **Arabic** languages; originated as a **Pidgin** for trade, then evolved into a **Creole** and mature language.

**Great Zimbabwe** **(900s-1500s C.E.)**

- An **inland capital** of **trade.**
- “Zimbabwe” in Bantu means “stone houses”.
- The city consisted of **stone edifices**, constructed without mortar.
- Built and occupied by the **Bantu** peoples.
- Reached its height of prosperity (around 1300 C.E.)
- **Artifact evidence indicates:**
  - **International trade.**
  - A likely **monarchical** government.
- **Decline** **(1500s C.E.)**
  - **Overpopulation**, **Civil war**, and **Dwindling trade.**
  - Depleted resources, and, thus, a shift of **gold** production and trade further into the **interior.**

**West Africa**

**Ghana** **(300s-1235 C.E.)**

- First **great trading state** in West Africa.
- Formed by the **Soninke** people in 300s C.E..
- Located in the **upper Niger River valley**; comprised grasslands.
- **Lifestyle**
  - Skilled **iron-makers.**
  - Had an **agricultural** economy of separate villages under **local rulers.**
- **Trade patterns**
  - Traded across the **Sahara** via **Berbers** on camel caravans.
  - **Camels** “fleets of the desert”.
  - **Exported**: **gold**, iron, ivory, ostrich feathers, hides, and slaves.
  - **Imported**: **salt**, textiles, horses.
- **Politics**
  - Ghana kings
    - Acted as **war leaders.**
    - Negotiated **trade** with foreigners.
    - Governed without written laws.
  - Powerful and large armies
    - The Ghanaians were **expansionists** who conquered more territory.
- Reached **peak** of its power (1050 C.E.)
- **Tunka Manin**, Ghana’s most powerful ruler, commanded a 200,000 men army (1067 C.E.)
- Began to decline in 1000s C.E.
- **Berbers** invaded and destroyed the city of **Kumi Saleh** (1076 C.E.)
- Soninke lost control of the salt trade to neighboring tribes.
- The **Malinke** people overthrew Ghana and established the Empire of **Mali** **(1235 C.E.)**

**Mali**

- **Sundiata Keitu**
  - **Founded** the Empire of Mali.
  - Defeated the **Ghanaians** (1240 C.E.)
  - United the Malians under a **strong government.**
  - Regarded as the father of his nation.
- Territory included part of the **Atlantic coast** and ran east to the city of **Timbuktu.**
- Built its wealth and power on the **gold-salt trade.**
- Most of its citizens were farmers who were ruled by local authorities that answered to the Malian king.
- Reached the peak of its power under **Mansa Musa** (1312-1337 C.E.)
  - **Doubled** the size of Mali.
  - Had a strong, centralized government.
  - Governed the kingdom by dividing it into provinces.
  - Was a **devout Muslim**
    - Traversed to Mecca on a famous **pilgrimage.**
    - Was lavishly generous with gold, so much so that he caused inflation.
    - Demonstrated his great power and wealth as well as his kingdom’s.
    - Absorbed new ideas about Islamic learning & architecture.
  - **Timbuktu** became the center of Islamic learning & culture.
- Divided by **civil war** (1349 C.E.)
- Supplanted by **Songhai** rule within 100 years.

**Ibn Battuta**

- **Moroccan**-born **traveler** & explorer.
- Traveled through the **Islamic world,** from North Africa to China (1325-1354 C.E.)
- His writings were valuable **primary sources** about the Islamic world in Africa.

**Songhai**

- Songhai culture developed **east of Timbuktu**, along the **Niger River.**
- **King Kossi** establishes the **Dia dynasty** (1009 C.E.)
  - Converts to **Islam**
  - The kingdom benefited from **Muslim trade routes**
- **Sunni Ali** founds another dynasty, the **Sunni** (1464 C.E.)
  - The dynasty expanded to include **Timbuktu & Djenne.**
  - Established control over the **trading empire.**
  - Maintained the **existing** trade patterns.
- **Askia Muhammad** (also called **Muhammad Ture**) overthrew Sunni Ali’s son (1493 C.E.)
  - Continued policy of **expansion**, maintaining power through a navy and cavalry.
  - Established a **complex government** centered in **Gao.**
  - Home to many great centers of **learning.**

**Influence of Islam in West Africa**

- Provided **universal faith**, **sense of community**, and a **powerful political/legal system.**
- **Royal Cult**
  - Defined to be the religious/quasi-religious veneration of a monarch.
  - Rulers **reinforced authority** through Muslim ideology.
  - Monarchs established themselves as **spiritual** and **political** leaders.
- Many Africans exposed to Islam also retain their **indigenous** religion.
- Myriad **Sudanic matrilineal** societies were **hesitant** to convert to Islam, as it **restricted women** more than these societies did.
- **Intraregional Trade**
  - The **slave trade**, which had existed since Classical times, was **globalized** by Islam.
  - Slavery & the slave trade grew in scale and **prominence**, with **7 million** slaves traded.
- The majority of Africa **remained isolated** from larger networks even after the introduction of Islam.

**Middle Ages**

**Definitions and Periods**

- **Classical Civilization** (**Greek** and **Roman**)
- **Middle Ages** (**Fall of Rome** to the **Renaissance**)
  - **Early Middle Ages** (500–1000 C.E.)
  - **High Middle Ages** (1000-1350 C.E.)
  - **Late Middle Ages** (1350-1500~ C.E.)
- **Modern Times** (**Scientific Revolution** to today)

**"The Dark Ages"**

- **Fall of Rome** to the **Renaissance**
- Implies a time of **oppression**, lack of **culture**, and lack of **creativity**.

**The Fall of Rome**

- A time of **Germanic** tribes and people groups: **Germani**, **Alemanni** (who styled themselves "**Theut**"), **Franks**, and **Vandals**.
- **Goths** were considered less "problematic" because they were more **Romanized**.
- Resulted in the **disruption of trade**, a **shift in education**, a **language shift**, and a **cultural shift** toward **Germanic** traditions.

**Germanic Culture and Society**

- **Clans**: Consisted of up to 50 related households.
- **Feud**: **Blood vengeance** taken on rival clans; **Germanic law** was designed to stop blood feuds.
- **Wergild (Compensation)**: Money paid to end a feud; the amount was dependent on the **importance of the person**.
- **Raiding**: Acted as an annual income supplement in some cases.
- **Chief**: A great military leader elected by clan leaders.

**Trial by Ordeal**

- Methods included **hot iron**, **hot water**, **cold water**, and **judicial combat**.

**Frankish Kingdoms**

**Clovis (481–511** **C.E.)**

- **Frankish King** of the **Merovingian Dynasty**.
- A shrewd strategist and warrior.
- Established the era of the **"Long-Haired Kings."**
- Converted to **Christianity**.
- Conquered **Alaric II** and the **Visigoths**.
- Intermarried with the **Gallo-Romans**.
- Allied with the **Christian Clergy**.
- Established the capital at **Paris**.

**The Merovingians (457–751** **C.E.)**

- Known as the **"Long-Haired Kings"** and **Warriors**.
- Characterized by **plundered land**, **fratricide**, and frequent **civil war**.
- **"Mayors of the Palace"** gained authority while kings lost it.
- **Charles "The Hammer" Martel** (718–741 C.E.):
  - Served as the **Mayor of the Palace**.
  - Champion of **Christianity** and a French hero.
  - Led the **Battle of Tours**; while sometimes overblown as the sole cause for the end of Muslim expansion in Europe, it marks a significant endpoint.
  - Extended **Frankish control** to the south.

**Pepin the Short (751–768** **C.E.)**

- Overthrew the **Merovingian** line.
- Crowned by the **Pope** as the **"Protector."**
- Defeated the **Lombards** who were threatening Rome—this led to the **Donation of Pepin**.
- Crystallized the **alliance** between the **Pope (Stephen III)**, the **Mayor**, and the **Missionaries**.
- Established the **Carolingian Dynasty**.

**The Carolingian Dynasty (751–987** **C.E.)**

- **Charlemagne (742–814** **C.E.)**:
  - Son of Pepin and the most powerful **Carolingian**.
  - Founder of **"Europe"** by merging **Christian**, **Roman**, and **German** elements to politically unite West and Central Europe.
  - First **Emperor** in 325 years (crowned in the year 800).
  - Led a **revival** and **renaissance** in **education**, **culture**, and **unification**.
- **Administration**:
  - Divided into 300 counties.
  - **Count**: The King's local representative in law and finance.
  - **Duke**: The King's local military official.
  - **Baron**: Lesser lords of smaller estates.
  - **Office**: A conferred position of authority to act for the **public good**.

**Monasticism**

**A Way of Life**

- Involved renouncing **worldly pursuits** and taking vows of **poverty**, **chastity**, and **obedience**.
- Followers lived by a **Rule**—a life dedicated to God.
- Despite leaders like Charlemagne, **monks** were the primary **missionaries** from 500 to 1100 Common Era.

**Monasteries**

- **Monasteries** appeared as a response to the perceived **worldliness** of the church.
- Commissioned for purposes of **education** and the **well-being** of the general population.
- **Functions**:
  - **Religious studies**, **schooling**, local **economies**, providing **lodging and food**, and helping the poor.
- **Monks preserved knowledge**:
  - They preserved and added to **Classical texts** and the writings of the **Church Fathers**.
- **Scriptorium (Libraries)**: Places of vast knowledge where texts were copied (for example, it took two scribes one year to copy one Bible).
- Provided **standardized education**.

**The Liberal Arts**

- Considered essential for a **free person**.
- Goal: To produce **virtuous**, **knowledgeable**, and **articulate** people able to participate in **civic and religious life**.
- Divided into the **Trivium** and the **Quadrivium**.
- **The Quadrivium**:
  - **Arithmetic**: For trade and industry.
  - **Astronomy**: For navigation and seasons.
  - **Geometry**: For logic and construction.
  - **Music**.
- Provided **specialized skills** and **higher education**.
- Students learned **theology**, **philosophy**, and **medicine**
- Earned degrees such as **Baccalaureates**, **Masters of Arts**, and **Doctors**.

**The Church in Medieval Life**

**The Church as the Center of Life**

- Involved in everything from **birth (Baptism)** to **death (Last Rites)**.
- **Local Saints**:
  - **Patrons** to be honored and prayed to; they gave specific areas prestige.
- **Parish Priests** managed local spiritual life.
- **The Mass**:
  - The center of life, viewed as necessary for **salvation** and the stability of society.

**Carolingian Decline**

**The Decline of the Empire**

- No lasting empire was maintained **post-mortem** (after death).
- **Partible Inheritance**:
  - **Louis the Pious**, son of Charlemagne, divided his kingdom among his three sons: **Louis the German**, **Lothar**, and **Charles the Bald**.
- **Competition**:
  - Multiple German and Italian kings began calling themselves **"Holy Roman Emperors."**
  - **Local lords** held the majority of power in Germany, and **city-states** re-emerged in Italy.
- **Bureaucracy** was not yet strong enough to maintain unity.
- Resulted in strong **cultural unity** through the church, but a **politically divided** landscape.

**Carolingian Successors**

- **West Francia** was ruled by the **Capetian Dynasty** (**987 to 1328** **C.E.)**
- They slowly **consolidated royal power** and built a central **administration**.
- **East Francia** was ruled by the **Ottonian**, **Salian**, and **Hohenstaufen** families as **Holy Roman Emperors**.

**The Vikings** **(~790-1066 C.E.)**

**Basic Knowledge**

- **Viking** was a verb and a job description.
  - They were **explorers**, **traders**, and **warriors**.
- By the end of the era, many settled in other regions.
- Left significant **linguistic**, **cultural**, and **social impacts**.

**Kievan Rus** **& Magyars**

**Kievan Rus**

- A mix of mostly **Slavs** and **Vikings**.
- **Swedish and Danish Kings** were invited to become leaders—**Rurik** is the most famous.
- Created **trading settlements**, including **Kiev** and **Novgorod**.
- Modelled their society based on the **Byzantines**.
- **Aristocrats (Boyars)** held less power than in other regions.
- **Peasants** were mostly **free farmers**.
- Known for **ornate churches**, highly religious **literature** and **art**, and a strong **monastic movement**.

**Magyars**

- **Semi-nomadic Hunnic peoples** who periodically raided and invaded Southeastern and Central Europe in the 9th and early 10th centuries.
- The **Ottonian Kings** of the East Franks slowly halted these invasions.

**The Great Schism**

- The **permanent split** between the **Orthodox Church** and the **Latin Church**.
- Resulted from **papal claims of supremacy**, accusations of **idolatry**, and the **Filioque Controversy**.
- **Filioque Controversy**: A dispute over whether the Holy Spirit proceeds from the **Father and the Son (Latin)** or from the **Father alone (Greek)**.

**Feudalism and Manorialism**

- **Feudalism**: A system of **political and military obligations** that bound **Lords** and their **Retainers**.
- **Manorialism**: A system of **economic and social relationships** between **Landlords** and their **Peasant laborers**.

**Nobility**

- A **hereditary ruling class**.
- The **first-born male** typically inherits the estate (**primogeniture**).
- Other children often became members of the **Clergy**, **political leaders**, or **Knights**.
- Noblemen were given a **fief (estate)** by the King
- Expected to pay **taxes** and provide **soldiers**.
- On a **fief**, the **Lord's word is law**.

**Knights**

- **Mounted soldiers**
- Usually owed 40 days of military service per year.
- Responsibilities included **guard duty**, attending **court**
- Given **land** and **prestige** in return.
- **The Code of Chivalry**:
  - **Prowess**: Excellence with the sword and horse; never laying down arms.
  - **Courage**: Being brave (but not foolhardy) and protecting the weak.
  - **Generosity**: Practicing hospitality and showing mercy to those who ask.
  - **Loyalty**: To the Lord, the King, and the Church.
- Reality was not always **chivalrous**:
  - When bored, some would **raid villages**, **pillage**, and commit acts of violence.
  - The Church and some Kings looked to **reduce knights' violence** through the **Crusades**, the **Code of Chivalry**, and **tournaments**.

**Peasantry**

- **Freemen / Freeholders**:
  - Owned their own land
  - Contributed **labor**, **crops**, and **taxes** to the Lord.
- **Serfs / Villeins**:
  - **Landless peasants** who were tied to the Lord's land.
  - Lived a simple but **protected life**.
  - Could not be **bought or sold** (they were not slaves).
  - Could **pass on property** and rights through inheritance.
  - Were generally left alone if they **met their obligations**.

Pre-Capitalist Society

- The system was underpinned by **generational and personal loyalty**, **services** **for protection**, **religious devotion**, **honor**, and **tradition**.
