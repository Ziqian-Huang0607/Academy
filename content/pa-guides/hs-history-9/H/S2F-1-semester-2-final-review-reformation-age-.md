**15 Europe Transformed: Reform and State-Building**

**The Reformation of the Sixteenth Century**

- Protestant Reformation: a religious reform movement that divided the western church into Catholic and Protestant groups
  - Begin with Martin Luther

**Background to the Reformation**

- 15th century late: attempt to reestablish the centralized power of monarchical governments
  - Known as ‘Renaissance states’ / ‘new monarchies’ -> France, England, Spain
- New Renaissance monarchs: crafty men obsessed with acquisition and expansion
  - Concentration of royal authority
  - Attempts to suppress nobility
  - Efforts to control the church are in their hands
  - Obtain a new source of revenue -> increase royal power & military
- Niccolò Machiavelli -> The Prince (1513)
  - Middle age: many political theorists stressed the ethical side of the prince
    - Based on the Christian moral principles
  - Machiavelli: abandon morality as the basis for the analysis of political activity
    - Emphasize reaching the results regardless of the methods employed
- Trade increased after previous economic recessions in the 14th century
  - Society was separated into 3 estates (Mainly in the Middle Ages, some in now)
- Clergy -> First Estate
- Nobility -> Second Estate
- Peasants and inhabitants -> Third estate
- Nobles (2~3% of population) -> declining real incomes
  - Some old Nobles survived, but new ones also came
  - Still dominated society and held important political posts and was an advisor
- Peasants (85~90% of Third Estate), except in the urban areas of Flanders and northern Italy
  - Serfdom decreased as labor dues owned soon became rent paid in money
  - 1500s: More peasants became legally free
    - They also grew to resent their social superiors
    - Fought for a greater share of the benefits coming from labor
    - This led to a peasant revolution in Germany in the 1500s
- Inhabitants (10~15% of the Third Estate):
  - Originally, only artisans and merchants
  - 1500s-> Top of the urban society: Patricians
    - Wealth from capitalistic enterprises in trade, industry, and banks
    - Dominate urban community economically, socially, and politically
  - > Second level: Petty burghers
    - Shopkeepers, artisans, guild masters, guildsmen
    - Concerned with providing goods and services for locals
  - > Third level: propertyless workers (30~40% of urban inhabitants)
    - Pitiful wages/ unemployed -> miserable lives
    - Led them to radical religious reforms by the 1500s
- Printing development
  - 1100s: printing on hand-carved wooden blocks
  - 1400s: multiple printing with movable metal type
    - A gradual process that culminated sometime between 1445 and 1450
- Johannes Gutenberg of Mainz played an important role
  - Gutenberg’s Bible -> first true book under movable type
  - 1500s: a thousand printers in Europe (8~10 million copies)
- Encouraged scholarly research & lay reading in public
  - New religious ideas of the Reformation spread
  - Allowed European cultures to compete with Eastern civilizations
- Christian Humanism / Northern Renaissance Humanism since 1550s+
  - Believed in the ability of humans to reason & improve through classical texts
  - Whilst still having an inner piety towards Christianity
  - Believed that to change society, they must first change humans
  - Begin with Desiderius Erasmus:
    - Formulated and popularized Christian humanism
    - Named it ‘philosophy of Christ.’
      - Christianity should be a guiding philosophy for daily life instead of the medieval practices
      - Laid the road for the Lutheran Reformation
- Corruption leads to reform
  - 1450~1520: Renaissance popes failed to meet the church’s needs
  - Supposed to be spiritual leaders, yet involved themselves in worldly concerns
    - Julius II led armies against enemies -> denounced by pious Christians
  - Many high church officials regard their office as an opportunity to gain wealth
    - Others are ignorant of their spiritual duties
- Ordinary ppl was clamoring for meaningful religious expression & salvation
  - Some salvation processes became almost mechanical
  - Many people used relics (bones/ other objects associated with saints)
    - Frederick the Wise amassed nearly 19000 relics
    - People earn the right to buy indulgences by visiting relics

**Martin Luther and the Reformation in Germany**

- Martin Luther: Monk and professor at the University of Wittenberg
  - Between 1513~1516, his study of the Bible led him to question salvation
- Catholic emphasized 1. Faith 2. Good works
  - Luther: humans could never do enough good works to merit salvation
    - Humans are not saved through God’s works but through faith in God
    - *Indulgences are considered as good work merit in the Catholic POV*
    - This is the primary doctrine of the Protestant Reformation
    - His doctrine arrived from the Bible -> Bible = religious truth
- Luther did not consider himself a rebel
  - But was upset by indulgence sales (e.g., Monk Johann Tetze)
  - 1517: issued the 95 theses
    - Thousands of copies quickly spread throughout Germany
  - 1521 Jan: excommunicated by the church
  - Summoned to appear before the imperial diet of Emperor Charles V(HRE)
    - Members were outraged and demanded that he be arrested
      - But Elector Frederick of Saxony defended him
- 1524: Peasants’ War erupted
  - A religious revolt that peasants looked to Luther for support
    - However, Luther is conservative on economic and social issues
    - 1525: Luther supported a German prince to suppress the peasants
      - He believed in maintaining peace and order for God
- Luther then became dependent on state authorities for the growth of the reformed church
  - Gained support from 300ish German states in the HRE
    - The ruler took control of local churches
  - The Lutheran church then became a territorial/ state church
    - Luther instituted a new religious service to replace the Catholics
    - Focused on Bible reading, preaching the word of God, and singing hymns
- The reformation is closely tied to political activity
  - Charles V ruled a vast empire -> hope to tie the empire through the Catholic Church
    - However, the HRE consists of many independent states
    - 1555-> Peace of Augsburg: Charles needed to negotiate a truce
      - Division of Christianity
      - The Lutheran and Catholic Churches have the same legal rights
      - states are free to choose between the two
      - *An individual inside the state must follow the state*
      - Thus, Christian unity was lost

**The Spread of the Protestant Reformation:**

- Switzerland Reformation: 1) Zwinglianism 2)Calvinism
  - Zwingli-> became a priest-> led reform to destroy the Catholic practice
    - Relics & images destroyed, paintings & decorations removed
    - Monasticism, pilgrimages, papal authority, etc., were all abolished
  - He wanted alliances with Luther (failed)
    - Yet is unable to agree on the interpretation of the Lord’s Supper:
      - Zwingli -> body/blood (figuratively)
      - Luther -> Body/ Figure (Literally)
  - War between Swiss Protestants & Catholics -> death of Zwingli
    - Led to the rise of Calvinism
  - John Calvin -> from France, but fled to Switzerland to escape the Catholics
    - 1536: Institutes of the Christian Religion
      - Secured his position as the leader of Protestantism
  - Calvin aligns with Luther on faith and has achieved salvation
    - But also believe in the emphasis on the absolute sovereignty of God
    - Predestination-> God predestined some ppl to be saved (the elect) and others to be damned (reprobate)
  - 1536: begin to work on the reformation of Geneva
    - Tightly organized church -> both clergy and laymen in service
    - Consistory: a special body enforcing the moral discipline
    - Citizens of Geneva were punished for singing, dancing, swearing, etc
  - Geneva became the center of Protestantism
    - Missionaries from Geneva were sent all over Europe
- The English Reformation was rooted in the politics of King Henry VIII
  - Want to divorce w/ wife 1: Catherine of Aragon (b.c. no son)
  - And want to marry Wife 2: Anne Boleyn
    - Pope: unwilling to grant divorce -> So turned to the English church court
    - England Church Court -> Allow divorce & remarriage
  - 1534: Parliament moved to break the church of England from Rome
    - Act of Supremacy: The King was the only supreme head on earth of the church of England -> Gave Henry control of everything
      - But the religious activities ppl are very similar to Catholics
- 1547: Edward VI (Henry’s Son) *Underage, so he was more of a symbol*
  - The Archbishop Cranmer inclined towards Protestant doctrines
    - Anglican/English Church -> In a more Protestant direction
    - Gave clergy the right to marry & create a new church service
- 1553: (Bloody) Mary
  - Tried to return England to Catholicism
  - Burned more than 300 Protestant heretics
  - But England became more Protestant than before

**The Social Impact of the Protestant Reformation**

- Protestantism allowed the family to be the center of human life.
  - B.c. Catholicism saw Celibacy (to stay unmarried) as holy
    - Protestantism yet allows the clergy to marry
  - Husbands are the rulers and wives are the obedient servants in the household
- Female Role: Family life is the only destiny
- Be obedient to your husband
- Bear Children (considered as the divine plan)

**The Catholic Reformation**

- The Catholic Church lost power during the 1500s -> thus, it changed to gain strength.
- This act is called the Catholic Reformation or the Counter-Reformation.
  - Catholic Reformation: Emphasize that elements of the rejuvenation of the catholic church took place even before the protestant reformation
  - Counter-Reformation: Emphasis on the reformation of the Catholic church is used to fight against the Protestant Reformation
- Franciscans, Dominicans, Augustinians -> Preach to the lay people
- Oratory of Divine Love -> emphasize personal spiritual development and charity
- Overall, allow the Catholic church to keep medieval traits whilst also adjusting
  - E.g., revival of monasticism
- Jesuits (Society of Jesus):
  - From Ignatius of Loyola (a Spanish noble man)
    - Absolute obedience to the papacy and the Catholic church
    - Use of education
    - Dedication to engage in ‘ conflict for God.’
  - Restored Catholicism in parts of Germany & Eastern Europe
  - Propagation of the Catholic faith among non-Christians (e.g., to Japan, China, India)
- Reformed Papacy against Corruption
- Council of Trent: intermittently from 1545~1563 for 3 major sessions
  - Bible and Church are considered equally important
  - Both faith and good works are necessary for Salvation
  - The use of Purgatory and Indulgences is strengthened
  - However, indulgence sales are banned to prevent corruption

**Politics and the Wars of Religion in the Sixteenth Century**

- The French Wars of Religion (1562~1598)
  - Although only 7% of the total population are Huguenots (the French Calvinists), 40~50% of the nobility are Huguenots
    - Stood as a great threat to the French Monarch
  - The French Catholics and Huguenots fought for 30 years (Catholic dominance)
  - After a Henry (Huguenot) succeeded to the throne, he converted to Catholicism (Or else no one would accept him)
    - With his coronation, the war of religion ended
  - Edict of Nantes (1598) :
    - Catholicism: the official religion of France
    - Also guarantee Huguenots the right to enjoy all political privileges
- King Philip II of Spain -> strongest enforcement of Catholicism
  - Insisted on strict conformity to Catholicism to help him govern
  - His control over the Netherlands and the killing of Calvinists led to a revolt
  - 1566: Revolt led by William of Nassau
    - 1609 Twelve Years True: recognized independence
    - Laid the foundation of the modern Dutch state
  - Effect: Spain is still a great power, but dominance has shifted to England
- Queen Elizabeth solved the religious problems her sister Mary caused
  - Act of Supremacy -> Elizabeth as the supreme of the church and state
  - The church is protestant but not that extreme on Catholics
- Philip II of Spain attempted to invade Queen Elizabeth I
  - Prepared an armada (fleet of warships)
  - Yet failed to conquer

**Economic and Social Crisis**

- Decline in population in the 1650s due to War, famine, and plague
  - *Used to be an increase after the black death in the 1300s*
- 100,00+ ppl were persecuted due to witch trials
  - Common ppl are more likely to be accused of being witches
  - 75% Female; most of them are single/widowed / over 50+ years old
    - This is b.c. ppl believe Satan may turn women into witches through lust
  - The witch trials eventually declined as time went on
- Mercantilism:
  - Prosperity of a nation is measured by Gold and Silver
  - They want to reach a surplus in their balance of trade
    - High tariff on foreign goods -> reduce imports, increase exports
  - The government should grant trade monopolies and investment in industry
    - Build a canal, transportation systems, and give subsidies
  - Colonies are valuable as sources of raw materials and markets for finished goods from the central country
- In all, Mercantilism encouraged Europe’s overseas expansion
  - Led to early world trade (but the Intereurope trade is still dominant)
  - Goods that used to be only for the wealthy make their way to the normal people
- Although Capitalism starts to form, it is still an agricultural economy
  - 80% of Europeans work on land
  - Though serfdom disappeared, the quality of life stayed similar

**Seventeenth-Century crises: Revolution and War**

- Thirty Years’ War (1618~1648):
  - HRE due to the struggle between Catholics and Protestants
  - Then Denmark, Sweden, France, and Spain joined the war due to political incentives
    - But most of the Battles were fought on German soil
  - Peace of Westphalia:
    - Proclaimed that all German states are free to determine their own religion
    - This led the HRE to lose unity and dominance
      - *Dutch Independence*
    - France became the dominant nation in return
    - Reveal that political motives became the guiding force in public affairs
- Military Power became increasingly important
  - Pressure to build an effective military machine
  - Increased use of firearms and cannons
    - Greater flexibility and mobility in tactics
    - Better disciplined and better trained armies
  - Thus, to support this pressure, they increase taxes on people
    - Creation of large bureaucracies to supervise military resources
    - Growth in the power of the Governments.

**Peoples of the Americas, Exploration, Colonization, and Its Effects**

**Why Did Europeans Begin Exploring?**

**Trade and Wealth**

- Europeans wanted direct access to Asian goods:
  - Spices (pepper, cloves, cinnamon)
  - Silk
  - Porcelain
- Trade routes became more difficult after the expansion of the Ottoman Empire: the traditional trading route of the Silk Road was controlled by the Ottoman’s and travelers received high tax. The route across the Red Sea was the second option, but the entire route required going inland before moving all the cargo back onto a ship.
- Portugal and Spain wanted to bypass the Turkish Muslim’s control of the Silk Road and Italian middlemen’s hold on the Mediterranean.

**New Technology**

- Compass and astrolabe improved navigation.
- Better cartography (mapmaking).
- Portuguese developed the **caravel**, a fast, maneuverable ship capable of long ocean voyages.

**Religion**

- Europeans wanted to spread Christianity.
- Exploration was often presented as a religious mission against Muslims and non-Christians.

**Portugal Leads the Way**

**Prince Henry the Navigator**

- Lived: 1394–1460.
- Sponsored voyages along the African coast.
- Established navigation schools and funded mapmaking.
- Motivated by:
  - Scientific curiosity
  - Wealth from trade
  - Spread of Christianity

**Portuguese Expansion**

- Explored farther down the coast of Africa throughout the 1400s.
- Established trading posts rather than large colonies.
- Created a **Trading-Port Empire**.

**Important Locations**

- Madeira Islands
- Azores Islands
- West African coast
- India
- Southeast Asia

**Commercial Agriculture**

- Portuguese plantations grew:
  - Sugar
  - Tobacco
- Imported African slaves to provide labor.
- Created a plantation model later copied throughout the Americas.

**Alfonso de Albuquerque (1511)**

- Captured key ports in the Indian Ocean.
- Helped Portugal dominate trade between Europe and Asia.
- Shifted trade profits away from Muslim merchants.

**Spain Enters the Competition**

**The Royal Wedding**

**1469**

- Ferdinand of Aragon married Isabella of Castile.
- Unified much of Spain politically.

**Reconquista**

**1492**

- Spain conquered Granada, the last Muslim kingdom in Iberia.
- Completion of centuries-long Christian reconquest.

**Religious Unity**

- Spain strongly promoted Catholicism.
- Jews and Muslims faced expulsion or conversion.

**Columbus and the Americas**

**Christopher Columbus**

**1492**

- Sailed west seeking a route to Asia.
- Funded by Ferdinand and Isabella.
- Reached the Caribbean instead.

**Goals**

- Gold
- Spices
- Wealth for Spain

**Result**

- Began permanent contact between Europe and the Americas.

**Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)**

- Agreement between Spain and Portugal.
- Created a Line of Demarcation.
- Spain received most of the Americas.
- Portugal received Brazil and routes around Africa.

**Major Civilizations of the Americas**

**Maya**

**Location**

- Yucatán Peninsula and Central America.

**Achievements**

- Complex writing system.
- Accurate calendars.
- Advanced mathematics including the concept of zero.
- Large city-states.

**Aztec Empire**

**Location**

- Central Mexico.

**Capital**

- Tenochtitlán.

**Characteristics**

- Military empire.
- Collected tribute from conquered peoples.
- Population estimated at over 200,000.

**Religion**

- Polytheistic.
- Practiced human sacrifice to honor gods.

**Inca Empire**

**Location**

- Andes Mountains of South America.

**Capital**

- Cusco.

**Achievements**

- Over 25,000 miles of roads.
- Terrace farming.
- Highly organized bureaucracy.
- Used quipus (knotted cords) for record keeping.

**Colonization and Conquest**

**Hernán Cortés**

**1519–1521**

- Conquered the Aztec Empire.
- Allied with Indigenous enemies of the Aztecs.
- Captured Tenochtitlán.

**Francisco Pizarro**

**1532–1533**

- Conquered the Inca Empire.
- Captured Emperor Atahualpa.
- Seized vast amounts of gold and silver.

**Effects of Colonization**

**Columbian Exchange**

**From Europe to the Americas**

- Horses
- Cattle
- Wheat
- Smallpox
- Measles

**From the Americas to Europe**

- Corn (maize)
- Potatoes
- Tomatoes
- Cacao
- Tobacco

**Disease**

**Smallpox**

- Most devastating disease.
- Indigenous peoples had no immunity.

**Impact**

- In some areas, over 90% of the population died.
- Entire communities disappeared.

**Atlantic Slave Trade**

**Why?**

- Indigenous populations declined dramatically.
- Colonists needed labor for plantations and mines.

**Result**

- Millions of Africans were forcibly transported to the Americas.
- Plantation economies expanded throughout the Atlantic World.

**Long-Term Significance**

**Economic Effects**

- Massive flow of gold and silver into Europe.
- Growth of capitalism and global trade.
- Spain became one of Europe’s wealthiest powers.

**Cultural Effects**

- Spread of Christianity.
- Spread of Spanish and Portuguese languages.
- Mixing of Indigenous, African, and European cultures.

**Historical Significance**

- Connected Europe, Africa, Asia, and the Americas into a global network.
- Marked the beginning of globalization.
- Created both enormous opportunities and devastating consequences for Indigenous peoples.
