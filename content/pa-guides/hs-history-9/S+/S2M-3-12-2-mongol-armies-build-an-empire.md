# S2M-3 12.2 Mongol Armies Build an Empire

> From the Grade 9 Resource Department's Semester 2 Midterm History S+ review guide (PA). Writers: Vivienne Yu, Katie Hsieh 9-3. Editor: Vivienne Yu, Katie Hsieh 9-3. Range: 8.5 12.2 13.1 13.2. Student-made reference material: where it differs from class, follow class.

Background:

- Mongols were nomadic people.
- Grazed horses and sheep on steppes of Central Asia.
- Rival Mongol clans spent much time warring with each other.

Genghis Khan (c. 1162-1227)

- Originally named Temujin.
- At age 9: rival Mongol clan poisoned his father.
- At age 15: taken prisoner; locked in wooden collar and paraded before enemies (never forgot humiliation).
- After regaining freedom, wandered among drifting clans.

- Took revenge on clan that imprisoned him.
- Became supreme ruler of all Mongols.
- Once despised, became admired and feared across two continents.
- United warring tribes and took name Genghis Khan ("Universal Ruler").

Mongol Conquests:

- Under Genghis Khan, Mongol forces conquered empire from Pacific Ocean to Eastern Europe.
- Imposed strict military discipline and demanded absolute loyalty.
- Highly trained, mobile armies had some of most skilled horsemen in world.
- Reputation for fierceness (could order massacre of entire city) but also generous (rewarded bravery of single fighter).

Conquering China:

- Mongol armies conquered Asian steppe lands with ease.
- Encountered problem of attacking walled cities in China.
- Chinese and Turkish military experts taught Mongols to use cannons and other new weapons.
- Mongols and Chinese launched missiles at each other from metal tubes filled with gunpowder.
- This use of cannons in warfare soon spread westward to Europe.

After Genghis Khan:

- Genghis Khan did not live to complete conquest of China.
- His heirs continued to expand Mongol empire.
- For next 150 years, Mongols dominated much of Asia.
- Toppled empires, spread destruction from southern Russia through Muslim lands to China.
- India avoided invasion (protected by mountain ranges).
- Mongols devastated Sichuan province and annihilated its capital Chengdu.

Mongol Rule After Conquest

Not oppressive rulers:

- Allowed conquered people to live much as before – as long as they regularly paid tribute to Mongols.

Genghis Khan's example:

- Ruled conquered lands with toleration and justice.
- Had no use for city life but respected scholars, artists, and artisans.
- Listened to ideas of Confucians, Buddhists, Christians, Muslims, Jews, and Zoroastrians.

Pax Mongolica (Mongol Peace):

- In 1200s and 1300s, sons and grandsons of Genghis Khan established peace and order within domains.
- Today historians refer to this period as Pax Mongolica.
- Political stability set stage for economic growth.
- Under Mongol protection, Silk Road trade flourished across Eurasia.
- Contemporary quote: "people enjoyed such a peace that a man might have journeyed from the land of sunrise to the land of sunset with a golden platter upon his head without suffering the least violence from anyone."

Cultural exchanges:

- Foods, tools, inventions, ideas spread along protected trade routes.
- From China to Europe: windmills, gunpowder.
- From China to Middle East: techniques of papermaking.
- From Middle East to East Asia: crops and trees.

China Under Mongol Rule

Kublai Khan (Genghis Khan's grandson)

- Needed nearly 70 years to conquer southern China after Genghis Khan subdued northern China.
- 1279: Toppled last Song emperor.
- Capital at Khanbaliq (present-day Beijing).
- Ruled all of China as well as Korea, Tibet, and Vietnam.

All-Mongol Government:

- Tried to prevent Mongols from being absorbed into Chinese civilization (as other conquerors had been).
- Decreed that only Mongols could serve in the military.
- Reserved highest government jobs for Mongols.
- Put Mongols in charge of armies.
- Placed a Mongol commissioner in every Chinese province to watch over local administrators.

Yuan Dynasty (1279-1368):

- Mongol dynasty in China.

Marco Polo (Venetian trader)

- Served Kublai Khan for many years.
- The Travels of Marco Polo: described splendor of Kublai Khan's court and empire.
- Described Khanbaliq as walled city with great palace (walls painted with silver/gold; vaulted ceiling with paintings; main reception room seats 6,000+ people).
- Reported that Hangzhou was 10 or 12 times size of Venice (one of Italy's richest city- states).
- Polo's reports sparked European interest in riches of Asia.

Mongols Continue Outside Contact:

- Mongols tolerated variety of beliefs.
- Pope sent Christian priests to Beijing.
- Muslims set up their own communities in China.
- Chinese products moved toward Europe: gunpowder, porcelain, and playing cards.

The Ming Restore Chinese Rule

Decline of Yuan Dynasty (after Kublai Khan's death in 1294):

- Most Chinese despised foreign Mongol rulers.
- Confucian scholars retreated into own world (saw little to gain from barbarians).
- Heavy taxes, corruption, natural disasters led to frequent uprisings.

Zhu Yuanzhang (peasant leader)

- Forged rebel army that toppled Mongols and pushed them back beyond Great Wall.
- 1368: Founded new Chinese dynasty called Ming (meaning "brilliant"). Early Ming Policies:
- Sought to reassert Chinese greatness after years of foreign rule.
- Initially moved capital to Nanjing (more characteristics of Chinese).
- Eventually moved capital back to present-day Beijing.
- Restored civil service system.
- Confucian learning again became road to success.
- Civil service exams became more rigorous than ever.
- Board of censors watched over bureaucracy, rooting out corruption and disloyalty. Economic Growth under Ming:
- Fertile, well-irrigated plains of eastern China supported population of more than 100 million.
- Chang River valley peasants produced huge rice crops.
- Better methods of fertilizing improved farming.
- Terraces: horizontal steps cut into steep hillsides to gain soil for crops.
- New crops from Americas (1500s): corn and sweet potatoes.
- Chinese cities (Nanjing) had industries: porcelain, paper, tools.
- Ming repaired extensive canal system linking regions → easier trade → cities grew.
- New technologies increased output (better printing methods led to flood of books). Cultural Flourishing under Ming:
- Revival of arts and literature.

- Ming artists developed own styles of landscape painting.
- Created brilliant blue and white porcelain.
- Ming vases among most valuable and popular Chinese products exported to West.
- Confucian scholars continued classical poetry.
- New forms of popular literature for common people emerged.
- Novels: The Water Margin (outlaw gang tries to end injustice by corrupt officials).
- World's first detective stories.

Chinese Fleets Sail the Seas

Zheng He (Chinese admiral and diplomat)

The Voyages (1405-1433):

- Starting in 1405, Zheng He commanded first of seven expeditions.
- Departed at head of 62 huge ships and over 200 smaller ones.
- Crew of about 28,000 sailors.
- Largest ships measured 400 feet long (enormous for the time).

Goals of each expedition:

- Promote trade.
- Collect tribute from lesser powers across the "western seas."

Destinations explored:

- Coasts of Southeast Asia and India.
- Entrances to Red Sea and Persian Gulf.
- Many ports in East Africa.

Results of expeditions:

- Chinese merchants settled in Southeast Asia and India → became permanent presence in trading centers.
- Exotic animals (giraffes) imported from foreign lands.
- Voyages showed local rulers power and strength of Chinese empire.
- Zheng He's tablet: proclaimed that Ming had unified "seas and continents" even more than Han and Tang.

Why China Stopped Exploring

1435 (year Zheng He died):

- Ming emperor suddenly banned building of seagoing ships.
- Later, ships with more than two masts were forbidden.
- Zheng He's huge ships retired and rotted away.

Reasons for ending exploration:

- Historians are not sure.
- Some speculate fleets were costly and did not produce profit.
- Confucian scholars at court had little interest in overseas ventures and commerce.
- To them, Chinese civilization was most successful in world.
- Wanted to preserve ancient traditions (source of stability).

Consequences:

- Rigid loyalty to tradition would eventually weaken China and leave it prey to foreign domination.
- Fewer than 60 years after China halted expeditions, Christopher Columbus sailed west from Spain in search of sea route to Asia.
- Columbus's voyage made Spain a major power and had dramatic impact on entire world.
