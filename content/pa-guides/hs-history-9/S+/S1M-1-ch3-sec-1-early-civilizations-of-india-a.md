**Geography—Location**

- Subcontinent: large landmass that juts out from a continent (Indian Subcontinent)
- Modern countries of India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, and Bhutan

**Geography—Natural Barriers and Isolation**

- Mountains:
- Himalayas: forms the northern and northeastern barrier of the Indian Subcontinent. Stretches eastward from northern Pakistan through India, Nepal, Bhutan, and into Tibet. Snow covered
- Hindu Kush: forms the northwestern barrier of India. Mostly in Afghanistan and northern Pakistan, west of the Himalayas. Snow covered
  - (Both Himalayas + Hindu Kush):
  - Defensive Barrier = prevented invasion
  - Limited contact with other cultures = India’s culture developed on its own
  - Mountain Passes allowed entry = Aryans through the Khyber pass in the Hindu Kush
- Vindhya mts: central India, divides northern India (Indo Gangetic Plain) and southern India (Deccan Plateau)
  - Distinct cultures, language, and traditions in Northern and Southern India
- Eastern and Western Ghats: two mountain ranges that run down the lower half of the Indian subcontinent

**Geography – Rivers**

- Indus River: northwestern India, runs through modern day Pakistan, fed by melting Himalayan snow
  - Supported the Indus Valley Civilization
- Ganges River: Northern India into Bangladesh
  - Fertile Plain along its banks (Gangetic plains) supported agriculture and large settlements
- Brahmaputra River: Originates in Tibet, flows through northeastern India and Bangladesh
  - Brings meltwaters from the Himalayas = supports fertile land in northeast India

**Monsoons and Climate**

- Monsoons: seasonal winds that dominate India’s climate
  - Life depends on timing and intensity:

late or weak monsoon = famine, heavy monsoon = floods

- Winter monsoon (Oct-May): blows from northeast, dry and hot, kills crops
- Summer monsoon (June-Sept): blows from southwest, brings rain

**Three Major Geographic Zones** **(common early settlement)**

- Gangetic Plain (North)
  - South of Himalayas, fertile
  - Watered by 3 major rivers: Indus, Ganges, Brahmaputra
  - Annual floods
- Deccan Plateau (Central/South):
  - Raised, dry plateau extending into the Indian Ocean
  - arid, limited irrigation, depended on monsoons and rains
- Coastal Plains (East and West)
  - Separated from the Deccan by Eastern and Western Ghats (Mountain Ranges)
  - Fertile, rivers and heavy rains
  - Early centers for fishing and sea trade

**Other Important Locations (Indus Valley Civ Cities)**

- Harappa: northeastern Pakistan, in the Punjab region, along the Indus river
- Mohenjo-Daro: southern Pakistan, in Sindh, near the Indus River delta flowing toward the Arabian Sea

**Indus Civilization (2600-1900 B.C.)**

- General overview:
  - Emerged around 2600B.C. in the Indus River Valley (Modern Pakistan)
  - Lasted for 700 years
  - Rediscovered in the 1920s at sites: Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro
  - Covered larger area than any other ancient civ until Persia
  - Writing remains undeciphered, little known about rulers, religion, politics
- **1. Urban Planning and Government**
- Suggests strong, organized, and centralized government
  - Planned cities: grid patterns, rectangular blocks, wide streets
  - Buildings with uniform clay bricks
  - Advanced plumbing and sewage
  - Evidence of standardized weights and measured
- **2. Economy and Trade**
  - Most were farmers: grew wheat, barley, dates, melons, cotton
    - Agricultural mastery
  - Possibly the first to weave cotton
    - Technological innovation
  - Traded cotton cloth, grain, copper, pearls, ivory combs
  - Sea trade with Sumer via the Arabian Sea and Persian Gulf
    - Economically connected and engaged in long-distance commerce = strong government and technical/logistical support
  - Unique writing system not related to cuneiform
    - Cultural independence
- **3. Religion and Culture**
  - Polytheistic
  - Mother goddess = worshipped as source of creation
  - Animals (bulls and buffalo) = seen as sacred, early roots of cow veneration
- ***7 elements of civilization:** cities, government, religion, social structure, writing, art and architecture, technology and innovation
  - Cities (Harappa, Mohenjo-Daro)
  - Government (organized city planning, standardized bricks, sanitation, trade)
  - Writing (Indus script on seals)
  - Social structure (urban planning implies coordination, trade)
  - Religion (mother goddess statues, animal veneration)
  - Art/Architecture (public baths, houses, pottery, seals)
  - Technology (plumbing, weights/measures, cotton weaving, trade)
- **Decline of the Indus Civilization**
  - 1990 B.C. urban life collapsed: decline in craftsmanship, writing disappeared, cities were abandoned
  - Possible causes: environmental damage (deforestation), flooding or earthquakes (evidence of river mud/seismic activity)
  - Combination of natural disasters rather than invasion

**Aryan Civilization and the Vedic Age (1500-500 B.C.)**

- **Aryan Migration:**
  - Nomadic Indo-European people migrated from southern Russia into India through Hindu Kush passes
  - Arrived by 1500 B.C.
  - Brought horses, chariots, and Indo-European languages
  - No cities, left little archaeology, knowledge comes from Vedas
  - Acculturation: when Aryans settled in India, blended with local Dravidian peoples in religion, language, and social practices
- **The Vedas:**
  - Collection of hymns, chants, and rituals told orally by priests
  - Describes Aryan life as warrior-like, cattle-herding, and tribal
  - Period 1500-500 B.C. Vedic Age
- **Society and Government**
  - Organized into tribes led by rajahs (skilled war leader, elected by warriors, advised by elders)
    - Valued warrior spirit
  - Social Hierarchy formed basis of the caste system: Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (warriors), Vaisyas (herders, farmers), Sudras (laborers and servants)
- **Religion and Spiritual Development**
  - Polytheistic: gods represented natural forces (Indra, Agni, Varuna)
  - Later belief evolved toward Brahman (Universal Spiritual power)
  - Rise of mysticism: seeking direct union through meditation
  - Mystical ideas and Brahman concepts = influenced Hinduism
- **Aryan Epics and Cultural Values**
  - **Mahabharata:**
    - record of early Aryan society: warfare, kingship, and struggles
    - stayed India specific
  - **Ramayana:**
    - Pan Hindu cultural text, spread across South and Southeast Asia
    - Moral and cultural mode: ideal model
    - Deeply religious and cultural, ethics

## Ch3 Sec 2 Hinduism and Buddhism

|  | **Hinduism** | **Buddhism** |
|---|---|---|
| Supreme power | One universal spiritual force, shown through many gods | Does not rely on gods, focuses on individual effort and inner wisdom |
| Life after death | Endless cycle of rebirth until spiritual union is achieved | Rebirth continues until a person reaches full understanding and peace |
| Goal of Life | To merge with the universal spirit | To achieve enlightenment, freedom from suffering and rebirth |
| Path to the Goal | Follow duties and live morally within social role | Follow the middle way:; Self-control, mindfulness, compassion |
| Social System | Caste system determines hierarchy | Rejects caste, everyone can reach enlightenment, regardless of birth |
| Priests and Rituals | Worships led by priests | Focus on meditation and personal practice, not ritual or priestly authority |
| Violence and Compassion | Teaches nonviolence and respect for life | Extends equally to all beings |

**Spread of Hinduism**

- Early beliefs from Indus Valley and Aryans combined, forming the foundation of Hinduism.
- Vedas composed; rituals performed by priests (Brahmins).
- Upanishads (ancient indian writings) introduced meditation, moral living, and the idea of a universal spiritual force.
- Devotion to gods like Vishnu, Shiva, and Devi became widespread.
- Revived under Gupta Empire; temples, texts, and art spread Hindu values across India.

**Spread of Buddhism:**

- Buddha’s followers recorded his teachings in the Tripitaka (“Three Baskets of Wisdom”).
- Missionaries and traders spread it throughout Asia.
- Split into two sects:
  - Theravada – original, strict, monks/nuns only (Sri Lanka, SE Asia).
  - Mahayana – more accessible, Buddha seen as divine; emphasized compassion (China, Korea, Japan).
- Declined in India; absorbed by Hinduism (Buddha seen as a Hindu god).

**How Religion and Society Were Intertwined**

- Belief shaped behavior: Karma and dharma defined one’s role in life and society.
- Social structure justified by religion: caste = spiritual hierarchy.
- Daily life = religious duty: work, marriage, food, and purity tied to faith.
- Religion provided stability: People accepted their social position as part of cosmic order.
- Reform movements (Buddhism, Jainism) emerged when spiritual ideas clashed with rigid social systems.

## Ch 3 Sec 3 Indian Empires

**Maurya Empire (321-185 B.C.)**

- Founder: Chandragupta Maurya unified Northern India, first Indian empire
- Conquered Ganges Valley and expanded south
- **Government and Administration**
  - Centralized beaucracy: officials built roads, harbors, managed factories, collected taces
  - Justice system in courts, harsh enforcenmentt, secret police monitored time, corruption, and dissent
  - Military protection included palace warriors
- **Ashoka** **(268-232 B.C.)**
  - Converted to **Buddhism** after war, rejected violence
    - Reduced wars, greater peace and stability
    - Welfare of people
  - Promoted moral governance and religious tolerance
    - Strengthened social unity
  - Built hospitals, roads, wells, rest houses, trees
    - Facilitated trade, communication, public health for citizens
  - Sent missionaries to spread buddhism across India and Sri Lanka
- **Decline**
  - After Ashoka’s death, northern India returned to divided kingdoms

**Gupta Empire (A.D. 320-540)**

- **Government:** local villages had more autonomy, self-governed, involved merchant class
- **Economy**
  - Trade and farming flourished
  - Exports: cotton, pottery, metalware to East Africa, Middle East, Southeast Asia
- **Golden Age of Culture**
  - Education: religious school, Nalanda University (math, medicine, language, literature, physics)
  - Arts: sculpture, painting, dance, music, religious themes prominent
  - Literature: Sanskrit fables, plays
  - Math/science: decimal system, concept of zero, numerals later called “Arabic”
  - Medicine: herbal remedies, simple surgery, smallpox vaccination
- **Society and Family Life**
  - Joint family structure: extended families living together, patriarchal
  - Children trained in caste duties, family interests above individual desires
  - Villages: self-sufficient, governed by councils, cooperative projects (irrigation, roads, temples)
  - Agriculture: dependent on monsoons
  - trade supplemented village needs

**Key Themes**

- Peace and order achieved through strong government, moral example, and local governance.
- Religion influenced law, governance, arts, education, and moral conduct.
- Culture and learning thrived in periods of stability (Maurya under Asoka, Gupta golden age).
- Economy linked kingdoms via trade and infrastructure, strengthening unity and prosperity.
