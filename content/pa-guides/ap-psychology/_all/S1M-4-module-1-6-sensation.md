## 4.1 Foundations

- **Sensation.** Detecting raw information from the environment (light, sound, pressure).
- **Sensory receptor.** A cell that detects a specific type of energy (light, sound waves, chemicals).

- **Transduction (trans- = across; ducere = to lead).** Converting sensory energy into neural signals the brain can read. Three steps: receive → transform → deliver.
- **Bottom-up processing.** The brain builds perception from raw sensory data, starting with the senses.

- **Top-down processing.** Interpreting sensations using expectations and memory, starting with what you already know.
- **Psychophysics.** The study of how physical stimuli relate to what we mentally experience.

## 4.2 Thresholds and Key Terms

| **Concept** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute threshold | Smallest amount of a stimulus you can detect 50% of the time | A candle flame seen 30 miles away on a dark night |
| Difference threshold (JND) | Smallest difference between two stimuli you can detect | Noticing your phone got slightly heavier with a new case |
| Weber's Law | JND is a constant percentage of the original stimulus | +1 candle is obvious with 10 candles, invisible with 100 |
| Signal detection theory | Detection depends on signal strength AND your expectations/alertness | A sleepy parent hears the baby; a partygoer doesn't |
| Subliminal | Too weak for conscious awareness | An image flashed too briefly to "see" |
| Sensory adaptation | Receptors get used to a constant stimulus — you stop noticing it | You stop smelling your own house |

## 4.3 Vision

Light is energy. Wavelength determines hue (the color you see); amplitude determines intensity (brightness).

Light path through the eye: cornea → pupil → lens → retina → optic nerve. The lens changes shape to focus (accommodation).

![Anatomy of the eye: cornea, pupil, iris, lens, retina, fovea, blind spot, and optic nerve.](/media/pa-guides/ap-psychology/s1m-fig-09.png)

*Figure 9. Anatomy of the eye: cornea, pupil, iris, lens, retina, fovea, blind spot, and optic nerve.*

|  | **Rods** | **Cones** |
|---|---|---|
| Detect | Black/white, dim light | Color and fine detail |
| Where | Edges of the retina | Clustered at the fovea (center) |
| Signal path | Photoreceptors → bipolar cells → ganglion cells → optic nerve | Same; fibers cross at the optic chiasm |

![Retinal processing: light triggers rods and cones, which activate bipolar cells, then ganglion cells, whose axons form the optic nerve.](/media/pa-guides/ap-psychology/s1m-fig-10.png)

*Figure 10. Retinal processing: light triggers rods and cones, which activate bipolar cells, then ganglion cells, whose axons form the optic nerve.*

- **Blind spot.** Where the optic nerve exits — no receptors there, so you can't see it (your brain paints over it).
- **Trichromatic theory (tri = three).** Three cone types (red, green, blue) mix to make all colors.

- **Opponent-process theory.** Cells work in opposing pairs (red vs. green, blue vs. yellow) — explains afterimages.

Both color theories are correct: they describe different stages of processing.

![Trichromatic theory (three cone types mix) vs. opponent-process theory (opposing cell pairs).](/media/pa-guides/ap-psychology/s1m-fig-11.png)

*Figure 11. Trichromatic theory (three cone types mix) vs. opponent-process theory (opposing cell pairs).*

- **Feature detectors.** Brain cells that fire only for specific features (edges, lines, movement); the brain combines them with parallel processing.

## 4.4 Audition (Hearing)

Frequency (waves per second) determines pitch (how high or low); amplitude determines loudness, measured in decibels (dB).

![Frequency → pitch; amplitude → loudness.](/media/pa-guides/ap-psychology/s1m-fig-12.png)

*Figure 12. Frequency → pitch; amplitude → loudness.*

Sound path: pinna → eardrum → hammer/anvil/stirrup → oval window → cochlea → hair cells bend → auditory nerve.

![Anatomy of the ear: outer, middle (ossicles), and inner ear (cochlea, hair cells, auditory nerve).](/media/pa-guides/ap-psychology/s1m-fig-13.png)

*Figure 13. Anatomy of the ear: outer, middle (ossicles), and inner ear (cochlea, hair cells, auditory nerve).*

| **Conductive hearing loss** | **Sensorineural hearing loss** |
|---|---|
| Problem in the mechanical parts (eardrum, ossicles) — sound can't get through | Damage to the cochlea or auditory nerve — the signal never becomes neural (loud noise kills hair cells; causes tinnitus) |

- **Place theory.** Explains high pitches — different spots on the basilar membrane vibrate.
- **Frequency theory.** Explains low pitches — hair cells fire at the same rate as the wave.

- **Loudness.** How many hair cells fire.
- **Stereophonic hearing (stereo = 3D).** Comparing the ears to locate sound.

Decibels are logarithmic: each +10 dB is roughly 10× the intensity.

![The decibel scale is logarithmic: each +10 dB represents a tenfold increase in intensity.](/media/pa-guides/ap-psychology/s1m-fig-14.png)

*Figure 14. The decibel scale is logarithmic: each +10 dB represents a tenfold increase in intensity.*

## 4.5 Touch and Pain

Skin detects pressure, warmth, cold, and pain — with separate receptors for each. Nociceptors (noci = harm) are pain receptors.

- **Gate-control theory.** Rubbing an injury floods the spinal "gate" with touch signals, so the pain signal is blocked. Endorphins are the body's own painkillers.
- **Phantom limb sensation.** Feeling an itch or pain in a missing limb — the brain generates the feeling.

- **Tinnitus.** Ringing in the ears with no external sound.

Pain can be controlled by placebos (fake treatments that work because you believe), distraction, and social/cultural factors — pain is part perception.

## 4.7 Body Senses and Interaction

| **Sense** | **What it is** | **Receptor** |
|---|---|---|
| Kinesthesis (kine = movement) | Knowing where body parts are without looking | Proprioceptors in joints, tendons, muscles |
| Vestibular sense | Balance and head position | Semicircular canals + vestibular sacs (fluid in the inner ear) |

- **Sensory interaction.** Senses influence each other. McGurk effect: watch lips say "ga" while hearing "ba" → you hear "da".
- **Embodied cognition.** The body influences the mind: sit on a hard chair → judge criminals as "hard on crime."

- **Synesthesia (syn = together).** Senses cross-wired — some people see music as colors or taste words.
