## 6.1 Concepts and Schemas

- **Concept.** A mental category of similar things ("bird," "furniture").
- **Prototype.** The most typical example of a concept (a robin is a better "bird" than a penguin).

- **Schema.** A mental framework that organizes knowledge.
- **Assimilate.** Fit new info into an existing schema (wolf → "big dog!").

- **Accommodate.** Change the schema itself (learning wolves are their own category).
- **Metacognition (meta = about).** Thinking about your own thinking.

## 6.2 Creativity

Creativity produces ideas that are both new and valuable.

| **Convergent thinking** | **Divergent thinking** |
|---|---|
| Narrows to one correct answer (standard tests) | Expands to many possible answers ("uses for a brick?") |

Five components of creativity: Expertise, Imaginative thinking, a Venturesome personality, Intrinsic motivation (doing it for its own sake), and a creative Environment. Mnemonic: "Every Idea Ventures If Encouraged."

Obstacles: fixation (getting stuck on one approach) and functional fixedness (only seeing an object's usual use — the candle box as a shelf).

## 6.3 Problem-Solving

| **Strategy** | **How it works** | **Trade-off** |
|---|---|---|
| Algorithm | Step-by-step rule that guarantees the answer | Slow but certain |
| Heuristic | Mental shortcut, rule of thumb | Fast but can mislead |
| Executive functions | The brain's "CEO" — planning, focusing, switching | Fatigues without sleep |
| Insight | Sudden "Aha!" — the answer clicks at once | Can't be forced |

Classic insight puzzle: connect 9 dots with 4 straight lines — impossible until the lines go outside the box ("think outside the box").

## 6.4 Decision Traps

| **Bias** | **Definition** | **Everyday example** |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation bias | Seek only evidence that supports your belief | Only reading news that agrees with you |
| Mental set | Solve problems the same old way | Restarting the router the same way every time |
| Representativeness heuristic | Judge by stereotypes, ignoring real odds | "Poetry lover = librarian" (salespeople vastly outnumber librarians) |
| Availability heuristic | Judge likelihood by how easily examples come to mind | Fearing planes after one crash story |
| Overconfidence | Overestimate how right you are | "I'm 99% sure I'll finish Friday" |
| Belief perseverance | Cling to beliefs even after they're disproven | Still believing a debunked rumor |
| Framing | Same facts, different wording → different choice | "90% survival" vs "10% mortality" |
| Anchoring bias | First number heard skews your estimate | "Was Gandhi older than 114?" — guess creeps up |

In short: heuristics make us fast; biases make us wrong.
