## 2.1 Nature vs. Nurture

Nature covers natural selection and evolutionary psychology; nurture covers environment and heredity.

**Exam note.** "50% genetic" means 50% of the INDIVIDUAL DIFFERENCES in the trait — not 50% of your height.

Identical vs. fraternal twins form the basis of twin studies.

## 2.2 The Nervous System

| **Part** | **Contents** |
|---|---|
| CNS (central) | Brain + spinal cord. Bundles of axons = TRACTS; clusters of soma = NUCLEI. |
| PNS (peripheral) | Everything outside the CNS. Bundles = NERVES; clusters = GANGLIA. |
| PNS branches | Somatic = voluntary. Autonomic = automatic: sympathetic (arousal) vs. parasympathetic (calm). |

**Exam note.** CNS terms are tracts and nuclei; PNS terms are nerves and ganglia. Exam questions may use them without defining them (the optic nerve is a named exception).

Sensory neurons carry input; motor neurons carry output. The reflex arc runs: sensory neuron → INTERNeuron → motor neuron.

## 2.3 The Neuron

Know the parts: soma, dendrites, axon, myelin sheath, node of Ranvier, axon hillock, and axon terminal. Glial cells support neurons; Schwann cells produce myelin in the PNS.

**Exam note.** If you are labeling a CNS neuron, do NOT write "Schwann cell" — write "glial cell" (oligodendrocyte earns bonus-level credit).

The action potential: resting potential −70 mV, threshold −55 mV; the response is all-or-none, followed by a refractory period. The sequence is depolarization → repolarization → hyperpolarization.

## 2.4 Neural Communication

At the synaptic cleft, neurotransmitters are released from the pre-synaptic axon terminal...

**Exam note.** ...and they BIND receptors on the post-synaptic neuron. Never write that neurotransmitters GO INTO the post-synaptic neuron.

Signals are excitatory or inhibitory; reuptake recycles neurotransmitters back into the sending neuron.

## 2.5 Neurotransmitters

| **Neurotransmitter** | **Role** | **Linked to** |
|---|---|---|
| Acetylcholine (ACh) | Muscle action, learning, memory | Alzheimer's disease |
| Dopamine | Movement, learning, attention, emotion | Schizophrenia, Parkinson's |
| Serotonin | Mood, hunger, sleep, arousal | Depression |
| Norepinephrine | Alertness, arousal, mood | — |
| GABA | MAJOR inhibitory | Undersupply: seizures, tremors, insomnia |
| Glutamate | MAJOR excitatory; memory | Migraines, seizures |
| Endorphins | Pain & pleasure perception | — |
| Substance P | Pain perception, immune response | — |

**Exam note.** If the question says adrenaline or noradrenaline, it means the HORMONE. Norepinephrine is the neurotransmitter.

Agonists enhance a neurotransmitter's activity; antagonists block it. Reuptake inhibitors such as SSRIs act as agonists.

## 2.6 The Endocrine System and Drugs

Hormones travel via glands — know the adrenal gland and pituitary gland, and the hormone oxytocin.

| **Class** | **Examples** |
|---|---|
| Depressants | Alcohol, barbiturates, opioids |
| Stimulants | Nicotine, cocaine, methamphetamine, MDMA (ecstasy) |
| Hallucinogens | LSD, MDMA, marijuana |

Substance use disorder: addiction vs. tolerance (needing more of the drug for the same effect).

## 2.7 Studying the Brain

- **Approaches.** Biopsychosocial approach (at most 1 MCQ); neuroplasticity; lesion studies.
- **Imaging.** Structural: MRI, CT. Functional: EEG, MEG, PET, fMRI.

## 2.8 Brain Structures and Functions

| **Structure** | **Function** |
|---|---|
| Brainstem (medulla + pons + midbrain) | Life-support basics; the medulla and pons are parts OF the brainstem, not separate from it. |
| Reticular formation | Arousal |
| Thalamus | Sensory relay to cortex |
| Hypothalamus | Homeostasis; talks to the endocrine system via the pituitary; reward center |
| Cerebellum | Balance, coordination |
| Amygdala | Emotion (fear) |
| Hippocampus | Memory |
| Cerebral cortex | Outer grey matter OF the cerebrum |

## 2.9 The Cerebral Cortex

Four lobes: frontal, parietal, occipital, temporal. The motor cortex sits at the rear of the frontal lobe; the somatosensory cortex in the parietal lobe; association areas fill the rest. One odd fact: the right temporal lobe also handles facial recognition.

- **Language.** Broca's area (production) and Wernicke's area (comprehension) — BOTH on the LEFT hemisphere.
- **Contralateral organization.** Each side of the brain connects to the body's opposite side.

- **Hemisphere specialties.** Left: language, logic, analytical. Right: music & art, visual-spatial, creativity.

## 2.10 Classic Case Studies

- **Phineas Gage.** Frontal lobe damage → personality change.
- **Neurogenesis.** Forming new neurons.

- **Split-brain.** Corpus callosum severed. Right visual field → the person can SAY what they saw; left visual field → the LEFT HAND picks the object (contralateral processing).

**Exam note.** If you can explain split-brain studies, you understand how the brain works — a top exam favorite.
