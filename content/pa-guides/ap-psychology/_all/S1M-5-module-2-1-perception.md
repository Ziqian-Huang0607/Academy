## 5.1 Attention

Perception is the brain organizing and interpreting sensory information. Attention selects what gets processed.

- **Selective attention.** Focusing on one thing while ignoring everything else.
- **Cocktail party effect.** Tuning out a noisy room but instantly hearing your name.

- **Inattentional blindness.** Missing something obvious because attention is elsewhere (the invisible gorilla experiment).
- **Change blindness.** Failing to notice a change in a scene (the person you're talking to gets swapped).

- **Perceptual set.** Expectations shape what you perceive (top-down) — expect danger, and a stick looks like a snake.

## 5.2 Gestalt Organization

Gestalt (German for "shape/form") means the whole is more than the sum of its parts — the brain auto-organizes what it sees.

Grouping principles: proximity, similarity, closure, continuity.

- **Figure-ground.** Separating the object (figure) from the background (ground) — the vase/faces illusion.

## 5.3 Depth Perception

Depth perception is seeing in 3D. The visual cliff experiment showed babies avoid the "deep" side of a glass table — depth perception is partly innate.

- **Binocular cues (need both eyes).** Retinal disparity & convergence.

## 5.4 Motion Perception

| **Illusion** | **What tricks you** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Stroboscopic movement | Rapid still frames read as motion | Movies, animation (24 frames/sec) |
| Phi phenomenon | Lights blinking in sequence = one light moving | Neon signs, runway lights |
| Autokinetic effect | A stationary dot in the dark appears to drift | Staring at a single star |

## 5.5 Perceptual Constancy

Perceptual constancy means perceiving objects as stable even as the raw image changes.

- **Color/brightness constancy.** White looks white in sun or candlelight, judged by relative luminance (light reflected compared to surroundings).
- **Shape constancy.** A door still looks rectangular when its image is a trapezoid.

- **Size constancy.** Friends don't shrink as they walk away.

The moon illusion: the horizon moon and the overhead moon have the same retinal size, but context cues near the horizon trick size constancy.

![The moon illusion: context cues near the horizon make the moon look huge; it is the same size overhead.](/media/pa-guides/ap-psychology/s1m-fig-19.png)

## 5.6 Critical Period and Perceptual Adaptation

- **Critical period.** An early window when the brain must receive sensory input or perception never develops properly (restored-vision adults can't learn to see faces well).
- **Perceptual adaptation.** The brain adjusts to changed input (upside-down goggles start looking normal after days).

Bottom line: your brain CONSTRUCTS your perceptions.
