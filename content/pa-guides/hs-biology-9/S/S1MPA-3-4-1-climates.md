Weather is the day-to-day condition of Earth’s atmosphere.

Climate refers to average conditions over long periods and is defined by year-after-year patterns of temperature and precipitation.

Two regions can have the same climate but different weather.

-Climate is rarely uniform even within a region.

-Environmental conditions can vary over small distances, creating microclimates.

3 Factors that effect climate

1. Solar Energy and Greenhouse Effect

-Greenhouse effect: Sunlight hits Earth. Part of the energy turns into heat. Some heat is blocked by atmospheric gases. This greenhouse effect keeps Earth’s average temperature stable. -greenhouse gases: Carbon dioxide, methane, and water vapor

-if greenhouse gas concentration rise, earth warms because they trap more heat.

2. Latitude and Solar Energy

Near the equator, solar energy is intense. Polar areas receive less intense solar energy

3. Heat Transport in the Biosphere

Wind:

-Warm air near the equator rises, spreads toward the poles and cools down.

-Cold air near the poles sinks and flows back along Earth’s surface.

-This movement of air creates wind and transports heat.

-If Earth did not rotate, each hemisphere would have only one large circulation cell. -Earth’s rotation causes winds to blow generally from west to east over the

temperate zones and from east to west over the tropics and the poles.

Ocean currents:

-Surface currents affect the weather and climate of nearby landmasses

-Deep ocean currents form when cold polar water sinks and moves along the seabed. The water rises in warmer areas, and this process is upwelling.

## 4.2 Niche and Environmental Interactions

Tolerance: the ability to survive and reproduce under a range of environmental circumstances. Every species has its own range of tolerance.

Habitat: place where an organism lives

Niche: The range of physical and biological conditions in which a species lives and the way species obtains what it needs to survive and reproduce. Niche describes the occupation or position that an organism has in its environment. Each species occupies a distinct niche Physical aspects of Niche：abiotic factors

Biological aspects of Niche: biotic factors

Resource: any necessity of life (water, nutrients, light, food, or space)

-Competition occurs when organisms attempt to use the same limited resource in the time and place. It can occur between members of the same species (infraspecific competition), or between members of different species (interspecific competition).

-No two species can occupy exactly the same niche in exactly the same habitat at exactly the same time. This is because if two species was to occupy the same niche, one species would beat the other species and exclude the other species.

-Species divide their limited resources to avoid competition.

-Competition shape community by: helping to determine the number and kinds of species in a community, determining the niche each species occupies, causing species to divide resources

Predator-Prey Relationship: Interaction where one animal captures and feeds on another animal. Predators affect the size of prey populations and determine the places prey can live and feed. Usually in a community, the amount of prey is greater then the amount of predators. Herbivore-Plant Relationships: interaction where one animal feeds on producers. Herbivores can affect the size and distribution of plant populations and can determine the places certain plants can survive and grow.

Keystone Species: species that play a large roll in an ecosystem.

Symbiotic Relationships: The close relationship that exists when two or more species live together.

Mutualism : (+/+) benefit each other (eg. The clownfish is immune to anemone stings. When threatened by a predator, clownfish seek shelter by snuggling deep into an anemone’s tentacles. Commensalism: (+/0) one benefits and another is neither helped nor harmed. (Eg. Barnacles attach to the head of the whale and feed on plankton wherever the whale swims. The barnacles don’t harm the whale).

Parasitism: (+/-) one organism lives inside or on another organism and harms it. (Eg.Tapeworms live in the intestines of mammals, where they absorb large amounts of their hosts’ food. Fleas,

ticks, lice, and the leech shown, live on the bodies of mammals and feed on their blood and skin).

## 4.3 Succession

Ecological succession: A series of predictable changes that occur in a community over time. Primary Succession: The establishment of a community in an area of exposed rock that does not have any topsoil. Usually occurs very slowly at first.

Pioneer species: the first organism to appear. It helps to: Fix nitrogen, break down rocks, add organic matter to soil. Secondary Succession: Succession occurs where existing communities are not completely destroyed by disturbances. Proceeds faster than pri mary succession.

## 4.4 Biomes

Tropical Rain Forest

-Abiotic: Hot and wet all year round. Thin, nutrient-poor soil, easy to erode. Heavy rainfall (≥2 m per year)

-Biotic: Highest species diversity, tall trees form canopy, understory plants adapt to low light, animals use camouflage, many can climb or fly.

Tropical Dry forest

-Abiotic: Warm all year, clear wet and dry seasons, rich but easily eroded soil -Biotic: Deciduous trees drop leaves in dry season to save water, some plants have thick waxy leaves, animals may estivate or migrate for water

Tropical Grassland / Savanna / Shrubland

-Abiotic: Warm, seasonal rainfall, compact soil, frequent lightning-caused fires -Biotic: Scattered trees + grasses, grasses grow from base to survive grazing, many animals migrate in dry season, small animals burrow and stay dormant

Desert

-Abiotic: Less than 25 cm rain per year, extreme temperature swings, mineral-rich, low-organic soil -Biotic: Plants like cacti store water and have spines, most animals are nocturnal, some get water only from food

Temperate Grassland

-Abiotic: Hot summers, cold winters; moderate seasonal rain; fertile soil; occasional fires -Biotic: Grasses grow from base, tolerate fire and grazing; seeds spread by wind; small animals use camouflage and burrow

Temperate Woodland and Shrubland

-Abiotic: Hot dry summers, cool moist winters, thin, nutrient-poor soil, regular wildfires -Biotic: Woody shrubs with waxy fire-resistant leaves, some seeds need fire to germinate, animals eat mixed plant food

Temperate Forest

-Abiotic: Cool to moderate winters, warm summers; year-round rain, fertile humus-rich soil -Biotic: Mix of deciduous trees (lose leaves in winter) and conifers, many animals hibernate or migrate in cold months

Northwestern Coniferous Forest

-Abiotic: Mild temperature, heavy rain in fall/winter/spring, dry summer, rocky acidic soil -Biotic: Tall conifers (redwood, spruce), moss covers trees, animals use camouflage, browsers eat varied plants

Boreal Forest

-Abiotic: Long cold winters, short mild summers, moderate rain, high humidity, acidic, nutrient-poor soil

-Biotic: Conical evergreen conifers with needle leaves to reduce water loss, animals have thick insulation or migrate south for winter

Tundra

-Abiotic: Strong winds, low precipitation, short soggy summers, long dark cold winters, permafrost -Biotic: Low-growing plants (mosses, lichens), plants hug ground to avoid wind, some animals migrate, year-round species have adaptations to reduce heat loss

Other land areas:

-because some land areas do not fall into the main biomes, they have a different categor. Mountain Ranges:

-exist on all continents and in many biomes

-abiotic factors and biotic factors all change with the elevation.

Polar Ice Caps

-border the tundra, cold year round

-few plants but algae grow, Mosses and lichens also

-marine mammals, insects, and mites are typical animas

## 4.5 Aquatic Ecosystems

What factors affect life in aquatic ecosystems?

1. Temperature and Currents

2. Nutrient Availability

3. Water depth

-Sunlight penetrates only a short distance through water

Photic Zone:

- sunlight region near the surface , photosynthesis takes place
- may be as deep as 200 m in tropical seas, but just a few meters deep or less in rivers and Swamps.

Aphotic Zone:

-no photosynthesis

Benthic Zone:

-borrom of lake, stream and oceans -benthos: attached organisms or resting on bottom.

-below the photic zone, chemosynthetic autotrophs are the only producers.

Major Categories of Freshwater ecosystems

Rivers and Streams:

-water flows from the headwater(can be underground springs or snowmelt) to the mouth(place where flowing water empties into)

-high oxygen level

-running water

Lake and Pond:

-inland body of standing water

Freshwater Wetlands:

-wetland: place where water either covers the soil or is present at or near the surface for at least part of the year

-good for agriculture

-breeding grounds fro many organisms

-Purify water by filtering pollutants and help prevent flooding by absorbing and slowly releasing large amounts of water

-Three main types of freshwater wetlands: Bogs (decayed or decaying peat moss), Marshes (a variety of reeds and grasses), Swamps (woody plants and trees)

Estuaries:

-where a freshwater river meets a salty sea

-also called Saltwater wetlands

-contains a mixture of freshwater and salt water.

-spawning and nursery grounds for many ecologically and commercially important fish and shellfish species.

Two main types of Estuaries:

-Salt marsh (temperate estuaries)

Salt tolerant plants above low tide line

Sea grasses underwater

-Mangrove swamp (tropical estuaries)

Salt tolerant trees (mangroves)

mangrove tree: a tree with roots which are above the ground and that grows along coasts or on the banks of large rivers in hot count.

Oceans are divided into zones based on depth and distance from shore.

- intertidal zone:

-narrow band where the ocean meets the land

-constantly changing communities because of the disturbance such as daily tides and wave movements

-at high tide（涨潮): organisms submerged in seawater

-at low tide(退潮): organisms exposed to air and sunlight

-barnacles(small saltwater animal with a protective shell-like covering 藤壶) and seaweed attach to the rocks

- coastal ocean (neritic zone)

-Extends from the low-tide mark to the outer edge of the continental shelf, the relatively shallow border that surrounds the continents.

-Kelp forests and coral reefs are two important coastal communities

- pen ocean

-Begins at the edge of the continental shelf and extends outward.

-More than 90 percent of the world’s ocean.

-divided into two zones based on light penetration: the photic and aphotic.

Open ocean photic zone:

-low nutrient levels and only supports the smallest species of phytoplankton. -most photosynthesis on earth occurs

Open ocean photic zone:

-Permanently dark

-Includes the deepest parts of the ocean.

-Deep-sea vents, where superheated water boils out of cracks on the ocean floor, support chemosynthetic primary producers

Photic zone (0–200 m): light available, photosynthesis can happen
