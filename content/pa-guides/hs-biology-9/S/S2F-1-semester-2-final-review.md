**1. Factors That Led to Natural Selection**

Before Darwin developed his theory, several observations helped shape his ideas:

**Overproduction:** Organisms produce more offspring than can survive.

**Variation:** Individuals within a population are different from one another.

**Competition:** Resources such as food, water, and space are limited.

**Adaptation:** Some traits help organisms survive and reproduce better.

**Inheritance:** Beneficial traits can be passed to offspring.

**2. Darwin’s Theory of Evolution**

**Evolution:** Change in inherited characteristics of populations over time.

**Darwin's Main Idea:** Species evolve through natural selection.

**Key Points**

- Individuals vary.
- Some variations are heritable.
- More offspring are produced than survive.
- Individuals with advantageous traits leave more offspring.
- Populations change over generations.

**3. Natural Selection:** The process by which individuals with favorable traits survive and reproduce more successfully.

**Requirements**

- Variation
- Heritability
- Competition
- Differential reproductive success

**Result**

The frequency of beneficial traits increases over time.

**4. Evidence of Evolution**

**Fossils:** Show changes in organisms over time.

**Comparative Anatomy**

- Homologous structures → same ancestry, different functions.
- Analogous structures → similar functions, different ancestry.

**Vestigial Structures:** Reduced structures inherited from ancestors.

**Embryology:** Similar development patterns suggest common ancestry.

**Molecular Evidence:** DNA and protein similarities indicate evolutionary relationships.

**Biogeography:** Species distribution matches evolutionary history.

**5. Testing Natural Selection**

Scientists look for:

- Variation in traits.
- Differences in survival/reproduction.
- Heritability of traits.
- Changes in trait frequencies over generations.

**Example:** Antibiotic-resistant bacteria survive treatment and reproduce.

**6. Genetics Basic Terminology**

**Gene:** Segment of DNA that codes for a trait.

**Allele:** Different versions of a gene.

**Genotype:** Genetic makeup (AA, Aa, aa).

**Phenotype:** Physical expression of genotype.

**Homozygous:** Two identical alleles.

**Heterozygous:** Two different alleles.

**Dominant Allele:** Expressed when present.

**Recessive Allele:** Expressed only when two copies exist.

**Population:** Group of individuals of the same species.

**Gene Pool:** All alleles in a population.

**7. Evolution Short Answer Structure**

Struggle in environment → different variations → adaptations → the advantages → higher probability of surviving and reproducing → higher allele frequency → change of gene pool

**8. Sources of Genetic Variation**

**Mutation:** Random changes in DNA.

**Genetic Recombination:** Occurs during meiosis.

- Crossing over
- Independent assortment

**Random Fertilization:** Different gametes combine.

**Gene Flow:** Movement of alleles between populations.

**9. Natural Selection on Polygenic Traits**

**Polygenic Traits**

Controlled by multiple genes.

Examples: height, skin color

**Types of Selection:**

**Stabilizing Selection:** Average phenotype is favored

**Directional Selection:** One extreme is favored

**Disruptive Selection:** Both extremes are favored

**10. Genetic Drift:** Random change in allele frequencies

Most significant in small populations.

**Founder Effect:** Small group starts a new population.

**Bottleneck Effect:** Population size drastically decreases.

Changes occur by chance, not because the traits are advantageous.

**11. Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium**

**Purpose:** Predicts allele frequencies when evolution is not occurring.

A population remains stable if:

- No mutations
- Random mating
- No natural selection
- Very large population
- No migration

**12. Hardy-Weinberg Calculations**

**Formula**

$${p}^{2}+2pq+{q}^{2}=1$$

**Meaning**

- p = dominant allele frequency
- q = recessive allele frequency
- p² = homozygous dominant
- 2pq = heterozygous
- q² = homozygous recessive

Also:

$$p+q=1$$

**Steps**

- Identify q².
- Find q by taking the square root.
- Calculate p = 1 − q.
- Find p² and 2pq.

Remember to write the formula even if you do not know how to do the question!

Also check if it is stated that the scenario is in a Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.

**13. Process of Speciation**

**Speciation:** Formation of new species.

**Steps**

- Population becomes isolated.
- Gene flow stops.
- Mutations and selection create differences.
- Reproductive isolation develops.
- New species forms.

**Causes of Reproductive Isolation**

**Behavioral Isolation**: When organisms act/behave differently → they don’t reproduce tgt.

**Temporal Isolation**: When organisms reproduce at different times or are at different places in different times → they don’t reproduce tgt.

**Geographical Isolation**: When organisms are different geographic zones or micro-environments and rarely encounter one another.

**14. Speciation Short Answer Structure**

The populations became geographically isolated → very far → lack of gene flow. Over time, mutations and natural selection caused genetic differences. Eventually they could no longer reproduce successfully together (reproductive isolation), resulting in speciation.

**15. Linnaean Classification System**

**Levels (largest → smallest)**

**Domain → Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**

For example for humans:

- Domain: Eukarya
- Kingdom: Animalia
- Phylum: Chordata
- Class: Mammalia
- Order: Primates
- Family: Hominidae
- Genus: Homo
- Species: sapiens

Binomial Nomenclature: [Genus], [Species]

**16. Systematics & Phylogenetic Systematics**

**Systematics:** Study of evolutionary relationships.

**Phylogeny:** Evolutionary history of a group.

**Phylogenetic Systematics:** Classifies organisms based on evolutionary relationships.

**17. Cladistics**

**Cladistics**

Method of classification based on shared derived characteristics.

**Clade:** An ancestor and all descendants.

**Derived Character:** Trait that evolved in a lineage.

**Monophyletic group:** single common ancestor and all of its descendants → a clade

**Paraphyletic group:** a common ancestor and some, but not all, of its descendants → not a clade

**18. Cladograms**

**Cladogram:** Diagram showing evolutionary relationships.

- Branch points = common ancestors.
- Organisms sharing a recent branch point are more closely related.
- Branch length usually does not indicate time.

Prokaryotic

Unicellular

**20. Pattern and Process of Evolution**

**Process**

Mechanisms causing evolution:

- Natural selection
- Genetic drift
- Mutation
- Gene flow

**Patterns**

Results of evolution:

- **Divergent evolution** (adaptive radiation): process where related species evolve from a shared ancestor and become increasingly different over time.
- **Convergent evolution**: process where unrelated or distantly related species independently evolve similar physical traits, behaviors, or adaptations.
- **Coevolution**: process where two or more species evolve in response to each other

**21. Gradualism:**

Evolution occurs through small changes over long periods.

Opposite concept:

**Punctuated Equilibrium:** Long periods of little change interrupted by rapid evolution.

**22. Fossil Formation Process**

- Organism dies.
- Rapid burial occurs.
- Soft tissues decay.
- Minerals replace tissues.
- Fossil forms.
- Erosion may expose fossil.

**23. Dating Earth's History**

**Relative Dating:** Determines older vs younger fossils.

**Absolute Dating:** Determines actual age using radioactive decay.

**24. Calculating Half-Lives**

**Half-Life:** Time required for half of a radioactive sample to decay.

**Example:** 100 g → 50 g → 25 g → 12.5 g **→** Three half-lives have passed.

**Formula (no need to use it if you can logically calculate, just for reference)**

$$N={N}_{0}{\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)}^{n}$$

- N = remaining amount
- N₀ = original amount
- n = number of half-lives

**Important Events:**

**26. Coevolution:** Two species evolve in response to one another.

**Examples**

- Flowers and pollinators
- Predators and prey
- Parasites and hosts

**27. Formation of Earth**

Earth formed about **4.6 billion years ago** from dust and gas surrounding the young Sun.

There was carbon dioxide, water vapor, nitrogen, methane, ammonia, hydrogen sulfide, carbon monoxide; contain little or no oxygen

Early Earth was:

- Extremely hot
- Volcanically active
- Frequently hit by asteroids

**28. Miller-Urey Experiment**

**Purpose**

Test whether organic molecules could form under early Earth conditions.

**Results**

Produced amino acids from simple gases using electrical sparks.

**Importance**

Showed that building blocks of life can form naturally.

**29. Prokaryotes: First Life Forms**

**Simple cells that:**

- Lack a nucleus.
- Lack membrane-bound organelles.
- Have DNA floating freely in the cell.

Prokaryotes are simpler than eukaryotes, so scientists believe they evolved first.

Some early prokaryotes evolved photosynthesis. Photosynthetic bacteria gradually released oxygen into the atmosphere, leading to the Great Oxygenation Event. This increase in oxygen eventually made more complex life possible.

**30. Origin of Eukaryotic Cells**

Cells with:

- A nucleus
- Membrane-bound organelles
- Greater complexity

Scientists believe eukaryotes evolved from prokaryotes.

**31. Endosymbiotic Theory & Evidence**

**Theory**

A larger prokaryotic cell engulfed smaller bacteria.

Instead of being digested, the bacteria lived inside the host cell.

Over time, they became organelles.

**Results**

- Aerobic bacteria became mitochondria.
- Photosynthetic bacteria became chloroplasts.

**Evidence:** Mitochondria and chloroplasts -

- Have their own DNA.
- Reproduce independently.
- Have ribosomes similar to bacteria.
- Have double membranes.

**32. Multicellularity**

Some eukaryotic cells began living together in groups.

Over time:

- Cells specialized for different functions.
- Organisms became larger and more complex.

This eventually led to plants, animals, fungi.
