### 16.1 Darwin's Voyage of Discovery

**Charles Darwin's Contribution to Science**

- Darwin developed a scientific theory of biological **evolution** (the process by which organisms change over time). His theory explains how modern organisms evolved over long periods of time through descent from common ancestors.

**Observations Aboard the Beagle (Three Patterns of Biodiversity)**

Darwin observed three distinctive patterns of biological diversity:

**1. Species vary globally**

- Different, yet ecologically similar, animal species inhabited separated, but ecologically similar, habitats around the globe.
- Example: Flightless, ground-dwelling birds like rheas in South America, ostriches in Africa, and the emu in Australia all look and act similarly but live on completely different continents.

**2. Species vary locally**

- Different, yet related, animal species often occupied different habitats within a local area.
- Example (Galápagos Tortoises): Tortoise shells varied in predictable ways based on their specific island habitat:
  - **Isabela Island:** Abundant vegetation close to the ground. Tortoises here have dome-shaped shells and short necks.
  - **Hood Island:** Flat, dry, and has sparse, high vegetation. Tortoises here have curved, open shells around their long necks and legs, enabling them to reach higher for food.
- Example (Galápagos Birds): Different varieties of small brown birds (unique species of finches) had distinct beak shapes adapted to their specific island's resources.

**3. Species vary over time**

- Darwin collected **fossils** (the preserved remains or traces of ancient organisms).
- He noticed that some fossils of extinct animals were remarkably similar to living species.
- Example: Fossils of the long-extinct glyptodont, a giant armored animal, were found in the same area as the modern armadillo, which appeared to be a smaller, living version of the ancient glyptodont.

**Conclusion**

The evidence strongly suggested that species are not fixed. Darwin hypothesized that unique species (like those on the Galápagos) might have evolved from mainland ancestors over time through natural processes.

### 16.2 Ideas That Shaped Darwin's Thinking

**An Ancient, Changing Earth (Geology)**

- **James Hutton:** Concluded that Earth is extremely old. He introduced the concept of **deep time** (the idea that Earth's history stretches back over a period of time so long it is difficult for the human mind to imagine). He proposed that slow-acting geological forces shape the planet (e.g., forces beneath the surface pushing rock layers upward to build mountains).
- **Charles Lyell:** Geological process in action today must be the same ones that shaped Ewarth millions of years ago.
- Influence on Darwin: Darwin asked himself: If Earth can change over time, could life change too?

**Lamarck's Evolutionary Hypotheses**

- **Jean-Baptiste Lamarck:** One of the first naturalists to suggest that species are not fixed and to try to explain evolution scientifically using natural processes.
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Acquired Characteristics:** Suggested organisms could change during their lifetimes by selectively using or not using various parts of their bodies (e.g., a bird acquiring longer legs by making an effort to wade in deeper water).
  - **Inheritance of Acquired Characteristics:** Suggested individuals could pass these acquired traits on to their offspring, enabling species to change over time.
- **Flaws:** We now know organisms do not have an inborn drive to become perfect, and traits acquired by individuals during their lifetime cannot be passed on to offspring.

**Population Growth**

- **Thomas Malthus:** If Human population grew unckecked=>not enough living space and food for everyone.
- Influence on Darwin: Darwin realized Malthus's reasoning applied even more to other organisms (which produce far more offspring than humans). He realized most organisms don't survive to reproduce, leading him to wonder which individuals do survive, and why.

**Artificial Selection**

- **Artificial selection**: The process in which nature provides the variations, and humans select those they find useful (e.g., farmers selectively breeding only the cows that produce the most milk or trees that produce the largest fruit).
- Influence on Darwin: Darwin recognized that natural variation was not just a series of minor defects, but the crucial raw material needed for evolution to occur.

### 16.3 Darwin Presents His Case

**Evolution by Natural Selection**

- **The Struggle for Existence:** If more individuals are produces than can survive, members of a population must compete to obtain food, living space, and other necessities of life.
- **Variation and Adaptation:** Individuals possess natural variations among their heritable traits.
  - **Camouflage, mimicry, behaviors**
- **Survival of the Fittest:** Differences in adaptations directly affect an individual's fitness.
  - **Fitness**: Describes how well an organism can survive and reproduce in its environment.
  - Individuals with adaptations well-suited to their environment have **high fitness**.
  - Individuals with characteristics not well-suited to their environment have **low fitness** (they either die without reproducing or leave few offspring).
  - Note: In evolutionary terms, "survival" doesn't just mean staying alive; it means reproducing and passing adaptations on to the next generation.
- **Natural selection**: The process by which organisms with variations most suited to their local environment survive and leave more offspring. Unlike artificial selection, the environment (not a farmer or breeder) influences fitness.
  - **6 factors of natural selection:**
    - The environment influences fitness
    - Well adapted individuals survive and reproduce
    - Generation to generation, population continue to change as environment change
    - Natural selection only acts on inherited traits
    - Natural selection does not make organisms “better”
    - Natural selection doesn’t move in a fixed direction

**Common Descent**

- Darwin proposed that, over many generations, adaptation could cause successful species to evolve int new species.
- **Descent with Modification:** Living species are descended, with modification , from common ancestor.
  - Hutton and Lyell's concept of deep time provided the necessary timeframe for this to occur.
  - Darwin drew the very first **evolutionary tree** to visually illustrate how descent with modification produces the diversity of life.
- **Principle of Common Descent:** All species—living and extinct—are descended from ancient common ancestors. A single "tree of life" links all living things.

### 16.4 Evidence of Evolution

**Biogeography**

- **Biogeography** is the study of where organisms live now and where they and their ancestors lived in the past. Patterns in the distribution of living and fossil species tell us how modern organisms evolved from their ancestors.
- Two biogeographical patterns are significant to Darwin's theory:
  - **Closely related but different (Adaptive radiation):**
- One species evolved into several different forms that live in different ways.
- Adapt to different environment
- Share 1 common ancestor
  - **Distantly related but similar (Convergent evolution):**
- Unrelated organisms live in similar environment and come to resemble on another
- Adapt to similar environment.
- Do not char common ancestor.

**The Age of Earth and Fossils**

- **The Age of Earth:** Evolution takes a long time. Radioactive dating indicates that Earth is about 4.5 billion years old—plenty of time for evolution by natural selection to take place.
- **Recent Fossil Finds:** Many recently discovered fossils form series that trace the evolution of modern species from extinct ancestors.

**Comparing Anatomy and Embryology**

- **Homologous structures**: Different species have structure adapted for different functions, but these structures show many similarities
  - Evidence of common ancestor
  - Example: The front limbs of reptiles, birds, and mammals (which contain the same basic bone structure).
- **Vestigial structures**:Structures that are inherited from ancestors, but they have lost their original function due to different selection pressures on the descendants
  - Example: Hind limbs of whales and dolphins and pelvis
  - Evidence of Common ancestor.
- Analogous Structures: Body parts that share a common function, but do not have common structure.
  - Example: The wing of a bee and the wing of a bird.
  - Do not provide evidence of Common ancestor.

**Comparing Embryology**

- Similar patterns of embryo development.
- Evidence of common ancestor.
- At some time in their development, all vertebrate embryo exhibit common structures: a tail, a notochord, and pharyngeal pouches.

**Genetics and Molecular Biology**

- **Life's Common Genetic Code:** All living organisms use the same universal genetic code.
- Similarities in DNA/RNA/Protein sequences provide the most powerful evidence of common ancestor.
- **Homologous Molecules:** Homologous proteins and genes share extensive structural and chemical similarities across different organisms.
  - Homologous Proteins: Cytochrome c, which functions in cellular respiration, is found in almost all living cells.
  - Homologous Genes: Hox genes, which help determine the head-to-tail axis in embryonic development, are found in almost all multicellular animals.

**Testing Natural Selection**

- One way to gather evidence for evolutionary change is to observe natural selection in action.
- **The Grants' Work:** Peter and Rosemary Grant spent over 35 years studying Galápagos finches. They realized Darwin's hypothesis rested on two testable assumptions:
  - For beak size and shape to evolve, there must be enough heritable variation in those traits to provide raw material for natural selection.
  - Differences in beak size and shape must produce differences in fitness.
