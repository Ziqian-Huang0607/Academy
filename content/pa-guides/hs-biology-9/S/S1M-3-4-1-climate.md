# S1M-3 4.1 Climate

**Weather vs. Climate**

  - **Weather**: The day-to-day condition of Earth's atmosphere at a particular time and place (e.g., "It's raining and 10°C today").
  - **Climate:** The average, year-after-year conditions of temperature and precipitation in a particular region (e.g., "Seattle has a temperate, rainy climate").
  - **Microclimate**: Climate conditions in a small area that differ from the surrounding areas (e.g., the sunny, dry side of a tree vs. the shady, moist side).

**Factors That Determine Global Climate**

**1. Solar Energy and the Greenhouse Effect**

  - The main force that shapes climate is solar energy.
  - The **Greenhouse Effec**t is a natural process where certain gases in the atmosphere (greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide, methane, and water vapor) trap heat, much like glass in a greenhouse.
  - This trapped heat makes Earth warm enough to live on. An increase in these gases increases the warming effect.

**2. Latitude and Solar Energy**

  - The curvature of Earth causes the sun's rays to strike at different angles, creating three main climate zones:
  - **Polar Zones**: Receive the least intense, most indirect sunlight. Cold year-round.
  - **Temperate Zones**: Experience seasonal variations as Earth tilts toward or away from the sun.
  - **Tropical Zone**: Receives nearly direct, intense sunlight year-round. Generally warm.

**3. Heat Transport in the Biosphere**

  - The **unequal distribution** of heat drives winds and ocean currents, which transport heat and moisture around the planet.
  - **Winds**: Created by the rising of warm air and the sinking of cool air.
  - **Ocean Current**s: Warm currents carry heat from the tropics toward the poles; cold currents bring cold water from the poles back toward the equator. These currents strongly influence the climate of nearby landmasses.

**4.2 Niches and Community Interaction.**

**Niches and Habitats**

  - A **habitat** is the general place where an organism lives (its "address").
  - A **niche** is the full range of physical and biological conditions in which a species lives and the way the species obtains what it needs to survive and reproduce (its "profession" or "role").
  - This includes the **tolerance** (range of environmental conditions it can withstand), resources it uses, and how it interacts with other species.

**Community Interactions**

  - **Competition:** Occurs when organisms of the same or different species attempt to use the same limited ecological resource.
    - **Competitive Exclusion Principle:** No two species can occupy exactly the same niche in the same habitat at the same time. One will be better at competing and will eventually exclude the other.
    - Species often **divide resources** to reduce competition.
  - **Predation:** An interaction where one animal (the predator) captures and feeds on another animal (the prey).
  - **Herbivory:** An interaction where an animal (the herbivore) feeds on producers (plants).
- **Symbiosis**
  - Any relationship in which two species live closely together.
  - **Mutualism:** Both species benefit from the relationship (e.g., clownfish and sea anemone).
  - **Parasitism:** One organism lives on or inside another organism (the host) and harms it (e.g., tapeworms, ticks).
  - **Commensalism:** One organism benefits and the other is neither helped nor harmed (e.g., barnacles on a whale).
- **Keystone Species**
  - A single species that can have a dramatic and disproportionate effect on the structure of an entire community.
  - Example: Sea otters eat sea urchins. If otters are removed, urchin populations explode and destroy kelp forests, which collapses the habitat for many other species.

## 4.3 Succession

- **Ecological Succession** is a series of more-or-less predictable changes that occur in a community over time after a disturbance.
- **Primary Succession**
  - Occurs on surfaces where **no soil exists** (e.g., after a volcanic eruption or retreating glacier).
  - The first species to colonize are called **pioneer species** (e.g., lichens and mosses). They help break down rock and form soil.
- **Secondary Succession**
  - Occurs when a disturbance affects the community **without completely destroying the soil** (e.g., after a wildfire, hurricane, or farming).
  - Proceeds faster than primary succession because soil and seeds are already present.
- **Climax Communities**
  - A mature, stable community that was thought to be the end result of succession.
  - Modern ecology shows that climax communities are not always uniform or stable and can be a patchwork of different stages due to frequent disturbances.

## 4.4 Biomes

**Climate vs. Weather**

  - **Weather** is the day-to-day condition of Earth's atmosphere.
  - **Climate** refers to average conditions, including temperature and precipitation, over long periods.

**Factors That Affect Global Climate:**

  - **Solar Energy and the Greenhouse Effect:** The trapping of heat by certain gases in the atmosphere (CO₂, methane, water vapor).
  - **Latitude:** The curvature of Earth causes solar energy to strike at different angles, creating three main climate zones: **polar, temperate, and tropical.**
  - **Heat Transport:** Winds and ocean currents transport heat and moisture around the globe.

A **biome** is a complex of terrestrial communities that covers a large area and is characterized by certain soil, climate conditions, and specific groups of plants and animals.

**Major Biomes**

  - **Tropical Rain Forest:** Hot and wet year-round; greatest species diversity; nutrient-poor soil.
  - **Desert:** Less than 25 cm of rain per year; can be hot or cold; organisms adapted to conserve water.
  - **Temperate Grassland:** Seasonal precipitation; fertile soils; maintained by fire and grazing.
  - **Temperate Forest:** Cold winters and warm summers; deciduous trees.
  - **Boreal Forest (Taiga):** Dense coniferous forests; long cold winters, short mild summers.
  - **Tundra:** Characterized by **permafrost** (a layer of permanently frozen subsoil); very cold, dry, and windy; low-growing vegetation.

## 4.5 Aquatic Ecosystems

**Factors Affecting Aquatic Ecosystems:**

  - **Water Depth:** Determines amount of sunlight.
    - **Photic Zone:** Sunlit upper layer where photosynthesis can occur.
    - **Aphotic Zone:** Permanently dark layer below.
  - **Temperature, Currents, and Nutrient Availability.**

**Freshwater Ecosystems**

  - **Rivers and Streams:** Water flows in one direction; organisms adapted to current.
  - **Lakes and Ponds:** Still water; food webs based on plankton.
  - **Freshwater Wetlands** (Bogs, Marshes, Swamps): Water covers the soil or is present at the surface; highly productive; filter pollutants and prevent flooding.

**Estuaries**

  - A special wetland formed where a river meets the sea, containing a mixture of fresh and salt water.
  - **Extremely important** as spawning and nursery grounds for many ecologically and commercially important fish and shellfish.

**Marine Ecosystems**

  - **Intertidal Zone:** Exposed to air and sun at low tide, submerged at high tide; organisms must tolerate extreme changes.
  - **Coastal Ocean:** From low-tide mark to the edge of the continental shelf; includes highly productive **kelp forests** and **coral reefs**.
  - **Open Ocean:** The vast, deep ocean.
    - **Photic Zone:** Base of most oceanic food webs (phytoplankton).
    - **Aphotic Zone:** Includes the deep sea, where food webs are based on material falling from above or on **chemosynthesis** at hydrothermal vents.
