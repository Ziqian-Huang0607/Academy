**3.1: What is Ecology?**

- Biosphere - All life on Earth, and all parts of Earth where life exists
  - Atmosphere - Extends to 8km above sea level
  - Lithosphere - Extends to 11 km below sea level
  - Hydrosphere
- Ecology - Study of interactions between organisms and their physical environment
- Levels of organization
  - Individual organism
    - Specie: A group of similar organisms that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring
  - Population - A group of the same species that live int he same area at the same time
  - Community - An assemblage of different populations in the same area and time
  - Ecosystem - All organisms in a community and their physical environment
  - Biome - Group of ecosystems with similar climate and typical organisms
  - Biosphere - All life on Earth, and all parts of Earth with life
- Biotic and Abiotic factors
  - Biotic factor - Interactions between living parts of a system
  - Abiotic factor - Physical and non-living parts of an environment
- Biological methods
  - Observation
  - Experimentation
  - Modeling

**3.2: Energy, Producers, and Consumers**

- Energy transfers from one organism to another
- Primary Producers / Autotrophs
  - Use solar and chemical energy to assemble inorganic compounds into complex organic compounds
  - Photosynthesis
    - Use light energy to convert water and carbon dioxide into oxygen and sugars
    - i.e. Plants, algae, cyanobacteria, phytoplankton
  - Chemosynthesis
    - Convert inorganic molecules like hydrogen sulfide into sugars
    - i.e. Carbon dioxide + Hydrogen Sulfide + Oxygen = Sugars + Sulfur compounds
    - Deep sea, volcanoes, and hot springs
- Consumers/Heterotrophs
  - Obtain energy by ingesting other organisms
  - Types
    - Carnivore - Eats animals
    - Herbivore - Eats plants
    - Omnivore - Eats plants and animals
    - Scavenger - Consume carcasses
    - Decomposer/Saprotroph - Digest dead animals to produce detritus
      - i.e. bacteria and fungi
    - Detritivore - Ingest detritus to break down into smaller pieces
      - i.e. worms and shrimp

**3.3: Energy Flows in Ecosystems**

- Energy flows through ecosystems in a one-way stream
- Food chains and Food webs
  - Food chains
    - Series of steps in which organisms transfer energy by eating and being eaten, only 10% goes to next level
  - Food webs - Interconnected food chains where energy flows
    - How food chains interact
- Decomposers - Not on food web
  - Convert dead organisms into detritus
  - Release nutrients to environment to be used by producers
  - Recycle nutrients
- Trophic levels
  - Each step in a food chain/web
  - i.e. Producer, Primary Consumer (i.e. zooplankton), Secondary Consumer
  - Ecological pyramids - Show relative amounts of energy/ biomass/ numbers at each trophic level

**3.4: Cycles of Matter**

- Matter vs. Energy
  - Matter
    - Recycled through ecosystems
    - Bio (Biological) geo (Geological) chemical cycles and human activity
    - Matter vs. Nutrient
      - Matter - Has volume and mass
      - Nutrient - Chemical substance that must be obtained from environment to sustain life
  - Energy
    - Enters as sunlight, leaves as heat and material not consumed
    - Flows through trophic levels (90% lost each step)
    - Passed to detritivores and decomposers
- The Water Cycle
  - Evaporation - Water (liquid) becomes water vapor
  - Transpiration - Water loss from plant leaves
  - Condensation - Cooling water vapor condenses into droplets around dust
  - Precipitation - Falls from clouds as rain, hail, sleet, or snow
  - Runoff - Groundwater flows into rivers and seas
  - Percolation - Water soaks land and saturates underground
- The Carbon Cycle
  - Carbon on Earth
    - Living organisms - Carbon-containing compounds
    - Atmosphere - Carbon dioxide and Carbon monoxide
    - Lithosphere - Carbonate rocks, fossil fuels, natural gas
    - Hydrosphere - Dissolved carbon dioxide
  - Events of the cycle
    - Photosynthesis - Plants turn carbon dioxide and water to glucose and oxygen
    - Respiration - Combusting (internally) sugars to produce energy and release carbon
    - Feeding - Animals feed on plants for glucose produced by photosynthesis
    - Decomposition - Waste is digested by decomposer to release carbon
    - Fossilization - Form in ocean, where dead organisms are covered in silt to form sedimentary rock (abiotic)
    - Combustion - Burning (i.e. volcanoes and burning fossil fuels)
- The Nitrogen Cycle
  - Uses
    - All cells need nitrogen to make amino acids (form proteins) and nucleic acids (form DNA)
    - Forms of nitrogen
      - In air - Nitrogen gas (78% of Earth's atmosphere)
      - In soil
      - NH3 - Ammonia
      - NH4+ - Ammonium
      - NO2- - Nitrite
      - NO3- - Nitrate (Man-made fertilizer)
      - In plants and animals - organic nitrogen compounds
  - Processes
    - Nitrogen Fixation
      - Bacteria (Rhizobium in legumes) convert Nitrogen gas to ammonia and ammonium
      - Can also be done in small amounts by lightning
    - Nitrification - Bacteria convert ammonia and ammonium into nitrite and nitrate
    - Assimilation - Plants absorb nitrite and nitrate to make DNA and protein
    - Ammonification - Decomposers break down organic nitrogen compounds in dead organisms to release ammonium
    - Denitrification - Other soil bacteria break down nitrate into nitrogen gas
- The Phosphorus Cycle
  - Uses
    - Vital component of DNA and RNA
    - Not common in the atmosphere
    - Forms
      - Inorganic phosphate (PO4(3-)) on land, rock, soil, minerals, ocean
      - Organic phosphorus compounds in living things
  - The Cycle
    - Short-term cycle (Daily)
      - Phosphorous in phosphate is cycled from soil to producers
      - Cycled form producers to consumers
    - Long-term cycle (Decades - Centuries)
      - Weathering and erosion of rock slowly adds phosphorus
      - Limits the growth of producers

**Nutrient Limitations**

- If ample sunlight and water are available, the primary productivity of an ecosystem would be limited by limiting nutrients
  - Primary productivity: Rate at which producers produce organic material
  - Limiting nutrient: Nutrient whose supply limits productivity (such as nitrogen and phosphorus)
- Without nutrient limitation: Runoff from heavily fertilized field causes algal bloom (eutrophication)
  - Prevent sunlight from shining through, underwater plants produce less oxygen
  - Animals die and decompose to consume oxygen
  - Eutrophication - Excess of Nutrients
