**Human Effects**

-agriculture, development, industry

-impact quality of natural resources (soil, water, atmosphere)

-Agriculture

-row crops

-monoculture: only plant one type of highly productive crop year after year efficient but exploiting natural resources too much

-clear large areas of land

-fertilizer production consumes fossil fuels

-pesticides enter water biological magnification (chemicals increase along food chain & web)

-Development

-large and dense communities (take up space)

-wastes lead to pollution

-consume farmland and divide natural habitat into fragments

-Industrial growth

-require lot of energy to power

-fossil fuel wastes pollution (discarded directly into air, water, soil)

**Ecosystem Goods and Services**

-goods: tangible, physical products from ecosystems

-services: processes that sustain life and produce goods(i.e. clean water and air, nutrient cycles)

**Resources**

-renewable: can be produced or replaced by healthy ecosystem

-E.g. Solar energy, wind, water, trees (not forests)

-non-renewable: resources that cannot be replenished within a reasonable amount of time

-E.g. Nuclear resources, oil, natural gas

**Sustainable Development**

-using natural resources to meet human needs without causing long-term environmental damage

-use resources at a rate which they van be replaced/recycled

-preserve the ecosystems that produce natural resources

## 6.2 Using Resources Wisely

**Soil Resources**

-topsoil: mineral and nutrient-rich portion of soil

-absorb & maintain nutrient, rich in organic matter, low in salt, renewable if managed properly

-healthy soil supports both agriculture and forestry

-soil profile: layers

-Soil Erosion

-the removal of soil (topsoil) by water or wind

-deforestation: loss of forests severe erosion when followed by grazing or plowing

-Consequences:

-desertification: farming, overgrazing, seasonal drought, climate change farmland change into desert

-Soil Quality and Sustainability / actions on soil erosion

-leaving stems and roots of previous year’s crop in soil to hold soil in place between plantings

-crop rotation: planting different crops at different seasons/year to prevent nutrient loss

-contour plowing: planting fields of crops across, not down, the slope of land

-nutrients wouldn’t slide down and be lost

**Freshwater Resources**

-humans need freshwater resources for ecological goods and services

-drinking water, industry, transportation, energy and waste disposal

-Water pollution

-nonpoint sources;

-Industrial and agricultural chemicals biological magnification

-PCBs (organic chemical) and DDTs (pesticides)

-residential sewage nitrogen & phosphorus algae/bacteria bloom “dead zone”

-watershed: all land whose groundwater, streams, rivers drain into the same place (i.e. large lake or river)

-Water quality and sustainability

-protect natural system involved in water cycle. Critical part of watershed conservation

-pollution control

-sewage treatment

-agriculture can use integrated pest management instead of pesticides

-use predators / parasites to regulate for pests, less poisonous sprays and crop rotation

**Atmospheric** **Resources**

-provides oxygen we breath

-ozone layer protects skin from rays that cause cancer

-greenhouse gases regulate global temperature

-Air pollution

-Smog: gray-brown haze formed by chemical reactions among pollutants released by industrial progresses and automobile exhaust (photochemical smog)

-Acid rain: N and S compounds released by burning fossil fuels form nitric acid and sulfuric acids in rain acid rain

-kill plants by damaging leaves

-changes chemistry of soils and surface water

-dissolves and releases toxic elements from soils

-Greenhouse gases

-excess greenhouse gases cause global warming and climate change

-burning fossil fuels carbon dioxide. Raising cattle and farming rice methane

-Particulate

-microscopic particles (dust/ash) breath in through nose, mouth, lungs

-Air quality and sustainability

-Automobile emission standards

-clean air regulation

**Pollution**

-pollutant: harmful material, can enter biosphere

-sources: point and nonpoint

-point: from a single source (i.e. discharged pipe and smoke stacks)

-nonpoint: from many smaller sources (no definite place and time)

## 6.3 Biodiversity

-biodiversity: total of all the genetically based variation in all organisms in the biosphere

-types

-ecosystem diversity: variety of habitats, communities, and ecological processes in the biosphere

-species diversity: total of different species in a particular area

-genetic diversity: total of all different forms of genetic info carried by one particular species

**Calculating Biodiversity**

-richness: number of different species present

-a habitat with more species highest richness

-evenness: habitat has evenness if a habitat has similar abundance for each species present

-greatest evenness=when each species has similar abundance

Simpson’s Diversity Index

-considers richness and evenness

-higher D = greater biodiversity

-D max = 1 (infinite diversity)

-D min = 0 (no diversity, only 1 species found)

Shannon Diversity Index

**Valuing Biodiversity**

-Medical Value

-wild species are sources of many medicines (i.e. aspirin, penicillin)

-Agriculture Value

-wild plants carry genes we can use

-disease and pest resistance, other useful traits

-Ecosystem Value

-number and variety of species can influence ecosystem’s stability, productivity, and value to humans

**Threats to Biodiversity**

-Altered Habitats

-habitat fragmentation: process where development or agriculture splts ecosystems into pieces

-for habitats: habitats with larger size, more complete, clustered, with habitat corridor

better

-Hunting Wildlife

-can put species to extinction by hunting

-CITES (Conventnion on International Trade in Endangered Species) bans trade in endangered species

-Introduced Species

-alien species become invasive threaten biodiversity outcompete native species

-Pollution

-pollutants also threaten biodiversity

-Climate Change

-if conditions go beyond the organism’s tolerance, organism moves to a more suitable location or it goes extinct

-species in fragmented habitats are vulnerable (can’t migrate)

**Conserving Biodiversity**

-Captive Breeding

-animals bred and raised in protected surroundings

-goal: release them back into the wild

-Ecological Hot Spot

-place where significant numbers of species and habitats are in immediate danger of extinction

## 6.4 Meeting Ecological Challenges

**Ecological footprint**

-total area of functioning land & water ecosystems needed both to provide resources and produce wastes

-used to estimate biosphere’s K for humans

-Limitations

-complicated calculation, no universal accepted way

-only gave a snapshot of the situation at a particular time

-Comparing footprints

-useful for making comparisons among different populations

-calculate footprint of typical citizen then multiply it by population size
