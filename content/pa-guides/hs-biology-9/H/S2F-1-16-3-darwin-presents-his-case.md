-Jean-Baptiste Lamarck: ideas on the inheritance of acquired characteristics had been ridiculed by scientists, including some of Darwin’s own teachers.

-Alfred Russell Wallace: English naturalist who wrote an essay in Malaysia with ideas on evolution almost identical to Darwin’s, which was presented alongside Darwin’s observations at an 1858 scientific meeting

-Charles Darwin: worked out the main points of natural selection after reading Malthus, but did not publish for 20 years; after reviewing Wallace’s essay in 1858, he published On the Origin of Species in 1859

**Evolution by Natural Selection**

-In On the Origin of Species, he combined his own thoughts with ideas from Malthus and Lamarck.

- The Struggle of Existence

-if more individuals are produced than can survive, members of a population must compete to obtain food, living space, and other limited necessities of life

- Variation and Adaptation

-individuals in a population vary in heritable characteristics, and Darwin hypothesized that some of those variants are better suited to life in their environment than others

-Adaptation: any heritable characteristic that increases an organism’s ability to survive and reproduce in its environment

- Mimicry: an adaptation in which an organism copies or mimics a more dangerous organism (Batesian mimicry)
- Camouflage: an adaptation that allows an organism to blend into its background and avoid predation
- Behaviors
- Survival of the Fittest

-Fitness: how well an organism can survive and reproduce in its environment

-Survival of the Fittest: the difference in rates of survival and reproduction

-Survival (in evolutionary terms): reproducing and passing adaptations on to the next generation

- Natural Selection

-the process by which organisms with variations most suited to their local environment survive and leave more offspring

-occurs in situation in which more individuals are born than can survive(the struggle for existence), there is natural heritable variation(variation and adaptation), and their various fitness among individuals(survival of the fittest)

- Case Study: Peppered Moth (Biston betularia)

-environmental pressure: predation by birds

-variation: black and white moth; white moth are common before industrial revolution because they are camouflaged against white lichen on trees

-environmental change: during the industrial revolution, pollution from factories darken the tree trunks

-result: white moth decline due to predation; black moth is camouflaged by black soot, black moth increases

- Case Study: Antibiotic Resistance

-the resistance of bacterium to an antibiotic that was originally effective for treatment of infections caused by it

- In high number of bacteria, few are resistant to antibiotics
- Antibiotics kill bacteria causing the illness and good bacteria protecting the body from infection
- The resistant bacteria now have preferred conditions to grow and take over
- Bacteria can even transfer their drug resistance to other bacteria through lateral gene transfer, causing more problems

Natural Selection vs. Evolution

-often used interchangeably

-natural selection is a mechanism of evolution, organisms can evolve in other ways

**Common Descent**

-over many generations, adaptation could cause successful species to evolve into new species

-descent with modification: living species are descended, with modification, from common ancestors

-fossil records provide for evidence – intermediate fossils forms show evolution of modern species from ancestors

-all species--living and extinct--are descended from the same ancient common ancestor

## 16.4 Evidence of Evolution

**Biogeography**

-the study of where organisms live now and where they and their ancestors lived in the past

-patterns in the distribution of living and fossil tell how modern organisms evolved from their ancestors

**Geographic Distribution of Species**

- Closely Related but Different (adaptive radiation)

-one species evolved into several different forms that live in different ways

-adapt to different environments and share a common ancestor

- Distantly Related but similar (convergent evolution)

-unrelated organisms live in similar environment and come to resemble on another

-adapt to similar environment and don’t share a common ancestor

**Comparing Anatomy and Embryology**

- Homologous Structures

-structures that are shared by related species and that have been inherited from a common ancestor

-evidence of common ancestor

-different species have structure adapted for different function but these structures show many similarities

-similarities and differences among homologous structures help determine how recently species shared a common ancestor

- Analogous Structures

-body parts that share a common function, but not common structure

-don’t provide evidence of common ancestor

- Vestigial Structures

-structures that are inherited from ancestors, but have lost much of their original function due to different selection pressures on the descendant

-evidence of common ancestors

- Comparing Embryology

-similar patterns of embryological development provide further evidence that organisms have descended from a common ancestor

-e.g. all vertebrate embryos exhibit common structures: a tail, a notochord, and pharyngeal pouches

**The Age of Earth and Fossils**

- The Age of Earth

-evolution requires a very old Earth because it takes a long time; if life has evolved, then our planet must be billions of years old.

-radioactive dating shows Earth is about 4.5 billion years old, giving evolution by natural selection time to happen.

- Recent Fossil Finds

-fossils: preserved or mineralized remains or imprints of an organisms that lived long ago

-many recently discovered fossils form series that trace the evolution of modern species from extinct ancestors

**Genetics and Molecular Biology**

-all living organisms use the same universal genetic code

-similarities in DNA/RNA protein sequences provide the most powerful evidence of common ancestors

- Homologous genes help to determine the head to tail axis in embryonic development

-homologous hox genes are found in almost all multicellular animals

- Homologous proteins function in cellular respiration

-similar versions of cytochrome c are found in almost all living cells
