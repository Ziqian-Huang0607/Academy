Polarity and Hydrogen Bonds

- Water (H₂O) is a polar molecule: oxygen attracts shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen, so the oxygen has a partial negative charge (symbol δ−, delta minus), meaning that oxygen pulls the shared electrons closer to itself, while the hydrogen atoms have a partial positive charge (symbol δ+, delta plus). However, since they share electrons in a covalent bond, they don’t become ions.

- Oppositely charge particles attract to each other, so does the positive hydrogen atom of one water molecule that attracts to the negative

oxygen atom on a neighboring water molecule. This electromagnetic

force that attracts them between two separate molecules is known as a hydrogen bond.

- These two characteristics of water bring many special properties.

Cohesion, Adhesion, and Capillary Action

- Cohesion: attraction between molecules of the same substance. Water molecules cohere to each other because of each’s polarity which results in hydrogen bonds.
- Adhesion: attraction between water and a different substance, such as water sticking to the sides of a narrow tube. Caused by the partially positive or negative charge of one atom within a water molecule that attracts to an opposite charged particle on the different substance.
- E.g. Meniscus forms when adhesion between water and glass is stronger than cohesion between water molecules.

- Capillary action: water rises in narrow spaces because adhesion to the surface and cohesion between water molecules work together.

Heat Capacity of Water

- Compared with many other liquids, water has a high specific heat capacity and latent heat (the ‘c’ and ‘L’ you learned in 8th grade physics), which is the amount of energy needed to rise water temperature or change water state.

- Also caused by hydrogen bonds: they make water bound to each other, resisting to increase in speeding up of the molecules, and changing states require breaking these bonds

- This property moderates environmental temperature, especially in coastal areas, giving them a milder climate because of the difference in specific heat capacity between water and land.

### Solutions and Suspensions

- The positively charged hydrogen side attracts other particles, so water mixes readily with other substances, especially polar ones.
- A **solution** is a homogeneous mixture in which a **solute** dissolves in a **solvent**.
- A **suspension** contains particles dispersed in a fluid that do not dissolve completely.
- Blood is a suspension, with cells as the dispersed particles. With the cells and proteins removed, blood is a solution in which sodium, calcium and potassium are solutes and water is the solvent.

### pH and its Calculation

- The **pH** value measures acidity ("sourness"). Substances with higher acidity are **acids**; those with lower acidity are **bases**.
- Acidity is determined by the concentration of hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$) in water.
- Water dissociates into two ions, $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{OH}^-$.
- Two main equations:
  1. $[\text{H}^+][\text{OH}^-] = 10^{-14}$
  2. $\text{pH} = -\log[\text{H}^+]$
- The logarithm finds the exponent on base 10, e.g. $\log(10^{-14}) = -14$.
- Treat $[\text{H}^+]$ and $[\text{OH}^-]$ as two variables, like $x$ and $y$, that are inversely proportional. Their unit is molarity (M, mol/L).
