**Assigning Scientific Names**

- Common names can be confusing, as they vary among languages and in different places
- Eg. Buzzard refers to hawk in UK but refers to a vulture in US

**Dichotomous (single-access) Key**: tool used to identify organisms by making a series of choices between two contrasting characteristics

**Binomial Nomenclature:** two-word naming system in where each species is assigned a two-part scientific name

  - By Carolus Linnaeus, 1730s, Swedish
  - The first part (capital) is the genus the organisms belongs to, second part (lowercase) is unique to each species (Italics, underline in handwriting)
  - **Genus:** group of similar species

**Systematics**: science of naming and grouping organisms

  - goal of systematics: organize living things into groups called taxa that have biological meaning

**The Linnaean Classification System**

  - 7 hierarchical taxa: **Kingdom > phylum > class > order > family > genus > species**

**Problems with Traditional Classification:**

  - based on **morphology** (form and structure) similarity and difference
  - classifying organisms by visible similarities and differences seem simple, but how to determine which is the most important?
  - Only species in the 7 hierarchical taxa is a “natural” definition (since species is defined by whether organisms can mate each other and produce fertile offspring)

## 18.2 Modern Evolutionary Classification

**Evolutionary Classification**

**Evolution**: descent with modification

**Phylogeny**: evolutionary history of lineages

  - **Lineages**: evolutionary lines

**Phylogenetics systematics**: (aka evolutionary classification)

- goal: to group species into categories that reflect lines of evolutionary descent, rather than overall similarities and differences
- Organisms grouped based on how recently they share a common ancestor
- **Clade**: group of species that includes single common ancestor and all descendants
  - **Cladistics:** method of classifying species of organisms into clades
  - Many traditional taxonomic groups do form valid clades, but many don’t
    - Eg. Linnean class Reptilla excludes birds, so class Reptilla is not clade Reptilla.
- **Monophyletic group**: single common ancestor and all its descendants (eg. Clade)
- **Paraphyletic group**: common ancestor but excludes one or more group of descendants
    - invalid evolutionary classification

**Cladograms**

**Cladogram**: tree diagram that links groups of organisms by showing how lineages branched off from common ancestors

  - Bottom (root): common ancestor shared by all organisms on the cladogram
  - Branching points (nodes): denote speciation
  - Patterns indicate degrees of relatedness among organisms
  - **Ingroup**: group of organisms on cladogram that are being studied and share a more recent common ancestor with each other
  - **Outgroup**: organism or group that is closely related to the in-group but branched off earlier and is used as a reference point for comparison

**Derived character**: trait that arose in most recent common ancestor of particular lineage clade and was passed along its descendants

  - absence of a trait isn’t used as derived character, since distantly related groups of organisms can lose the same character

**Primital (ancestral) character**: character possessed by members of particular clade but that evolved before branching of clade defined

**DNA in Classification**

**Genetics**: primary basis for grouping organisms to clades and determining likely paths of evolutionary descent

- use of DNA in cladistics analysis has helped to make evolutionary trees more accurate
- Since all genes mutate over time, shared gene contains differences that can be treated as derived characters in cladistics analysis. So, similarities and differences in DNA can be used to develop hypotheses about evolutionary relationships.

**In general, more derived genetic characters two species share, more recently they shared common ancestor, and more closely they’re related in evolutionary terms.**

## 18.3 Building the Tree of Life

**Six Kingdoms: Animalia, Plantae, Fungi, “Protista”, Eubacteria, Archaebacteria**

**Cell Types: Prokaryote (**原核生物**), Eukaryote (**真核生物**)**

**Cell Number: Uni/Multicellular**

**Mode of Nutrition: Auto/Heterotroph**

  - **Autrotrophic modes of nutrition: photosynthesis/chemosynthesis**

**Oxygen use: Aerobic/Anaerobic**

**Cell Structure: Cell wall (peptidoglycan, cellulose, chitin) / chloroplast - chlorophyll**

- By 1990s, researchers realized eubacteria and archaebacteria in Monera where genetically and biochemically different, so, monera was separated into two.

**Domain:** larger, more inclusive category than a kingdom.

- **Bacteria, Archaea, Eukaryota**

**The Tree of All Life**

**The Tree of Life:** shows current hypothesis regarding evolutionary relationships among taxa within 3 domains of life

**Domain Archaea:** lives in extreme environments; may oblige to anaerobic conditions

**Domain Eukarya:** has a nucleus

  - **Animalia:** usually mobile, **Plantae**: usually nonmobile
  - **“Protista”:** paraphyletic, some display characteristics that resemble animals, plants, and fungi (since it’s paraphyletic, it is considered invalid evolutionary classification, thus it has “”)
