**Describing Populations**

-Population: a group of organisms of a single species that lives in a given area

1. Geographic Range

-the area inhabited by a population

-varies in size depending on the species

2. Density and Distribution

-Population Density: number of individuals per unit area

a.Random Quadrant Sampling

-counting numbers in a small, randomly located square within the total area of plants

-Population size=Mean number per quadrant x Total area / Area of each quadrant

-only for organisms not moving, like plants

b.Capture-Mark-Release-Recapture

-Population size=number of individuals captured and marked initially*number of individuals captured the second time/number of individuals marked in the second sample

-result higher than the true value

-for moving organisms like animals

-assumes no change in total population (No immigration/emigration/death/birth)

-Attention: Marks cause no harm to individuals, marks doesn’t fall off, marked individuals are released evenly

-Spatial Distribution: the pattern of spacing of a population

-Random: each has an independent position, no strong attractions or repulsion

-fish in the sea

-Uniform: evenly spaced social interactions (territoriality)

-King penguins

-Clumped: aggregate in patches due to resource availability and behavior

-Plants

3. Growth Rate

-determines whether the size of the population increases, decreases, or stays the same

-Zero growth rate: stays the same

-Positive: increase in size

-Negative: decrease in size

4. Age Structure

-number of males and females of each age in a population

-Reproduction ability: most species reproduce until reaching a certain age, refer to females

-Population pyramid: double-sided bar graph

**Population Growth**

-Natality (Birth Rate): number of births, expressed per 1000 population per year

-Mortality (Death Rate): number of deaths, expressed per 1000 population per year

-Immigration: process that individuals move into a population from elsewhere

-Emigration: process that individuals move out a population to elsewhere

-might be due to overcrowding, food shortage, or change in environment

Growth: N+I>M+E

Stability: N+I=M+E

Decline: N+I<M+E

**Exponential Growth**

-the larger a population, the faster it grows

-under ideal conditions with unlimited resources, a population will grow exponentially

-J-shaped growth curve: growth rate proportional to the size of the population

**Logistic Growth**

-S-shape curve: population’s growth slows, then stops. This follows a period of exponential growth

-All populations grow exponentially until some limiting factor slows the population’s growth

-limited by energy, water, oxygen, and nutrients available

-Carrying Capacity (K): the maximum number of individuals in a species that an environment can support for the long term

-a variety of factors stabilize the population at K

-E.g. limitation of energy, water, nutrients available etc.

Phase 1: Exponential Growth Phase (Fast growth)

-an organism moves into a new place

-few predators, less diseases (density-dependent factors don’t spread much with little population)

-plentiful resource, no competition

-high BR, low DR, high GR

Phase 2: Transitional Phase (Growth slows down)

-predators increase, disease outbreaks

-competitions for resource increase (intra+interspecific)

-BR decreases, DR increases, GR decreases, population still increase

Phase 3: Plateau Phase (Growth stops, dynamic equilibrium)

N+I=M+E

-Population growth is zero. Highest K

Limiting Factors (contribute to K):

-food and water limitations

-space for territories and nesting (overcrowded)

-availability of mates

-disease, predation

-environmental change, climate change

## 5.2 Limits to Growth

-Limiting Factor: factor that controls the growth of a population

-determine the K of an environment for a species

-keep natural populations from extinction and overrunning the planet

**Density-Dependent Limiting Factors**

-operates strongly only when population density reaches a certain level

-do not affect small, scattered populations as much

1. Competition

-individuals compete for food, water, space, sunlight, and other essentials

-lower BRs, increasing DRs (affected by ability to raise offspring/starvation)

-also among different species (interspecific) that are attempting to use similar or overlapping resources

2. Predator-Prey Relationship

-populations of predators and prey may cycle up and down over time

-a result of inter- and intra-specific competition

3. Herbivore Effects

-populations of herbivores and plants cycle up and down

-difference with Predator-prey relationship: prey moves and changes area, plants stay still

4. Parasitism and Disease

-parasites and disease-causing organisms feed at the expense of their hosts

-the denser the host population, the more easily parasites and disease can spread from one host to another

-Comparing with Predator-prey relationship: Both the prey and host are harmed. Preys are instantly killed, hosts would live longer to sustain life of the parasite

5. Stress from Overcrowding

-species fight among themselves if overcrowded, causing high levels of stress

-weaken the body’s ability to resist disease

-females neglect, kill, and eat their offspring

-lower BR, raise DR, increase emigration

**Density-Independent Limiting Factors**

-affect all populations in similar ways, regardless of population size and density or age

-climate change, natural disasters: hurricane, drought, wildfire, flood

-wipe out local species

-sometimes vary with population density by working with density-dependent factors

-e.g., natural disasters causing starvation by limiting food and resources

-human activities place ecological communities under stress

Limiting Factors:

-Top-down: pressures applied at higher trophic levels

-Bottom-up: involve resources at lower trophic levels

-keystone species exert top-down influence by preventing the lower levels from monopolizing

Introduced Species:

-Endemic (native) species: species native to an area

-Alien species: species not native, introduced by humans to a new area

-Invasive species: alien species that lack predators and out-compete native species

## 5.3 Human Population Growth

**Historical Overview**

Industrial Revolution

-food supply sufficient, goods shipped around the world

-improved nutrition, sanitation, medicine, and healthcare. Reduce DR

-BR remains high, population grows rapidly

Prediction of Malthus

-population will be regulated by competition (war), limiting resources (famine), and parasitism (disease)

**Patterns of Human Population Growth**

- Demography: scientific study of human populations

-examines characteristics of the human population and explains how populations change over time

-involves: BR, DR, human population size, density, distribution, movement (immigration, emigration), age structure

- Demographic Transition Model

Stage 1: Pre-Industrial

-High BR, High DR, GR=0

-advances in nutrition, sanitation, medicine

Stage 2: Transitional

-High BR, Decreasing DR

-Better medication, healthcare, nutrition

-Improved sanitation

-Increasing GR

Stage 3: Industrial

-Decreasing BR, Low DR

-More women receive college educations

-Women obtain more rights, job chances

-Late first marriage age

-Family planning

-Decreasing GR

Stage 4: Post-Industrial

-Low BR, Low DR, Almost 0 GR

Stage 5:?

-Negative GR?

-Aging problem in developed countries: a large portion of the population is concentrated in post-reproductive age

-Fewer people work for and contribute to the community

- Age Structure

-Number of males and females in each age group

-pre-reproductive: children

-reproductive: adults

-post-reproductive: elders

**Urban Sprawl and Cage Home**

-Urban Sprawl: Unlimited growth in many urban areas of housing, commercial development, roads over large expanses of land, with little concern for urban planning

-less open land->decrease percolation->decrease underground reservoirs->increase chance of flooding

-Fragmenting fish: Human activities divided once-connected rivers, leaving fish in poor-quality habitat

-wildlife habitats destroyed

-Cage Home: Type of residence with 1 bed surrounded by a metal cage

-safety problems

-unhygienic

-limited space->psychological problems
