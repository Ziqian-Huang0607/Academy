## 2.2 Properties of Water

- Polar Molecule
  - Oxygen is more electronegative than Hydrogen in a water molecule (More likely to find shared electrons close to the oxygen atom)
  - Uneven distribution of electrons makes the molecule polar
  - Negatively-charged Oxygen atoms attract Positively-charged Hydrogen atoms (Hydrogen bond between water molecules)
  - Ice is less dense than water
    - Allows ice to float, insulating aquatic ecosystems below
- Cohesion and Adhesion
  - Cohesion - Attraction between molecules of the same substance
    - Also known as surface tension
    - Results from hydrogen bonds
  - Adhesion - Attraction between molecules of different substances
    - Meniscus - Adhesion between water and glass is stronger than the cohesion of the water itself, forming a U-shaped curve of the water surface
    - Capillary action - Adhesion between water and glass causes it to rise in narrow tubes against gravity
      - Plants draw water into xylem in stem and leaf
      - Cohesion holds water together as it rises upwards
- High Heat Capacity
  - Heat capacity - the amount of heat energy required to raise a substances temperature
  - Large amounts of energy are needed to make water’s many hydrogen bonds move and raise its temperature
  - Environments containing large amounts of water have stable temperatures
    - i.e.: Rainforests, lakes, and oceans
- Universal Solvent
  - Water’s polarity allows it to dissolves ionic and polar compounds
  - i.e. Dissolving table salt in water
    - Table salt - Solute
    - Water - Solvent
    - Salt water - Solution
  - Suspensions - when materials do not dissolve but seperate into small pieces so small that they do not settle out
    - Water can form suspensions, like blood (includes cells and undissolved particles)

**The pH Scale**

- pH - measures the concentration of H+ ions in a solution
- On a scale from 0 - 14
  - pH of 7
    - H+ and OH- ions are equal
    - Water has a pH of 7
  - pH > 7: Basic
    - More OH- than H+
    - Higher pH, more basic
  - pH < 7: Acidic
    - More H+ than OH-
    - Lower pH, more acidic
- Calculating pH: For any aqueous solution at 25 degrees Celsius, the product of the concentration of H+ and OH- is constant: H+ x OH-=10^-14
  - pH=-log[H+]
