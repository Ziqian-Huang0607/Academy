**Fossils and Ancient Life**

-fossils: the preserved remains/traces of ancient life

-from fossil record, paleontologists learn about the structure of ancient organisms, their environment, and the ways in which they lived

-specific conditions are needed for fossilization

-only tiny % of living things become fossils

Types of Fossils

- Petrified Fossils: minerals replace all/part of an organism
- Trace Fossils: evidence of the activities of ancient organisms, such as footprint burrow
- Mold: hollow area in sediment in the shape of an organism/part of an organisms
- Cast: copy of the shape of an organism

-other fossils form when the remains of organisms are preserved in substances such as tar, amber, or ice

-amber preserved fossils: organisms that become trapped in tree resin that hardens after the tree is buried

-preserved remains form when an entire organism becomes encased in materials such as ice

Fossils in Sedimentary Rock

**Dating Earth’s History**

- Relative Dating

-places rock layers and their fossils into a temporal sequence

-allows paleontologists to determine whether a fossil is older or younger than other fossils

-Index fossils: distinctive fossils used to establish and compare the relative ages of rock layers and the fossil they contain

-existed only in a few layers (during specific spans of time)

-occured in large geographic areas

-include trilobites

- Radiometric Dating

-relies on radioactive isotopes, which decay, or breakdown, into stable isotopes at a steady rate

-compares the amount of radioactive to nonradioactive isotopes in a sample to determine its age

-half life: time required for half of radioactive atoms in a sample to decay

-Carbon-14: 5730 years

**Geologic Time Scale**

-timeline of Earth’s history

-based on both relative and absolute dating

**Life on a Changing Planet**

- Physical Forces

-building mountains, opening coastlines, changing climates, and geological forces have altered habitats of living organisms repeatedly through Earth history

-the theory of a plate tectonic explains how solid continental plates move slowly above Earth’s molten core--continental drift

-forces from space have even altered Earth’s physical environment

-strong evidence: comets and meteors have crashed into Earth many times in the past

- Biological Forces

-first photosynthesis organisms began absorbing carbon dioxide and releasing large amounts of oxygen

-actions of living organisms over time have changed conditions in the land, water, and atmosphere of Earth

## 19.2 Pattern and Process of Evolution

**Speciation and Extinction**

-macroevolution: grand transformation in anatomy, phylogeny, ecology, and behavior, which usually take place in clades larger than a single species

-speciation of larger clades

-extinction of larger clades

-microevolution: the change in allele frequency that occurs over time within a population

- Macroevolution and Cladistics

-fossils are classified using the cladistic techniques, based on shared derived characteristics, that are used to classify living species

-in some cases, fossils are placed in clades that contain only extinct organisms; in other cases, fossils are classified into clades that include living organisms

- Adaptation and Extinction

-if rate of speciation in a clade >= rate of extinction, the clade continue to exist

-if rate of extinction in a clade >= rate of speciation, the clade eventually become extinct

- Patterns of Extinction

-background extinction

-extinct because of the slow but steady process of natural selection

-mass extinction

-extinct over relatively short period of time

-entire ecosystem vanish

-whole food web collapse

-natural selection can’t compensate quickly

-factors include volcanic eruptions, moving continents, changing sea levels

**Rate of Evolution**

-evidence shows that evolution has often proceeded at different rates for different organisms at different times over the long history of life on Earth

- Gradualism

-Darwin

-evolution occured by a long sequence of continuous change

-species slowly change through a series of intermediate forms

-lack of intermediates--incomplete fossil record

- Punctuated Equilibrium

-Stephen J Gould and Niles Eidrege

-long periods of relative stability in a species are punctuated by periods of rapid evolution

-no long sequence of intermediate forms

**Adaptive Radiation and Convergent Evolution**

- Adaptive Radiation

-single species evolves over a relatively short time into several different forms that live in different ways

-species display variations on the ancestral body plan

-occupy different ecological niches

- Convergent Evolution

-groups of organisms evolve in different places at different times, but similar environments

-organisms face similar selection pressure

-produces similar structures and characteristics

-emus, rheas, ostriches

**Coevolution**

-two species evolve in response to changes in each other over time

- flowers and pollinators:
- orchid with long spur and moth with long feeding tube
- plants and herbivorous insects:

-plant evolve bad tasting or poisonous compounds

-insects evolved variants that could alter, inactive, or eliminate poisons
