Elements of Graphic Novels

- A graphic novel is a medium for expressing ideas through sequential images, often supported by words, captions, symbols, and layout choices.
- **Elements of Comics**
  - Frame, panel, gutter, sound representation, speech & dialogue representation
  - Onomatopoeia, frame/panel size, dialogue placement, caption style, and panel transitions all affect pacing and meaning.
    - How do these relate to overall theme and ideas of the novel?
  - Gutter: The blank space between panels where readers infer closure, motion, and the passage of time.

○ Frame: The border that contains a panel; a stable frame can suggest control/order, while a broken or irregular frame can suggest chaos, pressure, or psychological instability.

○ Caption: A box of narration that can explain setting, time, inner thought, or background information. Captions often guide interpretation more directly than images alone.

○ Speech bubble: Shows spoken dialogue; its shape, size, and placement can reveal volume, emotion, interruption, or social tension.

○ Thought bubble / inner narration: Shows private mental experience. In analysis, connect it to identity, fear, isolation, or conflict between public appearance and inner feeling.

○ Sound representation / onomatopoeia: Words such as “bang,” “crash,” or “ahh” make sound visual and can intensify violence, shock, comedy, or physical discomfort.

○ Panel size: A large panel slows the reader down and gives importance to a moment; a sequence of small panels can speed up action or show fragmentation.

○ Close-up: Focuses attention on facial expression, body detail, or an object, often making emotion more intense and unavoidable.

○ Long shot: Shows a character in relation to the setting and can emphasize loneliness, smallness, vulnerability, or social distance.

○ High angle / low angle: A high angle can make a figure look weak or trapped; a low angle can make a figure look powerful, frightening, or overwhelming.

○ Contrast / black-and-white style: Strong contrast can create harsh mood, moral tension, fear, or emotional coldness.

  - Panel: A distinct visual unit; one panel usually captures one moment, perspective, or meaningful beat.
    - Shows movement
  - Gutter: Tell story in a more condensed way, formed by reader's imagination.
    - Shows motion

**Metamorphosis**

Gregor Samsa, a traveling salesman who supports his family, wakes up transformed into a giant insect. He worries about missing work, not his bizarre new body. His horrified family locks him away. His sister Grete is kind at first but then grows resentful. Unable to work, Gregor becomes a burden. One night he crawls out to hear her play violin, frightening their boarders. Grete declares they must get rid of "it." Heartbroken, Gregor dies. His family feels relieved and moves on as if he never existed.

- Metamorphosis = transformation/change, both physically and psychologically.
  - Franz Kafka: a Prague-born German-language writer who studied law and worked in the insurance industry; his writing often explores alienation, bureaucracy, guilt, and dehumanization.

○ Key idea: the most disturbing part of the story is not only Gregor becoming an insect, but how quickly the family, workplace, and social system treat him as useless once he can no longer work.

○ Gregor’s body changes first, but the family’s moral attitude changes gradually: pity becomes annoyance, annoyance becomes resentment, and resentment becomes rejection.

○ The story criticizes conditional love: Gregor is valued when he earns money, but once he becomes dependent, his humanity is no longer recognized.

○ Important theme - dehumanization: Gregor is still mentally human, but others increasingly describe and treat him as an object, pest, or burden.

○ Important theme - economic pressure: the family’s financial dependence on Gregor turns family relationships into transactions, making affection unstable.

○ Important theme - alienation: Gregor is alienated from his job, his body, his room, his family, and finally from his own identity.

○ Important symbol - the room: his room begins as a private space but becomes a prison, storage space, and visual sign of his separation from the household.

○ Important symbol - the violin: Grete’s music briefly reconnects Gregor to beauty and humanity, but his attempt to approach it only confirms that the family sees him as monstrous.

○ Exam reminder: avoid summarizing the plot only. Always connect Kafka’s choices to a larger meaning, such as alienation, family duty, economic value, or loss of humanity.

    - Represents social and economic systems that structure human relations
    - Physical transformation of Gregor mirrors mental transformation of his family - barely tolerating him to attacking him
    - Describes parasitic social relationships created by institutions such as marriage and family

| Loneliness | The emotional feeling of sadness, disconnection, or lack of companionship. It is internal and psychological. | Ex. Gregor experiences loneliness when he is locked in his room and hears life continuing outside without him. |
|---|---|---|
| Isolation | The physical state of being separated or cut off from others, either by force or by choice. It is external and spatial. | Ex. Gregor is physically isolated because his family locks him in his room and controls when he can be seen. |
| Alienation | The loss of belonging, identity, or recognition; a person feels like a stranger to others or even to himself. | Ex. Gregor is no longer treated as a brother/son, but as a burden or object to be removed. |
| Dehumanization | The process of denying a person’s full human identity, value, or dignity. | Ex. Gregor’s family gradually refers to him as “it,” focusing on his usefulness instead of his inner humanity. |
| Conditional love | Affection that depends on usefulness, obedience, productivity, or benefit. | Ex. The family tolerates Gregor while he supports them, but rejects him after he can no longer work. |

  - Stark style: Kafka often uses plain, matter-of-fact language to describe absurd events, making the horror feel ordinary and emotionally cold.
