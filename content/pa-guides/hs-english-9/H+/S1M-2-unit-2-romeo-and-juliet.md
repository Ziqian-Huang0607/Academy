**Sonnets**

- Have 14 lines, iambic pentameter, and a volta (the shift)
- To find the volta…
  - Check the structure- usually between line 12 & couplet for Shakespearean sonnets + after 8th line for Petrarchan sonnets
  - Spot the signal words (eg. but, yet, however, then, now, so, or, therefore)
  - Look at punctuation- a major shift is often marked by a period, semicolon, dash, or stanza break
  - Ask key questions- Did the poem change its… Topic? Time? Tone? Audience?
- Shakespearean sonnets: 3 quatrains (12 lines) + rhyming couplet (2 lines)

**Literary** **Devices**

- Irony: when there’s a contrast between what is expected & what happens, often for emphasis or humor
  - Dramatic Irony: when the audience knows something the characters don’t:
    - Eg. The audience knows Juliet isn’t dead, but Romeo doesn’t and, tragically, kills himself.
  - Situational Irony: when what happens is the opposite of what was expected to happen:
    - Eg. A fire station burning down.
  - Verbal Irony: when someone says something while meaning another:
    - Eg. Juliet claiming she can’t wait to marry Paris, but the audience knows she despises the thought.
- Foil: a character who contrasts with another character to highlight main trait(s); eg. Romeo and Mercutio, lovesick vs cynical
- Allusion: lit. device where a write makes an indirect reference to another story or situation
- Hyperbole: an exaggeration used for emphasis or effect
- Simile: a comparison between two different things using “like” or “as”
  - Eg. Juliet is as pure as a saint.
- Metaphor: a comparison between two different things without using “like” or “as” eg. Time is a thief.
- Imagery: Descriptive language that creates a mental picture through use of the five senses eg. The Underworld was a pit of burning fires.
- Anaphora: A rhetorical device where a word is repeated at the beginning of a line/clause for emphasis or creative appeal

**Relevant Texts**

- The story of Pyramus and Thisbe by Ovid was Shakespeare’s inspiration/base for Romeo and Juliet…
  - It tells the tale of two lovers in ancient Babylon who are forbidden to be together (familial loyalties and duties), so they communication through a crack in the wall between their homes
  - When Pyramus sees a bloody lioness, he thinks Thisbe is dead and kills himself; after finding out, Thisbe takes her own life.
  - Pyramus is characterized as devoted and rash; Thisbe is characterized as reactive, determined, & impulsive (similar to R&J)
  - Theme(s): Impulsivity takes root in naïve love, so think before you act. Being overly indulged on love will blind you from reality.
- “A Dream Within a Dream” by Edgar Allan Poe…
  - This poem contrasts sharply with Mercutio’s **Queen Mab** speech
  - “O God! Can I not grasp/Them with a tighter clasp?/O God! can I not save/One from the pitiless wave?” (Poe, 19-22).
    - The speaker clings to reality (or the escape from reality— dreams): helpless, desperate, in denial, sorrowful
  - Mercutio believes dreams should not be taken seriously and distort one’s sense of reality; Poe questions that dream and reality are interconnected (as reality itself is an illusion that is slipping away).

**Romeo and Juliet: Act 1** **Key Moments**

- 1.1: Duel sets up family feud and its immature nature
- 1.2: Paris as a foil to Romeo: Paris is traditional in asking for marriage and is encouraged by the Capulets, while Romeo pursues Juliet in a forbidden manner
- 1.3: Establishes Juliet’s role in the family is to be married----women and girls as trading tokens for connections, and heirs.
- 1.4: **Mercutio’s Queen Mab speech:**
  - Foil: characterizes Mercutio as cynical and mocking of love, while Romeo is poetic, idealistic, and a hopeless romantic
  - Romeo fears Mercutio speaks the truth (of romance being nothing but a vain fantasy/dream) but is stubborn to accept the harsh reality of love.
  - Queen Mab: idea of Celtic mythology, a field of interest in Elizabethan era (when the play was written) framed as a midwife fairy who gives birth to dreams
  - Shakespeare calls out idealists and cautions them to be more realistic and wary in their desires
- 1.5: Tybalt shows murderous tendencies towards Romeo; for the first time, Romeo sees Juliet (quickly forgets Rosaline) and they meet
  - **Tybalt** is characterized as hyper-aggressive and macho; character foil with Benvolio— the voice of **reason** vs immediate **aggression**
    - Shakespeare criticizes the idea of being toxic masculine aggression
  - Romeo utilizes **light imagery** **(contrasts between light and dark)** to describe Juliet to underline how she is celestial, pure, innocent, ethereal, holy— akin to a goddess, angel, pearl; comparison to jewel to assert how she is precious, rare, valuable, and stands out among the ‘dark’ crowd
    - Romeo seems to have pure physical attraction to Juliet at first
  - Romeo is a pilgrim while Juliet is a saint (**extended metaphor** **on unrealistic and delusional expectations**)

**Romeo and Juliet: Act 2 Key Moments**

- 2.1: Mercutio compares Romeo to King Cophetua (a king who sees a beggar maid, is smitten, and immediately proposes)
  - Concern for Romeo’s impulsivity and rashness in love
- 2.2: **The Balcony Scene**
  - Balcony mirrors/parallel the physical distance between them to the figurative barrier (family feud and society)
    - Archetype: star-crossed lovers
  - Juliet is described as Romeo’s sun— his source of brightness, center of his world, and life-giving (foreshadowing)
  - Meeting in the dark is a symbol for forbidden love in hiding, as they can only express their true emotions in the dark.
- 2.3: Friar Lawrence as a ‘father figure’ towards Romeo, says love is a potent force if not taken in moderation (using medical and herbal analogies) and warns Romeo against his fickle love.
  - Feels that this alliance may fix the family feud
- 2.4: Mercutio depicts love as violent/cutting/destructive through imagery and diction; he also establishes Tybalt’s skill as a swordsman
  - The Nurse comes to visit Romeo and expresses that despite endorsing this marriage for Juliet’s happiness
  - Nurse and Friar are a source of guidance for the lovers
- 2.5: Juliet describes love with youthful qualities— it’s swift, energetic, fleeting, and vigorous; she is impulsive, dramatic, and desperate for love
  - Describes the risks of naïve and youthful love---are these qualities leading to their eventual downfall?
- 2.6: Friar marries them despite feeling it may have a tragic outcome; Romeo is hubristic in his love and tempts fate (foreshadowing)
  - “Then love-devouring death do what he dare, it is enough I may but call her mine.”

**Romeo and Juliet: Act 3** **Key Moments**

- **3.1**: **Mercutio’s death and Tybalt’s death**
  - Mercutio is set as a comedic relief (jokester archetype), so his death signals the end of the comedic genre tragedy
  - HONOR and TOXIC MASCULINITY:
    - Tybalt challenges Romeo because he feels his honor has been impugned at the Capulet party.
    - Mercutio sees Romeo’s submission as a threat to his honor as submission is viewed as weak, and duels with Tybalt.
    - Romeo tries to break up the fight, but Mercutio is pierced by Tybalt under Romeo’s arm
    - Then Romeo’s love for Mercutio as well as guilt and need to repent his honor drives his impulse to kill Tybalt
    - He compares being a man to being steel: aggressive, merciless, fearless, unyielding, emotionless and stoic.
      - Expresses that his love for Juliet has made him soft---is he blaming Juliet for his ‘failure’ and does he value their relationship by killing her cousin?
- **3.2-5**
  - Juliet finds out about Romeo’s killing of Tybalt: complex emotions depicted through use of oxymorons; can’t reconcile Romeo’s angelic appearance with his devilish actions, feels betrayed
  - Friar Lawrence monologues about rigid/sexist gender roles, says men must be stoic, hard, unfeeling, and cold; woman are overtly emotional, akin to beasts- instinctual and primal
  - Capulet is abusive and aggressive because he feels his **honor/authority** was challenged by Juliet’s refusal; doesn’t care about Juliet’s consent to marrying Paris anymore (unlike in 1.2); and wants to slap, disown, and deny her existence.

**Romeo and Juliet: Act 4 Key Moments**

- **4.1: Planning for the “death”**
  - Paris meets with Friar Lawrence and Juliet to arrange the marriage on ‘next Thursday’ but Juliet is internally unwilling.
  - **J**uliet laments to Friar in private about her marriage and describes herself dying tortuously for Romeo
    - Foreshadowing for their tragic deaths and impulsive actions---is Juliet in need of comfort and guidance?
  - Friar Lawrence gives Juliet poison and a plan to elope with Romeo and Juliet is headstrong despite the risks
    - Common streak of stubbornness seen in Capulets
    - Foreshadowing as short-sightedness in the plan leeds to terrible unforeseen consequences
- 4.2
  - Juliet pretends to agree to the marriage and earns Lord Capulet’s favor again
    - A woman’s worth is tied to marriage and motherhood
- **4.3: Juliet’s “Death”**
  - Juliet drinks the poison according to Friar’s plan although scared of the risks.
    - Juliet shows her naïve and impulsive side that leads to her ultimate side
    - Her fear shows her lack of guidance and innocence, are the adults responsible for her fate?
- **4.4-5**
  - The Capulets are overjoyed for the wedding but are devastated and distraught when Juliet ‘dies’
    - Lord Capulet may have felt guilt for her death; their devastation shows a parental connection to Juliet----is she selfish for troubling her parents?
  - Paris arrives on the scene and is more disappointed with not marrying Juliet than her actual ‘death’
    - Objectification of women as mere items for men and building connections.
    - Is Juliet more mature than Romeo since she is willing to ‘die’ and take risks for their relationship?

Extra: Essay Writing Tips

**In exams…**

- Useful tip: check the topic for the essay **as soon as you can**, then brainstorm while writing the rest of the exam
- Make sure you know the number of body paragraphs that are required and read the prompt **carefully**
- **Plan your essay:** even if its just 2-5 bullet points, planning your essay can reduce repetition and help organize your thoughts, using mind-maps would be very effective in the planning space
- Some people may prefer to write the essay first as the former sections would prob be quicker to finish

**In general…**

- Thesis statements:
  - Focus on literary devices/elements: consider POV, imagery, simile, tone, diction (word choice), metaphor, irony, juxtapositionetc.
  - You can use this template: In [Text], [Author] presents [Literary Element(s)] in order to [Author's Purpose], ultimately illustrating that [Thematic Statement].
  - Make it arguable and specific---MAKE SURE EACH POINT CAN BE CLEARLY UNDERSTOOD AT FIRST GLANCE
  - Best to reread it from a fresh perspective after finishing your essay for double-checking
- Topic sentences:
  - Not simply restating the thesis, add a deeper level of analysis please connect to the authors purpose—emphasize, emphasize, emphasize.
  - Think about the claim (from the thesis) and why (it matters), not just the how (author’s method)
  - You can use this template: “The author uses [device] not just to show [what], but to argue that [why].” Or “the author uses [device] to [authors purpose] thus [theme]”
- Be assertive by using powerful diction such as: asserts, affirms, champions, argues, reveals, criticizes, critiques, challenges, condemns, advocates, underscores (Check BB resources!)
