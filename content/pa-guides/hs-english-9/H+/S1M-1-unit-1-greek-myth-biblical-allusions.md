**Introduction to Mythology**

- Why do we write stories?
- Metaphysical: to explain the **why** (society’s spiritual systems)
- Cosmological: to explain the **how** (natual phenomenons)
- Sociological: to teach people about social structures/taboos
- Pedagogical: to act as a guide to life
- The Hero’s Journey (Monomyth)
- 1) Separation-Hero leaves 2) Initiation-Hero changes 3) Hero returns
- Heroes often represent the values/flaws of their culture; heroes are reactive while villains are proactive.
- Eg. Batman is a wealthy capitalist, Superman represents traditional American values (justice, truth, moral clarity)

**Archetypes**

- Definition: a very typical example of a certain person/thing
- IMPORTANT Situational Archetypes:
- The Journey: the hero journeys afar for a necessary cause to restore justice and/or harmony to the community; they usually encounter trials and obstacles both mentally and physically
- The Initiation: the call of action for the hero
- The Fall: descent in action from a higher to lower state of being and nature
- Death and Rebirth: self-explanatory, someone dies but is then followed by some sigh of birth/rebirth (eg. Jesus Christ’s resurrection)
- Good vs Evil: usually the good side triumphs (internal and external)
- The Magic Weapon: an exceptional weapon the hero can use for the journey/prove their worth/fight evil (eg. Orpheus’ lyre)
- IMPORTANT Character Archetypes:
  - The Hero (eg. Harry Potter)
  - Mentors (eg. Dumbledore to Harry)
  - The Devil Figure (eg. the snake in the story of Adam and Eve)
  - The Trickster (eg. Bugs Bunny)
  - The Outcast (eg. Prometheus)
  - Hunting Group of Companions (eg. Ron and Hermione)
  - The Star-Crossed Lovers: love fated to end in tragedy due to disapproval of gods, society, friends, or family (eg. Romeo and Juliet)
  - FEMALE ONES WILL BE SEPERATELY DISCUSSED

**Feminist** **Lens: Women in Mythology**

- Feminism: gender equality; patriarchy: reform on gender divide and oppression
- Archetypes… are flat, largely focused on beauty & sidelines women, yet they’re ingrained in our collective psyche:
  - The Earth Mother (eg. Mother Teresa)
  - The Temptress (eg. Medusa)
  - The Platonic Ideal (eg. Hermione)
  - The Unfaithful Wife: woman married to a man she views as dull, therefore attracted to a more interesting/handsome man
  - The Damsel in Distress (eg. Eurydice)
- God: active, more negative traits, educated and powerful(eg. Apollo, Zeus)
- Goddess:usually passive, romance-related, shallow (eg. Aphrodite)
- Semonides in “Types of Women” creates a bestiary (catalog of beasts), associating women to pigs, dogs, horses, etc.
- Monsters are monsters because they defy social norms and embody social fears/anxieties, for example:
  - Godzilla: toxic/nuclear devastation post WW2 Japan
  - Frankenstein: fear of science going beyond its limit
  - Dracula: fear of immigrants & the flouting of cultural hegemony through interracial relationships; metaphor for intercourse- anxieties of sex disease and female sexuality: (during Industrial Revolution- consider “London” by William Blake and its negativity towards the gov.& ppl)
  - Witches: fear of women being smart and/or single— not controllable by men— broomstick used to challenge patriarchy

**The Bible**

- Theism: belief in the existence of deity(s) and the divine (polytheism- gods; monotheism- a singular god)
- Theology: study of religious belief and context
  - Theologian: expert or a person who engages in theology
- God is… Omnipotent (all powerful), Omnipresent (in all places), Omniscient (all seeing), Omnibenevolent (kind to all)
- The Holy Trinity:
- The Old Testament depicts God as angry and wrathful
- The New Testament depicts God as forgiving and loving
- The story of **Adam and Eve** is from the Old Testament
  - Used to justify suffering, work, the menstrual cycle, childbirth, shorter lives, war, patriarchy
  - Theme(s): Deception is not an excuse, God’s power/word is absolute, harsh punishments are deserved for disobedience
  - Symbol(s): Apple- forbidden fruit, sexual promiscuity, knowledge, temptation; Fish- secret symbol signifying one is Christian
- Atonement: Jesus Christ died for our sins
- The Satan: the enemy, not a specific person (in the Old Testament); some see Satan as a fallen angel, a contrast to God
- **Pilgrim:** a person who journeys to a sacred place for religious reasons
