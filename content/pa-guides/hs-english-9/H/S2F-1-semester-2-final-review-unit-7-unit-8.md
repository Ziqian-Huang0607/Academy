**Logical** **Fallacies**

Logical fallacies are faults or common errors in reasoning that undermine the logic of an argument.

**Common** **Types** **of** **Logical** **Fallacies**

Hasty Generalization: Jumping to a broad conclusion based on a few, unrepresentative examples

Slippery Slope: An argument claiming a small initial action will inevitably lead to a chain of extreme consequences without sufficient proof

Circular Argument: Restating the original claim in reasoning instead of proving it

Straw Man: Misrepresenting an opponent’s argument to make it easier to attack and refute

Begging the Claim: Assuming the truth of what still hasn’t been proven in the argument

Red Herring: Introducing an irrelevant topic to divert attention away from main issue

Non-sequitur: A conclusion that logically does not follow from the preceding claim

Ad Hominem: Directly attacking a person’s traits or characters instead of addressing their argument

**“A** **Raisin** **in** **the** **Sun”**

**Context**

-Chicago, 1950s

-The “Black Belt of the City”

-The hard-working Younger family living in a cramped tenement apartment

-Family awaits the life insurance payment following the passing of Big Walter (father of the household)

**Lorraine** **Hansberry**

-First black woman playwright to have a play performed on Broadway

-Her black family moved into an all-white community (Washington Park) — mirroring the events in this play

-Friends with Langston Hughes, borrowed this play’s title from his poem “ Harlem (Dream Deferred)”

**Into** **the** **Stage** **Play**

-The Younger Family hopes to move to a nicer neighborhood and fulfill individual dreams, yet their aspirations get repeatedly held back and delayed by systemic racial prejudice or social discrimination across the US

**Walter Lee Younger:**

-It was a patriarchal society, but the fact of being in a matriarchal family deeply annoyed

Walter — the drama reveals the conflict between him and Mama as two powers who compete to take charge of the family household

-Walter desires to become rich quickly and devises plans to acquire wealth with his friends (by building up his liquor store)

-Is evident that the liquor store startup will not be out of his personal interests, but rather based purely off the potential gains that may come along with a successful business

**Lena “Mama” Younger:**

-Her plant pot symbolizes hope and resilience

-She initially imposes her own values on her children (Christianity etc.)

-Later on, her heroism as a loving parent is her ability to see the harmfulness of her authority as her children get older

**Ruth:**

-She is the housewife, does all the chores for the family

-She has no hope for the future, and decides to accept the reality **Beneatha**:

-She is a more complicated character, and she focuses on discovering her true identity

Please note that PA materials are student-created and for reference only.

-She has been dating with George Murchsion and Asagai, two very different people

-George Murchison: rich and upper class black citizen, assimilates into white culture, and

only wants a simple-minded yet sophisticated wife (sophisticated here meaning that his wife is able to conform to white elitist standards, and acts in a polished manner that is compliant to George’s social status)

-Asagai: He is not rich,but neither is he poor ( is able to afford plane tickets from Africa to the US and for education). He focuses on discovering true black identity and connecting to

African roots.

**Mrs. Johnson:**

-The Younger family’s neighbor

-Her intent is to clearly belittle the importance and success of the Younger family’s opportunity to move to a new apartment (does this out of jealousy)

-Also known as a “comic relief” ; her appearance onto the stage is to lighten the tense atmosphere while also to subtly highlight racial fears in society

**The Idea** **of “Assimilation”** **:**

-Process in which a minority group develops the same values, behaviors, and beliefs as the dominant culture

-Beneatha is deeply inspired by Asagai’s character, and her straightened hair shows that she has been trying to hide her true African identity. Her efforts to reject cultural assimilation and embrace African culture through dance, dress, and music indicate the double-consciousness of an African-American.

**African American Vernacular English (AAVE):**

-Variety of American English spoken by urban working/middle class black people

-Originated as a result of forcing black people (especially slaves) into learning English but without providing any proper education

**The Act of** **Lynching:**

-When somebody accused of a crime and killed without the benefit of a full and fair trial

-Enforced white supremacy and maintained order while intimidating black people

**Core** **Themes**

-The American Dream: pursuit of individual dreams amid racial and financial hardships

-Racial Discrimination: Prejudice and segregation facing black families

-Family: Generational conflicts, unity versus individualism, opportunity costs and trade offs between decisions

-Identity: Double-consciousness, remain true to black culture or conform to dominant white society?

-Dignity and Pride: Defending self-respect against oppression (Walter’s excessive pride and stubbornness drive his risky investments and poor choices, leading to more troubles for the family)

**Vocabulary** **&** **Grammar**

**Vocabulary (Some easy vocab is excluded)**

Negritude: A consciousness of pride in one’s African identity and heritage

Cultural Assimilation: When a minority conforms to the expectations, behaviors, and culture of a majority

Marginalization: The treatment of a group of people or a concept as less important or peripheral

Apathy: A lack of caring or feeling, generally

Empathy: Caring for others as if they were you yourself; “putting yourself in another’s shoes”

Jim Crow: Jim Crow Laws were laws designed to segregate and marginalize African Americans in post-slavery America

Hegemony: Total mastery of one group, person, or idea over another

Segregation: The action or state of separating one people to another

Vernacular: The language or dialect spoken by common people
