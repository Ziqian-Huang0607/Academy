**Horror**

A genre in which the author introduces elements that serves to inspire **a sense of terror, extreme disgust, surprise, or unfathomable** to the reader.

**Gothic horror is a subgenre**, it is a combination of horror and romantic elements. It also includes a **satisfying ending**, which does not necessarily mean a “happily ever after”, more of **an** **ending** **where** **the mystery** **introduced in the story** **is solved** and the reader is left with a release of tension. Horror and mystery is displayed without any cliffhangers.

Gothic horror also has a unique atmosphere. It often takes place in stormy nights and large and/or desolated structures (Mansions, castles and large abandoned buildings are common) along with isolation from civilization.

**Key** **Horror** **elements include:**

- Blackout: not necessarily total darkness, but a general limitation of light. Gothic settings are rarely set in a sunny day in the middle of summer.
- Secrets: there is a mystery that is obvious to the reader, but a key component missing prevents readers from fully understanding the story and the horror yet.

**Key Gothic elements include:**

- Doubles, repetition (related to Freud)
- Ghosts or monsters
- Uncanny: familiarity that is unsettling and troubling. Often portrayed through doppelgangers (apparition or double of a living person)
- An imaginary character who represents the morally abhorrent, evil side of human nature

**Historical Context**

Gothic horror is often linked to the **Victorian era**, because protestants often linked the trope of old castles and buildings to the **oppressive rich class that held power**, and made it inspire fear.

**Victorian Era Characteristics**

- **Moral values:** this often refers to **the concept of evil and good, justice and injustice,** as well as social norms such as **mannerism**.
- They had a strict belief that **if one is to do wrong and commit sins, consequences will follow** no matter what. This is reflected in **Victorian literary works:** all characters that are portrayed as evil, (breaking social conduct/trespassing moral values) no matter if they are the protagonist or antagonist, they will face consequences.
- **Social hierarchy:** there is a **clear division of social classes** in the Victorian era, mostly through **wealth and** **family** **legacy**: wealth was passed down through generations of a family. The **rich remained rich,** **while** **the poor remained poor,** for **little to no chance of** **climbing up the hierarchy.** This is shown (and criticized) heavily through Victorian literature, as **characters** **are** **treated** **differently depending on their social classes.**
- **Reputation**: what others think of you deem who you are in society.

**Id and Freud’s Theory of Personality**

**Id,** **ego,** **and superego** **are** **three aspects that make up someone’s personality.**

**Superego** is the aspect that **sets moral standards,** the “angel” side in other words. It sets expectations for our actions based on our upbringing, so **this moral standard will differ between different societies.** An example would be Friar Lawrence in Romeo and Juliet.

**Id** is our **primal instincts and the desire to satisfy them.** It focuses on **fulfilling concurrent desires** without considering the consequences. Id or simply put, **a lack of restraint,** is a heavily used element that reoccurs throughout Gothic horror and was an individually discussed element during this unit.

**Ego** is our **consciousness,** the aspect that **weighs the influences of id and superego when facing different situations.** Ego works as a **balance between two extremes**: the ‘angle’ side of us, which is superego, and the ‘devil’ side of us, which is id.

In Gothic horror, characters are **not** **capable of controlling their id,** resulting in their id often overshadowing their superego, leading them to make **instinctual decisions.** Considering the **historical context** of the Victorian era, since **characters face consequences for all actions,** we can deduce that **characters** **that** **follows their id too much** (in Gothic horror) will **meet corresponding consequences** for their wrong deeds.

In the curious case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde:

**Jekyll is** **often portrayed as the** **moral and civilized character,** or in this case, the superego of the novella. He carries strict values on social conduct, is polite and respectful, and maintains a **good reputation.**

**Hyde** is the **id** **of the novella** because he is often described as apelike. His assaults and murders **devoid any material motivation,** they are purely consequences of his controllable id.

Not only this, Jekyll and Hyde represents bipolar sides of the same person. In the story, **Victorian societal norms and moral codes act as the superego** and is represented by characters that uphold these values, primarily Mr.Utterson and Dr.Lanyon.

**Lexis**

- **Uncanny**(adj.): strange or mysterious, often in a way that is slightly frightening.
- **Doppelganger**(n.): an apparition or double of a living person
- **Grotesque**(adj.): involves freakish caricatures of people’s appearance and behavior, usually horrific but close to comedic
- **Sublime**(adj.): producing an overwhelming sense of awe or other high emotion through being vast or grand
- **Macabre**(adj.): causing shock and fear because connected with death, especially strange or cruel death
- **Eerie**(n.): strange in a frightening and mysterious way
- **Supernatural**(adj.): caused by forces that cannot be explained by science
- **Desolation**(n.): the state of a place that is empty or where everything has been destroyed
- **Foreboding**(n.): a feeling that something very bad is going to happen soon
- **Decay**(v.): to become gradually damaged, worse, or less; to cause something to do this
- **Labyrinth**(n.): a confusing set of connecting passages or paths in which it is easy to get lost
- **Hersey**(n.): a belief that rejects the orthodox tenets of a religion
- **Juggernaut**(n.): a massive inexorable force that seems to crush everything in its way
- **Incoherence**(n.): the fact of expressing yourself in a way that is not clear
- **Haggard**(adj.): showing the wearing effects of overwork or care or suffering
- **Ransack**(v.): search thoroughly
- **Volatile**(adj.): liable to lead to sudden change or violence
- **Apparition**(n.): the spirit of a dead person appearing in a form that can be seen
- **Insidious**(v.): (of something unpleasant or dangerous) gradually and secretly causing harm
- **Vengeance**(adj.): the punishing of someone for harming you or your friends or family, or the with for such punishment to happen
