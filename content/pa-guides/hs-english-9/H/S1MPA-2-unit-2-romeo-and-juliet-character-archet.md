# S1MPA-2 Unit 2: Romeo and Juliet: Character Archetype Analysis

> From the Grade 9 Resource Department's Semester 1 Midterm (PA) English H review guide (PA). Writers: Stanley Wang 9(12). Editor: Shean Yang 9(5). Range: Units 1-2. Student-made reference material: where it differs from class, follow class.

1. Juliet

- Archetypes: Innocent maiden → Tragic Heroine
- Role: Challenges the hate between Capulets and Montagues. Moves from an obedient young girl to someone willing to risk everything for love. Explores themes of passion vs family duty.
- Evidence: Balcony-scene sonnet in iambic pentameter shows her genuine love for Romeo.
- Femininity lens: Teenage noble girl trapped by arranged-marriage expectations; she fights to make her own choices.

2. Romeo

- Archetypes: Tragic Hero; Hopeless Romantic
- Role: Tragic protagonist. His impulsiveness and passionate emotion fuel the play’s tragic outcome.
- Evidence: Quickly shifts infatuation from Rosaline to Juliet; impulsive killings; hasty suicide.

3. Friar Laurence

- Archetype: Mentor (imperfect / flawed mentor)
- Role: Advises the lovers, secretly marries them, hopes to end the family feud. His risky, well-meaning plan backfires and contributes to the tragedy.
- Common exam mistake: Do not only label him “mentor”. Explain that his flawed choices drive tragedy.

4. The Nurse

- Archetypes: Mother-figure (surrogate mother for Juliet); Comic relief
- Role: Supports Juliet’s secret romance and passes messages. Later abandons Juliet’s wishes, leaving Juliet isolated. Foil to Juliet’s biological mother.

Note: Romeo and Juliet is NOT a monomyth / Hero’s Journey story — do not force that framework onto these characters.

Motifs & Themes

Light / Dark Imagery, Celestial Imagery, Fate, Time, violence, Duality (Foil) Love, Fate / Free will, Violence / Passion, Desire, Societal Expectation, Familial Duty

Critical Lens: Femininity / Feminist Lens (Think about Medusa, Penelope and Persephone, what are their archetypes.)

Use these question prompts when analyzing female characters in both units:

1. What expectations does society/myth place on this female character?

2. How much agency (power to make their own decisions) does the character have?

3. Are powerful female characters framed as threatening or dangerous? (example: Circe)

4. How do female characters respond to limitation or loss of freedom?
