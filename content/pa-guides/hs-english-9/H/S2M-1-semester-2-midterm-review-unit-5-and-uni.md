- due to the ills of industrialization

- development of the factory culture

- stereotype of the factory owner develops

Karl Marx

- he was a German philosopher, and he supported socialism and communism

- wrote “Manifesto of the Communist Party” with Engels

- was unhappy with capitalism and the unfair exploitation of the working class

Manifesto of the Communist Party

- Marx and Engel’s greatest work

- published in 1848, also known as the Marxist Bible

He believed that after the Proletariat has overthrown the bourgeoisie

- communism would gradually rise

- no need of government

- no separate classes in society

- everyone works and receives according to their abilities and needs

Marx’s Theory

- he studied the way that power,industry and finance were controlled

Primitive communism -> feudalism -> capitalism -> socialism -> communism (Marxism)

Primitive Communsism: community, small tribes, everything was shared, but eventually someone desires for power and leadership

Feudalism: king, emperor or leader present, and they give land or privileges to nobles and lords. Many people were kept uneducated, but soon trade develops and some people gain wealth.

Capitalism: business owners/capitalists get richer, while workers do all the labor. Capitalists serve their own interests, and through this system it creates jobs for the working class. Workers eventually demand for change.

Socialism: workers control population, not leaders and capitalists, all ppl benefit from services, nothing is made for profit.

Marxism: sharing, all human activities benefit each other, no police/army is needed. Creates a classless, borderless utopia with no single dictator.

Of Mice and Men

Author: John Steinbeck

Background: the 1930s, California (Soledad), on the Ranch

Characters

Lennie: tall and strong, but mentally immature (childlike mentality). He was unable to control strength, leading to the inevitability of the tragedies.

George: he is a leader, practical and caring.

- similarities: both George and Lennie are migrant workers, white men

Curley: big-headed son of the ranch Boss,has the Napoleon complex. He doesn’t show love or companionship towards his wife and most workers.

Curley’s Wife: flirty, troubled, and pretty. She uses body to attract attention of farmhands, leading to more trouble.

Marginalization— the exclusion of individuals from participating in society (e.g. Lennie, Candy, Crooks)

- might happen due to being unskilled, aged, or having disabilities

Character comparisons: Candy vs Candy’s Dog

- both are disabled, marginalized, not treated well. They were once great workers, but as they aged they became forgotten.

Farm Life

- farm life is functional and lacks comfort

- working men had very few personal items, suggesting that their lives were lacking identity and meaning beyond work

- Ranch life was a masculine world of hard work, and many men were friendless or desperately lonely

Steinbeck’s Message

- Steinbeck wanted his readers to understand the dehumanization workers suffered as they traveled across ranches in California

- migrant workers are with feelings

The Dream: represents hope, stability and freedom for the workers

The reality: harshness, poverty, isolation and exploitation

- Steinbeck emphasizes that the American Dream most workers had were fragile and unattainable

Other important ideas in the novel:

- George’s decision (at the end of the novel) to find Lennie himself reflects the complexity of their friendship — one marked by loyalty, protection, and an inevitable sacrifice

- George later realizes that Lennie’s actions have made their dream impossible

Themes: friendship, isolation, harshness of reality

- The American Dream is often out of reach for those who are disadvantaged and most vulnerable

-Significance of the Luger (pistol) : Used for shooting Candy’s dog, foreshadowed the death of Lennie that happened later. Both were killed by the exact same gun.

-Lennie’s hallucinations before he dies reflect his fears and sense of guilt.

Key Message: Hope is futile in a world ruled by economic hardship and social prejudice.

Rhetoric/Persuasive Techniques

Ethos, Pathos, and Logos

- Dates all the way back to the time of Aristotle

Ethos: credibility of the speaker, their reputation, image and popularity. Can we trust them? Are they knowledgeable? (E.g. celebrity endorsement leads to credence)

Pathos: emotional/motive appeals — relates to the audience’s wants, needs, ambitions or hopes

Logos: claim plus reasoning (argument), usage of examples, comparisons, facts and statistics

Monroe’s Motivated Sequence

Alan Monroe -> five-step sequence (established in the 1930s)

Step 1: Attention

- hook ( use stories or questions or statistics ), shock and surprise the audience

Step 2: Need

- tell audience what they need to take action in, make the problem relate to them personally

Step 3: Satisfaction

- be specific and realistic about the solution you have, how can we implement it?

Step 4: Visualization

- results if we take/don’t take action, use specifics, do not tell but show

Step 5: Action

- tell them exactly what to do, be immediate (call to action)
