Important terms:

Archetype: Recurring character, symbol, or motif that appears across many stories and cultures. Basically the role a character plays in the story. One character can hold multiple archetypes.

Examples: sage, jester, lover, mentor……

Monomyth / Hero’s Journey: Joseph Campbell’s universal hero plot structure.

- Main stages: Call to adventure → Crossing the threshold → Trials & ordeals → - Reward → Return home………

Fear: how fear is represented in a medium often reflects the historical background and cultural anxieties.

- Examples: Godzilla - the fear of nuclear technology, Frankenstein - the fear of technology, ethics

Xenia: Hospitality, social regulations imposed on treatment of family, friends, and strangers

Critical Lens: A perspective to interpret text.

- Examples: feminist lens on stories like Medusa and Penelope

Purposes of Myths

1. Sociological: Explain why things happen, natural phenomena.

- Example: Persephone myth explains the changing of seasons (winter when she lives in Underworld; spring when she returns above ground).

2. Pedagogical: Teach moral lessons / cultural values (Didactic).

- Example: Cyclops myth teaches the importance of hospitality.

3. Cosmological: Explain origins of the world, humans, or customs. Tell how things began, where rituals come from.

4.   Metaphysical: Entertain; make sense of death, loss, human suffering

Poetic Forms

1. Sonnet: 14-line poem. Shakespearean sonnet: ABAB CDCD EFEF GG rhyme scheme. Often used for love or deep reflection. Explains a problem, and proposes the solution after the turn, or volta .

- Shakespeare writes in blank

verse

2.    Iambic Pentameter: 10 syllables per

line. Used by noble characters during

sincere, serious moments (love

declarations, soliloquies).

- Ordinary servants often speak in prose (no strict rhythm).

Dramatic / Literary Devices in Shakespeare

Definitions :

Monologue: a speech(es) delivered by a character to another character or to the audience. A monologue is usually used to reveal something about the character’s thoughts, feelings, or motivations.

- Soliloquy: A character speaks alone on stage, revealing private inner thoughts. Aside: a type of speech in which a character speaks directly to the audience, but is not intended to be heard by other characters on stage. Frequently used to express personal thoughts, feelings, and / or motivations while also able to provide comedic relief Foil: A character who contrasts another character to highlight particular traits.
- Example: Mercutio (cynical, realistic) is Romeo’s foil (romantic, passionate). Dramatic Irony: Audience knows information characters do not know; builds tension. Foreshadow: Literary device used for hinting at possible future event.

Tragic Flaw: The fatal reason as to why the character dies

- Example: Hubris, wrath, sloth. Mercutio dies of hubris, etc …
