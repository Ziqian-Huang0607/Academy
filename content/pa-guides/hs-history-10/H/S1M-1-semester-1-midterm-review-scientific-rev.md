**The Scientific Revolution**

**Background and Origin**

The Scientific Revolution was a sweeping intellectual and cultural transformation that took place roughly from the mid1500s to the early 1700s. It emerged first in Europe, even though the Islamic world had led the globe in science from 800 to 1400 and China had unmatched technological achievements.

**Scientists**

**Copernicus** proposed heliocentrism, challenging the Earthcentered view of the Church.

**Kepler** proved planets move in elliptical orbits.

**Galileo** used the telescope to gather empirical evidence that undermined traditional cosmic beliefs, but he was condemned by the Church.

**Newton** unified physics with universal laws of motion and gravitation, portraying the universe as a selfregulating machine governed by natural laws.

**Key idea**

Knowledge comes from evidence, reason and experiment — not from tradition, religion or ancient authority.

**Science & Religion**

There was serious conflict with the Catholic Church, yet early scientists remained religious. Over time, science and religion were treated as separate realms.

**Legacy**

It reshaped European views of humanity and the cosmos, laid the intellectual groundwork for the Enlightenment, and eventually became a global marker of modernity.

**Enlightenment**

**Background** **and Origin**

The Enlightenment first emerged in the late 1600s, growing directly out of the Scientific Revolution. Discoveries by **Isaac Newton, Galileo Galilei and other scholars** proved that the natural world could be explained through reason, logic and empirical observation, rather than religion or ancient tradition. This rational way of thinking was soon applied to human society, law and politics.

The movement began in England, where thinkers such as **John Locke** rejected the divine right of kings and built the early theoretical framework for liberal government and individual rights. To summarize, the Enlightenment used reason and observation, inspired by the scientific revolution, to build new theories of government.

**Enlightenment Philosophers**

**Thomas Hobbes**

**Main idea**: people need an all-powerful ruler to avoid chaos.

**State of nature:** no laws, and people pursue self-interest. Life is "solitary, poor, nasty, brutish and short."

**Social contract:** people give up their freedom to a sovereign. The agreement is only among the people, not with the king.

**Best government:** an absolute monarchy. Once power is given, people have no right to revolt.

**John Locke**

**Main idea:** government exists to protect natural rights, and people can revolt if it fails. (Two Treatises of Government)

**Natural rights:** life, liberty, and property are inalienable, meaning they cannot be taken away or given up.

**Social contract:** an agreement between the people and the ruler.

**Right to revolt:** if a ruler violates natural rights, the contract is broken and people can set up a new government. This influenced the Declaration of Independence.

**Government:** limited ("govern lightly"), with a supreme legislature. Property is the most important right, and only male property owners should vote.

**Montesquieu**

**Main idea:** divide government into separate branches so none becomes too powerful. (The Spirit of the Laws)

**Three branches:** legislative, executive, and judicial, each checking the others.

Uniting these powers, as in the absolute monarchy of France, leads to despotism.

He admired England's system but misread it, since power there was mostly in Parliament. Americans later used his theory with others as the basis of the U.S. Constitution.

**Jean-Jacques Rousseau**

**Main idea:** all power belongs to the people, who express it through the general will. (The Social Contract)

**State of nature:** people are naturally good, and society corrupts them. Property created inequality and war.

**Old social contract:** a fraud by the rich and powerful against the common people.

**True social contract:** people give all their rights to the whole community, which is the sovereign.

**General will:** everyone votes directly on laws for the public good (**direct democracy**). There are no representatives and no separation of powers.

**Immanuel Kant**

**Main idea:** enlightenment means thinking for yourself, and it requires freedom to reason publicly.

**Self-incurred immaturity:** being unable to use your own understanding because you lack the courage to try.

**Why people stay immature:** laziness, cowardice, and guardians (such as priests and officials) who do the thinking for them.xxx

Revolution does not change how people think. Enlightenment comes slowly through freedom.

**Atlantic Revolutions (1770s1840s)**

A wave of revolutionary movements across the Atlantic world, inspired by Enlightenment ideas. ScientificRevolutioninspired faith in human progress, and anger over unfair taxation, political inequality, and old hierarchical systems. Includes American, French, Haitian, and LatinAmerican revolutions.

**The American Revolution**

**Causes**

British taxed colonists after the Seven Years’ War; colonists argued no taxation without representation**.**

Enlightenment influence: John Locke’s naturalrights & socialcontract theory.

Resentment of British control over colonial political and economic life.

**Major Events**

1776: Declaration of Independence (Jefferson; Lockeinspired: life, liberty, pursuit of happiness)

War against Britain; France provided military support

1783: Treaty of Paris → US independence

**The French Revolution**

**Four Main stages**

Stage 1: National Assembly (17891791) – Early Moderate Revolution

Stage 2: Radical Revolution (17921794) – Reign of Terror

Stage 3: The Directory (17951799)

Stage 4: Age of Napoleon (17991815)

**Causes**

**Social:** **The Three Estates System**

1st Estate (Clergy): ~1% population; owned 10% land, paid almost no taxes

2nd Estate (Nobility): ~2% population; owned 25% land, taxexempt

3rd Estate (Commoners: peasants, urban workers, bourgeoisie): 97% of people, owned 65% land, paid nearly all taxes. Deep resentment of unfair hierarchy.

Enlightenment influence

Ideas of reason, natural rights, equality challenged the old social order.

**Economic**

**Massive national debt: Seven** Years’ War, French support for American Revolution, costly Palace of Versailles, lavish royal spending.

**Bad harvests (17881789):** bread prices skyrocketed. Bread was the main food for poor Parisians; hunger fueled popular anger.

**Unfairness:** King Louis XVI tried to raise taxes, but only the Third Estate bore the burden.

**Political: Absolute Monarchy**

King Louis XVI held total power: he made laws, set taxes, used lettres de cachet to jail people without trial, censored speech/press.

EstatesGeneral (representative body) had not met since 1614. This meant many reformative ideas and reformations were suppressed and unable to be proposed.

**Trigger Event**

May 1789: Louis XVI calls the EstatesGeneral to fix France’s financial crisis. Each estate got only one vote → First + Second Estate always outvoted Third Estate. Nothing was changed and the members of the third estate got angry.

**National Assembly (17891791)**

**Tennis Court Oath (June 20,1789):** Third Estate broke away, created the National Assembly. Locked out of their meeting hall, they swore they would not disband until they wrote a constitution limiting royal power.

**Storming of the Bastille (July 14, 1789):** Paris revolutionaries attacked the Bastille fortress, a symbol of royal tyranny. This date is France’s national holiday. Marked the start of popular uprising.

**August Decrees (Aug 4,1789):** Abolished feudal privileges of clergy and nobility.

**Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen (Aug 26,1789):** Enlightenmentinspired document, states Men are born and remain free and equal in rights. Rights: liberty, property, security, resistance to oppression, free speech, innocent until proven guilty.

**Women’s March on Versailles (Oct 1789):** Hungry Parisian women marched to Versailles, forced King Louis XVI and MarieAntoinette to move to Paris. Royal family now under popular watch and are

**Constitution of 1791:** Created a limited constitutional monarchy: King kept executive power, lawmaking power went to elected National Assembly. Judicial branch elected by people.

**Monarchy escapes:** King and Queen try to flee Paris to Varennes (Northeastern France), but are found and taken back. Monarchy loses trust and evokes public hate.

**Radical Revolution (17921794)**

**War begins (April 1792):** European monarchs (Austria, Prussia) feared revolution would spread and threatened France. France declared war on them.

**Fall of monarchy** **and** **Radicals took power** **(January 1793):** Louis XVI executed by guillotine. France became a republic without a king.

**Committee of Public Safety (17931794)**: Led by Maximilien Robespierre. Purpose: defend France from foreign armies and internal counterrevolutionary enemies.

**Reign of Terror (17931794)**

Suspended many individual rights; spies hunted “enemies of the revolution”.

Thousands executed by guillotine. Most victims were peasants & urban workingclass people, not just nobles or clergy.

Attacked Catholic Church power in France.

End of Terror: Thermidorian Reaction (July 1794) Members of the National Convention arrested and executed Robespierre. The Reign of Terror ended.

**Reasons of execution:** People feared Robespierre’s terror and that they might be on the list of execution. Another reason was because Robespierre hosted a ceremony declaring a new god instead of Catholicism where he dressed up as a godly servant in front of the whole of Paris. People believed he went nuts.

Achievements: Defended France from foreign invasion.

Limits: Mass humanrights abuses; fear and bloodshed tore French society apart.

Note: September Massacres – Parisians killed thousands of suspected counterrevolutionary prisoners; newspapers (Marat) helped spread radical calls for violence.

**The Directory (17951799)**

New Constitution of 1795 set up the Directory: 5man executive group.

Goal: Avoid oneperson tyranny (reaction against Robespierre).

Weaknesses: Corruption, unstable politics, coups, still fighting foreign wars, unable to solve economic problems.

People grew frustrated with Directory failures.

**Age of Napoleon (17991815)**

**Coronation (Dec 1804):** Napoleon crowned himself **Emperor of France**. Revolution’s antimonarchy ideal was reversed.

**Napoleonic Wars (18031815):** Napoleon’s armies conquered much of Europe, spreading revolutionary legal ideas across the continent. But constant war drained France’s resources.

**Invasion of Russia (1812):** Catastrophic defeat for Napoleon.

**Defeat &Exile (1814):** Napoleon defeated; exiled to island of Elba. Bourbon royal family restored: Louis XVIII became French king (constitutional monarchy, not absolute).

**Return & downfall (1815):** Napoleon escaped Elba, returned to power → defeated finally at **Battle of Waterloo**. Exiled to remote SaintHelena, where he died.

**Congress of Vienna (18141815)** European leaders met after Napoleon’s fall. The goals were to restore old preNapoleon borders, create “balance of power” to prevent future largescale European war.

Coup of 1799 Popular military general Napoleon Bonaparte overthrew the weak Directory. He became First Consul (neardictator).

Napoleonic Code unified French law, preserved many revolutionary gains: equality before law, end of feudalism, property rights.

Negative affect: Rolled back women’s rights; men held full authority over family.

**Overall Significance:** **Failures**

Revolution began fighting for liberty & equality but turned to mass violence in the Terror.

Women’s rights were mostly ignored; later rolled back by Napoleon.

The revolution aimed to reform social systems to benefit the third estate and the lower class, but instead the ones affected most negatively were arguably the commoners and peasants.

After decades of revolution and war, a monarchy returned to France.

**Overall Significance:** **Successes**

Permanently ended feudalism in France.

Spread Enlightenment ideals: liberty, legal equality, popular sovereignty across Europe.
