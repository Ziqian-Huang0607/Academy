**Polynomials:**

- Concepts:
- Polynomial Functions: (of degree n) is a function of the form:
- $$f\left( x \right)={a}_{n}{x}^{n}+{a}_{n-1}{x}^{n-1}+…+{a}_{2}{x}^{2}+{a}_{1}x+{a}_{0}{a}_{n}\ne 0$$
  - $n$ is a nonnegative integer and ${a}_{n},{a}_{n-1},…,{a}_{0}$are real numbers
- Degree of a polynomial is the degree of the term of highest degree
- Terms: ${a}_{n}{x}^{n},{a}_{n-1}{x}^{n-1},…,{a}_{2}{x}^{2},{a}_{1}x,{a}_{0}$
- Coefficients: ${a}_{n},{a}_{n-1},…,{a}_{0}$
- Leading term: the term containing the highest power of $x-{a}_{n}{x}^{n}$
- Leading coefficient: ${a}_{n},$
- Special names of polynomials:
  - Constant: 0
  - Linear: 1
  - Quadratic: 2
  - Cubic: 3
  - Quartic: 4
  - Quintic: 5
- Zeros of a function is the roots of the equation $f\left( x \right)=0$
- Nested form:
- The polynomial function $f\left( x \right)=5{x}^{2}+3x+7$ in nested from is $f\left( x \right)=\left( 5x+3 \right)x+7$
- Using nested form, multiply $\to$ add (or subtract). At the beginning ${a}_{n}x+{a}_{n-1}$
- Synthetic division

**The Remainder and Factor Theorems**

- Remainder Theorem:
- When polynomial $P(x)$ is divided by $x-a$, the remainder is $P(a)$
  - $$P\left( x \right)=\left( x-a \right)\times Q\left( x \right)+R\to P\left( a \right)=R$$
- Factor theorem:
- A number $a$ is a solution of the polynomial equation $f\left( x \right)=0$ if and only if $x-a$ is a factor of $f(x)$
- Substitute $a$ into $f(x)$ and if $f(x)$ is $0$, then $a$ is a factor of $f(x)$

**Graphing Polynomial functions**

- The graph of a function of the form $f\left( x \right)=a{x}^{n}$
- $n$ is even: if $a$ if positive, the graph will rise at both the left and right. If $a$ is negative, the graph will fall at both the left and right
- $n$ is odd: if $a$ is positive, the graph will fall at the left and rise at the right. If $a$ is negative, the graph will rise at the left and fall at the right.
- Special note
- If the equation of the graph includes an absolute value, reflect the negative part ($y<0)$of the graph to the positive part $(y>0)$.

**Solving Polynomial Equation by Factoring**

- Solving polynomial equation by factoring after grouping terms:
- Bring all terms to one side of the equation + make the whole equation equal to zero.
- Group the four terms into two pairs (usually first two together, last two together)
- Factor out the GCF from each group separately
- Factor out the common binomial factor
- Solve each smaller equation
- Solving a polynomial equation that has a quadratic form:
- Rewrite the original equation and identify the substitution $u$
- Factor (or quadratic formula) the quadratic in $u$
- Solve for $u$
- Substitute $u$ back
- Solve each new equation for $x$
- Note: the number of the highest degree of the polynomial = number of roots (including multiplicity and imaginary roots)
- Rational root theorem
- Using factoring to solve the general polynomial equation:

$${a}_{n}{x}^{n}+{a}_{n-1}{x}^{n-1}+…+{a}_{2}{x}^{2}+{a}_{1}x+{a}_{0}=0$$

$$\left( qx-p \right)\left( \right)=0$$

- If $qx-p$ is a factor (so that $x=\frac{p}{q}$ is a root), then $q$ must divide ${a}_{n}$, and $p$ must divide ${a}_{0}$
- When solving questions: list all the factors of $\text{ qandp }$ then list out all possibilities of $\frac{p}{q}$, substitute $\frac{p}{q}$ back to the equation, then use synthetic division to find out the other roots.

**General Results for polynomial equations**

- Theorem 1: The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra
- In the complex number system consisting of all real and imaginary numbers, if $P(x)$is a polynomial of degree$n(n>0)$ with complex coefficients, then the equation $P(x)=0$ has exactly $n$ roots (provided a double root is counted as 2 roots, a triple root is counted as 3 roots, and so on)
- Theorem 2: Complex Conjugates Theorem
- If $P(x)$ is a polynomial with real coefficients, and $a+bi$ is an imaginary root of the equation $P(x)=0$, then $a-bi$ is also a root
- Theorem 3:
- Suppose = is a polynomial with rational coefficients, and $a$ and $b$ are rational numbers such that $\sqrt{b}$ is irrational. If $a+\sqrt{b}$ is a root of the equation =, then $a-\sqrt{b}$ is also a root
- Theorem 4:
- If $P(x)$ is a polynomial of odd degree with real coefficients, then the equation $P(x)=0$ has at least one real root.
