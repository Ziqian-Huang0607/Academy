**Representing a Vector**

Line and Arrow

-Length of the arrow: the magnitude of the vector, proportional to magnitude

-Direction of the arrow: the direction of the vector (e.g. ‘+’ is up, ‘-‘ is down)

-The value of the scale of a vector is representing should be a factor (except the vector’s value itself) of the vector

**Addition of Vectors by Graphing**

-Resultant/Net: the sum of two or more vectors

Commutative Property: Vector A + Vector B = Vector B + Vector A

- Tip-to-Tail Method

-Move the tail of one vector to the head of another vector without changing the magnitude or direction of the vectors

-Points from the tail of the first vector to the head of second (last) vector: Resultant force

-If vectors all lie on a same line, draw them as parallel easier to distinguish

-Magnitude of vector sum is always less than or equal to the sum of magnitude of each contributing vector

-If 3 or more vectors form a closed polygon, there is no resultant (net=0)

-3 vectors can form a triangle if the sum of two sides is larger than the largest side

- Parallelogram Method

-Place component vectors tail-to-tail

-Use the 2 vectors as adjacent sides to construct a parallelogram

-Diagonal form the common origin: Resultant vector

-Only can be used to find 2 vectors’ resultant

**Subtraction of Vectors by Graphing**

-Two vectors are negative if they have the same magnitude but opposite direction

Vector A=-Vector B

-Adding a negative vector=Subtracting the vector

-In Parallelogram Method, connecting two tails of the two vectors form the difference of two vectors

**Multiplication of Vectors by a Scalar**

Vector A * Scalar C

-If c>0, the product has the same direction and same magnitude AC

-If c<0, the product has the opposite direction to Vector A, the magnitude is still AC

**Adding by Components**

-Vector Resolution: the process of breaking down vectors into components (when placed in a coordinate plane)

-Law of Sines: In any triangle, the ratio of the length of a side to the sine of its opposite angle is constant

-Law of Cosines: R²=A²+B²-2AB

**Projectile Motion**

-Projectile: an object shot through the air

-Trajectory: the curved flight path that is followed by a moving object

-After projectile is launched: ignoring air resistance, there are no contact forces. Fnet = gravity.

**-**Projectile motion is a combination of 2 independent motions

Horizontal motion component: motion with constant velocity when there is no air resistance

Vertical motion component: motion with constant acceleration

**Projectile Motion Launched Horizontally**

-No initial vertical velocity

-Horizontal: constant v

-Formula- Range: dx= vix t

-Vertical motion: free fall

-Formula- Height: dy= gt2 /2

-The velocity vector at each instant is always tangent to the parabola

-Fv before hitting the ground

Formula- magnitude: v= √vx2+ vy2

Formula- direction: θ= tan-1 (Vy/ Vx)

-Described by Galileo: an object projected horizontally will reach the ground in the same time as an object dropped vertically

-The time from the launch to hit the target is the same for both horizontal and vertical motion

**Relative Velocity**

-Relative velocity: the velocity of 1 body relative to another

-ex: V car to ground is the car’s velocity using the ground as a reference frame

-Formula: VAB+ VBC= VAC , Vector sum

VAB= -VBA, same magnitude but opposite direction

-Boat equation: VBW+ VWG= VBG
