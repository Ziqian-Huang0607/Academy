### 2.1–2.4: Foundations of Motion

**Reference frame:** an object used for comparison when measuring **position**, **distance or speed**.

**Particle model:** a simplified motion diagram in which the moving object is replaced by a **series of particles (dots)**, showing the object's position at **equal time intervals**.

**a) Vector vs. scalar**

**Vector: magnitude and direction**

- Position (vector): location of an object in a coordinate plane.
- Displacement (vector): change in position of an object ($\Delta d = d_f - d_i$).
- Velocity (vector): speed with both magnitude and direction.
- Acceleration (vector): rate at which velocity changes.

**Scalar: magnitude only**

- Distance (scalar): total length travelled by an object.
- Speed (scalar): distance travelled per unit of time.

**b) Velocity and acceleration: basic formulas**

- Average velocity: $\bar v = \frac{\Delta d}{\Delta t}$
- Instantaneous velocity: $v = \frac{\Delta d}{\Delta t}$ with $\Delta t$ infinitely close to 0
- Average acceleration: $\bar a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$
- Instantaneous acceleration: $a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$ with $\Delta t$ infinitely close to 0

### 2.5–2.6: Motion at Constant Acceleration

**Definition:** **uniformly accelerated motion** = motion with constant acceleration (the acceleration doesn't change).

**The 5 core kinematic formulas (very important):**

1. $a = \frac{V_f - V_i}{t}$
2. $V_f = V_i + at$
3. $d = V_it + \frac{at^2}{2}$
4. $d = \frac{(V_i + V_f)\,t}{2}$
5. $d = \frac{V_f^2 - V_i^2}{2a}$

**Problem-solving strategy:**

1. List the known variables (e.g. $V_i$, $V_f$, $a$, $t$, $\Delta d$) and mark the direction.
2. Choose the formula that contains the 3 knowns and 1 unknown.

### 2.7: Falling Objects

**a) Free fall**

- **Definition:** the motion of a falling object when **air resistance is negligible** and the motion is **due to gravity** alone.
- **Galileo's hypothesis:** at a given location on Earth, in the absence of air resistance, **all objects fall with the same constant acceleration** (regardless of mass).
- **Acceleration due to gravity:** $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, directed toward the centre of the Earth.

> Object dropped: $V_i = 0$ m/s; object thrown down: $V_i \ne 0$ m/s. Use $+g$ when downward is positive and $-g$ when upward is positive.

**b) Motion when thrown up** (upward taken as +)

1. The **magnitude** of gravity always stays the same ($g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$).
2. $V_i$ is (+), initial $\Delta d = 0$.
3. **Highest position:** $V = 0$, and $\Delta d$ is largest in the (+) direction.
4. **Above** the initial position: $\Delta d$ is (+); **below** the initial position: $\Delta d$ is (−).
5. **Rising:** $V$ (+), $\Delta d$ (+). **Falling:** $V$ (−), $\Delta d$ (+) above the start or $\Delta d$ (−) below it.
6. For the **same $\Delta d$**, the **speed is the same** (in opposite directions).
7. On returning to the **initial position**: $V = -V_i$, $\Delta d = 0$, and $t_{rise} = t_{fall}$.

### 2.8: Graphical Analysis of Linear Motion

Three major graphs for motion: position–time (d–t), velocity–time (v–t) and acceleration–time (a–t).

**a) Position–time graph (d–t)**

1. **No motion (at rest):** a horizontal straight line, parallel to the x-axis.
2. **Uniform motion (constant v):**
   - a slanted straight line;
   - y-intercept = initial position;
   - where two lines intersect, the two objects meet;
   - since velocity is constant, instantaneous velocity = average velocity ($v = \frac{d}{t}$);
   - **slope = average velocity** ($v = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$).
3. **Accelerated motion:**
   - (a) many straight segments: an instant change in velocity at each corner;
   - (b) a curved line (parabola): **average velocity** = slope of the chord connecting two points on the parabola; **instantaneous velocity** = slope of the tangent line at that moment.

**Relationships between displacement, velocity and acceleration** (from the d–t curve's shape):

| Curve | Signs | Motion |
|---|---|---|
| rising, getting steeper | $d > 0,\ v > 0,\ a > 0$ | speeding up ($v$, $a$ both +) |
| falling, getting steeper | $d > 0,\ v < 0,\ a < 0$ | speeding up ($v$, $a$ both −) |
| falling, levelling off | $d > 0,\ v < 0,\ a > 0$ | slowing down ($v$, $a$ different signs) |
| rising, levelling off | $d > 0,\ v > 0,\ a < 0$ | slowing down ($v$, $a$ different signs) |

**b) Velocity–time graph (v–t)**

1. **Uniform motion ($a = 0$):**
   - **horizontal line:** $v$ = constant, $a = 0$;
   - the **y-axis** gives the velocity at any moment directly; the sign gives the direction: (+) above the x-axis, (−) below.
2. **Uniformly accelerated motion:**
   - a slanted straight line;
   - slope = acceleration = $\frac{V_f - V_i}{t}$ (average = instantaneous, since $a$ is constant);
   - **rising line:** $a > 0$; **descending line:** $a < 0$; **steeper:** larger $|a|$.
3. **Changing acceleration:**
   - **curved line:** instantaneous $a$ = slope of the tangent at that moment. For example, a curve rising ever more steeply means speed increasing with $v$ (+) and $a$ (+) increasing; a curve rising and levelling off means speed increasing with $a$ (+) decreasing.

**Area between the graph line and the x-axis = displacement.**

- Area above the x-axis: $\Delta d > 0$; below: $\Delta d < 0$.
- Displacement = (positive-direction area) − (negative-direction area), i.e. $A_1 - A_2$.
- Two objects meet when they have the same area (not when their lines cross).

**c) Acceleration–time graph (a–t)**

- **Uniformly accelerated motion:** a horizontal line parallel to the x-axis.
- Area under the a–t graph = change in velocity ($\Delta v = at$).

**d) Graph conversion (d–t ⇄ v–t ⇄ a–t)**

- **d to v:** the slope of d–t gives v–t.
- **v to d:** the area under v–t gives the displacement for d–t.
- **v to a:** the slope of v–t gives a–t.
- **a to v:** the area under a–t gives $\Delta v$.
- **Curves:** use the relationships table above.
- **Corners:** sudden changes in displacement and velocity appear as $\pm\infty$ spikes on the a–t graph.
