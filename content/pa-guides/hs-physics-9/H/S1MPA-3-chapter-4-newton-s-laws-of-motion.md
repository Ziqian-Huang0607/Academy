### 4.1 & 4.6 Force

- **Force:** a push or pull exerted by one object on another; a vector, unit N ($\text{kg·m/s}^2$).
- **Contact force:** exerted when two things touch.
- **Field force:** exerted without contact (e.g. gravity).
- **3 factors of a force:** magnitude, direction, point of application.
- An ***agent*** applies a force to a ***system***.
- **4 fundamental forces:** gravitational, electromagnetic, strong and weak.

**Hooke's law:** $F_{sp} = -kx$, where $F$ is the spring force, $k$ the spring constant and $x$ the change in the spring's length compared with its original length. On an F–x graph the extension is directly proportional to the force, up to the **elastic limit**: the point beyond which the spring no longer returns to its original shape when the force is removed.

**Friction:**

- **Sliding friction:**
  - static friction: $F_{fs,\max} = \mu_sF_n$;
  - kinetic friction: $F_{fk} = \mu_kF_n$, with $F_{fk} \le F_{fs,\max}$. (On a force–time graph, friction rises with the applied force until the object slides, then drops to the constant kinetic value.)
- **Rolling friction.**
- **Fluid friction:** e.g. air resistance, friction in water.

**Air resistance factors:**

- shape: streamlining reduces friction;
- size: larger area, more friction;
- speed: faster, more friction;
- viscosity: more viscous, more friction.

**Balanced forces:** two forces acting on the same object with the same magnitude and opposite directions.

### 4.2 Newton's First Law

1. Every object continues in its state of motion until a net force acts on it.
2. **Inertia** is the tendency of an object to maintain its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line (more mass, more inertia).

> The first law only applies in an inertial reference frame!

**Free-body diagram tips:**

- the normal force is perpendicular to the surface;
- gravity always points down;
- friction acts opposite to the object's motion;
- static friction exists whenever an object has a tendency to move (e.g. pressing an eraser against a wall, there is still upward static friction on the eraser).
