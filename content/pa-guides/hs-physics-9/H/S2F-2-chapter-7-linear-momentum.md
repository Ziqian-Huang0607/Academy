## 7.3 Collision and Impulse

**Momentum**

-Momentum: the product of the object’s mass and its velocity

-Momentum is a vector, same direction as velocity

-Formula: p=mv

-Change in momentum: Δp= Δpf- Δpi= mvf- mvi= mΔv

-Unit: kg·m/s

**Impulse**

-Impulse: the product of the force on an object and the time interval over which it acts

-Impulse is a vector, direction same as force

-Formula: I= FΔt

-Unit: N·s, or (less common) kg·m

-If Force is constant, I= FΔt

-If Force is not constant: I= area under a F-t graph, as before the area under the t-axis is negative momentum

**Impulse- Momentum Theorem**

-Newton’s Second Law of Motion in terms of Momentum: the rate of change of momentum of an object is equal to the net force appled to it

Formula: $\sum F=\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$

-Impulse- Momentum Theorem: the impulse on an object is equal to the object’s final momentum minus the object’s initial momentum

Formula: $I= \Delta p$, $F∙\Delta t= {p}_{f}-{p}_{i}$

## 7.2 Conservation of Momentum

**Conservation of Momentum**

-No external force exerted on the system: momentum is a conserved quantity

-Momentum of the system is conserved: the sum of the momenta of the balls is the same before and after the collision

Formula: pAi+ pBi= pAf+ pBf

-The law of conservation of momentum: the momentum of an isolated system of objects remains constant

-Isolated system: the system in which the only significant forces are those between the objects in the system

-Law of conservation of momentum is also useful for certain types of explosions: rocket propulsion, recoil of gun

-Consider the rocket and fuel as an isolated system, where the total momentum of rocket plus fuel is 0

-When the fuel burns, the total momentum remains unchanged: the backward momentum of the expelled gases is just balanced by the forward momentum gained by the rocket itself

mAvAi+ mBvBi= mAvAf+ mBvBf

mAvAf+ mBvBf= 0

vAf= -mBvBf/ mA

-After explosion: momenta of the 2 objects equal in magnitude, opposite in direction

-The velocities depend on their relative mases: less massive object= greater velocity

## 7.5 Elastic Collisions in One Dimension

**3 Kinds of Collisions**

-Elastic collision: total KE conserved

-Inelastic collision: total KE decreases after collision

-Superelastic/ explosive collision: total KE increases after collision (example: explosion)

**Elastic Collision**

-Elastic collision: the collision in which the total KE is conserved

-The 2 objects are very hard and no heat or other form of energy is produced in the collision

-At the atomic level the collisions of atoms and molecules are often elastic

-Characteristics of elastic collision: conservation of momentum, conservation of kinetic energy

-Formulas: pi= pf

mAvAi+ mBvBi= mAvAf+ mBvBf

KEi= KEf

1/2 mAv2Ai+ 1/2 mBv2Bi= 1/2 mAv2Af+ 1/2 mBv2Bf

-Head- on elastic collision: all the motion is along a line

-Formulas: vAi- vBi= vBf- vAf

or: vAi- vBi= -(vAf- vBf)

-For any elastic head- on collision, the relative speed of the 2 objects after the collision has the same magnitude but opposite direction as before the collision, no matter what the masses are

-2 objects of equal masses will exchange velocities after a 1 dimension elastic collision

-For 1 object moving hits another at rest

-Formulas: vAf= (mA- mB)/ (mA+ mB) *vAi

vBf= 2mA/ (mA+ mB)

## 7.6 Inelastic Collisions

**Inelastic** **Collisions**

-Inelastic collision: the collision in which total KE decreases

-Objects made of soft, sticky material, such as clay, act in this way

-Typical macroscopic collisions are inelastic

-The KE loss is changed into other forms of energy, such as thermal energy

-The total energy is still conserved

-Formulas: KEAi+ KEBi= KEAf+ KEBf+ Ethermal or other

pi= pf

mAvAi+ mBvBi = mA VAf+ mBvBf

-Completely inelastic collision: 2 objects stick together
