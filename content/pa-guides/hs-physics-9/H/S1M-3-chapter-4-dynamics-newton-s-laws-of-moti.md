**Force**

-Force: a push or pull exerted by one object on another. Force is a vector

-System: to whom the force is exerted

Agent: by whom the force is exerted

-The effect of a force: 1) to cause a deformation: a change in dimension/ shape

2) to change the state of motion: change velocity

-Unit: Newton (N). I N= 1kg m/s2

-Measurement device: Spring scale

The larger the force, the spring is stretched longer, hence larger reading

-3 factors of Force

Magnitude: the strength of the force vector

Direction: in which direction the force vector acts

Point of application: the location the force is exerted

-Graphical representation: line segment with an arrow

**Types of Forces**

-4 fundamental forces: Gravitational force, electromagnetic force, strong force, weak force

-Contact force: an object from the external world touches a system and thereby exerts a force on it

e.g. friction, drag force (air resistance/ fluid resistance), tension, normal force, spring force, applied force

These are non-fundamental forces, and are instead consequences of fundamental forces

-Field force: exerted without contact

e.g. gravitational force, electric force, magnetic force

-Spring force: Fsp=-kx

Fsp: force exerted by the spring on the attached object (N)

x: the displacement of the spring end from its equilibrium position (cm)

k: spring constant (N/cm)

- : F is opposite to x

-Hooke’s law: the magnitude of the force is directly proportional to the amount of stretch or compression within the limit of a spring

-Elastic limit: point beyond which the spring will no longer return to its original shape when the force is removed

**Friction**

-Sliding friction: occurs when 2 solid surfaces slide over each other

Static friction (Ffs): caused by tendency of relative motion, where there is no relative motion between 2 objects in touch

Kinetic friction (Ffk): caused by relative motion between 2 objects in touch

-Rolling friction: occurs when an object rolls over a surface. Rolling friction is smaller than sliding friction

-Fluid friction: occurs when an object move through a fluid

e.g. air resistance/ friction in water/ oil

-Air resistance depends on 4 factors

1) shape of object. Streamline reduce friction

2) size of object. Air resistance increases when surface area increases

3) speed of object. Air resistance increases when speed increases

4) fluid type/ viscosity. Air resistance increases when fluid is more viscous

**Formulas for Friction**

-Ffs= Fcause

-Ffs max= μs Fn

fs max: maximum static friction

μs: coefficient of static friction, increased when surface is rougher

FN: normal force between 2 surfaces

Range of fs: 0<fs≤fs max

-Ffk= μk FN

μk: coefficient of kinetic friction, does not change with Fcause

Ffk: kinetic friction

FN: normal force

-μs≥ μk

-Coefficients of friction can also >1

**Balanced Forces**

-Equilibrium state: at rest or uniform motion, Fnet=0

-balanced forces: 2 forces on the same line on the same object that add up to 0

-Unbalanced forces: net force ≠ 0

Consequences: start/ stop moving, change direction. Unbalanced forces will change object’s motion

-Judge if 3 forces can be balanced or not: can/ cannot form triangle

**Newton’s First Law of Motion**

-Statement: every object continues in its state of rest, or of uniform velocity in a straight line, as long as no net force acts on it

-Also called law of inertia

-Inertia: tendency of an object to maintain its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line

Inertia is not a force. The greater the mass, the greater the inertia

-In accelerating frame of reference, Newton’s first law does not hold

-Inertial reference frames: reference frames in which Newton’s first law does hold

A reference frame moving with constant v relative to an IRF is also an IRF

e.g. Earth/ ground can be seen as an inertial reference frame

-Noninertial reference frames: reference frames in which the law of inertia does not hold

**Mass**

-Inertial mass: mass is equal to the ratio of the net force exerted on an object to its acceleration

-Mass is a measure of inertia of an object

-Formula: minertial = Fnet/ a

-Gravitational mass: mass as used in the law of universal gravitation determines the size of the gravitational force between 2 objects

-Formula: mgrav= r2Fgrav/ Gm

-Principle of Equivalence: Newton- inertial mass and gravitational mass are equal in magnitude

**Newton’s Second Law of Motion**

-Statement: the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it, and is inversely proportional to its mass. The direction of the acceleration is in the direction of the net force acting on the object

-Formula: ΣF= ma

ΣF: vector sum of all forces

-Unit: Newton

**Apparent weight**

-A spring scale measures weight, not mass

-Apparent weight: the force exerted by the scale

-Scenarios in an elevator:

1. Equilibrium: Fsp= Fg

2. Uniform acceleration upward: Fnet= Fsp- Fg, Fsp> Fg

Apparent weight> actual weight: overweight

3. Uniform acceleration downward: Fnet= Fg- Fsp, Fsp< Fg

Apparent weight< Actual weight

4. Elevator in Free fall: Fnet= Fg- Fsp= 0, Fsp= 0

Apparent weight= 0: Weightlessness

Weightlessness: no contact forces pushing up on the object, which makes its apparent weight 0. The object’s actual weight is not 0.
