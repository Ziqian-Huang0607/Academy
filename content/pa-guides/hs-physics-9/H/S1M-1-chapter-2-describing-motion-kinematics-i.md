-Mechanics: study of motion and force

**Reference Frames**

-Motion: an object changes position relative to a reference frame (or reference point)

-Reference frame: a place or object used for comparison to determine something is/is not in motion. It is assumed to be stationary.

-Motion diagram: series of images showing the positions of a moving object at equal time intervals. Its purpose is to visualize displacement and velocity.

-Particle model: simplified version of motion diagram, which the object in motion is replaced by dots.

For example: every dot is after a unit of time has passed

**Average Velocity**

-Speed: the rate an object moves, the distance the object travels per unit of time SCALAR

Speed=Distance/Time Average Speed=Total Distance/Total Time Unit: m/s

-Velocity: speed with direction, represented by the + or – symbol VECTOR

Velocity=Displacement/Time Average Velocity=Total Displacement/Time Unit: m/s

-Uniform motion: motion in a straight line with constant velocity

**Instantaneous Velocity**

-Instantaneous velocity (/speed): the velocity (/speed) at any instant of time

Magnitude of instantaneous velocity=instantaneous speed

**Please note that PA materials are student-created and for reference only.**

-in constant speed, the instantaneous and average speed is the same

**Acceleration**

-Acceleration: the rate at which object’s velocity changes

Acceleration=Change of Velocity/Time=(Vfinal-Vinitial)/Time Unit: m/s²

-Moving to “+” direction, speeding up: Positive a

-Moving to “+” direction, slowing down: Negative a

-Moving to “-” direction, speeding up: Negative a

-Moving to “-” direction, slowing down: Positive a

-both Vinitial’s direction and +/- Acceleration determines speed up or slow down

**Motion at Constant Acceleration**

-Uniformly Accelerated Motion: motion with constant acceleration

-Acceleration has constant magnitude + same direction

Circular motion does NOT belong to motion at Constant Acceleration because the acceleration has different directions

Instantaneous Acceleration = Average Acceleration

**Motion Not at Constant Acceleration**

How to Find:

-Average Acceleration: Connect A and B on the graph, find the slope of the line

Average Acceleration=(Velocity B-Velocity A)/Time=Slope

-Instantaneous Acceleration: Find the slope of the tangent line of A

-When time is closer to 0, the Average and Instantaneous Acceleration gets closer values

**Special Formulas**

- Velocity Final = Velocity Initial + Acceleration*Time
- Displacement = (Velocity Initial + Velocity Final)*Time/2
- Displacement = Velocity Initial*Time + (Aceleration*Time²)/2
- Displacement = (Velocity final² - Velocity initial²)/2*Acceleration

**Falling Objects**

-Free Fall: motion of a falling object when air resistance is negligible, and the action can be considered due to gravity alone

-Galileo’s Hypothesis: At a given location on the earth and in the absence of air resistance, all objects fall with the same constant acceleration

Gravitational Acceleration on earth: g=9.8 m/s² toward the center of earth
