**Work Done by a Constant Force**

Work: the transfer of energy (energy: the ability to do work)

W = F||d = Fd|| = Fd cosθ

(F: force acting on the object; d: displacement of the object; F||: component of the force parallel to the displacement; d||: component of the displacement parallel to the force)

θ: the angle between the directions of the force and the displacement

0° ≤ θ < 90°: cosθ > 0, W > 0（when θ = 0°, cosθ = 1, W = Fd）

θ = 90°: cosθ = 0, W = 0

90° < θ ≤ 180°: cosθ < 0, W < 0

Work is a scalar. Energy is a scalar.

Wnet = change in total energy of the system

Wnet = Fnetd cosθ = W1 + W2 + W3 + …

Unit of work and energy: joule(J) 1J = 1N·m = 1kg·m2/s2 1kW·h = 3.6×106J

**Work Done by a Varying Force**

W = area under F||d graph (area above d-axis +, area below d-axis -)

W = A1 – A2

**Kinetic Energy** **and The Work-Energy Principle**

Kinetic energy: the energy of motion

KE = $\frac{1}{2}$mv2

Derivation of the work-energy principle: Wnet = Fnetd = mad = m($\frac{{{v}_{f}}^{2}-{{v}_{i}}^{2}}{2d}$)d = m($\frac{{{v}_{f}}^{2}-{{v}_{i}}^{2}}{2}$)

Wnet = ΔKE = KEf - KEi

**Potential Energy**

Potential energy: the energy associated with forces that depend on the position/configuration of an object relative to the surroundings (can only be defined for a conservative force) (belongs to a system and not a single object)

In general, ΔPE associated with a particular force = -W done by the force

*You should define the reference point when talking about potential energy.

**Gravitational potential energy**: The stored energy in a system resulting from the gravitational force between Earth and the object. (system: object-Earth)

GPE = mgh (h: object’s height above reference point, if not specified, then in reference to the ground)

ΔGPE = mgΔh (physically meaningful) （assuming that m & g are constant）

**Elastic potential energy**: the stored energy in an object as a result of its change in shape.

Hooke’s Law: F spring on external object = -kx (F external object on spring = kx)

(k: spring stiffness constant, unit N/m; x: displacement from unstretched position)

Derivation of formula for EPE:

Note that as the spring is stretched, F is not constant.

EPE = $\frac{1}{2}$kx2 (area under F-x graph)

ΔEPE = $\frac{1}{2}$kxf2 - $\frac{1}{2}$kxf2 ≠ $\frac{1}{2}$k(xf – xi)2

**Conservative and Nonconservative Forces**

Conservative forces: forces for which the work done does not depend on the path taken (only on the initial and final positions) (return to the same point -> no net work done on object) e.g. gravity, elastic force

Nonconservative forces: forces for which the work done depends on the path taken e.g. friction, motor propulsion, push or pull by a person

(Dissipative forces: forces that decrease mechanical energy, always nonconservative)

**Mechanical Energy and** **Conservation** **of Mechanical Energy**

Total mechanical energy of a system: KE + PE

When no nonconservative forces do work, ΔKE + ΔPE = 0 (mechanical energy is conserved)

**The Law of Conservation of Energy**

Energy can be transformed from one form to another, and transferred from one object to another, but the total amount remains constant.

**General problem-solving methods:**

**Power**

Power: the rate at which work is done (the rate at which energy is transformed)

$\overline{P}$ = $\frac{W}{t}$ = $\frac{\Delta E}{t}$

$\overline{P}$ = $\frac{W}{t}$ = $\frac{Fd }{t}$ = F$\overline{v}$ (F: the component of the force in the same direction as the displacement)

Unit of power: watt(W) -- 1W = 1J/s = 1kg·m2·s-3 horsepower(hp) -- 1hp = 746W
