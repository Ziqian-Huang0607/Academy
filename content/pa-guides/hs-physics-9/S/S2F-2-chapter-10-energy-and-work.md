## 10.1 Energy and Work

## Part 1: Work

- **Work** is a measure of how much energy is transferred
- Formula for constant force: W = Fdcosθ
- Unit: **joule** **(J)** = 1 Nm = 1 kgm2/s2
- Work is a scalar but can be positive, negative, or zero
- When θ = 0° (cos = 1): positive work → object speeds up
- When θ = 180° (cos = -1): negative work → object slows down
- When θ = 90° (cos = 0): zero work → no change in speed
- For a **force-displacement** **graph**, the area under the curve equals to the work done
- Area above the d-axis = positive work, area below = negative work

Example Questions:

1. A sailor pulls a boat 0.30 m along a dock using a rope at a 25.0° angle with the horizontal. He exerts 255 N of force. How much work does he do?

- Use W = Fdcosθ
- W = (255)(0.30)(cos 25.0°)
- W = (255)(0.30)(0.906) = 69 J

2. A 100 kg pig falls freely by 1 m. What is the work done by weight?

- F = mg = (100)(9.8) = 980 N
- Angle between weight (down) and displacement (down) is 0°
- W = (980)(1)(cos 0°) = 980 J

3. A person uses a rope at 30° above horizontal to pull a 50 N box forward 30 m. Tension is 100 N. Find work done by tension, gravity, and normal force.

- Work by tension: W = (100)(30)(cos 30°) = 3000(0.866) = 2598 J
- Work by gravity: angle between gravity (down) and displacement (horizontal) is 90° → cos 90° = 0 → W = 0 J
- Work by normal force: normal is perpendicular to displacement → W = 0 J

4. From a force-displacement graph: a force increases linearly from 0 N to 20 N over 1.50 m. Find work done.

- Area under the graph is a triangle: W = (1/2)(base)(height)
- W = (1/2)(1.50)(20) = 15.0 J

5. From a force-displacement graph: force varies from 150 N to 250 N over a distance of 25.0 m (from 10.0 m to 35.0 m). Estimate the work done.

- Use area of trapezoid: W = (1/2)(Fmin + Fmax)(dB - dA)
- W = (1/2)(150 + 250)(35.0 - 10.0)
- \( W = (1/2)(400)(25) = 5000 J

## Part 2: Kinetic Energy and Work-Energy Theorem

- **Kinetic** **energy** is energy due to motion: KE = (1/2)mv2
- KE is always positive (or zero)
- Doubling mass → doubles KE
- Doubling speed → quadruples KE (because v is squared)
- **Work-energy** **theorem**: Wnet = △KE = (1/2)mvf2 - (1/2)mvi2
- If Wnet > 0: KE increases (speeds up)
- If Wnet < 0: KE decreases (slows down)
- If Wne} = 0: KE constant (constant speed)
- Braking distance derivation: d = (mvi2)/(2Ff)

Example Questions:

1. A 145 g baseball is thrown so it acquires a speed of 25 m/s starting from rest. Find its KE and the net work done.

- First find KE: KE = (1/2)(0.145)(25)2 = (1/2)(0.145)(625) = 45.3 J
- Work-energy theorem: Wnet = △KE = 45.3 - 0 = 45.3 J

2. A 1 kg bird pushes itself forward with a force of 10 N through a distance of 10 m (no friction). Its original speed is 2 m/s. Find final speed.

- Use work-energy theorem: Wnet = △KE
- F d = (1/2)mvf2 - (1/2)mvi2
- (10)(10) = (1/2)(1)vf2 - (1/2)(1)(2)2
- 100 = 0.5vf2 - 2
- 102 = 0.5vf2 → vf2 = 204 → vf = 14.3 m/s

3. A 5.0 kg mass moving at 12.0 m/s is brought to rest by friction over 12.0 m. Find the friction force.

- Use work-energy theorem: Wnet = △KE
- (-1)Ffd = 0 - (1/2)mvi2
- (-1)Ff(12.0) = -(1/2)(5.0)(12.0)2
- (-12.0)Ff = -(1/2)(5.0)(144) = -360
- Ff = 30 N

4. A car traveling 60 km/h stops in 20 m. If it travels 120 km/h instead, what is the stopping distance? (Assume same braking force)

- From d = (mv2)/(2F), distance is proportional to v2
- Speed doubled → v2 is multiplied by 4
- New stopping distance: (20)(4) = 80 m

## Part 3: Power

- **Power** is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred
- Formula: P = W / t
- Unit: **watt** **(W)** = 1 J/s
- Alternative formula: P = Fv (when force and velocity are in the same direction)

Example Questions:

1. An engine does 1000 J of work in 2 seconds. What is the power?

- P = W / t = 1000 / 2 = 500 W

2. A crane lifts a 300 N load through a height of 5 m in 10 seconds at constant speed. Find the output power.*

- First find work: W = Fdcos0° = (300)(5)(1) = 1500 J
- Then power: P = 1500 / 10 = 150 W

3. A 60 kg woman climbs stairs to a level 11 m above her starting point in 66 seconds. Find her power.

- Force exerted equals her weight: F = mg = (60)(9.8) = 588 N
- Work done: W = Fd = (588)(11) = 6468 J
- Power: P = 6468 / 66 = 98 W

4. How much power is developed when a constant force of 200 N moves a canoe at 5 m/s?

- Use P = Fv = (200)(5) = 1000 W

5. A car (m = 3000 kg) has constant acceleration of 9 m/s2. Find power at v = 4.5 m/s and at v = 45 m/s.

- First find force: F = m a = (3000)(9) = 27,000 N
- At v = 4.5 m/s: P = (27,000)(4.5) = 121,500 W
- At v = 45 m/s: P = (27,000)(45) = 1,215,000 W
