G9S-Sec4.3 – Interaction Forces

#### 1. Newton’s Third Law (Action-Reaction Pairs)

- Definition: For every action force, there is an **equal** and **opposite** reaction force.
- Formula:

FA on B ​= −FB on A​

- Key Points:
  - Forces act on different objects.
  - Forces are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction.
  - They are the same type of force (e.g., both gravitational or both normal).
  - They do not cancel out because they act on different systems.

#### 2. Interaction Pairs vs. Balanced Forces

#### 3. Free-Body Diagrams & Examples

- Football on a Table:
  - Action: Earth pulls football (gravity)
  - Reaction: Football pulls Earth
  - Action: Table pushes football (normal force)
  - Reaction: Football pushes table

G9S-Sec1.1: A Physics Toolkit – Mathematics and Physics

#### 1. Math in Physics

- Physics uses math to model and solve real-world problems.
- Example formulas:
  - Ohm’s Law: V=I×R
  - Pressure: P=AF​

#### 2. SI Units (International System)

- Every measurement has a unit.
- A unit of measurement is a definite magnitude of a quantity that is used as a standard for measurement of the same kind of quantity.
- SI Units: a base 10 system of measurement that is the standard in science.
- 7 base SI units: originally defined in terms of direct measurements
- Other units are called “derived units” – created by combining the base units
- The metric system is adapted to state measurements
- To convert between SI units, multiply or divide by the appropriate power of 10.
- Prefixes are used to change SI units by powers of 10

#### 3. Dimensional Analysis

- Unit conversion using conversion factors (ex. 1 kg = 1000 g).
- Example:

43 km/h=43×36001000 ​≈ 12 m/s

#### 4. Significant Digits (sf)

- Rules:
  - All non-zero digits are significant.
  - Zeros between non-zero digits are significant.
  - Leading zeros are not significant.
  - Trailing zeros are significant if decimal is present.
  - Trailing zeros are not significant if no decimal.
- Examples:
  - 0.030 m → 2 sf
  - 3.00 m → 3 sf
  - 2,080,000 m → 3 sf
- Scientific Notation:
  - 0.000506 = 5.06 × 10⁻⁴ (3 sf)

#### 5. Math with Significant Digits

- Addition/Subtraction: Round to the least precise decimal place.
  - Example:

6.201 cm+7.4 cm+0.68 cm+12.0 cm=26.3 cm

- Multiplication/Division: Round to the least number of sf
  - Example:

139 cm×2.3 cm=320 cm2 (2 sf)

#### 6. Key point

- Always include units in answers.
- Check significant digits in calculations.
- Use dimensional analysis to convert units clearly

G9S-Sec1.2 + 1.3 – Measurement & Graphing

#### 1. Measurement Basics

- Measurement = comparison between an unknown quantity and a standard.
- No measurement is exact – all have uncertainty.

#### 2. Reporting Results

- Format: (measured value ± absolute uncertainty) unit
- Example: (14.6±0.2) cm
- Rule: Last digit of measured value must match the decimal place of uncertainty.

#### 3. Uncertainty Rules

- Analog device: Uncertainty= 0.5 ​× smallest division
- Digital device: Uncertainty = smallest division

#### 4. Propagation of Uncertainty

- Addition/Subtraction:

(A±ΔA) ± (B±ΔB) = (A±B) ± (ΔA+ΔB)

- Example:

(12.9±0.1) − (1.0±0.1) = (11.9±0.2) cm

#### 5. Precision vs. Accuracy

- Bull’s-eye analogy:
  - Accurate & precise: hits center every time
  - Precise only: tight group, off-center
  - Accurate only: scattered, average is center
  - Neither: scattered and off-center

#### 6. Graphing Data

- 5 Steps:
  - Choose scale – use most of the graph paper (ex. 1 square = 3 N).
  - Label axes – independent variable on x-axis, dependent on y-axis.
  - Plot points – use × or •, at least 5 points.
  - Draw line of best fit – straight line or smooth curve; do not connect dot-to-dot.
  - Check anomalies – redo measurement if a point is far off.

#### 8. Predicting Values

- Two ways:
  - Graphically: extend line of best fit to desired x-value.
  - Formula: use equation from linear fit (ex. y=0.05x+12.3 ).

G9S-Sec5.1 – Forces in Two Dimensions / Vectors

#### 1. Vectors vs. Scalars

- Vector arrow: length = magnitude, arrowhead = direction.

#### 2. Graphical Vector Addition

- Tail-to-Tip Method:
Connect vectors end-to-end; resultant runs from first tail to last tip.
- Parallelogram Method:
Use two vectors as adjacent sides; diagonal is the resultant.

#### 3. Drawing to Scale

- Choose scale (ex. 1 cm = 10 N).
- Measure length with ruler, direction with protractor.
- Label magnitude, unit, and direction on arrow.

#### 4. Perpendicular Vectors – Math Solution

- Pythagorean Theorem:
- Direction:

θ=tan−1(adjacent / opposite​)

- Example:
30 N north + 40 N east → R=50 N, 37∘ E of N

#### 5. Vector Resolution

- Break any vector into x- and y-components:

Vx ​= V cos θ, V y​ = V sin θ

- Signs depend on quadrant:
  - Q1: +x, +y
  - Q2: –x, +y
  - Q3: –x, –y
  - Q4: +x, –y

#### 6. Coordinate System Tips

- Horizontal/vertical motion: x = horizontal, y = vertical.
- Slope problems: x = parallel to slope, y = perpendicular to slope.
