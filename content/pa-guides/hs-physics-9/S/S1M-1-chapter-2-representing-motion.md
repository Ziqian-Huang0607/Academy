## Chapter 2 (part 1) Picturing Motion :

**Key Vocabulary:**

Motion Diagram: A series of images showing the positions of a moving object at equal time intervals.

Particle Mode: Simplified way of thinking about matter, which states that all substances are made of tiny, constantly moving particles that have spaces between them.

Center of Mass: A position defined relative to an object or system

of objects. It is the average position of all the parts of the system,

weighted according to their masses.

**How to draw** **a motion diagram?**

To draw a motion diagram, simply represent a moving object as a series of dots showing its position at equal time intervals, where the spacing between the dots reveals its speed and direction.

In short: Closer dots = slower, wider dots = faster, and a change in spacing or direction shows acceleration.

Rules:

- Dots show position
- Spacing shows speed
- Add velocity arrows (optional but helpful)
- Add acceleration arrows (for changing velocity)

**How to draw a** **particle model?**

Simplified model: Person transformed to dot.

Dot should be drawn on the center of mass.

Only used if: Size of object is much shorter than distance travelled

Internal motion can be ignored.

#### Chapter 3 Accelerated Motion

#### Chapter 3 (part 1) Acceleration :

**Definition of Acceleration:**

- The rate at which velocity changes. It is a **vector**.
- **Formula:** a = Δv / Δt = (v_f - v_i) / (t_f - t_i) Units: m/s²

**When does Acceleration Occur?**

- **Change in Speed:** Speeding up or slowing down.
- **Change in Direction:** Turning, even at constant speed.

**Velocity-Time Graphs (v-t graphs):**

- **Slope = Acceleration.** A steeper slope means a larger magnitude of acceleration.
- **Area under the curve = Displacement.**
- **Horizontal Line:** Constant velocity (acceleration = 0).

**Interpreting the Sign of Acceleration:**

- **Positive Acceleration (a > 0):** Does NOT always mean speeding up.
- **Negative Acceleration (a < 0):** Does NOT always mean slowing down.
- **The key is the direction of velocity (v):**
  - If v and a have the same sign (both + or both -), the object is speeding up.
  - If v and a have opposite signs, the object is slowing down.

#### Chapter 3 (part 2) Kinematic Equations & Free Fall :

**Four Kinematic Equations** **(for Constant** **Acceleration):**
These equations allow you to predict future motion without knowing the forces involved.

- vf = vi + aΔt (No displacement needed)
- Δd = viΔt + (1/2)a(Δt)² (No final velocity needed)
- vf² = vi² + 2aΔd (No time needed)
- Δd = ((vi + vf)/2) * Δt (No acceleration needed)

**Free Fall:**

- A specific case of motion with constant acceleration.
- **Acceleration due to Gravity (**g**):** On Earth, g = 9.8 m/s² (approximately 10 m/s² for estimation).
- **Direction:** Always directed **downward**, toward the center of the Earth.
- **All objects**, regardless of mass, fall with the same acceleration (when air resistance is negligible).
- **Choosing a Coordinate System is Crucial:**
  - If **UP is positive**, then a = -g = -9.8 m/s².
  - If **DOWN is positive**, then a = +g = +9.8 m/s².
  - At the highest point of its trajectory, an object's **velocity is zero**, but its **acceleration is still -9.8 m/s²**.

#### Chapter 4 Forces in one dimension

#### Chapter 4 (part 1) Forces & Free-Body Diagrams :

**What is a Force?**

- A push or a pull. It is a vector (has magnitude and direction).
- It can cause an object to accelerate or deform.

**Types of Forces:**

- **Weight (Fg):** The force of gravity on an object. Fg = m * g.
- Always straight down.
- **Normal Force (FN):** A contact force exerted by a surface, perpendicular (normal) to and away from the surface.
- **Tension (FT):** The pull exerted by a string, rope, or cable. Directed away from the object, parallel to the rope.
- **Friction (Ff):** A contact force that opposes sliding motion. Parallel to the surface, opposite the direction of (attempted) motion.
- **Spring Force (Fsp):** A restoring force; Fsp = -k*x .

Opposite the displacement from equilibrium.

- **Thrust (Fthrust):** A general propulsive force (e.g., from rockets, engines).

**Free-Body Diagrams** **:**

A visual representation of all the forces acting on a single object (the "system").

**Steps:**

  - Isolate the object of interest (draw it as a dot).
  - Identify all forces from the external world acting on it.
  - Draw vector arrows pointing in the direction of each force, with the tail on the object.
  - Label each force clearly (e.g. Fg, FN, FT).

#### Chapter 4 (part 2) Newton's Laws of Motion :

**Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia):**

- "An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and in the same direction, unless acted upon by a net external force."
- **Inertia:** The tendency of an object to resist changes in its motion. **Mass is a measure of inertia.**
- **Equilibrium:** If the net force on an object is zero (Fnet = 0), the object is in equilibrium (either at rest or moving with constant velocity).

**Newton's Second Law (The Cause of Acceleration):**

- "The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass."
- **The Fundamental Equation of Dynamics:** Fnet = ma
- **Steps to Apply:**
  - Draw a Free-Body Diagram.
  - Find the vector sum of all forces (Net Force).
  - Set Fnet equal to ma and solve for the unknown.
