## 11.1 Forms of Energy

- **Energy** is the ability to do work (unit: joule, scalar)
- **Kinetic** **energy**: KE = (1/2)mv2
- **Gravitational** **potential** **energy**: PE = mgh
- The height h is measured from a chosen reference level (where PE = 0)
- Only changes in potential energy are meaningful
- GPE can be negative if the object is below the reference level
- Elastic potential energy is the energy that is stored in stretched or compressed objects such as springs and rubber bands
- Chemical energy is the energy that is stored in chemical bonds
- Electrical potential energy exists due to position of charges in electric fields

Example Questions:

1. A 60 kg boy runs up 8 stairs, each 12 cm high. Find the potential energy gained.

- Total height gained: (8)(0.12) = 0.96 m
- PE = mgh = (60)(9.8)(0.96) = 564.5 J

## 11.2 Conservation of Energy

- **Law** **of** **Conservation** **of** **Energy**: In an isolated system, energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transformed
- **Mechanical** **energy**: ME = KE + GPE
- Conservation of mechanical energy (special case): If only gravity does work (no friction, no air resistance), then:
- KEi + PEi = KEf + PEf
- Also written as: △KE = -△PE
- If friction is present, mechanical energy is not conserved (some energy becomes heat), but the total energy is conserved

Example Questions:

1. A hammer is dropped from a height of 18 m. With what speed does it strike the ground? (Neglect air friction)

- Use conservation of ME: KEi + PEi = KEf + PEf
- Take ground as PE = 0
- 0 + mg(18) = (1/2)mvf2 + 0
- g(18) = (1/2)vf2
- vf = ((2)(9.8)(18))1/2 = (352.8)1/2 = 18.8 m/s

2. A skier slides down a frictionless slope from a height of 40 m starting from rest. Find speed at the bottom.

- 0 + mg(40) = (1/2)mvf2 + 0
- vf = ((2)(9.8)(40))1/2 = (784)1/2 = 28 m/s

3. A motorcyclist rides off the edge of a hill at 62 m/s. The bottom is 20.0 m lower. Find velocity at the bottom.

- (1/2)m(62)2 + mg(20) = (1/2)mvf2 + 0
- Cancel m: (1/2)(3844) + (9.8)(20) = (1/2)vf2
- 1922 + 196 = (1/2)vf2
- 2118 = (1/2)vf2 → vf2 = 4236 → vf = 65.1 m/s

4. A ball is thrown almost straight up at 12.3 m/s and falls back onto a roof 5.42 m above the launch point. Find speed just before it reaches the roof.

- (1/2)m(12.3)2 + 0 = (1/2)mvf2 + mg(5.42)
- Cancel m: (1/2)(151.29) = (1/2)vf2 + (9.8)(5.42)
- 75.645 = (1/2)vf2 + 53.116
- 22.529 = (1/2)vf2 → vf2 = 45.058 → vf = 6.71 m/s

5. From what height must a car be dropped to give it the same kinetic energy just before impact as when traveling at 60 km/h?

- First convert 60 km/h to m/s: (60)(1000/3600) = 16.67 m/s
- Set mgh = (1/2)m(16.67)2
- Cancel m: gh = (1/2)(277.9)
- 9.8h = 138.95 → h = 14.2 m

6. Three identical balls are thrown from the same height with the same initial speed: one horizontally, one upward, one downward. Which hits the ground with the fastest speed?

- Initial total energy (KEi + PEi) is identical for all three
- Final height is the same for all three (ground level)
- Therefore final \( KE \) is the same → final speed is the same
- Directions will differ, but the **magnitudes** are equal
- Therefore, all three balls have the same final speed
