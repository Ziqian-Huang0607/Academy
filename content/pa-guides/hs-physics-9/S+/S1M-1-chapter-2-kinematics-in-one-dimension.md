**Reference Point:**

The object/background used and assumed to be still to compare the motion of object(s).

**Motion & Particle Diagrams:**

Essentially shows the positions of objects at equal time intervals. A particle diagram is a simplified version of a motion diagram, where the object is replaced by particles.

**Vectors & Scalars:**

Scalars only contain magnitude, and Vectors contain magnitude and direction!!! Very important, do **not** mix these up.

Examples:

Speed, Distance, Time (Scalar)

Velocity, Displacement, Acceleration (Vector)

Velocity is represented by an arrow on a graph, where the length of the arrow is its magnitude, and the direction of the arrow is the direction of the vector.

**For calculation problems, you can choose to assume a direction as positive or choose a provided vector in the problem to be positive.**

**Distance & Displacement:**

Unit: meters

Imagine a lake: A hiker walks around the lake and records his path. This path is the **distance**. Now, imagine a line drawn from his initial starting point and his standing point now. That line is the total **displacement**. Distance is the accumulation of the path, and displacement is the object’s overall change in position. They are different.

**Speed, Velocity, & Acceleration:**

**Definition of Speed**: The distance the object travels per unit of time (m/s). (**Speed is written as just “speed” in formulas**)

**Velocity** is the same as speed, but since it is a vector, it has **direction**: Velocity is represented with a “v”, sometimes with an arrow on top to show that it is a vector. (The d here represents displacement, not distance.)

**Note**: It is quite common for the **units** given in the problem **may not** be **m/s**, so make sure to pay close attention to the values. It is recommended or even required for the final answer to be given in SI units, i.e., meters, seconds, kilograms, newtons, etc.

**Free Fall (And Variants):**

For S+, most free fall problems will **ignore** air resistance and only consider gravity alone. **Gravity** on Earth is generally assumed to be 9.8m/s^2. At a given location on the Earth and in the absence of air resistance, all objects fall with the same constant acceleration. – Galileo. Of course, **Gravity** is a vector, and its direction is towards the ground **or** the center of the Earth. In **Free Fall** questions, the initial velocity is **0**.

**Variants:**

There are a few variants of free fall, i.e. the object being thrown **up** or **down**, or an **elevation or depression*** compared to **ground level**. Essentially, either the initial velocity changes or the initial displacement is different compared to the final displacement. In these problems, you usually define upwards or downwards as **positive**. Free fall uses the **same** equations mentioned above for acceleration.
