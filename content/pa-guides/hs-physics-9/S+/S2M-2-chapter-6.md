**6-2 Work Done by a Varying Force**

**1. Work (Scalar)**

a. Definition: the product of the **force** and the object’s **displacement**.

b. Symbol: **W**

c. Unit: **Joule (J) 1 J = 1 N⋅m**

**2. Work done by constant force (constant in both magnitude and direction)**

Work is defined to be the product of the magnitude of the displacement

times the **components** of the force **parallel** to the displacement.

Formula:

W =F// d= Fd cosƟ

Ɵ: Angle between the direction of the force and the displacement.

F =F // cosƟ:Component of F parallel to d.

**Three Cases:**

a. F // d:

Ɵ=0, cos0=1,W=Fd, doing positive work.

Eg. You push a box forward: work done by push force.

Ɵ=180, cos180=-1, W=Fd, doing negative work.

Eg. Friction stops an object.

b. F ⊥d:

Eg.: You hold a heavy box to move forward at constant velocity: Your force on the book is upward

F ⊥d W=0

Centripetal force (motion of a planet around the Sun): FR ⊥v, W=0

c. F at an angle with d:

W=Fd = cosƟ

Ɵ = 90° ⟹ cos90° = 0 ⟹W=0 (No work)

*You hold a heavy box to move forward **speed up/slow down**

W done by your force on the book is not zero.

(3) Work is a scalar, which can be positive or negative.

a. cosθ > 0 ⇒ W **>** 0: force does **positive** work on the object⇒KEobject **↑(speed up)**

Eg. You push a car forward

b. cosθ < 0 ⇒ W **<** 0: force does **negative** work on the object⇒KEobject **↓** **(slow down)**

Eg. A ball stops rolling because of friction

c. cosθ = 0 ⇒ W **=** 0: force does **no** work on the object⇒ **No change KEobject(constant**

**speed)**

**3. Work done by a varying force:** **Graphical** **method**

- Draw **F-d** graph
- **Area** under the graph: work done by that force
- Area above d-axis: **+**
- Area below d-axis: **-**

**6-3 Kinetic energy and work-energy principle**

**1. Kinetic energy (Translational kinetic energy)**

(1) Energy

- Traditional way: the ability to do **work**.
- The ability of an object to produce a **change** in itself or the world around

(2) Translational kinetic energy

a. Definition: the energy of **motion**.

b. Symbol: **KE**

- Unit : **J (Joule)**

c. Formula: $KE=\frac{1}{2}m{v}^{2}$

Translational kinetic energy is directly proportional to the **mass** of the object and

proportional to **the square of the speed**.

- Mass doubles ⇒ kinetic energy **doubles**
- Speed doubles ⇒ kinetic energy **quadruples**

**(1)Work net:**

a.Formula: **Work net:**$\text{ Wnet }=\Delta k=\frac{1}{2}m{v}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m{v}_{0}^{2}$

b. Statement: The **net work** done on an object is equal to the **change** in the object’s

kinetic energy.

c. The work-energy principle is valid **only if W is the net work** done on the object (the

work done by all forces acting on the object).

**(2) Relationship between net work and kinetic energy**

Work is the transfer of energy by mechanical means.

Through the process of doing work, energy can move between the external world

and the system.

a. Net work done on an object is positive: the object’s kinetic energy **increases** by an

amount of W.

b. Net work done on an object is negative: the object’s kinetic energy **decreases** by

an amount of W. ⇒The **object** does work on the **external world**.

c. Net work done on the object is zero, the object’s kinetic energy remains **constant**

(speed is **constant**).

**6-4 Potential Energy**

**1. Potential energy:** the energy associated with forces that depend on the

**position** or **configuration** of an object relative to the surroundings.

**2. Forms of potential energy**

**(1) Gravitational potential energy**

a. Definition: The stored energy in a system resulting from the **gravitational**

force between Earth and the object.

b. Symbol: PEgrav or PEg

c. Formula: PEg=mgh

m: mass

g: acceleration due to gravity

h: height above the reference level

The gravitational energy of an object due to Earth’s gravity is the product of the

object’s **weight (mg)** and its **height h** above the reference level.

Reference level: the position where PE is defined to be **zero**.

The higher an object is above the ground, the **more** gravitational potential energy

it has

d. Change in gravitational potential energy:

∆PEg=PE2-PE1=mgh2-mgh1=mg∆h(2-1)

What is physically meaningful is **the change in gravitational potential energy**.

e. Relationship among Wext, Wg and PEg.

i) The work done by an external force to move the object (m) from point 1 to point 2

(**without acceleration**) is equal to the **change** in potential energy between

positions 1 and 2:

ii) The work done by gravity as the object (m) moves from point 1 to point 2 is equal

to the **negative of the difference** in potential energy between positions 1 and 2:

- If gravity did positive work: object’s gravitational potential energy **decreases.**
- If gravity did negative work: object’s gravitational potential energy **increases.**

f. Potential energy belongs to a **system** and not a single object along.

- Potential energy is associated with a force
- A force on one object is always exerted by some other object

⇒ Potential energy is a property of the system as whole. (object (m) and Earth (g))

g. Gravitational potential energy & vertical height

- Gravitational potential energy depends on the **vertical height** of the object

above the reference level.

- Change in gravitational potential energy depends only on the **change** in

vertical height and **not on the path taken**.

**Elastic potential energy**

a. Definition: the stored energy in an object as a result of its **change in shape**.

b. Example: **spring, rubber band, slingshots**

c. Hooke’s Law

- Statement: The spring force is directly proportional to the **displacement** of the

spring and **opposite** in the direction of the spring.

- Formula: Fsp = -kx

d. Spring force is a **restoring force**

- The spring exerts its force in the direction opposite the displacement, acting to

return it to its natural length.

e. Elastic potential energy:

Fsp=-kx

PEelastic = 1/2kx2

- k: spring stiffness constant
- x: compressed or stretched distance (elongation) from its natural length
- Choose the reference point for zero PEe at the spring’s natural position.
