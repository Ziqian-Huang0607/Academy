**Drawing Vectors:**

In problems that give you cardinal directions (north, south, east, west) and ask you to find the **final displacement**, it is required for you to at **least** make a graph using the **Tail to Tip Method**.

**Tail to Tip Method:**

Given two vectors with different directions, you reposition them so that it forms two sides of a triangle (**S+** only covers the **Triangle** method, the **Parallelogram** method is used only in **H**), and the resultant vector would be the **third** side length. (Note the arrow **direction**).

This is generally **good enough** for most questions that require you to find the **final displacement** or **average velocity**, but some may specify to draw the **vectors to scale** and to find the displacement graphically.

**Drawing to Scale** **Method:**

There are 4 key points to consider (They are worth points!!!):

- **The Scale**
- **The Ticks on the Arrow**
- **The Ratio calculated at the end to find the resultant vector**
- **The Direction***

*The direction usually uses simple **trigonometry** to calculate it accurately, but if the question specifies to **find the displacement graphically**, just use a protractor to find the angle. You’ll actually **lose points** if you calculate the angle when the question specifies for you to find it graphically.

To find the Resultant, just use the Pythagorean theorem & trigonometry to find the distance/speed and direction, respectively.

**Projectile Motion** **(Horizontally ONLY):**

These problems incorporate **both** the x-axis and the y-axis. Consider this as a **two-step** problem: Solve the x-axis vector, then y-axis, or vice versa. The vertical and horizontal motion are **independent**. In projectile motion, the **only** acceleration is **gravity** (Air resistance is ignored).

Since **S+** only learns horizontal projectile motion, there are a few things to remember:

- The **initial velocity** for the **y-axis** is **0**.
- The **velocity** for the **x-axis** is **constant**.

The velocity of one body relative to another.

The **relative velocity** of object A to object C is the vector sum of object A’s velocity relative to object B and object B’s velocity relative to object C.
