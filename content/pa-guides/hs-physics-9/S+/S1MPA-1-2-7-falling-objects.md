### Acceleration due to gravity (g)

**Definition:** the acceleration of an object in free fall that results from Earth's gravity, regardless of the object's material, mass or shape, the height it was dropped from, or whether it was dropped or thrown upward (there are small variations in $g$ at different places on Earth).

- On **Earth**: $g = 9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$
- On the **Moon**: $g = 9.80 / 6 = 1.6\ \text{m/s}^2$
- **Direction:** toward the centre of the planet (downward).

**Signs** (always define them):

- "**+g**": downward is positive, so $a = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.
  - Object **dropped**: $v_i = 0$ m/s.
  - Object **thrown down**: $v_i > 0$ m/s.
- "**−g**": upward is positive, so $a = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.
  - Object **thrown up**: $v_i > 0$ m/s.
  - Object **dropped**: $v_i = 0$ m/s.

**Kinematic formulas** ($a$ is $g = 9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$):

- **Velocity:** $v_f = v_i + at$
- **Displacement (position):** $\Delta d = v_it + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
- **Displacement (average velocity):** $\Delta d = \frac{(v_i + v_f)}{2}t$
- **Velocity (time-independent):** $v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2a\Delta d$ (or $\Delta d = \frac{v_f^2 - v_i^2}{2a}$)

### Graphs

**Velocity–time (v–t) graph** of free fall:

- always a **straight** line;
- **slope** = acceleration ($g$).

**Distance–time (d–t) graph:** always a curve, a **parabola** ($d = \frac{1}{2}at^2$).

**Converting the d–t graph into a straight line (for calculation):** plot $d$ against $t^2$, because $d = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$; the **slope** is then $\frac{1}{2}g$.

**Drop ratios** (from rest, after 1 s, 2 s, 3 s): speed ratio = 1 : 2 : 3; distance ratio = 1 : 4 : 9.

### Tips and types of problems

**Dropped object:** $v_i = 0$ ($\Delta d = \frac{1}{2}at^2$).

**Thrown downward:** $v_i \ne 0$.

**Thrown upward:**

1. $a$ is the same throughout (rising and falling): $a = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.
2. The initial $v$ is "+"; the initial $\Delta d$ is 0.
3. At the highest position $v = 0$, and $\Delta d$ is largest in the "+" direction.
4. If the object is above its initial position, $\Delta d$ is "+"; if below, $\Delta d$ is "−".
5. Rising: $v$ is +, $\Delta d$ is +. Falling: $v$ is −, $\Delta d$ is + (if above) or − (if below).
6. Same $\Delta d$ (height), same speed.
7. On returning to the original position: $v_f = -v_i$, $\Delta d = 0$, and the time rising ($t_{rise}$) equals the time falling ($t_{fall}$).

**Released from a moving object:** $v_i \ne 0$ m/s.

## 2.8 Graphical Analysis of Linear Motion

### Position–time (d–t) graph

**No motion (at rest):** a horizontal straight line (parallel to the t-axis).

**Uniform motion (constant velocity):** a slanted straight line.

- y-intercept: initial position;
- intersection of graph lines: the two objects meet (same position, same time);
- slope: average velocity ($\bar v$).

**Accelerated motion:** a curved line (parabola).

**Instantaneous velocity:**

- for uniform motion: equals the average velocity;
- for accelerated motion: the slope of the tangent line at a specific point.

**Instantaneous position:** read from the y-axis at any particular instant.

**Equation of motion:** $d_f = \bar vt + d_i$.

**Distance–time graph:** similar, but with no negative distance.

### Velocity–time (v–t) graph

**Uniform motion ($v$ = constant, $a = 0$):** a horizontal line.

- "+" or "−": the object is moving in the "+" or "−" direction;
- objects meet when their areas are equal;
- average acceleration (slope) = 0;
- instantaneous acceleration = 0.

**Displacement** = the area between the graph line and the t-axis:

- above the t-axis: $\Delta d > 0$ (+);
- below the t-axis: $\Delta d < 0$ (−).

**Uniformly accelerated motion (constant $a$):** a slanted straight line.

- slope = average acceleration = instantaneous acceleration;
- rising line = positive acceleration ($a > 0$);
- descending line = negative acceleration ($a < 0$);
- steeper line = larger magnitude of acceleration ($|a|$).

**Changing acceleration:** a curved line. **Instantaneous acceleration** = slope of the tangent line at a specific point.

Speed-time graph: similar, but no negative speed (never goes below t-axis)

Acceleration-time graph

At rest / uniform motion: Horizontal line exactly along the t-axis (a = 0)

Uniformly accelerated motion: Horizontal line parallel to the t-axis (constant a, either positive or negative)

Area: change in velocity (Δv)

Tips

Describing Motion:

1. Direction of motion (+ or -)

2. If speed is increasing, decreasing, or constant

3. Sign of acceleration (+ or −)

4. Type of motion (Uniform vs. Accelerated)
