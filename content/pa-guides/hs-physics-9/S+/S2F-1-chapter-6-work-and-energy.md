**6-5:** **Conservative and Nonconservative Forces**

Conservative force: forces for which the work done does not depend on the path taken but only on the initial and final positions.

Conservative forces look only at displacement, where only the endpoints matter. Therefore, different paths between the same two points result in the same amount of work.

Note: Not all forces may be conservative even when MEi = MEf. For instance, if your applied force* were to equal the force of kinetic friction (with opposing directions), these nonconservative forces cancel, with potential energy and kinetic energy being constant.

***Applied force is considered to be nonconservative**

Some Conservative Forces include gravity and spring force (These are the only two conservative forces covered in 9th Grade)

Nonconservative Force: Forces for which **Work** depends on the actual path of the force. There are two kinds of Nonconservative Forces: **Applied and Dissipative.**

Dissipative Forces are those where mechanical energy within a system is converted to heat or sound. (Friction, Air Resistance)

Applied Forces are those in which mechanical energy is added to or removed from a system without heat or sound (There are other cases, but we haven’t learned them yet, so just assume that the force in which mechanical energy can be added/removed is Applied Force).

For example, when you push a box up a rough incline, the kinetic friction constantly generates heat/sound as the box moves, but the total mechanical energy is increasing as you are applying force to a box at rest, where both the gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy increase.

**6-6 & 6-7: Mechanical Energy and Its Conservation**

**Mechanical Energy** is the sum of the kinetic and potential energy within a system.

**Principle of Conservation of Mechanical Energy**:

If only Conservative Forces act within a system, the total mechanical energy of the system never decreases or increases at any given time.

Formula: MEi + Wnc= MEf (Wnc would be 0 if only conservative forces are at play). Wnc is the total Work of all Nonconservative Forces.

Since Mechanical Energy is the sum of the kinetic and potential energy within a system, ME can be rewritten as KE + PE = KE + PEgravitational + PEelastic = 1/2(mv2) + mgh + 1/2(kx2)

**6-8 & 6-9: Law of Conservation of Energy**

There are some other forms of energy aside from kinetic and potential energy:

-Electric, Nuclear

-Thermal Energy: The internal energy associated with the random motion of particles.

Chemical energy in food and fuel: the potential energy stored by virtue of the relative positions of atoms within a molecule due to electric forces between the atoms (chemical bond)

**Law of conservation of energy**:

The total energy is neither increased nor decreased in any process. Energy can be transferred from one form to another, and transferred from one object to another, but the total amount remains constant.

Wnc = ΔKE + ΔPE

**6-10: Power**

The rate at which work is done. P = W/t or ΔE/t or Fvcosθ (assuming force is constant)

The SI Unit is considered to be the watt (W), or one joule per second (J/s).

Some other units encompass kW (kilowatt, or 1000 Watts) and hp (Horsepower, 746 Watts – **This must be known for the finals**)

**Efficiency**: The ratio of the useful power output to the power input:

e = Pout/Pin

Efficiency can never be greater than 1. In realistic scenarios, Efficiency is always less than 1. An engine can never create energy, and no engine can transform energy without some energy going to other non-useful forms of energy, such as thermal or friction energy.
