1-1 The Science of Geography

-Geography: the science that studies the relationships among natural systems, geographic

areas, society, & cultural activities, & the interdependence of all of these, over space

-Spatial: the nature & character of physical space, it’s measurement, & the distribution of

things within it

-5 spatial themes: location, region, human-earth relationships, movement, place

Geographic Analysis

-spatial analysis: geography synthesizes topics from many fields, integrating information to

form a whole-earth concept

-Process: a set of actions / mechanisms that operate in some special order

-Physical geo: the spatial analysis of all the physical elements, processes, & systems that

make up the environment

-Earth systems science: the area of study that seeks to understand Earth as a complete

entity, an interacting set of physical, chemical, & biological systems

The Scientific Process

-Scientific method: the traditional recipe of a scientific investigation

-hypothesis:

A proposed explanation for some phenomenon based on limited evidence

Not scientifically tested or proven

Based on limited data

-theory:

An idea or set of ideas that is intended to explain facts or events

Scientifically tested or proven

Based on a wide range of data

Human-Earth Interactions in the 21th Century

The Human Denominator

-Human denominator: the totality of human impact on Earth

Global Sustainability

-sustainability science: a new, integrative discipline, broadly based on concepts of

sustainable development related to functioning earth systems

-The Three Pillars of Sustainability:

1. Environmental: Earth's systems, biodiversity, reducing pollution, and resource

management.

eg. How does the activity affect the forest system? Does it cause positive feedback like

melting ice

2. Social: human well-being, equity, health, education, community

eg. How does it affect local populations?

3. Economic: economic growth, cost-efficiency, resource availability

eg. Does it provides jobs?

1-2 Earth Systems Concepts

System theory

-System: any set of ordered, interrelated components & their attributes, linked by flows of

energy & matter, as distinct from the surrounding environment outside the system

Open Systems

-Open systems: inputs of energy & matter flow into the system, & outputs of energy & matter

flow from the system. Not self-contained

-eg. Earth’s energy system. A forest

Closed Systems

-Closed systems: a system that is shut off from the surrounding environment so that it is self-

contained

-eg. Earth’s systems of physical matter & resources

System Feedback

-Feedback loops: pathways that allow output functions (that

influence its own operations) to return to various points in the

system

-Negative feedback: feedback info that discourages change in the

system, opposes system changes & leads to stability

-Positive feedback: Feedback info that encourages change in the

system, stimulates system changes

System Equilibrium

-Steady-state condition: when an energy & material system remains balanced over time, in

which conditions are constant / recur

-Steady-state equilibrium: when the rates of inputs & outputs in the system are equal & the

amounts of energy & matter in storage within the system are constant

-Dynamic equilibrium: a condition when a steady-state system demonstrates a changing

trend

-Threshold: a tipping point. Where a system can no longer maintain its character, so it

lurches to a new operational level

Models of Systems

-Model: a simplified, idealized representation of part of the real world

Systems Organization in Geosystems

Earth’s 4 Spheres

-Abiotic: nonliving systems

atmosphere: (energy) gaseous envelope

hydrosphere: (water, weather, climate systems) all water

lithosphere: (earth, atmosphere interface) earth’s crust & upper mantle

-Biotic: living system

biosphere: (soil, ecosystems, biomes) the zone where life exists, overlaps with the other 3

1-3 Location & Time on Earth

Latitude

-Latitude:An angular distance north / south of the equator

-Parallel: (latitude) a line connecting all points along the same latitudinal angle

-Physical distances of a degree:

A degree of latitude = 111 km.

A degree of longitude = 111 km at the equator, 0 km at the poles.

Latitudinal geographic zones

Longitude

Longitude: an angular distance east / west of a point on earth’s surface

Meridian: a line connecting all points along the same longitude

Prime meridian: a meridian designated as 0 degree

Great Circles & Small Circles

-Great circle:

any circle of earth’s circumference whose center coincides with the center of earth

the shortest distance between 2 points on earth

-Small circle:

circles with centers that do not coincide with earth’s center

all parallels except the equator are small circles

Meridians & Global Time

-In International Meridian Conference, the prime meridian was set as official standard for the

world time zone system Greenwich Mean Time (GMT)

-24 standard meridians, 1h between each

-A.M. = ante meridiem (“before noon”)

-P.M. =post meridiem (“after noon”)

Coordinated universal time

-Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) time-signal system replaced GMT became legal time

reference

-UTC is based on avg. time calculations from atomic clocks collected worldwide

International date dine (IDL)

-180。meridian

-marks the place where each day officially begins

(at 12:01 AM)

-west side of the line always 1 day ahead of east

side (east -> west, gain a day)

-this westward movement of time is created by

Earth’s turning eastward on its axis

Daylight saving time (DST)

-time is set ahead 1h in the spring & set back 1h in the fall

-the idea to extend daylight for early evening activities at the expense of daylight in the

morning

-used mainly in temperate latitude countries

1-4 Maps & Cartography

-map: a generalized view of an area, as seen from above & greatly reduced in size

-cartography: the science & art of map-making

The scale of maps

-scale: the ratio of the image on a map to the real world

-eg. appropriate scale for a local map is 1:24,000

-representative fraction (RF):

expressed in either colon or slash (eg. 1:125,000 or 1/125,000)

no actual units of measurement, both parts of the fraction are in the same unit

-graphic scale:

a bar graph with units to allow measurements of distances on the map

-if map is enlarged/reduced, graphic scale enlarges/reduces with the map, while written &

fractional scales become incorrect

-the greater the denominator in a fractional scale, the smaller the scale of the map

-Large scale = small area, high detail. Small scale = large area, low detail.

-eg.

Map Projections

-large-scale flat maps: 2-dimensional representations to 2 causes distortion

-globe: the only true representation of distance, direction, area, shape, & proximity on earth

-Properties of a globe:

1. parallels always are parallel to each other, always are evenly spaced along meridians, &

always decrease in length toward the poles

2. meridians always converge at both poles & always are evenly spaced along any individual

parallel

3. the distance between meridians decrease toward poles, with the spacing between

meridians at the 60th parallel equal to one-half the equatorial spacing

4. parallels & meridians always cross each other at right angles

-map projection: the reduction of the spherical Earth to a flat surface

-flat maps always possess some degree of distortion, much less for large-scale maps than

small-scale maps

Equal area / true shape

-properties: equal area (equivalence) & true shape (conformality)

-a decision favoring 1 property sacrifices the other

Class of projections

-main map projection classes: cylindrical, planar (azimuthal), conic

-the standard line/standard point is the only place where all globe properties are preserved

(areas away from this line become increasingly distorted)

-a standard parallel/standard meridian is a standard line true to scale along its entire length

without any distortion (areas away from this become increasingly distorted)

-Mercator projection:

a cylindrical, conformal projection

meridians as equally spaced straight lines & parallels as straight lines that are spaced closer

together near the equator

poles are infinitely stretched

presents false notions of size (area) of midlatitude & poleward landmasses (eg. Greenland

bigger than South America)

rhumb line: a straight line of constant direction (not the shortest route), cross meridians at

same angles

-gnomonic/planar projection:

all great circle routes are projected as straight lines

-topographic map:

A map that uses contour lines to show elevation and terrain.

the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) National Map provides downloadable digital topographic

data

1-4 Modern Tools and Techniques for Geoscience

-GPS, RS, GIS

Global Positioning System

-receives radio signals from satellites (at least 4)

-GPS receiver calculates position using trilateration, to reports latitude, longitude, & elevation

-helps refine knowledge of earth’s exact shape & how this shape is changing

-used to measuring plate movement

Remote sensing

-the acquisition of info about distant objects without having physical contact

-photogrammetry: deriving accurate measurements from photographs

Satellite imaging:

->physical elements of earth’s surface emit radiant energy in wavelengths that are sensed

by satellites

->receiving stations sort wavelengths into specific bands

->scene is scanned & broken down into pixels

Passive remote sensing:

-record wavelengths of energy radiated from a surface

-relies on sunlight/emitted energy (eg. our own eyes)

Active remote sensing:

-direct a beam of energy at a surface & analyze the energy reflected back

Geographic info systems

-a computer-based data-processing

tool for gathering, manipulating, &

analyzing geographic info

-spatial data arranged in layers

-composite overlay: combined layers,

the resulting synthesis, used to

analyze complex problems

-Geographic information science

(GISci): the fuel that develops the

capabilities of GIS for use within

geography & other disciplines

-geovisualization: the technique of adjusting geospatial data sets in real time

Data Diagrams

-Vector data:

Uses mathematical geometry (points with x,y coordinates) to draw shapes.

Are used to map distinct objects

-Raster data:

a grid of square cells (pixels). Each cell holds a specific value (like elevation, temperature, or

color).

not good for showing sharp boundaries. is best for showing continuous data that changes

gradually (eg. elevation, temperature, or satellite imagery)

Maps

-Thematic Map. Shows a specific theme (e.g., population density, climate).

-Reference Map. General-purpose map (e.g., political boundaries, place names).
